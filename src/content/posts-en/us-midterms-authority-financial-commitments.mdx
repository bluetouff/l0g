**Trump has lost control of Congress, according to Reuters and Associated Press calls on the morning of November 4. Democrats have won the House and Senate; full certification is still pending.** The new terms begin in January. For companies building facilities, borrowing money or awaiting approval, an immediate question follows: how much does a delayed decision cost when the money is already committed? <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

Ohio offers a concrete example. For new data centers or expansions requesting at least **25 MW** (megawatts of electrical capacity), AEP describes an agreement that commits the customer before power arrives. If the customer cancels or delays the project by **more than twelve months**, before the target energization date, it must reimburse **100% of buildout costs**, according to the utility. The electricity service agreement’s term begins at actual energization. Construction can therefore commit money while servers are still waiting to operate. Individual contracts are needed to establish exceptions and the treatment of different causes of delay. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

That mechanism gives the new balance of power in Washington an economic dimension. A company may be waiting for a government order, a permit or a sufficiently clear rule to begin operations. It may already have staff to pay, equipment on order and customers who have paid in advance. Political disagreement becomes a financial risk where it meets those obligations.

The documents examined here establish commitments made before the election. Their future cost will depend on actual decisions and the contracts affected: **no loss at Oracle or Meta attributable to the vote has been established**. Tighter rules can also make an investment more secure. In Ohio, protecting other grid customers accompanied the resumption of connection requests.

## The outgoing Congress faces the first deadline

The new balance of power begins with a transition. Terms arising from the election start on **January 3, 2027**. Until then, the outgoing Congress can still legislate. Its lame-duck period deserves to be treated as a decision-making phase in its own right. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

One economic deadline arrives sooner: **December 11, 2026**. The continuing [appropriations](/en/glossary/credits-budgetaires-americains/) law enacted on September 2 provides that general endpoint, subject to earlier replacement and specific provisions. Temporary funding comes with restrictions, including on some new starts and military commitments. The statute, rather than a headline about government funding, determines which activities may actually begin. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup>

**December 11** is a general government-funding deadline. A Treasury default concerns a separate problem: its ability to honour debt payments. Confusing them would overstate the risk established by this law. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup>

The useful investigation follows the money from appropriation to payment. Authorized funding may still await an agency commitment. A signed contract may contain conditions for work to start. Completed work may await acceptance. Each position leaves a different exposure with the supplier and calls for different evidence. Political conflict can become a business cost in those intermediate stages, well before national statistics can isolate it.

## Democratic leverage meets Trump’s veto

Control of both chambers gives Democrats greater influence over the legislative agenda and the conditions attached to an agreement. Enactment remains a separate hurdle. The president retains a veto, which requires two-thirds of each chamber to override. In the Senate, ending debate on ordinary legislation generally requires three-fifths of senators duly chosen and sworn. That cloture rule, commonly described as a sixty-vote threshold, does not govern every decision. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a></sup>

[Budget reconciliation](/en/glossary/reconciliation-budgetaire/) provides a special route for certain revenue, spending and debt measures, with limited Senate debate. Its scope is constrained and the president retains a veto. A simple majority opens specific possibilities that require examination bill by bill. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-06" aria-label="Source 6">6</a></sup>

The Senate also acts on nominations requiring confirmation. Committees can investigate through hearings, document requests and programme oversight. Those tools can influence executive conduct or reveal poorly understood financial commitments, potentially affecting lenders’ confidence. Their scope remains distinct from removing existing officeholders or changing private contracts. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-08" aria-label="Source 8">8</a></sup>

For investors, the task is to replace a political label with a chain of decisions. A local permit, an agency rule, a Senate-confirmed appointment and a tax statute involve different authorities. The opposition may prevent a new law while an existing rule continues to take effect. The executive may retain delegated powers while lacking additional funding it wants. Congressional control does not bring all those calendars into alignment.

## The distance between an appropriation and a payment

An opinion from the Government Accountability Office, Congress’s watchdog, exposes the tension. On **September 29, 2026**, responding to the president’s September 25 special message, the GAO argues that proposing a rescission does not authorize the executive to withhold appropriations through their expiration. It defends Congress’s spending power. This opinion establishes the GAO’s legal position; any eventual court outcome and the actual release of funds require separate evidence. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

A supplier can have the stronger legal argument and still run short of cash. While it pursues payment, employees, subcontractors and lenders continue to expect theirs. Bridge financing, legal costs and the scope for postponing its own spending then become decisive. For a small contractor, a few weeks of waiting may matter more than a programme’s headline budget.

Contracts can also soften the impact. When incorporated into a procurement, **FAR 52.242-15** provides for a written stop-work order and, subject to conditions, adjustments to price or delivery schedules and the treatment of certain costs. It also requires the contractor to minimize costs attributable to the stoppage. The actual clause, notices and contract terms matter more than a blanket assumption that every interruption falls entirely on the supplier. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-11" aria-label="Source 11">11</a></sup>

Risk is distributed in stages. Compensation may preserve part of the economics while arriving after the expenditure. A disputed entitlement can require interim funding. A later restart may allow delivery even though the cost of retaining staff has already been incurred. Assessing a government supplier therefore requires the decision suspending work, the provision allocating its cost, and the expected payment timetable.

## Treasury’s funding needs predate the result

On November 2, Treasury estimated **privately-held net marketable borrowing of $618 billion for October–December 2026**, followed by **$800 billion for January–March 2027**. The assumptions include end-quarter cash balances of $850 billion and $1 trillion, respectively. The release predates the election. Its borrowing measure is neither the deficit nor gross issuance. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup>

Those cash assumptions belong alongside the borrowing figures. A government can raise money to finance net expenditure and to change its liquidity buffer. Maturing securities and their refinancing pose a further question. Calling the published amounts an “election bill” would strip away their definition.

Political change may nevertheless affect what debt buyers expect. A durable budget agreement might reduce uncertainty. A prolonged confrontation might increase the compensation demanded for particular risks. Weaker expected activity could lower the anticipated path of interest rates. Such effects can offset or reinforce one another. Their direction cannot be read from party control alone.

Monetary policy has its own starting point. On **October 28**, the Federal Reserve held its target range at **3.75–4.00%**. It funds its operations outside Congress’s annual appropriations process while remaining subject to its statutory mandate and institutional oversight. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a></sup>

A company’s credit cost then adds several layers. The reference rate matters, but a lender can also demand more compensation because commissioning is less certain, contractual protection is inadequate or cash receipts have moved further out. Easier monetary conditions could therefore coexist with more expensive finance for a particular project. Establishing that outcome would require separate observations of market rates, credit spreads and guarantees.

## Investment plans arrive with existing tax rules

Existing regulation is part of the environment the new majority inherits. On **January 14, 2026**, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) issued guidance on **100% additional first-year depreciation** for certain qualifying property acquired after January 19, 2025, subject to the prescribed conditions. The provision follows previously enacted tax legislation. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-15" aria-label="Source 15">15</a></sup>

A deduction reduces the base on which tax is calculated. Its value depends on the asset’s eligibility, the taxpayer’s position and when the benefit can be used. Reimbursement of a machine’s purchase price is a different transaction. An expected tax benefit, a grant still to arrive and equity already paid in offer resources at different moments in an investment plan.

The comparison with appropriations is instructive. A company may have a tax regime in force while waiting for an environmental approval. Another may have its permit without the funding needed to proceed. The sum of announced support rarely establishes how much investment can actually happen. The resources need to be available together when spending becomes difficult to reverse.

## Bank rules arrive with an existing timetable

On **September 30, 2026**, the Fed finalized changes to its stress-testing framework. They include greater transparency for the 2027 test and the use of two-test averaging in the stress capital buffer from 2028. The Fed expects averaging to reduce **year-to-year volatility in requirements by about 50%**, without a material effect on their aggregate level. The percentage concerns fluctuations in requirements, not a halving of required capital. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-16" aria-label="Source 16">16</a></sup>

Three dates need to remain separate: adoption, legal effectiveness and implementation of a particular mechanism. The rule published in the *Federal Register* on October 2 takes effect on **December 1, 2026**, while the averaging mechanism starts later. An electoral map dated November 3 cannot replace that timetable. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-17" aria-label="Source 17">17</a></sup>

The underlying debate remains substantive. In his dissent, Governor Michael Barr warns that transparency could encourage firms to optimize portfolios for the test and that the changes could reduce responsiveness to certain risks. The Fed majority emphasizes greater predictability. The documents establish these arguments; they do not determine in advance which effects will prevail. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-18" aria-label="Source 18">18</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-16" aria-label="Source 16">16</a></sup>

More predictable requirements may help a bank plan its lending. Borrower selection will still depend on collateral, expected returns and demand. A construction project with increasingly uncertain permits may become less attractive to its lender just as the bank’s own capital framework becomes more stable.

The new majority has oversight tools and legislative routes available. The **Congressional Review Act**, which provides an expedited mechanism to disapprove certain rules, still requires a resolution to become law. A presidential veto remains possible. This investigation has not calculated the review window for each rule. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-19" aria-label="Source 19">19</a></sup>

## Crypto has more than one legal timetable

On **October 21, 2026**, the OCC, which supervises national banks among other responsibilities, announced a final **GENIUS Act** implementation rule scheduled to take effect on **January 18, 2027**. It covers issuers and activities within its jurisdiction, including reserves, redemptions and risk management. The bulletin identifies separate work on anti-money-laundering and sanctions requirements. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-20" aria-label="Source 20">20</a></sup>

The relevant stablecoins are designed around a reference redemption value. The economic issue is practical: what assets support that promise, who can seek repayment and under what conditions must an issuer operate? The OCC timetable crosses the congressional transition. Changing it would require an identifiable act.

**CLARITY, H.R. 3633**, requires a different kind of monitoring. The Senate’s official **September 15** vote records failed cloture on the motion to proceed to its consideration. That establishes a dated procedural obstacle; subsequent enactment would require further action. This review found no confirmation that it had become law. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-21" aria-label="Source 21">21</a></sup>

For a business, the distinction is between preparing for an announced implementation timetable and betting on the outcome of legislative negotiations. Both may require legal staff, control systems and authorization work, yet rest on very different levels of certainty. Treating crypto as a single election trade conceals that variation.

## Every tariff needs a legal basis

On **February 20, 2026**, the Supreme Court held in *Learning Resources v. Trump* that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) did not authorize the tariffs at issue. The holding concerns the statutory power under review. A notice from the US Trade Representative (USTR) published on **July 28, 2026**, separately documents trade measures under **Section 301**. Other delegated authorities therefore need examination before any conclusion that tariff risk has disappeared. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-22" aria-label="Source 22">22</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-23" aria-label="Source 23">23</a></sup>

The president retains options within delegated powers. Congress can amend statutes, courts can review their use, and agencies implement measures. These interactions can generate trade volatility even when institutions prevent a comprehensive reform. The election changes bargaining power; it does not assign one tariff rate to every shipment.

For a European equipment supplier, the analysis starts with the product, origin and importer responsible for payment. Commercial terms then determine how much of the cost can be passed on. A supplier that has guaranteed a price may have a different exposure from one with a revision clause, depending on the contract and applicable law. Goods already shipped offer less flexibility than an order still being negotiated. These are contractual mechanisms to investigate, not findings about a named company.

Uncertainty can also change ordering behaviour: accelerate delivery, find a different supplier or wait for clarification. Each choice may alter [working capital](/en/glossary/bfr/), the cash tied up between paying for purchases and collecting sales. A manufacturer can therefore feel an effect before it pays an additional duty.

## Electricity decisions are spread across institutions

Data centers give these dependencies a physical form. Marketable computing capacity requires equipment, usable premises and electricity. Funding can move ahead while one of those conditions remains unresolved. A single development may depend on local decisions, state approvals and grid rules. A victory in Washington does not merge them.

**New York’s Executive Order 62, signed on July 14, 2026**, illustrates why scope matters. It suspends certain discretionary environmental approvals from the Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC) for data-center applications not determined complete before signature. The definition includes a **50 MW** threshold and exceptions. The suspension’s endpoint is tied to the specified final environmental report and findings. The relevant provision explicitly does not apply to local-government permits. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-24" aria-label="Source 24">24</a></sup>

Application status can matter as much as project capacity. The twelve-month deadline elsewhere in the order concerns a water report; the permitting suspension is tied to other conditions. Attributing damage to a company would require identifying its permit and whether its application was complete at the relevant date. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-24" aria-label="Source 24">24</a></sup>

At grid level, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) opened proceedings on **June 18, 2026**, requiring six regional operators to justify or adapt rules for connecting large loads. Its stated aims combine faster connections and protection for other customers. An explanatory page from its Office of Public Participation lists filings expected on **November 16**, and **November 20 for SPP**. Docket orders should be checked for any changes; opening the proceedings does not by itself establish a final nationwide tariff. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-25" aria-label="Source 25">25</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-26" aria-label="Source 26">26</a></sup>

Ohio supplies a particularly useful counterexample to an exclusively negative view of regulation. On **July 9, 2025**, the PUCO approved a data-center-specific framework and directed AEP Ohio to lift its connection moratorium. Customer protection and restarting projects were addressed together. The regulator’s account independently corroborates the framework described by the utility, although it does not replace individual contracts. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-27" aria-label="Source 27">27</a></sup>

The White House had itself published a **March 4, 2026** pledge covering the cost of power supply, infrastructure and voluntary negotiation of separate rate structures. That political statement establishes neither the signature of every contract nor actual reductions in customer bills. It shows that cost allocation was an issue before the vote and across partisan positions. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-28" aria-label="Source 28">28</a></sup>

## Oracle and Meta: the commitments behind the construction

AEP Ohio’s agreement addresses a customer cancelling or delaying its project before service begins. Establishing how an administrative delay would be treated requires the covered causes, exceptions, reimbursable costs and signatory’s guarantees. Those terms determine how a public delay would reach a company’s accounts. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

The financing structure then determines where to look for the consequence. A project company may have little equity, a parent guarantee or an advance from its future customer. The same technical delay can create different strains in each case. Who supplied the cash, who is waiting for service, and what protection does each have?

Two financial disclosures reveal actual forms of this allocation. In its cash-flow statement for the three months ended **August 31, 2026**, Oracle reports **$11.363 billion** on the line for increased deferred revenue from customer prepayments with a significant financing component. This is a specific line in unaudited interim accounts. Cash arrives before the corresponding service; receipt does not automatically mean revenue was earned during the quarter. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-29" aria-label="Source 29">29</a></sup>

An advance can support the supplier’s development. It leaves the customer with a right to future service, subject to the contract’s protections. If delivery slips, having received the money does not itself resolve the obligation. Must capacity be supplied elsewhere, timing extended, compensation offered or cash refunded? Public accounts do not disclose every such right. **No Oracle project is identified here as blocked by the midterm result.**

Meta reports a different mechanism. Its data-center venture note places **maximum residual value guarantees at $41.493 billion as of September 30, 2026**. Payments can arise following termination or non-renewal and subject to other conditions. Meta assesses payment as not probable and has recorded no liability for those guarantees. The maximum is therefore neither a certain expense nor an expected loss. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-30" aria-label="Source 30">30</a></sup>

A residual value guarantee can cover the shortfall between a reference value and an asset’s value following a defined event. It may protect a financing vehicle while returning some risk to the guarantor. The apparent flexibility of a user’s exit rights must consequently be assessed alongside its ancillary commitments.

Meta’s ventures are in Louisiana and Texas. They are distinct cases from New York’s permits and AEP Ohio’s tariff. Comparing these documents explains risk mechanisms; it establishes no shared exposure to a single blockage. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-30" aria-label="Source 30">30</a></sup>

## Six months of waiting produces three different numbers

Consider an entirely fictional project to measure the price of waiting. **Every number in this model is an educational assumption**, independent of Oracle, Meta and any identified development.

A project is expected to generate **$5 million of net operating cash each month for 36 months**, before financing and tax. The alternative scenario shifts every receipt back by **six months**, preserving both the number and size of the receipts. During the wait, additional standby costs run at **$500,000 a month**. Initial investment, identical in both cases, is omitted from their comparison.

By the end of month six, the original schedule would have generated $30 million. The delayed case has spent $3 million on standby costs. **The cumulative cash gap is $33 million.** This measures the distance between two paths; it does not establish the credit line a bank must provide. Existing cash, deferrable supplier payments and new equity could change actual funding requirements.

What follows matters just as much. The delayed case eventually receives all thirty-six monthly contributions. By the end of month 42, the original path has accumulated $180 million and the delayed one $177 million after the additional costs. **The final nominal difference is $3 million.** The initially missing $30 million was deferred; the $3 million of standby expenditure was consumed.

A third measure incorporates timing. With a **hypothetical effective annual discount rate of 8%**, converted into its equivalent monthly rate, the original cash-flow path has an initial present value of about **$160.22 million**, against **$151.23 million** for the delayed case. The difference is **$8.98 million**. Discounting reflects that a later receipt has a different value from an earlier one. The chosen rate is an assumption, not an observed loan rate.

The measures answer different questions. **$33 million** describes the temporary gap between the paths; **$3 million**, the extra costs permanently incurred; **$8.98 million**, the difference in value under the discount-rate assumption. Adding them would count parts of the same effect more than once.

This relatively favourable example assumes every customer waits and all operating receipts survive. A contractual deadline, cancellation, equipment obsolescence or lower selling prices would change the outcome. Renegotiation might reduce the cost. These factors, together with debt, taxes and terminal asset values, are deliberately excluded to isolate timing.

## Europe is exposed through orders and financing

A European company can encounter this risk without owning a US site. If an American customer postpones construction, equipment delivery may slip. The supplier may already have bought components or reserved capacity. Its cash exposure then depends on deposits received, opportunities to reuse the equipment and cancellation terms. A US project’s entire value does not automatically pass through to its suppliers.

In its Economic Bulletin 2/2026, the ECB described channels through which trade-policy uncertainty affects euro-area activity, including delayed investment and cross-border commitments. Federal Reserve research published in 2019 also examines such mechanisms. These studies support investigating the cost of waiting. Their historical estimates do not measure the cost of the November 2026 elections. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-31" aria-label="Source 31">31</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-32" aria-label="Source 32">32</a></sup>

Responses can differ with project maturity. A manufacturer that has committed nothing may postpone a decision at limited immediate cost. One that has ordered bespoke machinery has less room to manoeuvre. A supplier paid partly in advance differs from one paid on acceptance. Exposure is sensitive to irreversible spending and payment order, as well as industry classification.

A macroeconomic estimate would also have to account for substitution. Equipment from a cancelled development may move elsewhere. Another project may accelerate while one is delayed. A restriction might avoid building a network that would have lacked a customer. Ignoring such changes would turn a list of adverse announcements into an overstatement of net costs. The available documents establish transmission channels, not a defensible figure for growth lost because of the midterms.

## Tighter rules can make an investment financeable

The strongest challenge to an exclusively alarmist reading is already in the evidence. Ohio paired protection against stranded costs with the lifting of a moratorium. The FERC presents its work as a way to connect large customers faster by clarifying their obligations. Neither action proves all affected investments will succeed. Both show that an additional constraint can be part of the solution. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-27" aria-label="Source 27">27</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-25" aria-label="Source 25">25</a></sup>

From a lender’s perspective, a clear rule can make a disputed cost calculable. For other customers, a guarantee can reduce the risk of paying for infrastructure with no user. For the developer, a firmer commitment may enable the grid company to start building. Those benefits need to be weighed against upfront expense and lost flexibility.

The question is distributive: who bore uncertainty before the rule, and who will bear it afterwards? Moving costs to the electricity applicant may reduce private project returns while protecting other ratepayers. Conversely, an unnecessarily burdensome process can immobilize capital without improving grid security. Observed delays, allocated costs and delivered capacity are needed to distinguish those hypotheses.

## The next decisive act will be a signature

Measuring an effect of the midterms will require linking a new decision to its scope, then to a contract and its payments. A vote, a blocked appointment or a changed approval may start that inquiry. Any loss must then be measured at project level, allowing for compensation and spending avoided.

The next documentary milestones are identifiable. The announced November filings in the FERC proceedings will expose operators’ proposals. December’s funding deadline will show the outgoing Congress’s choices. The arrival of new members in January will make their first decisions observable. The OCC schedule offers a test of regulatory continuity. Each date calls for reading published acts and checking any postponement. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-26" aria-label="Source 26">26</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-20" aria-label="Source 20">20</a></sup>

The power to make a company wait can become the power to make it pay. The bill will depend on the signature still missing and the one already binding a customer, supplier or guarantor. That is where a political confrontation can reach businesses, and where investment promises must survive a reading of the contracts.

## Method and limitations

Documentary review as of November 4, 2026. Election calls are attributed to their newsrooms; laws and accounts describe their state at the dates shown. Treasury estimates are dated November 2. Court rulings, opinions, bills and final rules retain their different legal statuses. Individual project contracts are needed to establish an actual exposure.

The educational model uses end-of-month cash flows: +$5m in months 1 to 36 for the original case; −$0.5m in months 1 to 6 then +$5m in months 7 to 42 for the delayed case. The monthly rate is r = (1.08)^(1/12) − 1; present value sums each month t’s cash flow divided by (1 + r)^t. Tax, financing, cancelled receipts and terminal values are excluded. Independent recalculation checked cash gaps, additional nominal cost and present values separately.
