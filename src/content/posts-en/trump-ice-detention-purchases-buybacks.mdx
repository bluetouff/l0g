On November 17, 2026, the US government bought the Tacoma detention center for $453 million. GEO Group, the seller, kept the contract to run it. The following day, the company announced plans to use the net proceeds and some of its operating cash flow to buy back its own shares. The filings show how a building can become federal property while the seller raises cash and keeps the same customer. [S12](#source-S12) [S13](#source-S13) [S14](#source-S14)

*The Trump Clan’s War Chest, part four.* Earlier stories covered [federal money redirected to detention](/en/analysis/trump-frozen-funds-federal-transfers/) and [Washington’s power to authorize payments from other accounts](/en/analysis/trump-venezuela-oil-custody-accounts/). Tacoma raises a practical question. Why would the government buy a facility it already pays GEO to run?

## Washington buys the buildings

Congress gave Immigration and Customs Enforcement, or ICE, $45 billion in July 2025 to expand detention capacity. The funds are available through September 30, 2029. ICE used the appropriation to fund existing centers during partial government shutdowns. Having money available for several years gives the agency room to plan property deals beyond the annual budget. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

ICE first pursued warehouses it could convert. Officials told congressional auditors that seven of the eleven purchased properties were slated for resale. Buying an operating center can make space available sooner. ICE officials also said federal ownership gives the agency more leeway in areas where state or local authorities oppose expansion. The Government Accountability Office (GAO) wants the agency to assess the full costs over the life of the facilities before it proceeds. [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03)

The sellers’ SEC filings document six completed sales between July and November, covering **eight facilities and $3.609 billion** in gross prices. CoreCivic sold four sites. GEO sold the three properties in its Adelanto complex together, then Tacoma. CoreCivic still runs the four sites and expects to adjust its contracts to reflect the change in ownership. GEO still runs Adelanto. Assigning each deal to a specific appropriation would require the department’s own accounting records. ## GEO keeps its customer in Tacoma

The sale agreement describes the assets and provides for payment at closing through an escrow agent. The center and related assets passed to the government. The operating contract is separate. It took effect in October and is expected to run through 2036. GEO forecasts about $140 million in annual revenue. ICE can terminate the contract, including if funding runs out. [S12](#source-S12) [S14](#source-S14) [S36](#source-S36)

<TrumpDetentionFigure lang="en" kind="ownership" />

The $453 million price has drawn scrutiny. GEO carried the center at about $60 million on its books at the end of September. Senator Patty Murray cites a local tax assessment of $72.6 million in her letter requesting an investigation. One figure reflects accounting rules and the other serves a tax purpose. To assess the sale price, it would take the appraisal used for the purchase, the full set of assets involved and the cost of a comparable way to provide the same services. [S10](#source-S10) [S15](#source-S15) [S36](#source-S36)

The same question hangs over Appleton, Minnesota. CoreCivic sold the center for $495.6 million in August 2026. Members of Minnesota’s congressional delegation cite a previous $75 million offer that fell through and are asking for the appraisal and terms of the federal purchase. The earlier offer included work to put the center back into service, according to an email cited in their letter. Comparing the offers requires knowing the services and guarantees each included. [S04](#source-S04) [S37](#source-S37)

## The sellers get cash

CoreCivic says it received about $1.6 billion from its four sales after taxes and costs. It put some of that money into an [accelerated share repurchase](/en/glossary/rachat-accelere-d-actions/). It paid a dealer $500 million and received an initial delivery of shares. The final number depends on the stock price over the period set in the agreement. The company also reports repaying more than $700 million in debt. [S04](#source-S04) [S05](#source-S05) [S06](#source-S06)

GEO used the two transactions differently. After Adelanto it redeemed $650 million in bond principal. It expects about $340 million net from Tacoma and plans to add operating cash flow for a $500 million accelerated buyback. As of November 26, the latest public records still describe that buyback as planned. [S09](#source-S09) [S11](#source-S11) [S13](#source-S13)

<TrumpDetentionFigure lang="en" kind="buybacks" />

For shareholders who keep their stock, a buyback can increase their stake in the company. The effect depends in part on how many shares remain outstanding after the transaction. The sale proceeds go to the company. Establishing how much a particular shareholder benefits would require knowing their position at the time. [S04](#source-S04) [S06](#source-S06)

## Trump’s disclosed holdings

Donald Trump’s annual disclosure for 2025 lists CoreCivic and GEO Group shares and reports dividends from GEO. His periodic reports record a GEO purchase in March 2026 and a CoreCivic purchase in April. They also show sales, including CoreCivic in February and May and GEO in July. The forms give dollar ranges for trades. The holdings on the dates of the sales and buybacks would take portfolio statements from those dates to establish. [S20](#source-S20) [S21](#source-S21) [S22](#source-S22) [S38](#source-S38) [S39](#source-S39)

The White House told *The Daily Beast* that outside managers make investment decisions through discretionary accounts. Connecting a stock trade to a government decision requires more information than the closing dates of the property sales. It would take records of the negotiations, the information available to the managers and the instructions they received. [S25](#source-S25) [S28](#source-S28)

For people in detention, inspections matter in daily life. In a separate November audit, GAO said ICE still needed to assess the effects of changes to its inspections. [S33](#source-S33)

In Tacoma, federal ownership can last well beyond the deadline for committing detention funds. GEO can keep earning management revenue for as long as the contract runs. To judge whether the government got a good deal, the purchase price has to be weighed alongside the cost of owning the building and the bill for running it. Only then can taxpayers assess the price Washington paid to take control of the site. [S02](#source-S02) [S12](#source-S12) [S14](#source-S14)
