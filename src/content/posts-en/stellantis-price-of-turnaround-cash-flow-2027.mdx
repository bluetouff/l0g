A car can leave the factory while months of supplier bills remain to be paid. At Stellantis, the group behind Peugeot, Citroën, Fiat and Jeep, the recovery also depends on that gap between deliveries, supplier payments and the next generation of models. Its 28 October results let us follow the money. [1](#source-1) [7](#source-7)

Stellantis absorbed **€4.009 billion of industrial free cash flow in the third quarter**. The nine-month outflow reached €4.930 billion. Those figures show the scale of the financing burden. They also sit alongside a genuine improvement: cash absorption over the first nine months was €2.379 billion lower than in 2025. The results released on 28 October describe a business still under pressure, with a recovery whose pace and sources deserve close examination. [1](#source-1)

The detailed cash-flow reconciliation provides the most revealing evidence. Industrial investment and the associated payment movements consumed **€2.198 billion less** than a year earlier. Industrial operating cash flow, before those investments, improved by just €252 million. For now, a lighter investment bill accounts for much of the progress. The ability to sell vehicles consistently at margins that can support the next investment cycle still needs to strengthen. [2](#source-2)

## Production schedules become payment schedules

A factory can slow down well before its supplier bills stop arriving. Stellantis explains the timing in its accounts: it generally receives vehicle payments within a few days of shipment, while paying for components and materials later. If shipments fall, cash receipts weaken quickly. Bills for purchases made when production was higher continue to come due. [7](#source-7), p. 40

That mechanism helps explain the third quarter and makes like-for-like seasonal comparisons essential. In 2025, industrial free cash flow was negative €4.304 billion in July–September, before recovering to positive €2.784 billion in the fourth quarter. Moving from a €1 billion inflow in spring 2026 to a €4 billion summer outflow looks dramatic on a measure that swings sharply within the year. [3](#source-3), [5](#source-5)

Management had already said on 30 July that second-half performance would be weighted towards the final quarter, following summer production shutdowns. The seasonal explanation therefore predates the October results. It supports the anticipated shape of the year, while leaving the question of full-year cash generation unresolved. [3](#source-3)

<StellantisFigure id="01" lang="en" />

Each of the first three quarters of 2026 produced a better industrial cash-flow result than the corresponding quarter of 2025. Nine-month cash absorption fell from €7.309 billion to €4.930 billion, a **32.5% reduction**, calculated against the amount absorbed a year earlier. The positive second quarter is part of that improvement. Extrapolating the summer outflow would discard important evidence. [1](#source-1), [3](#source-3), [4](#source-4); l0g calculation

Industrial free cash flow is a company-defined measure, separate from profit and from the cash flows of the consolidated group. It separates industrial operations from financial services, deducts investment and certain equity contributions, and includes adjustments for items such as disposals and pension contributions. Automotive lending has its own funding cycle. Combining customer finance with factory spending would obscure how much cash the industrial business generates. [2](#source-2), “Non-GAAP financial measures”

## Inventory belongs to different businesses

The favourable timing between receipts and payments is known as **[negative working capital](/en/glossary/bfr/)**. Part of the business is effectively funded by the timing of its commercial transactions. When activity slows, that support can unwind. Cash or other funding is then needed to settle purchases already made. [7](#source-7), p. 40

A sale to a dealer and a sale to a driver occur at different points in the chain. A vehicle shipped to an independent dealer may already have generated a receipt for the manufacturer while remaining on the dealer’s forecourt. A retail sale can, conversely, clear a vehicle delivered earlier. Registrations, consolidated shipments and vehicles still owned by Stellantis therefore describe different stages of the process. [2](#source-2), shipment definitions; 5, inventory by owner

<StellantisFigure id="02" lang="en" />

Cutting production to clear dealer inventory can depress near-term manufacturer receipts while improving future commercial conditions, provided it reduces the discounts needed to move vehicles. The reverse can also happen: pushing more vehicles into the network can temporarily support shipments without establishing stronger retail demand. Shipment volumes, inventory and realised pricing need to be read together.

For the fourth quarter, Stellantis expects higher shipments than in the third, a positive working-capital contribution and North American year-end inventory approaching its December 2025 level. Those are assumptions behind management’s stated outlook. Their delivery will have to become visible in subsequent accounts and dealer inventory trends. [1](#source-1)

## Lower investment payments do most of the work

The industrial cash-flow reconciliation goes beyond the general language of improving operations. Over the first nine months of 2026, industrial capital expenditure, capitalised development and related payable movements totalled **€4.811 billion**, compared with **€7.009 billion** in the same period of 2025. The favourable difference was €2.198 billion. [2](#source-2), “Industrial free cash flows”

Operating cash offers a useful contrast. Removing financial services, net of inter-segment eliminations, leaves industrial operating cash flow of €360 million, against €108 million a year earlier. That €252 million improvement is real, but small beside the reduction in investment payments. Changes in equity contributions, disposals and the pension adjustment complete the reconciliation. [2](#source-2); l0g calculation

<StellantisFigure id="03" lang="en" />

The favourable investment movement equals **92.4%** of the €2.379 billion net improvement in industrial free cash flow. This is an arithmetic contribution to the change in cash flow. The investment line includes payment timing as well as spending on assets. It cannot be read as an equivalent reduction in future productive capacity, or attributed wholesale to one management decision. [2](#source-2); l0g calculation

That distinction matters for 2027. An expense permanently avoided releases cash on a lasting basis. A payment pushed into the following year moves the funding requirement. Cancelling a programme can save money while narrowing the future product range. The aggregate accounts establish the improvement but do not fully separate those effects.

Research spending also cautions against an overly simple interpretation. Total reported research and development expenditure over the nine months was almost unchanged, at €4.467 billion against €4.482 billion. Less was capitalised and more was recognised directly as an expense. Stellantis explains that some platforms written down in 2025 no longer qualify for capitalisation of the subsequent development costs. An accounting change can therefore alter the split between operating expenses and investment while the overall research effort remains similar. [2](#source-2), “Research and development costs”

The relevant test is whether the smaller investment envelope can support product launches, quality and industrial efficiency. Cash savings become a durable recovery when the resulting product range can earn enough to fund the next cycle.

Management offers a specific explanation for lower capex. On the 28 October call, it attributed the change to the normal development cycle for new products following the 2025 programme reset, and said investment remained consistent with the plan presented in May. That argument needs testing against projects delivered: lower payments improve current cash, while the quality of investment emerges in launches and subsequent profitability. [11](#source-11)

## Europe weakens North America’s return to profit

Stellantis’s two largest regions are moving in opposite directions. North American consolidated shipments fell 11.6% in the third quarter, yet adjusted operating income returned to a positive €64 million, compared with a €475 million loss a year earlier. Enlarged Europe shipped 10.1% more vehicles but recorded a €311 million adjusted operating loss, after earning €157 million in the corresponding 2025 quarter. [2](#source-2), segment results

The company attributes the North American improvement to better net pricing and industrial costs, despite lower volumes associated partly with planned Jeep shutdowns. In Europe, higher volumes met weaker net pricing and a less profitable sales mix. **Mix** is the composition of sales across models, specifications and channels. A manufacturer can sell more vehicles while earning less if that combination delivers a lower margin. [2](#source-2)

<StellantisFigure id="04" lang="en" />

The margins show how little room remains: 0.4% in North America and negative 2.2% in Enlarged Europe. South America contributed €431 million of adjusted operating income and Middle East & Africa €244 million. They helped support group earnings, which totalled €256 million after the other regions, other activities and unallocated items. This profit map says more about the recovery than the change in worldwide vehicle volumes alone. [2](#source-2)

The comparisons use the segment structure published in 2026 and its restated prior-year figures. Maserati has been allocated across the regions. The old regional disclosures cannot simply be combined with the new ones. The European figures here refer to the Enlarged Europe reporting segment, which differs from the EU30 sales-market perimeter. [2](#source-2), [5](#source-5)

Further cost reductions can help. Their cash benefit will shrink, however, if competitive pressure forces the manufacturer to pass the gains on through discounts. Europe’s recovery will need to appear in the return earned on sales, alongside the number of vehicles leaving factories.

## Last year’s charges still generate this year’s bills

A strong base effect supports the improvement in reported earnings. The third-quarter net loss narrowed from €4.532 billion to €453 million. The 2025 comparison included substantial warranty and product-programme charges. Their absence on the same scale in 2026 makes the change in reported profit particularly striking. [1](#source-1), [2](#source-2)

The bridge from adjusted earnings to IFRS results matters too. In the third quarter of 2026, €256 million of adjusted operating income corresponded to an **IFRS operating loss of €331 million**, after €587 million of adjustments. These included €223 million relating to Takata and a €118 million write-down of the Leasys investment. Items excluded from adjusted earnings differ in both their economic nature and payment timing. [2](#source-2), AOI reconciliation

An impairment records a loss of value and may require no immediate payment. A provision recognises an obligation whose settlement may come later. Adding all such charges to already reported cash outflows would count some effects twice and mix accounting losses with cash payments.

Management now expects approximately **€1.6 billion** of 2026 cash payments associated with second-half 2025 charges, compared with roughly €2 billion in July’s outlook. The revision lowers the expected outflow this year. The documents reviewed do not fully establish how much represents a permanent saving and how much reflects the settlement timetable. That distinction matters for the funding requirement carried into 2027. [1](#source-1), [3](#source-3)

## Hybrid capital has helped hold the balance sheet together

At 30 September, Stellantis reported €42.652 billion of industrial available liquidity. That reserve includes funding resources beyond bank deposits. Its industrial net financial position, which nets the relevant financial assets against industrial debt, was positive €5.400 billion, down from €6.694 billion at the end of 2025. The measures answer different questions: available funding capacity and the net financial position. [2](#source-2)

For the entire group, the breakdown is explicit: €32.489 billion of cash, cash equivalents and financial securities, €13.859 billion of undrawn committed credit lines, and €132 million of cash classified within assets held for sale. They add to €46.480 billion. Industrial available liquidity is a subset of that total. Combining group credit lines with industrial figures would mix reporting scopes. [2](#source-2)

<StellantisFigure id="05" lang="en" />

A major contribution arrived in March. Perpetual hybrid securities raised **€4.927 billion net**. The instruments have no fixed maturity and allow the company, subject to conditions, to defer coupon payments. Those features support their classification as equity under IAS 32. The proceeds appear in financing cash flows, outside industrial free cash flow. [2](#source-2), financing activities; [7](#source-7), p. 80

The remuneration is substantial. The two euro tranches initially pay 6.25% and 6.875%; the sterling tranche pays 8.25%. The terms provide for later resets and periods during which Stellantis generally cannot exercise its redemption option. Coupon deferral is governed by specific conditions, and deferred amounts can accumulate. Accounting classification reflects the contractual rights without making the funding costless. [7](#source-7), [8](#source-8)

This capital gives the company time. It also helps explain why the balance sheet has held up while industrial operations continue to consume cash. Dividing available liquidity by one summer quarter’s outflow would produce no credible estimate of financial runway. Seasonality, maturities, credit facilities and operating requirements all change over time.

## Savings have to survive the pricing battle

The plan announced on 21 May targets **€6 billion of annual run-rate cost savings by 2028**, relative to 2025. In October, management said it intended to implement at least 40% of those efficiencies before the end of 2026. It also expressed greater confidence about exceeding future savings targets on the results call, as confirmed by the official transcript and Reuters [11](#source-11). These remain management objectives. [9](#source-9), [1](#source-1), [10](#source-10)

An annual run rate is the benefit measures would deliver after operating for a full year. Forty per cent of €6 billion equals €2.4 billion on that basis. A measure introduced in December contributes for only a small part of the current year. The pace reached at year-end and the savings actually recognised during the year are different quantities.

Cash delivery then depends on the costs of implementing the measures, product quality and selling conditions. Avoiding a repair reduces a future expense. Lower purchasing costs can improve margin. A discount granted to a buyer may consume the gain. Stellantis also expects about €1 billion of net tariff costs in 2026 and additional fourth-quarter pressure from memory chips and recalls. Those factors are already included in its outlook commentary. [1](#source-1)

A positive industrial free cash-flow result in 2027 therefore requires progress that lasts beyond a seasonal rebound and a favourable investment-payment schedule. European margins and industrial operating cash generation will provide more demanding evidence than the announcement that cost measures have been implemented.

## Two very different hurdles for the final quarter

At the end of September, cumulative industrial free cash flow stood at negative €4.930 billion. The 2025 full-year figure was negative €4.525 billion. The arithmetic is straightforward: **a €405 million fourth-quarter inflow would match 2025**; anything higher would improve on it. Full-year break-even would require a **€4.930 billion fourth-quarter inflow**. [1](#source-1), [5](#source-5), [6](#source-6); l0g calculation

<StellantisFigure id="06" lang="en" />

Repeating the €2.784 billion inflow of the fourth quarter of 2025 would leave 2026 industrial free cash flow negative by €2.146 billion. This historical-repeat scenario is an arithmetic reference point, neither an l0g forecast nor a quantified Stellantis target. It illustrates the distance between improving on the prior year and funding the industrial business over the full year. [5](#source-5); l0g calculation

<StellantisCashCalculator lang="en" />

How much will the recovery ultimately consume? The past can be measured: **€15.500 billion of cumulative negative industrial free cash flow from January 2024 to September 2026**, adding 2024, 2025 and the first nine months of 2026. That is the deficit in this measure over thirty-three months, including operations, investment and the company’s specified adjustments. It is not an estimate of spending exclusively on restructuring. [1](#source-1), [6](#source-6); l0g calculation

The additional amount required remains unknown. Stellantis has liquidity, has raised capital and is improving its results on a comparable-period basis. Those facts lend weight to the recovery case. Yet the reconciliation also shows that industrial operations have contributed only a small part of the improvement in cash flow, while Europe is losing money despite higher shipments.

The annual results should allow readers to separate a seasonal release of working capital from recurring progress. For 2027, the test will be practical: restore European margins, preserve North American gains and fund investment with more operating cash. The available accounts establish an improvement. The final bill remains open.

## Method and limitations

The evidence cutoff is 1 November 2026. The 2026 accounts are interim and unaudited. Amounts are nominal euros unless sterling securities are explicitly identified. Quarterly flows are not seasonally adjusted. Industrial free cash flow and AOI follow Stellantis’s non-GAAP definitions. Inventory, liquidity and net financial position are balances at a date, distinct from flows over a period.

Product-level explanations, expected savings and the 2027 target come from the issuer. Reuters provides independent reporting of management’s comments, not proof that the targets will be achieved. This investigation contains no supplier or dealer interviews and no private contracts. The official Q3 results-call transcript was reviewed, including its discussion of investment and the timing of savings. The complete slide deck and webcast were not used. [11](#source-11) The analysis relies on releases, the interim report furnished to the SEC and the other referenced documents. Calculations use the published definitions and retain their stated reporting periods.

## Reading guide

**[Industrial free cash flow](/en/glossary/fcf/)**: Stellantis’s measure separating financial services and including industrial investment and specified adjustments. **AOI**: adjusted operating income, excluding selected costs under the company’s definition. **Mix**: the composition of sales across models, specifications and channels. **Working capital**: the cash timing associated with inventories, customer receivables and supplier payables. **[Perpetual hybrid](/en/glossary/obligation-hybride/)**: a security without a fixed maturity whose terms may support equity classification. **[Run rate](/en/glossary/run-rate/)**: the annualised pace of an effect once measures are in place.
