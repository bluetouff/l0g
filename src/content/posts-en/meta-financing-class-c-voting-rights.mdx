An investor subscribing to Meta’s offering pays $625 for a Class A share. At the other end of the arrangement, Meta expects to receive its money later, when an [equity forward](/en/glossary/forward-sur-actions/) settles. Between those two events, a new share class is due to arrive: Class C, [non-voting shares](/en/glossary/action-sans-droit-de-vote/) carrying economic rights. The financing and the governance changes meet in the clauses governing that transition. [S01](#source-s01) [S02](#source-s02)

The common stock offering has been increased from $22.5 billion to **$25 billion**, comprising 40 million shares, excluding the underwriters’ option. Pricing took place on 26 October, with closing expected on 29 October. At this investigation’s cut-off on 28 October 2026, that closing remains ahead. The announced amounts describe transactions still to be completed, with several different payment schedules. [S01](#source-s01)

Understanding what Meta gains from this structure means following three things: the resources received by the company, each investor’s share of its equity, and the votes that confer decision-making power. Each follows its own rules. The arrangement gives Meta flexibility to finance expansion. It also defines where future capital providers will stand alongside a founder who already controls a majority of the votes.

## A cash-generating business meets heavy investment

The third quarter illustrates the financing challenge. Meta generated **$32.79 billion** of operating cash flow while capital expenditures, including principal payments on finance leases, reached **$34.91 billion**. Free cash flow was therefore **negative $2.12 billion**, under the company’s non-GAAP definition. These quarterly amounts are rounded; the figure gives the calculation using the published inputs. Meta expects **$135–145 billion of capital expenditure in 2026**, including principal payments on finance leases, to support its existing business and AI initiatives. The offering documents formally allocate the new proceeds to general corporate purposes. AI provides the industrial context; the prospectus preserves a broader financial use. [S08](#source-s08) [S02](#source-s02)

A business investing over several years must choose a combination of resources that leaves it enough room to operate: retained earnings, debt, new equity and future payments. Each choice distributes risk and rights differently. Shareholders provide capital in return for a claim on future economic results; their influence over decisions then depends on the securities they receive.

## The shares move before Meta receives the cash

The planned transaction starts with a securities loan. Participants in the arrangement borrow existing Class A shares, which the underwriters sell to investors. The proceeds go to the forward counterparties. Meta, separately, commits to deliver shares at a later settlement in exchange for the contract’s forward price. The securities loan then has to be unwound. Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs hold the principal banking roles in the arrangement. [S03](#source-s03)

This sequence allows shares to be placed in the market now while the company’s issuance and receipt of cash follow another schedule. Under the announced structure, the forward settlements are expected by **19 November 2027**. The documents also provide for acceleration or a replacement direct issuance in certain circumstances, including problems borrowing shares. [S01](#source-s01) [S03](#source-s03)

The $625 public offering price and the **$622.91875 initial forward price** belong to different stages of that transaction. Their $2.08125 difference per share amounts to **$83.25 million across 40 million shares**: a calculation of the underwriting discount from the published terms. The initial reference amount for Meta is therefore $24.91675 billion, before expenses and subsequent adjustments. [S02](#source-s02)

That qualification matters. The contract adjusts the price for a floating interest-rate factor, a spread and expected dividends; provisions involving Class C introduce further adjustments. The initial price is a starting point. Establishing the amount actually received will require the settlement disclosures and the parameters applied. The underwriting discount therefore measures one component of the transaction’s cost. [S02](#source-s02) [S03](#source-s03)

Accounting follows a further timetable. Meta expects to record the forwards as derivatives measured at fair value through income. Potential shares can also enter diluted earnings-per-share calculations before their legal issuance, under the conditions described in the prospectus. A contract can therefore affect reported results while the cash remains due in the future. [S03](#source-s03)

## A new share class separates funding from voting

Class A common shares carry one vote each; Class B shares carry ten. The new Class C shares will have economic rights of equal rank, subject to the charter’s provisions, and no ordinary votes, with applicable statutory and charter exceptions. At **1 October 2026**, Zuckerberg controlled **60.6% of the voting power**, according to Meta’s information statement. That date precedes the financing transactions examined here. [S04](#source-s04)

The planned initial stock dividend will distribute **one C share for each A or B share**. The charter is due to become effective on 19 January 2027; the exact dividend record and payment dates remain to be set in the documents reviewed. Assuming the capital structure otherwise stays unchanged, the distribution mechanically doubles the common share count while preserving each holder’s fraction of that total. [S06](#source-s06)

Consider a fictional company with 80 A shares held by the public and 20 B shares held by its founder. The founder holds 20% of the shares and 200 of 280 votes, or 71.4%. After one C is distributed per existing share, the founder holds 20 B and 20 C: 40 out of 200 shares, still 20%, and the same 200 out of 280 votes. This is the arithmetic of a proportional distribution. The prices the market assigns to the different classes will also depend on their rights, liquidity and demand.

Class C then becomes available for future issuance. Meta could use it to raise funds, compensate employees or pay for an acquisition, depending on the transactions it pursues. Those providing the consideration would receive an economic interest; existing A and B holders’ relative voting power would be preserved. This is the financing role Meta describes alongside the continuation of a founder-led business. [S07](#source-s07) [S11](#source-s11)

The forward arrangement prepares for precisely this possibility. Following the C distribution and approval of its listing, the agreements are to split into A and C transactions. Subject to conditions, Meta will also be able to settle the A transaction by delivering C shares, with a price adjustment. The class delivered and the proceeds received will therefore depend on the contractual route ultimately taken. [S02](#source-s02)

## Equity dilution and stable voting power


Return to the fictional company after its dividend: 200 shares, of which the founder holds 40, retaining those shares without subscribing to the next issuance. If it issues **40 new C shares**, the founder’s share fraction falls from 20% to **16.7%**. Voting power remains **71.4%**. With **40 new A shares**, the founder would also hold 16.7% of the shares, but only **62.5%** of the new voting total. These calculations isolate the mechanism at an identical number of shares issued; different A and C prices could produce different amounts raised.

An issuance subscribed by new capital providers reduces existing shareholders’ fraction of the equity and brings consideration into the company. The effect on the value of their investment depends on the price obtained and what the company does with that consideration. An acquisition can create or destroy value; infrastructure can earn a return above or below its cost. The ownership percentage describes the division. The next task is to assess what is being divided.

This distinction also clarifies a secondary sale. When a shareholder sells C shares already held, the seller receives the proceeds and the outstanding share count stays constant. In a new issuance, the company receives the consideration and the share count rises. In the initial proportional dividend, each holder receives a share of the new allocation while supplying no new funding to Meta.

Class C preserves the relative voting power of all A and B holders. The most consequential effect concerns the person who already has a majority: that majority can remain intact as other investors finance the company. The economic importance of this benefit will depend on actual issuances, their prices and changes in voting-share ownership.

## The founder’s 80% subject to matching rules

Meta’s ability to issue C shares and Zuckerberg’s freedom to sell his own are governed separately. The Founder Alignment Agreement, signed on 20 October, places **80% of the initial C shares received by his holder group** under a matching rule; the other 20% are free of that restriction. The contract links sales of the affected shares to a sale of voting shares, conversion of B into A, or use of credits accumulated earlier. [S06](#source-s06)

A B share converted into A retains its place in the equity while moving from ten votes to one. That illustrates the intended connection between certain economic disposals by the founder’s group and a reduction in its voting weight. The detailed provisions include an adjustment ratio, permitted transfers and procedures for curing errors. The full contract therefore determines the implications of a particular sale. [S04](#source-s04) [S05](#source-s05)

The restriction eases over time: **2.5% of the initial quantity** of C shares is released for each qualifying quarter, subject to continued full-time service on the executive team or as executive chair. The mechanism runs over 32 qualifying quarters, which may be non-consecutive. It begins at the end of the first full calendar quarter following payment of the dividend. [S05](#source-s05)

On an illustrative base of 100 initial C shares, 20 are free of the matching restriction at the outset, rising to 30 after four qualifying quarters and 60 after sixteen. This progression shows how the agreement exchanges continued leadership for greater freedom to sell. Shares still subject to the rule can also be sold in compliance with the matching conditions. The arrangement therefore provides several routes to liquidity, with different effects on voting power.

For other shareholders, the question is the balance obtained: what continuity of leadership do they want to finance, for how long, and with what ability to intervene? Assessing that bargain requires reading both the options opened to the company and the obligations accepted by its leader.

## Two offerings and a further programme under consideration

The common stock forward is accompanied by a separate **$12.5 billion gross offering** of mandatory convertible preferred securities. These carry a **6.50% cumulative annual dividend**, with payment subject to the contractual terms and board declaration; payment can be in cash or shares under specified conditions. Mandatory conversion is scheduled for around **15 August 2029**. [S01](#source-s01)

These securities create another timetable: funding at placement, intermediate remuneration and, ultimately, common shares. The conversion formula determines the quantity delivered; the arrival of C can also change the underlying shares and conversion ratios. The value of the shares received can fall below the amount invested. Preferred ranking defines holders’ financial rights, while the conversion formula shapes their market exposure. [S12](#source-s12)

Meta is also considering an **approximately $60 billion at-the-market programme in C shares**, involving sales into the market over time. At launch, the company said the relevant agreement was still to be entered into, with timing and terms to be determined later. This represents an additional financing route yet to be put in place. [S11](#source-s11)

Together, these routes allow funding to be organised across several horizons. They also require several measurements: placement size, net proceeds after expenses, adjusted forward price, future conversion and actual sales under any eventual programme. Adding the envelopes can describe an ambition; measuring available resources requires dates and conditions for the cash receipts.

## Suppliers are taking an equity position too

On 26 October, Nvidia announced plans to invest **$3 billion** in Meta’s equity offering while expanding its technological collaboration with the company. The following day, AMD announced **$1 billion**, also in a statement about their infrastructure partnership. At the 28 October cut-off, these statements describe investments still to be completed. [S13](#source-s13) [S14](#source-s14)

Their dual position deserves attention. As suppliers, these businesses seek demand for their equipment and software. As investors, they gain exposure to their customer’s equity value. An industrial relationship and a financial one can reinforce each other: the former supports business activity, while the latter shares part of its risk and upside.

The announcements establish that dual relationship. Demonstrating a purchase obligation tied to the investment, or measuring a specific return of funds to a supplier, would require the corresponding contracts and cash flows. The next research question lies there: what commercial dependencies accompany the equity exposure, and at what price for each party?

## The future price of a vote

Facebook’s history contains a separate precedent. In April 2016, the company proposed two C shares for each A or B. Its board abandoned that reclassification in September 2017. The 2026 structure has its own documents, its one-C-per-share ratio and its founder agreement. The archives show the same governance problem returning at a different stage of the company’s industrial development. [S09](#source-s09) [S10](#source-s10)

In 2026, the new structure received the requisite shareholders’ written consent on 20 October. The information statement specifies that separate A-holder approval is not a condition of the transaction. It also presents the special committee’s favourable assessment and the structure’s risks, including a possible market discount on C shares. Those assessments and risks are the issuer’s own disclosures. [S06](#source-s06) [S04](#source-s04)

An eventual price difference between A and C would have practical financial consequences. Raising the same amount through a lower-priced share requires issuing more shares. Preserving voting power can therefore come with greater economic dilution, depending on observed prices. Future trading will allow that trade-off to be examined; this investigation treats a discount as a risk and assigns it no percentage estimate.

Meta’s arrangement structures an exchange across several dimensions. The company gains financing routes and flexibility over timing. Investors receive economic rights with different degrees of power. The founder retains an architecture favourable to his control, alongside specific rules for some of his disposals.

Computing capacity is paid for with money, contracts and time. The terms of its financing also help determine who will make the next decisions once the equipment is running. Meta’s subsequent issuances will make that connection between investment and authority measurable.
