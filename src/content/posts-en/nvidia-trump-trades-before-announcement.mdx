**Donald Trump says he persuaded Nvidia’s board to bring back Jensen Huang. Disclosures show that his own accounts had a substantial financial interest in Nvidia.** Put those facts together and the question is unavoidable. Who knew the board’s decision before investors did, and did anyone trade on that knowledge? A story about a spectacular options bet spread on Monday. Its numbers came from social media posts. The government filings, the public timeline and the exchange’s rules give us a way to test that story. ## From the board’s decision to the public release

Nvidia’s filing dates Huang’s reinstatement to Sunday, November 22, with immediate effect. As we reported in [part XXIII](/en/analysis/nvidia-jensen-huang-return-board-oversight/), the vote was unanimous and restored Huang as president, chief executive and director. The filing gives the date and leaves the hour open. The Wall Street Journal reports that directors spent hours talking to Huang on Sunday afternoon and that Trump knew by Sunday night that the board would bring him back. The Journal attributes those details to people familiar with the matter. [S01](#source-S01) [S16](#source-S16)

At **03:54 on Monday, November 23, Paris time**, or 9:54 p.m. Sunday in Washington, Trump publicly urged the board to rehire Huang. His message expressed a preference. Around **14:00 Paris time**, he spoke by phone with PBS correspondent Liz Landers, who says he hinted twice that Huang would return. Landers posted her account at **14:29:54**. Her account gave investors new public information about Trump’s expectations, while the company’s decision was still unpublished. Nvidia issued its release at **14:55**, and the SEC accepted its 8-K at **14:57:54**. The exact order of the Sunday vote and Trump’s first post remains unknown. ## The options claim needs an execution record

At **15:15:20 Paris time**, the account MrMikeInvesting posted about a purported Nvidia options bet. Senator Ruben Gallego called for an investigation of positions taken before the announcement at **15:45**, and accused the White House of corruption in the same post. Liquid posted its version at **16:34:49**. Mike’s post said an unidentified trader had spent **$3.4 million** near the close on Friday, November 20, on Nvidia calls with a **$160 strike** expiring Monday, and put their value on Monday at **$312 million**. Liquid put that figure forward as the value of the position. The reported return was about **9,000%**, with no trade confirmation or exit attached. The chart in the first post shows the underlying stock. [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03) [S05](#source-S05)

A call gives its holder the right to buy shares at the strike price. Verifying the position requires the exact contract series, the quantity, the premiums and their execution times. An image of the underlying stock chart cannot supply those details. A screen showing a large options print would still leave questions about which side initiated the trade, whether it opened a new position and whether it was one leg of a spread. A claim about the value of the calls on Monday would then need quotes or transactions for those contracts, at a time and size at which the position could actually have traded. A realized gain would require an exit or exercise record. The historical options prices and executions could not be independently checked through Massive Market Data. [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03) [S04](#source-S04)

Cboe’s listings establish that Nvidia had Monday expirations. Its C1 exchange had also introduced premarket trading in eligible Nvidia options from **13:30 to 15:25 Paris time**, with regular trading starting at **15:30**. An eligible option could therefore trade after the Landers post and before Nvidia’s announcement. Friday trades, Monday trades before the press release, and posts recounting a trade afterward belong to different parts of the timeline. The market schedule establishes an opportunity to trade. An execution record would show whether anyone took it. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03) [S04](#source-S04) [S15](#source-S15)

## Trump’s holdings make the question concrete

Trump’s certified financial disclosure for **2025** includes two Nvidia asset lines valued at **$5,000,001 to $25 million each** that report dividend income. The later periodic report lists four purchases and one sale under Nvidia’s company name in September 2026. They include a **$100,001 to $250,000** purchase on September 2 and a **$1,001 to $15,000** sale on September 24. The report groups amounts in bands and gives transaction dates, without a share count or an instrument identifier for each line. The combined record documents exposure and subsequent activity. Account statements and trade records would be needed to establish the position around the announcement. [S07](#source-S07) [S08](#source-S08) [S10](#source-S10)

The Financial Times estimated that the Monday rally could have lifted the value of Trump’s Nvidia holdings by up to **$15 million**. Its figure depends on reconstructing a possible position as of September 30 and carrying it forward. The upper bound describes an estimated increase in portfolio value. The exact position at Monday’s close and any proceeds from sales remain unknown. White House spokesperson Davis Ingle says independent financial institutions run the accounts using model portfolios that replicate indices, and that Trump and his family cannot direct transactions. That explanation addresses who can place orders. The economic exposure documented in the filings still matters when the president chooses to intervene at a company in the portfolio. Lawmakers had questioned the timing of an earlier Nvidia purchase. At a **June 3** hearing, Elizabeth Warren asked Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent whether the SEC should look into a disclosed **January 6, 2026** purchase in the **$500,001 to $1 million** band. The Commerce Department issued its announcement on H200 export licensing on **January 13**, and the rule took effect on **January 15**. The administration had already publicized the policy direction on **December 8, 2025**. Bessent pointed to outside managers. Establishing what the trader knew and who directed the purchase would take evidence beyond those dates. [S23](#source-S23)

## Nvidia gave to two separate recipients

The inaugural committee’s filing with the Federal Election Commission records a **$1 million donation from Nvidia Corporation**, received by the Trump Vance Inaugural Committee on January 13, 2025. The White House ballroom donation has a separate documentary trail. Nvidia confirmed in a December 16, 2025 letter to lawmakers that it had donated to the Trust for the National Mall, a nonprofit that partners with the National Park Service. Nvidia described the contribution as voluntary and charitable. The letter gives no amount. The Washington Post later reported a **$25 million commitment** based on an internal donor list it reviewed. Nvidia’s spokesman told the paper the gift was independent of its business interests. [S11](#source-S11) [S12](#source-S12) [S13](#source-S13)

The recipient, timing and proof differ for each gift. The inaugural committee reported money received. Nvidia confirms the ballroom donation, and the Post’s records supply the amount of the commitment. A receipt with the amount and dates of any ballroom installments would add a layer of evidence. Senator Richard Blumenthal had already renewed his request for records from Huang on November 5, before Huang’s departure and return. He asked for production to begin by November 30. Any agreement linking donations to official favors would require its own evidence. [S11](#source-S11) [S12](#source-S12) [S13](#source-S13) [S14](#source-S14)

## The president’s case for bringing Huang back

Trump said in his CNBC interview that he spoke to board members and that his intervention was important. He argued that Huang’s expertise was a strategic asset the United States could lose to China, and acknowledged that the market sell-off probably affected his thinking. The Journal describes administration officials relaying assurances they attributed to Attorney General Todd Blanche, and directors weighing Huang’s return against a thin field of successors. The board says it was satisfied with Huang’s candor and capacity to lead after talking to him, and cites input from senior US officials in its assessment of the security risk. These accounts provide several reasons to examine alongside Trump’s exposure. The board’s minutes and the advice it received could show how those factors were weighed. [S01](#source-S01) [S15](#source-S15) [S16](#source-S16) [S17](#source-S17)

Asked on CNBC whether he had considered a US government stake in Nvidia, Trump said he would have liked it and joked about the prospect of an insider-trading suit against the government. He later posted a manipulated image recasting Huang in the pose Trump struck after the 2024 shooting in Butler, Pennsylvania, with Huang holding up a red bra. The interview shows how openly Trump took credit for a private company’s decision. The stake was raised as a question on air. The securities filing documents a board vote and the restoration of Huang’s corporate offices. [S01](#source-S01) [S17](#source-S17)

Nvidia says its independent inquiry will continue. Export enforcement is moving separately. On **November 23** the Federal Register released a public-inspection copy of a BIS rule adding seven companies tied to the Megaspeed case to the Entity List. The text ties its effective date to public inspection. Formal publication is scheduled for **November 25**. [S01](#source-S01) [S22](#source-S22)

## The records an investigation would need

Gallego’s request points to an identifiable set of transactions that regulators could examine. The [Consolidated Audit Trail](/en/glossary/consolidated-audit-trail/) brings together order events in listed options and US equities. Regulators can trace routes, modifications, cancellations and fills, and follow account links where the records permit. It would take that trail, together with broker records and communications, to identify a beneficial owner, reconstruct the position and establish how information reached the decision-maker. US securities rules distinguish trading on material nonpublic information in breach of a duty of trust or confidence from a trade made on information already circulating in public. The time, size and profitability of a trade are questions to investigate. [S05](#source-S05) [S06](#source-S06) [S21](#source-S21)

Broker files matter even where regulators have a market-wide trail. The SEC proposed changes on November 20, as we explained in [our analysis of the CAT](/en/analysis/sec-cat-wall-street-audit-trail/). A January SEC order temporarily extends relief for certain links between customer orders and orders that brokers place on their behalf, where the broker has no systematic or direct connection between the systems that handle each side. The relief is specific. It illustrates why a regulator may have to go beyond the central database to match some orders to the accounts behind them. [S21](#source-S21)

Congress is asking for a different set of records. The three House chairs reconfirmed transcribed interviews with Huang and Nvidia counsel Tim Teter on December 1 and 2. Senator Elizabeth Warren asked the administration to name the officials who advised the board and explain their input. The basic federal financial-conflict statute in Section 208 operates through a definition that excludes the president and vice president. That statutory distinction leaves Congress with a question about how presidential discretion was exercised while the president’s accounts had a financial interest in the company. The dated approvals, board communications, portfolio records and broker executions are where the two inquiries could meet.
