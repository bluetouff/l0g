Kalshi has ended its relationship with Donald Trump Jr. as a private warning from the CFTC becomes public. The announcements invite a question: did the split with the president’s son influence the regulator’s treatment of the exchange? The information available on November 12, 2026 establishes no retaliation. It leaves crucial gaps between the dates of publication and the private decisions behind them. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03)

These [prediction markets](/en/glossary/marche-predictif/) trade contracts whose payout depends on the outcome of a defined event. The terms specify how that outcome is determined. [S09](#source-S09)

The business relationships are documented. Kalshi appointed Trump Jr. as an adviser in January 2025. That August, Polymarket announced his appointment to its advisory board and an investment from 1789 Capital, where he is a partner. Kalshi now attributes the separation to the investment firm’s increased stake in its competitor. That explanation comes from the company; the effective termination date and exit agreement remain undisclosed. [S06](#source-S06) [S07](#source-S07) [S01](#source-S01)

The stakes extend beyond an advisory contract. Federal decisions influence which products an exchange can offer, how it distributes them and what compliance costs it incurs. Political connections deserve scrutiny for their economic consequences. That scrutiny also needs to account for earlier regulatory warnings, decisions benefiting Kalshi and the investigation into Polymarket.

## A public split with an incomplete private timeline

On November 10, POLITICO reported that the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, the US derivatives regulator, had warned Kalshi of potentially imminent charges. An anonymous person familiar with the matter said the agency was examining notifications concerning dozens of new products and had been investigating for months. Reuters also reported the warning. The two outlets’ accounts should not be treated as evidence from independently identified witnesses. [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03)

Two days later, Reuters reported the end of Trump Jr.’s advisory role. A Kalshi spokesperson described the decision as mutual and said discussions began in August 2026, following further investment by 1789 in Polymarket. The announcement of Trump Jr.’s Polymarket role in August 2025 belongs to an earlier sequence of events. [S01](#source-S01) [S07](#source-S07)

The effective termination date, the date of the private regulatory warning and the point at which officials learned about the separation remain unknown. The public timeline therefore cannot establish the sequence of decisions necessary to substantiate a retaliation claim.

The scope of the dispute is contested too. Kalshi acknowledges a procedural error affecting a handful of contracts, many of which were not live, and says it is addressing the problem. Its response does not establish the seriousness of any shortcomings. The contracts and notification would be needed to reconcile the company’s description with the scope reported by the press. A warning also leaves room for a settlement; it does not establish a violation. [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03)

## Contract rules determine who gets paid

Consider a hypothetical contract bought for $0.40. It pays $1 if an event occurs and nothing otherwise. Before fees, the buyer gains $0.60 or loses $0.40. The financial outcome rests on the event’s definition, the chosen data source and the settlement rules. This example does not describe any product under investigation.

An ambiguous deadline, a revised statistic or conflicting sources can move money between participants. Contract documentation sets out the financial promise and how to verify its outcome. Its quality matters directly to customers and market oversight.

[Self-certification](/en/glossary/autocertification-d-un-produit/) allows a registered exchange to submit a product while certifying that it complies with the applicable requirements, without obtaining express prior approval for each one. The exchange must file the required information and analysis, and the CFTC retains oversight powers. Section 40.3 provides a separate route through a request for approval. [S08](#source-S08) [S28](#source-S28)

CFTC staff addressed the distinct features of event contracts in March 2026. On July 24, they warned against broad template certifications combining many possible variations: these can prevent adequate review of data sources, settlement methods and compliance. Closely related contracts can be submitted together under specified conditions. The guidance applies across the industry and does not name Kalshi. [S09](#source-S09) [S04](#source-S04) [S10](#source-S10)

The July warning predates the August separation discussions described by Kalshi. It provides a documented regulatory context for the dispute reported in November. It establishes neither the exact basis of the individual investigation nor how consistently officials applied the criteria to competitors.

## An application filed before the Polymarket appointment

The comparison with Polymarket adds another useful date. On August 26, 2025, the company announced the 1789 investment and Trump Jr.’s advisory board appointment. Financial terms were not disclosed. A regulatory letter concerning QCX LLC and QC Clearing LLC was dated September 2 and publicly announced the following day. [S07](#source-S07) [S13](#source-S13) [S14](#source-S14)

The letter’s first footnote dates the application to July 16, 2025, **41 calendar days before** the advisory appointment was announced. The process was therefore under way before the public announcement. That does not date any preparatory discussions or reveal whether the review was subsequently accelerated. [S13](#source-S13) [S07](#source-S07)

The substance of the letter matters as much as its timing. A [no-action letter](/en/glossary/lettre-no-action/) means staff will not recommend enforcement over specified requirements if defined conditions are met. Its reach depends on the named entities, provisions and conditions. It gives no blanket immunity to every business operating under a brand. [S12](#source-S12) [S13](#source-S13)

There are precedents. ForecastEx received a letter covering certain data obligations in July 2024. Kalshi obtained a supplemental position in January 2025. A December 2025 letter concerning Polymarket US expressly cited those precedents when changing a condition governing access through intermediaries. [S11](#source-S11) [S12](#source-S12) [S24](#source-S24) [S15](#source-S15)

The texts support a specific comparison without establishing identical treatment. The relevant differences would include information requested, conditions imposed, processing times and distribution arrangements allowed. Any advantage could emerge in those details. The proximity of an appointment and a regulatory letter cannot supply that analysis.

## Supporting a market while policing its exchanges

The public record contains several decisions benefiting Kalshi in 2026. On May 29, the CFTC approved its bitcoin-linked BTCPERP contract. In July, the agency intervened to prevent the cancellation of trades executed in Michigan; in August, it acted again in response to New York’s action. These decisions concern different issues, but each supported an activity or the continuity of Kalshi’s market. [S17](#source-S17) [S18](#source-S18) [S19](#source-S19)

The federal policy extends across the industry. The agency announced a lawsuit against Utah on October 27. On November 5, it set out its interpretation of impartial access to registered exchanges: domestic geographic restrictions it considers incompatible must be removed within thirty days, or their continuation explained. This is the regulator’s position and does not settle the litigation with the states. [S20](#source-S20) [S21](#source-S21)

Defending federal jurisdiction and examining product filings are distinct regulatory functions. Exercising both can be consistent. Equally, decisions benefiting Kalshi cannot rule out influence over a particular investigation.

Polymarket also faces scrutiny. Reuters reported on October 27 that the CFTC was investigating compliance with the 2022 settlement, including measures intended to keep US users off the international platform. Its anonymous sources also described scrutiny of onboarding and customer protections on the US platform. Polymarket says it has strengthened its controls; the company and CFTC declined to comment on the investigation. [S05](#source-S05)

The 2022 settlement concerned the operation of an unregistered platform. Later decisions involving the US structure address different obligations. Treating them as a single authorization would blur distinctions between legal entities and regulatory requirements. [S16](#source-S16) [S13](#source-S13) [S15](#source-S15)

An investigation on each side is nevertheless a poor measure of fairness. The conduct, cooperation, corrective action and possible penalties may differ. Public information does not support a quantitative comparison of the intensity of the proceedings.

European rules are examined separately in our [analysis of prediction markets in Europe](/en/analysis/polymarket-europe-betting-financial-products/). The US framework does not establish their European legal status.

## The financial interests behind market access

Trump Jr. established business ties with two companies whose expansion depends on federal decisions. The advisory appointment and 1789 investment are public. His personal compensation, economic rights and work performed are much less well documented. Ending an advisory role does not automatically entail selling a stake. These announcements alone cannot support a calculation of his personal gains. [S06](#source-S06) [S07](#source-S07) [S01](#source-S01)

A commercial conflict between competitors raises questions about an adviser’s loyalties. Political influence would involve using access to power to obtain an advantage. A legal violation would require further facts and specific legal elements. Each question calls for a different kind of evidence. The distinction also informs our analysis of [CLARITY’s conflict-of-interest problem](/en/analysis/clarity-act-trump-conflict-of-interest/).

The economic mechanism is more readily visible in the rules. A decision on access or intermediation can expand the potential customer base. Documentation requirements can affect launch costs and timing. The consequences for trading and revenue then depend on fees, users and competition. The cited documents identify possible channels; they do not quantify a causal effect on revenue or the adviser’s wealth. [S08](#source-S08) [S12](#source-S12) [S15](#source-S15) [S21](#source-S21)

A policy favorable to an industry may also benefit several companies connected to those in power. Comparable treatment between competitors would therefore leave wider conflict-of-interest questions unresolved. The rationale for the rules, relevant contacts and the involvement of financially interested people would still warrant examination.

## Decisions and contacts that need to become public

Congressional scrutiny predates November’s announcements. On May 22, 2026, the House Oversight Committee, chaired by Republican James Comer, requested information from Kalshi and Polymarket about safeguards against users trading on nonpublic information. On September 22, Republican Senator John Curtis called for an investigation into presidential families’ business dealings, specifically mentioning Trump Jr.’s prediction-market ties. Neither initiative establishes a violation by the platforms. [S22](#source-S22) [S23](#source-S23)

On November 10, Democrats Elizabeth Warren and Jeff Merkley also publicized a request to the White House Counsel for explanations about ethics safeguards and potential conflicts involving the platforms. They requested answers by November 23. Their intervention documents a demand for political oversight, without establishing interference in the Kalshi proceeding. [S29](#source-S29)

Testing the retaliation hypothesis would require linking the commercial separation to a decision to pursue, accelerate or intensify regulatory action. The crucial records would include the notification, decision dates, recorded reasons and relevant communications. Comparisons with genuinely similar contracts and applications would then help identify any difference in treatment.

An investigation that started earlier could take a later turn. A prior industry-wide warning could also help explain routine compliance action. The available timeline leaves these possibilities open. The split establishes no abuse, while regulatory precedents still leave the application of the rules open to scrutiny.

Verifiability is the central issue. Market participants need to understand their contracts. The public needs a basis for examining regulatory decisions and the financial connections of people close to political power. Dated decisions and meaningful comparisons would bring the next substantive advance on Kalshi. November’s announcements give good reason to seek them.

## Limitations

This analysis is current through 12 November 2026. The private notice, disputed contract filings and internal communications are not available in the cited public sources. The CFTC investigation is reported by the press; unnamed sources do not necessarily represent independent witnesses. Company and political statements remain attributed claims.

The regulatory letters address specific entities and requirements. They cannot measure relative enforcement intensity or establish identical treatment. The 41-day calculation uses documented dates and reveals nothing about private contacts. The payment example is hypothetical. No revenue effect, personal gain or causal amount is estimated.
