**A chip installed in a data center abroad can supply computing power to a Chinese company. Washington wants its controls to follow that use.** On **16 November**, the Bureau of Industry and Security, or BIS, announced a rule combining new destination-based licensing requirements with controls on the customers accessing computing capacity remotely. Its release says the rule was written by and at the direction of the **Treasury Department and the National Security Council**, or NSC. [S01](#source-S01)

The **132-page** document gives concrete form to proposals examined in [our fifteenth installment on Lutnick, Cantor and Bitdeer](/en/analysis/nvidia-lutnick-cantor-bitdeer-export-controls/). [The sixteenth](/en/analysis/nvidia-taiwan-political-outreach-server-trial/) followed commercial checks in a Taiwanese criminal case. This seventeenth installment examines the information Washington intends to require across the computing supply chain. The provisions cover categories of technology and transactions that can include Nvidia products. [S02](#source-S02)

BIS calls the measure an **interim final rule**, a procedure under which an agency issues a rule and then receives comments. The posted version makes its effective date depend on publication in the *Federal Register*, with that date still left to be inserted. At the **16 November check at 18:30 UTC**, or **19:30 in Paris**, the registers consulted returned no matching notice. The analysis below follows the text BIS has made public, with its calendar still incomplete. [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03)

## Two controls follow the same infrastructure

The first concerns destination. The rule adds a licensing requirement for exports or reexports to, and transfers within, **Brazil, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand** involving the covered advanced equipment. Applications would be reviewed case by case. BIS says that review will consider, among other factors, the destination’s export controls and cooperation with US authorities. [S02](#source-S02)

An exclusion covers certain consignees or end users headquartered in the United States, or whose ultimate parent is headquartered there. It depends on reporting to BIS about the parties, equipment and transactions, with reports required monthly. This exclusion belongs to the destination-based control. Other requirements under the US Export Administration Regulations, or EAR, require a separate assessment. [S02](#source-S02)

The second control follows the use of computing power. Under the new **section 744.24**, covered transactions require a license when there is knowledge that the equipment is destined for a data center providing, or planning to provide, access above the specified threshold to companies headquartered in **Macau or Country Group D:5**, which includes China, or whose ultimate parent is headquartered there. Data centers beyond the five named countries can fall within this control. [S02](#source-S02)

An operator therefore needs to examine both the equipment it receives and the customers buying its computing services. BIS’s explanation also covers intermediaries that train or supply models on behalf of an entity of concern. The commercial arrangements between the chip supplier and the laboratory using its products become part of the compliance assessment. [S02](#source-S02)

## The threshold adds up processing performance

The new control’s threshold is **strictly more than 12 million aggregate TPP**. *Total processing performance* is a regulatory measure calculated from chips’ technical characteristics. [Aggregate TPP](/en/glossary/tpp-agrege/) adds up the performance of components or equipment in the listed categories. Calculating it requires the precise hardware specifications. [S02](#source-S02)

The rule also combines capacity across **linked data centers**. Its definition includes facilities connected digitally to share resources, balance workloads or function as a unified whole. Spreading capacity across several buildings can therefore leave the same licensing assessment in place. GPU counts, electrical capacity measured in megawatts and revenue shares each describe something different from the regulatory performance threshold. [S02](#source-S02)

The equipment’s origin remains relevant, while the analysis extends into the network and the contracts granting access. A data center’s customers and allocation of computing resources can change during the servers’ operating life.

## Customer information travels back through the supply chain

The customer due-diligence provision, often referred to as KYC (*Know Your Customer*), expects suppliers to obtain enough information about a foreign data center’s checks on its customers. That includes examining direct or indirect ownership or control by persons located or headquartered in Macau or D:5, or with an ultimate parent headquartered there. If the center declines or cannot provide sufficient information, the text treats that as a red flag presumptively giving the supplier knowledge, unless the flag is resolved before the transaction proceeds. [S02](#source-S02)

Missing information thus carries a commercial consequence. The supplier needs records supporting its decision. The operator must understand its customers and their uses well enough to answer. The checks run back from the laboratory buying computing services, through the data center providing them, to the companies supplying the equipment.

A later change of use also matters. For transactions made from the planned publication date, the text requires suppliers to tell recipients that BIS authorization will be needed if the equipment moves into the use covered by the new control. A data center operator may therefore need authorization even if it took no part in the original shipment. [S02](#source-S02)

[End-user checks](/en/glossary/utilisateur-final/) become a continuing part of the relationship. At the point of sale, the assessment concerns the known project. During operations, it may involve reassigned capacity, a replacement customer or a different use. Handling those changes requires commercial records that can be matched to the machines’ technical characteristics.

## A narrow exception for commercial recommendations

The new section 744.24 contains an exception for computing used exclusively for **commercial recommender systems**, such as ranking products or content according to user behavior. It requires submission to BIS of the company’s identity, aggregate performance and the due diligence establishing that exclusive use. [S02](#source-S02)

The definition excludes systems generating new text, images, audio, video, software or other content, including those that also perform a recommendation function. Qualification depends on the service’s actual functions and the models it incorporates. A commercial label alone would leave those conditions untested. [S02](#source-S02)

The interaction with other provisions is crucial. In its explanation of section 744.23, BIS says some remote uses were already covered by its controls, including transactions made on behalf of a D:5 or Macau entity or involving that entity as the primary remote user. Its explanation also describes a licensing requirement after a transition for some recommendation services under that framework. The exception in the new section therefore has to be assessed alongside the other provisions governing the transaction. [S02](#source-S02)

## Existing operators face a transition too

For data centers already providing the covered capacity before publication, the text requires **obtaining a license within 90 days of the effective date** to continue those services, or face restrictions on future exports. Obtaining the license is the stated requirement. The clock’s starting date remains one of the details awaiting completion in the posted version. [S02](#source-S02)

This advances the analysis in the fifteenth installment, which discussed proposed terms reported by the press. BIS’s text now makes it possible to examine a transition affecting some existing activities. Applying it to a particular facility requires the dates of its transactions, the equipment involved and the uses covered.

## Eligible customers matter to infrastructure finance

Bitdeer’s **A201 project in Malaysia** illustrates the economic stakes. In its **10 November** results, the operator targeted commissioning and full deployment in the **second quarter of 2027**. It reported commitments covering **more than 70% of capacity**, representing **more than $1.7 billion in expected revenue over five years**. Those commitments and forecasts predate the BIS rule. [S04](#source-S04)

Assessing a project’s treatment requires information on its consignees, remote customers, equipment performance and computing uses. The financing question follows: completed capacity needs eligible customers on the expected schedule. If authorization becomes necessary or a customer has to be replaced, the billing timetable and each party’s contractual rights become central to the assessment.

Nvidia’s own commitments give it another reason to follow those customers. Its **10-Q for the period ended 26 July** describes arrangements under which cloud operators buy its infrastructure and Nvidia reserves computing capacity. Those operators can sell the capacity to third-party customers on more favorable terms. Nvidia’s remaining commitment declines as third parties use the capacity, or as Nvidia uses it for research. [S05](#source-S05)

[Our fourteenth installment](/en/analysis/nvidia-after-huang-customer-financing/) examined this circuit. BIS’s rule adds a question about the customers that could fill those machines: is their access compatible with the authorizations required? The financial consequences will depend on the relevant contracts, licenses and opportunities to reallocate capacity.

The documented public leverage lies in market access and conditions on equipment use. The announcement attributes the rule’s preparation to Treasury and the NSC, while BIS retains its licensing and enforcement machinery. For Nvidia and its partners, the decisive next records will concern binding dates, authorizations, operators’ responses and contractual changes actually carried out. ## Limits

This analysis concerns the BIS version retrieved, with its effective calendar still awaiting completion. The financial documents explain economic mechanisms; they do not establish a particular contract’s exposure to the new control. The targeted research documented no amount of loss, withdrawn financing, individual license granted or refused, or executed contractual change tied to this rule. Assessing a project requires its technical specifications, customers, uses and the other relevant EAR provisions.
