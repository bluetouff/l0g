On March 20, 2026, the US Navy joined Hadrian in opening a factory in Cherokee, Alabama. It is meant to make submarine components that shipyards have struggled to produce quickly enough. The Navy announced $900 million in public investment alongside private capital. Hadrian’s shareholders include 1789 Capital, the investment firm Donald Trump Jr. joined after his father won the election. [S06](#source-S06) [S07](#source-S07)

In August, Hadrian announced a new funding round that valued the company at $7.87 billion and named 1789 Capital among its investors. In November, *ProPublica* reported that the Pentagon was discussing a loan involving Hadrian and potentially other manufacturers. The outlet put it at $15 billion. The Pentagon disputed the amount and confirmed that talks were at an early stage. [S06](#source-S06) [S51](#source-S51)

A government customer can fill a factory’s order book. If it helps finance construction too, it changes the risk shareholders are taking. A decision in Washington can increase the value of an investment held elsewhere. On November 24, Jamie Raskin, the top Democrat on the House Judiciary Committee, made public requests for records from Hadrian and three other companies linked to 1789 Capital. He wants to know how the decisions were made and who had a hand in them. [S01](#source-S01) [S06](#source-S06) [S53](#source-S53)

## When the Pentagon is the lender

A factory needs money well before it ships its first part. A government loan on favorable terms could give Hadrian time to equip more sites and repay the debt as orders come in. The rate, collateral and award conditions would determine the value of that help. 1789 Capital has a minority stake and its exact size is private. The firm says it stays out of its portfolio companies’ dealings over federal contracts. Trump Jr.’s lawyer says his client played no part in Hadrian being considered for any contract. The Pentagon says investors’ political connections play no role in its choices. [S06](#source-S06) [S07](#source-S07)

<TrumpFamilyCapitalFigure lang="en" kind="hadrian" />

Vulcan Elements gives Raskin a reason to look closely. In November 2025, the Pentagon announced a conditional commitment to lend $620 million to Vulcan, a rare-earth magnet maker backed by 1789 Capital. *ProPublica* reported that White House adviser Peter Navarro intervened to expedite the deal. 1789 Capital says it played no role in securing the loan. Trump Jr.’s spokesperson says he did not discuss Vulcan with Navarro. [S08](#source-S08) [S57](#source-S57)

The companies also do business with each other. Trump Jr. was an investor and adviser at drone components maker Unusual Machines. Unusual Machines invested in Powerus, which subsequently ordered parts from it. The SEC filings show money and orders moving in opposite directions. Following the Pentagon contracts alone would miss that commercial relationship. ## The contractor behind the camera

In an advertisement for the Department of Homeland Security, Kristi Noem rides a horse at Mount Rushmore. When two senators followed the invoices, they found that the company awarded a master contract had been set up a week before the award. It subcontracted some of the work to The Strategy Group, run by the husband of Noem’s spokesperson. At a hearing, Noem confirmed the family connection and agreed to ask her adviser Corey Lewandowski whether he had been paid in connection with the contracts. [S19](#source-S19) [S20](#source-S20) [S56](#source-S56)

The appropriation paid for the campaign. The master contract named the principal supplier. The subcontracting records brought the connection to Noem’s circle into view. Senators raised another procurement question over aircraft bought from Daedalus, asking the department to justify the prices and its decision to award the contract without a competition. ## Cantor’s holdings and the chip decision

A department can also create value by allowing someone to sell. Three lawmakers have asked Cantor Fitzgerald for communications concerning Megaspeed, a company at the center of discussions about US restrictions on Nvidia chip exports. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick sold his stakes in Cantor in 2025. His sons now run the firm. The Commerce Department says he complied with his ethics commitments. [S12](#source-S12) [S13](#source-S13) [S18](#source-S18)

At September 30, Cantor reported just over one million Nvidia shares in its Form 13F, a periodic disclosure that certain investment managers file with the SEC. Cantor says its positions largely relate to transactions for clients. Under a [total return swap](/en/glossary/trs/), an intermediary can buy the shares as a hedge and pass their returns to a customer. Measuring how much of Cantor’s own money is at risk takes the contracts and hedges as well as the public filing. [S12](#source-S12) [S13](#source-S13) [S16](#source-S16) [S17](#source-S17) [Our investigation into Nvidia and Lutnick](/en/analysis/nvidia-trump-lutnick-national-security-influence/)

Trump himself intervened in another Nvidia matter this week. On November 22, the board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive. The next day, Trump told CNBC he had spoken to the board and backed Huang’s return. His annual financial disclosure lists purchases of Nvidia shares in 2025. His position in the company at the time of the board vote is unknown. [S55](#source-S55) [S61](#source-S61) [S62](#source-S62)

## A seat at the president’s dinner

On November 22, Donald Trump welcomed $TRUMP token holders to his golf club in Virginia. Under the organizers’ published rules, a chance at an invitation depended on ranking among the top holders. The terms promised no one-on-one meeting with the president. Trump’s financial disclosure gives a sense of the size of the business. It lists $635 million in 2025 *Celebration Coins* licensing fees at CIC Digital, a company held by his trust. The income covers the full year. [S46](#source-S46) [S47](#source-S47) [S48](#source-S48) [S55](#source-S55)

A quieter part of the family’s crypto business runs in the background. USD1 is a [stablecoin](/en/glossary/stablecoin/), designed to be worth a dollar. The assets held to redeem it can earn interest. The September attestation puts those reserves at about $4.4 billion. BitGo issues the token and holds the reserves. World Liberty Financial says that a Trump-affiliated company owns a stake in an affiliate entitled to a portion of the interest. The split among the parties remains private. [S29](#source-S29) [S54](#source-S54) [Inside USD1’s reserves](/en/analysis/usd1-trump-interest-rates-reserves-beneficiaries/)

The Emirati investment firm MGX invested in Binance, which says it received the payment in USD1. A payment like that can expand the pool of interest-bearing assets if additional USD1 is issued and stays in circulation. In August 2026, the US bank regulator gave preliminary, conditional approval to a World Liberty company that plans to issue USD1 itself for institutional customers. The September attestation still names BitGo as the issuer. [S29](#source-S29) [S54](#source-S54) [S58](#source-S58) [S59](#source-S59) [S60](#source-S60)

## The records Congress wants

Four days before Raskin publicized his requests, Donald Trump signed an executive order directing federal contractors to notify the White House and their agencies about congressional requests concerning their work or communications with the executive branch. The contractors are instructed to follow directions about withholding communications that the executive branch considers privileged. The White House says it is protecting the confidentiality of its discussions. How the order will apply to the Hadrian request remains an open question. [S01](#source-S01) [S52](#source-S52) [S53](#source-S53)

Raskin gave Hadrian until December 7 to produce its communications with 1789 Capital. Those exchanges, the Pentagon’s decision records and the financing terms would take the story from the factory in Cherokee to the people who decided who should bear the risk, and who might profit from the deal.
