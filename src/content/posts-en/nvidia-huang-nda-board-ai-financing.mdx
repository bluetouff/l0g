**The crisis has reached inside Nvidia. Directors are investigating the founder, a shareholder is challenging the company’s statements in court, and leadership uncertainty surrounds a man central to the business of AI. Washington also faces a corporate counterpart whose bargaining position may be changing.**

In the *New York Times* account published on 11 November, a bystander films Huang with an Nvidia executive at a hotel; security workers ask the person to sign a confidentiality agreement. The paper says it has not seen the video. The agreement, its signature and its terms remain unestablished. [S01](#source-s01)

That reported request gives investigators a concrete question. Who sought to protect which information, acting under whose authority and using whose resources? A public company can become involved through the means deployed around its chief executive, even when an episode begins in his personal life. Documents, the people informed and the procedures followed can establish the answers.

Our earlier investigations examined [warnings at the top](/en/analysis/nvidia-trump-warning-at-the-top/) and [interventions around chip export enforcement](/en/analysis/nvidia-trump-lutnick-national-security-influence/). This instalment examines Nvidia’s ability to exercise oversight over a founder whose success has given him exceptional authority.

Since 8 November, directors have been investigating a possible foreign intelligence effort targeting Huang. The commercial strand concerns Megaspeed, a Singapore-based customer suspected of facilitating Chinese access to chips subject to US restrictions. The alleged Powell relationship adds a workplace governance question to that security case. [S07](#source-s07) [S19](#source-s19)

## The phone, the security workers and the NDA

The executive named in the new report is Kimberly Powell, Nvidia’s healthcare vice president, who reports to Huang. The Times cites five people who worked at the company. Reuters carried the allegation and said Nvidia had not immediately answered its request for comment. Powell’s official biography separately confirms her role. [S02](#source-s02) [S21](#source-s21)

A [non-disclosure agreement, or NDA](/en/glossary/nda/), sets confidentiality obligations for specified information between parties. Establishing the terms of the hotel episode would require identifying those parties, the information covered and the commitments proposed. The document would also show the rights retained by the bystander and the consequences attached to disclosure. [S22](#source-s22)

There is already a public reason to examine the resources involved. Nvidia’s 2026 proxy describes a security programme for Huang and his family, approved by the board, with its nature and cost reviewed annually by the compensation committee. The reporting period ended in January, before the alleged March incident. The programme raises a question about who employed the hotel security workers and authorised their reported intervention; it does not establish those connections. [S06](#source-s06)

An NDA can also raise questions about reporting channels. US Rule 21F-17 protects direct communications to the Securities and Exchange Commission about a possible securities law violation. A confidentiality provision that impeded such communications could fall within the rule, including outside an employment relationship. Applying it to the hotel account would require the agreement and the relevant circumstances. [S11](#source-s11)

The KBR case illustrates the boundary. In 2015, the SEC took action over clauses used in internal investigations: witnesses were required to obtain legal department approval before speaking outside the company, under threat of discipline. The restriction on reporting possible securities violations was the issue. Nvidia’s investigators therefore have reason to examine both the confidentiality sought and the reporting channels preserved. [S23](#source-s23)

## The founder is covered by the code

Nvidia’s code applies to every employee and board member. It lists personal relationships among potential conflicts and calls for withdrawal from the relevant decisions when a conflict arises. A waiver requires written Compliance Committee approval; disclosure for directors and executive officers follows applicable rules. [S03](#source-s03)

Those provisions give the inquiry a defined task. A reporting relationship can affect compensation, promotions, budgets and performance reviews. Investigators can examine decisions involving Huang and Powell, any disclosures made and the safeguards adopted. Those questions remain open in the public record. They allow scrutiny of the company’s conduct without imputing a career advantage to Powell that the available evidence has not established.

Confidential interviews can protect witnesses. Their credibility depends on investigators being able to obtain information about the chief executive, including material he might prefer to keep from directors. The code provides internal and anonymous reporting channels and assigns the handling of concerns to the compliance committee and its delegates. [S03](#source-s03)

On 10 November, independent directors appointed Joon H. Kim and Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct their investigation. Its practical independence will depend on access to documents, staff and service providers, and on the decisions made after its findings. The precise scope covering Powell and the reported NDA episode remains to be publicly clarified. [S08](#source-s08)

## Directors have the power to remove the CEO

Huang founded Nvidia and has personified it for decades. The bylaws nevertheless give directors an explicit power: Section 32 allows a majority of serving directors to remove an officer, with or without cause. Removal from an executive position and removal from the board follow separate provisions. [S04](#source-s04)

The harder task is exercising that power. Removing a CEO means arranging continuity, distributing responsibility and maintaining the confidence of employees and business partners. A board’s ability to do that work is central to its purpose in a company dominated by its founder.

Nvidia’s governance policies provide for independent directors to meet without management and retain their own external advisers. The proxy describes the Lead Director’s role in facilitating independent oversight. These arrangements give directors ways to inform themselves beyond the CEO’s account. [S05](#source-s05) [S06](#source-s06)

## The lawsuit puts public assurances before a judge

Mark Kistenmacher filed a complaint against Nvidia and Huang on 9 November, seeking a class action for purchasers of securities between 9 October 2025 and 8 November 2026. It challenges, among other statements, descriptions of dealings with Megaspeed and Alice Huang as ordinary. The plaintiff invokes Sections 10(b) and 20(a) and Rule 10b-5 of US securities law. [S09](#source-s09) [S10](#source-s10)

The timing matters. The filing predates the Powell revelation and concerns information supplied to investors about Megaspeed. The new allegation may generate further governance questions; the existing complaint has its own challenged statements and proposed class period.

The mechanism is easier to understand through an investment decision. A company publicly offers reassurance about a sensitive commercial relationship. An investor later alleges that important information was withheld and that purchases relied on a misleading presentation. The court must examine the specific statements, the defendants’ knowledge and the connection to the alleged loss. Filing a complaint begins that examination; the allegations still require proof. [S09](#source-s09)

Shareholders also differ over Huang’s future. Armina Rosenberg, a portfolio manager at Minotaur Capital, which Reuters identifies as an Nvidia shareholder, stresses the cost of losing him. Litigation, fund managers’ opinions and a possible campaign by major holders are distinct forms of action. A coordinated mobilisation of the largest investors remains to be established. [S19](#source-s19)

## AI finance depends on the next decisions

The governance uncertainty surrounds a company whose commercial relationships extend beyond selling processors. At CoreWeave, Nvidia is a supplier, shareholder and buyer of computing capacity. Each role creates a different relationship with the same operator. [S14](#source-s14) [S24](#source-s24)

The capacity agreement announced in September 2025 had an initial value of **$6.3 billion**. Subject to delivery, availability and termination conditions, Nvidia agreed to buy residual unsold cloud capacity through 13 April 2032. For the operator and its lenders, such a customer can support revenue visibility. Establishing how much of that initial commitment remains available today would require further information. [S14](#source-s14)

The arrangement illustrates concentration risk. A supplier helps a customer expand its equipment, holds equity and may buy its service. Decisions at the supplier can then affect several parts of the financing. These relationships can support investment; their resilience depends on contractual obligations, capacity delivery and ultimate demand.

<NvidiaGovernanceFigure lang="en" kind="finance" />

CoreWeave’s results published on 10 November provide a sense of scale: **$132.5 billion of revenue backlog at 30 September**. Its definition adds other estimated contractual revenue, subject to delivery and availability conditions, to [remaining performance obligations, or RPO](/en/glossary/rpo/). The scope is broader than accounting RPO. It measures anticipated future business, with conditions separating that expectation from realised revenue. [S13](#source-s13)

For lenders, the questions are practical. Will equipment be available on time, will customers take the capacity, and will payments service the debt? A leadership crisis at Nvidia can add uncertainty to future decisions. Establishing transmission to credit would require evidence of delayed financing, tighter terms, withdrawals or defaults. Research published by the BIS describes the channels linking these commitments and their various providers of finance, offering a framework for examining the concentration. [S16](#source-s16)

## Corporate obligations extend beyond the CEO

Public contracts provide a useful distinction. The Nvidia–CoreWeave master agreement binds the companies and their successors; its provisions govern termination and give order forms a particular role. Replacing an executive and ending a commercial obligation therefore involve different acts. Redactions in the public versions require caution about specific conditions. [S25](#source-s25)

The SB Energy arrangements show another dimension. In August, Nvidia signed guarantees with an initial cumulative cap of **$105 billion** for a campus intended for an OpenAI affiliate. They respond to defined default circumstances and account for opportunities to recover value through reletting or sale. The cap describes a conditional exposure. [S12](#source-s12)

The public guarantee form binds the parties and their successors. It provides for choices following a default and written amendments; several termination provisions are redacted. Directors must therefore oversee long commitments while retaining control over new ones. A succession could change the appetite for additional risks before it changed performance of existing contracts. That is a management scenario to test against subsequent decisions. [S26](#source-s26)

This oversight requires an organisation capable of challenging commitments proposed by the CEO. As Nvidia participates in financing demand for its own products, directors need to assess the commercial return, counterparty concentration and guarantee triggers together. ## Washington may seek greater influence

The crisis raises a further political question. Could a weakened leadership team give the federal government an opportunity to negotiate additional commitments, potentially including an equity stake? Completed transactions and publicly discussed options provide a way to examine that scenario.

Intel offers a concrete mechanism. In August 2025, the company announced an **$8.9 billion** federal investment for a planned **9.9%** stake, using previously scheduled public funding. The announced holding was passive, without a board seat or additional information rights. Voting would follow board recommendations, subject to exceptions. The transaction establishes a financial holding with explicit limits on the exercise of shareholder power. [S27](#source-s27)

On 5 June, Trump said his team would examine public stakes in AI companies. On 17 June, Semafor reported two structures still under discussion: shares for the savings vehicles known as Trump Accounts, favoured by Bessent, and a sovereign wealth fund, favoured by Lutnick. The beneficiary and investment vehicle matter alongside the decision to obtain shares. [S28](#source-s28)

The February 2025 order preparing a sovereign wealth fund called for analysis of funding and legal arrangements, including potential legislative requirements, subject to available appropriations. An Nvidia transaction would require identifying the authority, funds, securities and attached rights. An export decision and an investment agreement follow different routes. [S32](#source-s32)

**Our hypothesis concerns a shift in bargaining power.** Investigations and leadership uncertainty could make Nvidia more receptive to demands concerning its markets, governance or investments. A negotiated federal stake would be another possible route, requiring its own financing and terms. The sources reviewed establish neither a federal Nvidia investment project linked to this crisis nor a deliberate strategy to drive down the company.

US decision-making also contains internal disputes. CNN reports, citing two sources, disagreement between Bessent and Lutnick over placing Megaspeed on the Entity List, restricting the customer’s access to US technology. The Commerce Department disputes the confrontation. These accounts concern export enforcement. They call for tracing each decision to its responsible officials and stated reasons. [S29](#source-s29) [S30](#source-s30)

Trump’s public support for Huang also belongs in the analysis. On 9 November, he said he believed Huang’s account that he had not known about the alleged spying. In June, Huang had told AP that he had held no dialogue about a public stake; that statement retains its date. Prolonged instability at the supplier would also weigh on the US ambition to expand AI. The administration has substantial negotiating tools alongside an interest in industrial continuity. [S31](#source-s31) [S33](#source-s33) [S17](#source-s17)

## Restoring control over decisions

The next financial appointment is 17 November. Nvidia will publish quarterly results and take questions from analysts and institutional investors on its conference call. The quality of answers about governance, leadership continuity and financial commitments will matter alongside any new organisational announcement. [S18](#source-s18)

The board already has rules, powers and external advisers. Its task is to establish the facts, apply the procedures and maintain decision-making continuity while exercising oversight that can function independently of one man.

Washington faces a corresponding test: make security decisions traceable and explain the terms of any economic intervention. If the crisis opens a new negotiation, records should allow scrutiny of the public interest, industrial choices and benefits granted to the parties. **Nvidia’s power makes that oversight more consequential as its commitments reach other companies and balance sheets.**
