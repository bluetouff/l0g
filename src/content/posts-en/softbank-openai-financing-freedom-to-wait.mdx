On 1 October 2026, SoftBank sent another $10 billion to OpenAI, using proceeds from the bond offering announced a week earlier. The same release closed out a different financing story: every borrowing under the March [bridge facility](/en/glossary/pret-relais/) had been repaid and its unused capacity cancelled. The investment had moved forward while a short-term funding commitment had disappeared. <sup><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup>

The new bonds mature in 2030, 2032 and 2034. They give SoftBank time to retain its investments. A separate financing agreement signed within Vision Fund 2 in August can nevertheless require cash before its scheduled maturity if the reference value of OpenAI shares falls significantly. Payment timing therefore depends on the financing contract. <sup><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a></sup>

Their interaction is the subject of this analysis. An investment holding company can build considerable wealth before receiving the proceeds of a sale. Its creditors have payment dates. Bridging that interval requires income, reserves, asset disposals or further borrowing. The freedom to wait depends on how those resources fit together.

**SoftBank’s disclosures show both the construction of that freedom and the conditions that could narrow it.** They document a completed financing programme and substantial assets. They also contain measurement choices and contractual obligations that change how those resources should be read.

## The bridge has left the repayment schedule

Three numbers in September described different stages of the same facility. The March agreement allowed SoftBank to borrow up to **$40 billion**. Cumulative drawdowns reached $30 billion. At the time of the 9 September announcement, **$25.9 billion** remained outstanding, with repayment scheduled for 15 September. The 1 October release confirmed that all borrowings had been repaid. The remaining $10 billion of unused capacity was cancelled on 30 September. <sup><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a></sup>

A facility limit authorises borrowing. A drawdown delivers funds. An outstanding balance records what still has to be repaid. Adding them together would manufacture debt that the disclosures do not describe. Similar care is needed when connecting a bridge to later financing: new borrowing may replace existing debt, fund an additional investment, or serve several purposes.

SoftBank specified the use of the bonds announced on 24 September: the final $10 billion OpenAI payment and general corporate purposes. The 1 October payment confirmed the first use. The documents examined do not provide a complete transaction-by-transaction reconciliation of the sources used to repay the bridge. The scheduled 15 September repayment also preceded the expected issue date of the new notes, 29 September. <sup><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup>

That distinction changes the forward-looking analysis. Keeping the March bridge on today’s debt-maturity chart would erase the refinancing that has taken place. Its closure, however, says little by itself about other loans, including the August agreement whose conditions belong to a different Vision Fund 2 financing.

On 14 September, Bloomberg also reported that SoftBank had secured an **$11.87 billion two-year loan** the previous week, from around twenty banks, citing people familiar with the matter. SoftBank declined to comment. The 23 September offering memorandum confirms an approximately $11.9 billion two-year senior loan, fully drawn. These figures must not be added together or treated as balances outstanding on 15 October. <sup><a href="#source-15" aria-label="Source 15">[15]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-14" aria-label="Source 14">[14]</a></sup>

## Bonds put dates around the investment

The foreign-currency offering comprises **$10 billion and €1 billion**. Its three dollar tranches carry [coupons](/en/glossary/coupon-obligataire/) of 8.625%, 9.250% and 9.750%; the two euro tranches pay 7.125% and 8%. Interest is payable twice a year, with principal due at maturity. The announcement specifies no collateral or guarantee. The bond issuer is SoftBank Group. <sup><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup>

The highest coupon makes a striking headline. To measure the recurring commitment, each rate must be weighted by the principal to which it applies. With all five tranches outstanding for a full twelve months, the disclosed terms imply **$941.25 million and €75.625 million of nominal annual interest**. This calculation keeps the currencies separate. It excludes fees, hedging, early redemptions and the treatment of the first interest period.

Those payments fall due even through a period with no investment disposals. Other portfolio resources can service them; the documents examined contain no scheduled OpenAI dividend earmarked to cover these coupons. The eventual return on an investment and the timing of its distributions need to be assessed together.

Fixed interest also provides useful protection. If investors demand a higher return to lend to SoftBank tomorrow, the existing coupons remain unchanged. A fall in the market price of the bonds does not, by itself, require an additional cash payment from their issuer. More expensive credit becomes particularly relevant when the group next borrows or refinances. Special conditions in other loans operate separately. Our analysis of [bond funding for AI infrastructure](/en/analysis/ai-debt-sovereign-borrowers-credit-costs/) also distinguishes the reference rate from the issuer’s own credit risk.

In the July annual-report message, the finance leadership describes the underlying choice: retain potential upside by combining funding sources, while keeping sales available as an option. That is a coherent position for a long-term investor. It requires resources capable of lasting until a disposal becomes attractive. <sup><a href="#source-13" aria-label="Source 13">[13]</a></sup>

## Leverage improves as debt rises

The June snapshot appears to give the group considerable room. Its ratio of debt to the value of its investments, or **loan-to-value (LTV)**, falls from **17% at the end of March to 13% at the end of June**. Over the same period, adjusted net debt increases from roughly ¥8.2 trillion to ¥10.8 trillion. The explanation lies in the denominator: the adjusted value of holdings rises from ¥48.3 trillion to ¥83.1 trillion, largely driven by Arm. <sup><a href="#source-6" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a></sup>

A more valuable investment genuinely strengthens the asset base supporting borrowing. It can increase the scope for sales or asset-backed finance. The valuation increase alone, however, transfers no funds into the account that will pay the next coupon. LTV describes asset coverage; cash flows and contractual commitments determine how that coverage can be used.

SoftBank’s detailed table also locates the concentration. On 30 June, Arm accounts for **¥49.91 trillion after the adjustment for financing against that holding**, within an adjusted portfolio of ¥83.11 trillion. The ratio is approximately **60.1%**. This l0g calculation uses the same scope in numerator and denominator. It shows how heavily a move in that holding can influence the overall picture. <sup><a href="#source-8" aria-label="Source 8">[8]</a></sup>

The company’s method excludes entities considered self-financing and makes adjustments for certain asset-backed borrowings and hybrid instruments. It is designed around an investment holding company. A ratio combining gross consolidated debt with adjusted portfolio value would measure something different. The policy limits, below 25% in normal conditions and capped at 35% in extraordinary circumstances, must also be distinguished from the contractual requirements of individual loans. <sup><a href="#source-4" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-8" aria-label="Source 8">[8]</a></sup>

S&P estimated June LTV at **20%–25%**, using a method that includes investment commitments among its adjustments. Its scope differs from the 13% ratio published by SoftBank. <sup><a href="#source-9" aria-label="Source 9">[9]</a></sup>

The March and June figures remain anchored to their reporting dates. Investments and refinancing have taken place since then. The figures explain the balance-sheet mechanism; they are not an estimate of the headroom available on 15 October.

## The meaning of a cash reserve depends on its boundaries

The same financial presentation reports a June cash position of approximately **¥2.3 trillion**. Reconstructing it is revealing. The SoftBank Group stand-alone measure starts with ¥2,509.2 billion of cash and qualifying investments. The company subtracts ¥684.9 billion of prime-brokerage borrowing collateralised mainly by a portion of the bond investments included in that measure. That leaves an effective position of ¥1,824.3 billion. Adding ¥489.5 billion of undrawn credit capacity produces **¥2,313.8 billion**. <sup><a href="#source-6" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a></sup>

These are stages in a reconciliation, rather than independent pools that can all be added together. The measure also includes short-term investments and bonds, making it broader than money immediately sitting in a bank account. An unused credit line offers a potential drawdown under its conditions. Drawing it will also create a liability.

The parent-company boundary matters just as much. Cash inside an operating subsidiary may be needed by its suppliers, customers or creditors. Moving it up to the holding company depends on that entity’s distribution capacity and commitments. Combining it with central cash and comparing the total against a parent-company maturity can obscure that boundary. SoftBank’s financial presentation explicitly distinguishes stand-alone and consolidated measures. <sup><a href="#source-6" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a></sup>

The group’s policy calls for a reserve covering at least **two years of bond redemptions**. That is a specific and useful benchmark. Interest, fresh investment, operating expenditure and possible collateral demands can require resources alongside those redemptions. Reading the policy as two years of protection against every cash outflow would give it a broader scope than the company states. <sup><a href="#source-4" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-13" aria-label="Source 13">[13]</a></sup>

None of this produces a reliable exhaustion date. That would require a current schedule of all sources and uses measured at the same entity level. It does establish the relevant questions: which resources are already available, which require a transaction, and which remain dependent on another party’s decision?

## A private valuation can create a cash demand

The August financial report discloses a separate set of obligations. A Vision Fund 2 vehicle, **SVF II TSUBAKI (DE) LLC**, entered into a $10 billion loan agreement on 5 August. Principal repayment is scheduled for August 2028. SoftBank Group guarantees the loan. The [collateral](/en/glossary/collateral/) identified in the disclosure is the **borrower’s cash collateral account**. <sup><a href="#source-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a></sup>

The agreement includes cash collateral and mandatory prepayment clauses triggered in specified circumstances, including a significant decline in the fair value of the OpenAI preferred shares referenced by the contract. In the investor briefing, the finance leadership explicitly stated that **OpenAI shares had not been pledged as collateral for this financing**. Their value is a contractual reference; the disclosed collateral consists of cash. <sup><a href="#source-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-7" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a></sup>

Follow the sequence. A valuation revision can reduce reported wealth. If it meets the loan’s conditions, it can also require cash to be set aside or debt to be repaid. The borrower then needs a usable resource even while retaining the shares whose valuation has changed. A single revision can therefore have two quite different effects.

The full thresholds, cure periods and amounts required under each condition are absent from the public summary examined. The 23 September offering memorandum confirms full drawdown on 7 August, without establishing the residual balance on 15 October. <sup><a href="#source-15" aria-label="Source 15">[15]</a></sup> This article describes the agreed mechanism without turning an illustrative cash call into an imminent event. SBG’s guarantee nevertheless provides a legal link between the subsidiary’s financing and the parent. <sup><a href="#source-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a></sup>

That mechanism extends l0g’s [August analysis of the liquidity risk tied to OpenAI’s valuation](/en/analysis/softbank-openai-liquidity-risk/). The September update adds an essential distinction: the repaid bridge and the valuation-linked loan are separate agreements. Successfully closing out one facility does not tell us whether the conditions of the other might be triggered later.

## A stock-market listing creates several possible cash routes

Listing an investment can broaden its pool of buyers and make its price more observable. Where the money goes depends on which shares are sold. When a company issues new shares, the company receives the proceeds. When an existing shareholder sells part of its stake, the proceeds go to that shareholder. An offering can combine both routes; their respective sizes determine the resources available to each party. Our examination of [the effects of an IPO on funding and guarantees](/en/analysis/openai-credit-rating-nvidia-guarantee-ipo/) follows these distinctions in a separate OpenAI-related financing.

SoftBank has already used the second route. At the closing of Arm’s September 2023 IPO, its selling vehicle disposed of 102.5 million American depositary shares, securities representing ordinary shares. The closing announcement stated expected proceeds of **$5.123 billion after underwriting discounts and commissions, before other expenses**. Arm remained a subsidiary. The disposal was accounted for within consolidated equity, while the seller obtained sale proceeds. <sup><a href="#source-11" aria-label="Source 11">[11]</a></sup>

The example helps explain the reverse pattern in investment accounting. SoftBank measures its OpenAI shares at fair value through profit or loss. An increase in that measure can raise reported profit without a corresponding sale. The historical Arm disposal and the OpenAI valuation treatment show why accounting earnings, asset value and realised cash need to be followed separately. <sup><a href="#source-4" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-11" aria-label="Source 11">[11]</a></sup>

The public documents examined provide neither a firm timetable for SoftBank to sell its OpenAI shares nor an amount it would receive from a possible IPO. An assumed listing cannot therefore be inserted as the cash receipt that balances a repayment schedule. The offering structure, the number of shares actually sold and any applicable restrictions would be the decisive evidence.

Borrowing against assets can bring cash forward while preserving their potential upside. In exchange, it creates a liability and contractual conditions. Whether the route is a sale, a dividend or a loan, the practical question remains: which transaction turns the investment into usable funds, when, and with what obligations left behind?

## The strongest case for the strategy

SoftBank has a record of capital-market access and has just demonstrated that it could fund its final investment instalment while closing the March bridge. These are completed transactions, stronger evidence than a promise of funding. A holding company does not need every asset to distribute cash immediately: it can manage portfolio rotation and spread out debt payments. <sup><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-13" aria-label="Source 13">[13]</a></sup>

On **16 July 2026**, S&P revised SoftBank’s outlook from negative to stable, affirmed BB+ and assessed liquidity as adequate. Its base case **did not factor in an OpenAI IPO**. The opinion predates the August loan and rests on other sources of liquidity. <sup><a href="#source-9" aria-label="Source 9">[9]</a></sup>

The group also taps different markets for different purposes. The ¥1 trillion domestic bond, announced on 4 September and issued on 17 September according to the offering memorandum, was intended for Japanese bond redemptions and part of the ABB robotics acquisition. Treating all of it as fresh money for OpenAI would disregard the stated use of proceeds. The overall funding strategy has to accommodate other projects and the renewal of existing borrowings. <sup><a href="#source-10" aria-label="Source 10">[10]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-15" aria-label="Source 15">[15]</a></sup>

On 9 October, SoftBank also announced early redemption on **22 October** of the **€405.103 million** outstanding on an older 2.875% issue. As of 15 October, that payment remains ahead. <sup><a href="#source-16" aria-label="Source 16">[16]</a></sup>

The strategy’s resilience depends on how long those resources remain genuinely diversified. A successful disposal can cover several maturities. An operating investment can make distributions. An open bond market can extend the schedule. Each provides flexibility; their availability needs to be assessed within the same market scenario.

## Risk grows when the exit routes start to converge

Consider a simultaneous reduction in expectations for AI. This is a transmission scenario, not a forecast with an assigned probability. Listed investments could lose value while private holdings are marked down. Buyers of share blocks or IPOs could demand discounts. New debt could become harder to place. A contract linked to a private valuation might require cash at the same time.

The difficulty would be the overlap. A reserve intended to provide time would be called on precisely when asset sales become less attractive. SoftBank could use other holdings, slow new commitments or accept less favourable financing. If the adjustment falls on investment, it could then reach equipment orders, infrastructure projects and suppliers. Funding and execution risks already appear in the company’s own risk disclosures; the macroeconomic chain described here remains conditional. <sup><a href="#source-12" aria-label="Source 12">[12]</a></sup>

The opposite sequence is equally coherent: assets appreciate, disposals become feasible, distributions grow and financing remains available. Long-dated bonds then allow the group to retain investments until a better selling window opens. Debt has helped the investor choose the moment rather than accept one imposed by a creditor.

To distinguish those paths, the next disclosures need to connect the same dates and entities: resources actually available after September’s transactions, the balance and terms of the August facility, net disposal proceeds, distributions received and commitments still to be funded. A spectacular valuation or a high coupon addresses only part of that task.

**As of 15 October, the documented outcome is completed funding alongside identifiable contractual risks.** The open question is the flexibility that would remain if several exit routes narrowed together. The quality of a portfolio gives a holding company financial weight. The ability to choose when to transact gives it time.
