**A Taiwanese whisky importer wants to get into AI computing. Its plan begins with a lease for some 300 servers equipped with Nvidia B300 chips, supplied by one of the chipmaker’s California partners.** Agencia Comercial Spirits signed a letter of intent in January. Its June 17 prospectus, included in the July filing, records a $3.5 million deposit, refundable under specified conditions. The proposed lease was worth roughly $120 million and was still awaiting a definitive agreement when the prospectus was filed. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

CBS traced the proposed lease as part of an investigation into two Nvidia partners, published on the evening of November 25 in the US. The story describes the $120 million as a signed deal. The SEC filing shows how far the project had progressed by June. There was a supplier, a prospective customer and money down. The filings leave two questions open. Where would the machines be installed, and who would use them? [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

This is the twenty-seventh instalment of our investigation, and it picks up where [the listing of Megaspeed and Apex](/en/analysis/nvidia-megaspeed-data-center-controls/) left off. Between the company selling a chip and the one using it, there may be a server maker, an equipment leasing firm and a data centre operator. Jensen Huang’s return put Nvidia’s board back at the centre of the story. In the same week, server makers and banks were checking the customers their projects depended on. [S05](#source-S05) [S07](#source-S07) [S09](#source-S09) [S11](#source-S11)

On October 16, Agencia reported it had signed a five-year deal for access to computing capacity in Indonesia. The filing traces the agreement to a project announced in July and places the supplier in Singapore. It does not tie that deal to the proposed RiCloud AI lease. The filings show two ways of buying computing power, by leasing the machines or reserving the service. [S17](#source-S17)

## Two companies with almost the same name

Agencia’s letter names **RiCloud AI Inc.** as the supplier. CBS links the company to Yuxing. The prospectus makes clear why the full company name matters. It identifies a separate entity, **Ricloud Corp.**, as a wholly owned Yuxing subsidiary. Yuxing’s annual report confirms the ownership of Corp. As for AI Inc., Agencia says its own chairman, Li Qiang, who also chairs Yuxing, is the company’s head of data centres. Agencia says he has no equity or control rights there. The shared executive is documented. Establishing the exact ownership of AI Inc. would take a corporate record for that company. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03)

CBS examines a second partner, **Aivres**. Nvidia’s annual report explains how a sale can be counted in the US even when a chip goes somewhere else. The company reports revenue by the headquarters of its direct customer. To find out where a server was deployed and who is using it, you have to follow the hardware to the site and look at the service contract. That is the work the export rules require in specific transactions. [S01](#source-S01)

## Megaspeed and the checks before a shipment

According to corporate records reviewed by *TIME*, three Chinese state entities indirectly held **1.9% of 7Road Holdings**, Megaspeed’s former parent, before the companies separated in 2023. The magazine traces the sale of Megaspeed’s shares to Swiftdata in November of that year. 7Road’s annual report confirms that it owned the Singapore company that became Megaspeed at the end of 2022. 7Road dates its exit to March 2023. The minority stake described by TIME concerns the period when 7Road still owned the company. Establishing who controls Megaspeed today would require tracing Swiftdata’s current ownership. *TIME* says Nvidia, Megaspeed and 7Road did not respond to its questions. [S04](#source-S04)

Swiftdata, Megaspeed International and Speedmatrix are now among seven companies added to the *Entity List* by the Bureau of Industry and Security, the US export control agency. The additions took effect on November 23. A licence is required for covered exports, reexports and in-country transfers of items subject to US export rules when a listed entity is a party to the transaction. The agency cites a risk of diversion. The Commerce and Justice departments date the start of US inquiries in the Megaspeed case to 2024. [S05](#source-S05) [S16](#source-S16)

Tim Fernholz’s analysis in *TechCrunch* brings the story down to a decision someone has to make at work. A customer certifies the intended use of its chips and names the [end user](/en/glossary/utilisateur-final/), the company that is meant to use them. If the supplier later learns something that calls this account into question, who looks into it, who hears about it and can the shipment leave in the meantime? Under [US export rules](/en/glossary/controle-export/), deliberately avoiding facts can help establish that a supplier was aware of a high probability. For Nvidia, the question is which warnings reached its staff, how they moved through the company and what people did with them. Compliance files and correspondence with partners are where the answer would sit. [S06](#source-S06) [S08](#source-S08)

## Shipping servers and renting their computing power

On **November 19**, an interim BIS rule took effect with new requirements for certain shipments of advanced chips to five countries. Brazil is on the list alongside Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand. The rule sets technical criteria for the equipment and provides for exclusions under specified conditions. A separate section covers [remote access](/en/analysis/nvidia-bis-remote-access-cloud-controls/). A cloud provider can keep every server in the same building and still have to assess the customer, the planned use and the computing capacity it is making available. Each set of requirements has its own reach. [S08](#source-S08)

That distinction helps explain the decisions reported by the *Financial Times* on **November 26**. According to the paper, Nvidia asked manufacturing partners to suspend shipments of servers containing its chips to four Southeast Asian countries. The FT says Dell and Supermicro notified buyers. Nvidia declined to comment to the paper. Which orders were affected and how long the pause may last remain open questions. Brazil appears in the BIS rule. The reported instructions to suppliers concerned four Asian destinations. [S07](#source-S07) [S08](#source-S08)

For a data centre filling racks as machines arrive, a few weeks can upset the schedule. A transition clause protects certain orders already on a carrier and en route on November 19. A server still waiting to be shipped is in a different position. Once equipment is running, the ability to keep renting it out depends on the remote access provisions, the customers using the hardware and the conditions that apply. BIS has published instructions for making certain reports under the exclusions. A report and a licence serve different purposes. [S08](#source-S08)

## Banks need customers who can pay

Consider [Aolani’s proposed loan](/en/analysis/nvidia-financing-guarantees-aolani-loan/). According to Bloomberg, the computing provider was preparing a Malaysian project that expected ByteDance to use its capacity. On November 17, Bloomberg reported that work had paused on a proposed **$480 million** financing. The FT has since reported that some lenders stopped extending about **$500 million** of credit and that ByteDance might walk away. Both figures concern the Aolani–ByteDance project. The reports describe different stages and give too little detail to reconcile the amounts. The financing documents, any drawdowns and the status of the customer contract still need to be established. [S07](#source-S07) [S09](#source-S09)

A lender counting on the sale of computing time has three dates to line up. When will the servers be ready? When can the customer lawfully use them? When do the bills start getting paid? If the second date slips, the developer may be paying for equipment and power before earning a dollar from either. Finding another customer then becomes a credit decision as much as a sales job. [S08](#source-S08) [S10](#source-S10)

<NvidiaEndUser2026 lang="en" variant="credit" />

Lenders are already responding differently. On November 25, Bloomberg reported that some were extending their checks on **Bridge Data Centres**. One bank decided against joining a proposed loan for **Digital Halo**, and at least one fund stepped away from a financing for **GMI Cloud**. The outcome of each deal remains open. Singapore’s **OCBC** confirmed it had contacted customers and said it expected little trouble given its exposure was mainly to large US technology companies. A bank still has to look at the particular building. Who will use it, how much revenue is under contract, and who could take the first customer’s place? [S09](#source-S09) [S10](#source-S10)

## The board and the record of warnings

On **November 24**, US officials told congressional staff that the evidence they had did not show Chinese intelligence had directed Alice Huang to spy on Jensen Huang, *The Wall Street Journal* reported. Alice Huang was a Megaspeed executive. The reported assessment concerned Beijing’s role in their relationship. Where the servers went and which warnings Nvidia received are separate questions. In its account of the negotiations over Jensen Huang’s return, CBS says the assurances to the board concerned Huang’s personal position. [S12](#source-S12) [S13](#source-S13) [S16](#source-S16)

Nvidia’s own board opened the door to those questions. In its November 23 statement, the board acknowledged receiving input from senior US officials before [reinstating Huang](/en/analysis/nvidia-jensen-huang-return-board-oversight/). The directors drew their own conclusion about the national security risk. They also said they would see their internal investigation through and cooperate with federal inquiries. The board also gave a committee of independent directors responsibility for overseeing export compliance. [S18](#source-S18)

On November 25, **Cory Booker**, the top Democrat on the Senate antitrust subcommittee, asked board chair Harvey Jones to identify the officials involved in bringing Huang back and explain what assurances they gave. He asked whether the conversations touched on export controls or antitrust enforcement. He wants Nvidia to preserve records dating back to **January 1, 2026**, and reply by **December 16**. His argument starts with Nvidia’s market power. A company that supplies the chips, the software that makes switching costly and some of its customers’ capital makes decisions whose consequences run well beyond its boardroom. [S11](#source-S11)

In London, MP Calum Miller submitted five written questions on November 25, listed in the Commons bulletin the following day. He wants to know, among other things, whether the government received warnings about ties to Megaspeed, whether it is considering remote access controls and how public computing infrastructure would cope with a break in Nvidia supply. Answers are due on November 30. Governments want answers from the board of a supplier whose machines their researchers and businesses still rely on. [S14](#source-S14)

**The board has settled Nvidia’s leadership question.** A board can bring back a chief executive over a weekend. Its partners still have to establish which servers can ship, who can take delivery and who can use them once they are installed. The document that best explains how Nvidia has handled the crisis could be quite ordinary. A dated compliance warning attached to a decision to ship, hold or change an order would show how the company acted when a question about the final customer reached someone with the authority to decide.

*An update to [our reporting on trades ahead of Huang’s return](/en/analysis/nvidia-trump-trades-before-announcement/).* On November 25, FT Alphaville examined the options series cited in the viral story of a $312 million gain. A specialist account of its analysis says the total premiums traded in that series during the session fell far short of the amount supposedly paid. Bloomberg found other profitable trades. The buyers and the information they had remain unknown. The review substantially weakens the viral example. [S15](#source-S15)
