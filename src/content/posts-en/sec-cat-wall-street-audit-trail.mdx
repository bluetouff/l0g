Take a buy order that goes to two trading venues. Some of it fills. The rest is changed, then cancelled. The broker knows how it ended. To an investigator looking for market manipulation, the route matters as much as the final trade. When the events are scattered across venues and brokers, someone has to put them back in sequence. That is the job of the [Consolidated Audit Trail](/en/glossary/consolidated-audit-trail/), or CAT, for US equities traded on exchanges or over the counter, and listed options. [S02](#source-S02) · [S07](#source-S07)

On **20 November 2026**, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) proposed taking management of CAT out of the hands of the exchanges and FINRA and putting it under federal control. The proposal would rescind Regulation NMS Rule 613 and create Rule 17a-26. Self-regulatory organisations and brokers would keep reporting substantially the same data, subject to existing exemptions. They would send it to the SEC or a provider it appointed. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02)

## Rebuilding the life of an order

The flash crash on **6 May 2010** showed how hard it was to reconstruct trading in a fragmented market. The SEC adopted Rule 613 in **2012**, then approved the national CAT plan in **2016**. The old trade reports covered different events from the order records kept by each exchange. FINRA says its earlier audit trail lacked options and did not directly receive records of orders traded on exchanges. [S02](#source-S02) · [S04](#source-S04) · [S07](#source-S07) · [S08](#source-S08)

CAT collects events such as order receipt, routing, amendment, cancellation and execution. It matches identifiers and timestamps across reporters. Regulators can examine a series of cancelled orders alongside trades that filled elsewhere. That helps them investigate spoofing, where someone places orders intending to cancel them to create a false impression of supply or demand. FINRA also cites insider trading investigations and reviews of execution quality. Its letter describes how the regulators use CAT. Measuring how many violations it helped uncover would take detailed results. For a related look at how order books shape prices, see [our investigation into prediction market volumes](/en/analysis/fifa-predictstreet-trading-volume-risk/). [S02](#source-S02) · [S07](#source-S07)

## Exchanges vote on the system used to monitor them

The participating exchanges and FINRA, the US broker-dealer self-regulator, each have one vote on CAT’s operating committee. The SEC counts **29 votes** in its proposal. Affiliates of **Cboe, Nasdaq, NYSE/ICE and MIAX hold 21** between them, enough to clear the two-thirds threshold for certain decisions. That count reflects corporate affiliations. The result of a vote depends on the positions individual participants take. [S02](#source-S02)

FINRA has one vote and, according to the SEC, accounts for roughly **40% of the portion of costs allocated to self-regulatory organisations**. Broker-dealers generate reports and have no committee vote. The SEC sets the regulatory requirements and uses the data, while the plan leaves oversight of the provider with the participants. In June submissions, FINRA and the industry association SIFMA both supported putting CAT under the SEC. Their letters represent organisations with a direct stake in trading or its oversight. [S02](#source-S02) · [S06](#source-S06) · [S07](#source-S07)

## The central budget and the work at brokers

The projected annual cost when the plan was approved in **2016** was about **$55.8 million**. The initial budget for **2025** exceeded **$248 million**. The company’s audited accounts recorded **$189.5 million** in operating expenses for that year, including depreciation. In March, CAT LLC budgeted **$147.0 million** in expenses for **2026**, including **$77.5 million** for cloud hosting, about **53%** of the total. The **2027** budget, published on **13 November 2026**, projects **$136.3 million** in expenses. These are budgets drawn up at different dates, as market volumes and the scope of the service changed. [S02](#source-S02) · [S03](#source-S03) · [S04](#source-S04) · [S11](#source-S11) · [S15](#source-S15)

The SEC expects steps it has already approved to simplify CAT to save more than **$100 million** a year. The budgets show the spending plans coming down. The accounts will show how much was actually saved. CAT LLC budgets for the central system. Separately, the SEC estimates an annual collection and reporting burden of around **$2.03 billion** for broker-dealers in its regulatory model. The same estimate appears under the proposed regime. It assigns a cost to compliance work and covers a different set of activities from the operator’s budget. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02) · [S03](#source-S03) · [S05](#source-S05) · [S09](#source-S09)

## From reserves to federal appropriations

In March, CAT LLC projected **$15.15 million** in revenue for **2026** and planned to use around **$131.90 million** of reserves to finance **$147.0 million** in spending. The March budget estimated **$23.51 million** in reserves at year-end. The budget published in November revised that projection to **$22.29 million**, drawing in part on accounts through August. For **2027**, it projects around **$148.0 million** in fees and a partial rebuilding of reserves. [S03](#source-S03) · [S11](#source-S11)

The funding plan approved in **2026** allocates a third of ongoing costs to self-regulatory organisations, a third to brokers on the buy side and a third to brokers on the sell side, using equivalent executed volumes. The SEC illustrates the split on **$147 million** with **$49 million** for the organisations and **$98 million** for brokers. The figures illustrate the cost allocation. Fees to be collected also depend on the use of reserves. [S02](#source-S02) · [S03](#source-S03) · [S12](#source-S12)

<SecCatFigure lang="en" kind="funding" number={3} />

Under the proposed arrangement, the SEC would need Congress to appropriate money to run CAT. The agency’s appropriations are offset by regulatory collections, including fees on certain transactions under Section 31 of the Securities Exchange Act. Moving the decision to Washington would change the funding process. The distribution of the cost would depend on actual appropriations and applicable fee rates. [S02](#source-S02) · [S07](#source-S07)

## Regulators can still link a customer’s activity

In January **2026**, the SEC approved removing directly identifying fields, including names, addresses and birth years, and deleting previously collected values. In March it approved a redesign of the reference system used to generate identifiers. CAT’s November newsletter scheduled one phase of that redesign for **7 December 2026**. [S05](#source-S05) · [S10](#source-S10) · [S13](#source-S13) · [S14](#source-S14)

The usual retention period in the repository is **three years**. Options market maker quotes and consolidated options market data are kept for **six months**. [S02](#source-S02) · [S05](#source-S05)

CAT can still connect a customer’s activity across brokers using a pseudonymous identifier known as a *CCID*. The data used to generate it is transformed and handled in a separate system. If an investigation needs a customer’s identity, regulators can ask the broker for the underlying records. The proposal restricts data access and use to authorised regulatory staff at the SEC and self-regulatory organisations for the purposes it sets out. Cross-broker tracing would continue under a provider selected and overseen by the SEC. [S02](#source-S02)

## The handover requires further decisions

The proposal allows **60 days** for comments from publication in the *Federal Register*. As of **23 November 2026**, the SEC’s docket still phrases the deadline in those terms. The agency envisages separate steps to rescind the plan and participant rules, take over some assets from CAT LLC or FINRA CAT and contract for operations. The proposed rule sets out a reporting framework. The price and schedule of a transfer and funding for continuity remain open. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02) · [S16](#source-S16)

A separate measure on **20 November** allows a time-limited collection of some historic development costs. The SEC explains that the existing plan and related rules must remain in place until that collection ends. Adoption of the rule, transfer of operations and federal funding therefore depend on different steps and timetables. [S02](#source-S02)

## Limits of the evidence

The investigation draws on public documents consulted on **23 November 2026**. Budgets and estimates are in nominal US dollars. The regulatory model quantifies a reporting burden. Matching it to the costs each firm actually incurs would require further data. The opening image is a digitally generated editorial illustration.
