The return of Japanese savings makes for a compelling story: higher yields at home could draw capital away from overseas markets and raise other borrowers’ funding costs. Documents available on 22 October 2026 make that mechanism plausible, while describing a much more selective transition. Its pressure point lies in the reinvestment decision, when the proceeds of a maturing investment reach an account and several markets compete for their next use.

The figure that brought the question back into focus comes from Japan’s Ministry of Finance. In September, life insurers in its reporting panel recorded **JPY288.6 billion in net disposals of foreign medium- and long-term debt securities**. The release was published on 8 October. Disposals include securities redeemed at maturity as well as sales; acquisitions are deducted to arrive at the balance. The statistic therefore records net disinvestment in a category of securities. Establishing where the money went requires further evidence. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup>

Our [earlier analysis of Japanese life insurers’ balance sheets](/en/analysis/japan-end-free-money-life-insurer-balance-sheets/) examined how interest-rate risk enters their accounts. This investigation follows the next stage: reported flows and the decision to reinvest overseas or in Japan. That distinction defines the investigation. An insurer can let a US bond mature, buy foreign equities, change its currency hedge or choose a Japanese government bond. Each decision shifts a different set of risks. Assessing the implications for global borrowing costs means following the return received in yen, the promise made to the policyholder and the investor available to take the insurer’s place.

## September extends an earlier shift

The monthly series puts September in perspective. Life insurers in the panel recorded **JPY1,757 billion in net disposals** during the first nine months of 2026, compared with JPY314.9 billion in the corresponding period of 2025. February and March 2026 had each exceeded JPY600 billion. These totals add the published monthly balances for foreign medium- and long-term debt, with redemptions included in disposals. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a>, <a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup>

The flows document net disinvestment in these securities. Reconstructing the underlying investment decisions requires retaining the survey’s scope. The ministry collects reports from designated institutions and classifies securities by the issuer’s residence. The foreign classification therefore does not, by itself, identify dollar exposure. The table does not identify each investment’s destination country, currency or hedge ratio. A negative balance cannot directly identify either US Treasury sales or purchases of yen. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup>

Other investor categories make a single national narrative harder to sustain. From January to September, banks’ own banking accounts recorded approximately **JPY11,739 billion in net disposals**, while trust banks’ trust accounts recorded approximately **JPY11,415 billion in net acquisitions** of the same category of foreign debt. The aggregate conceals substantial movements in opposite directions. Trust accounts describe an institutional account category; the entire amount cannot be attributed to pension funds from this series alone. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a>, <a href="#source-s05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a></sup>

That diversity matters for international risk. A bank reducing its portfolio, an insurer seeking a particular payment date and a trustee rebalancing assets for clients may trade different securities on different schedules. Adding them together measures a balance. Understanding prices requires identifying the segments involved. A withdrawal concentrated in a less liquid maturity can put more pressure on its yield than a larger flow spread across several deep markets.

## The relevant return arrives in yen

Japan’s domestic market now offers substantial reference points. In the finance ministry’s official curve for **21 October 2026**, the constant-maturity yield was **2.923% at ten years** and **3.980% at thirty years**. These are dated analytical benchmarks, calculated under the ministry’s methodology from secondary-market reference prices. They differ from an issue coupon or a price immediately available for execution. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s06" aria-label="Source 6">6</a>, <a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

For an insurer paying claims in yen, a dollar bond’s quoted yield is the beginning of the calculation. The eventual value also depends on the exchange rate at which dollars are converted. Yen appreciation reduces the amount received in the currency of the liabilities; depreciation increases it. A currency hedge can fix an exchange rate in advance for a specified amount and date by selling the expected dollars forward. Much of its economic cost is embedded in that forward rate, alongside transaction costs and, depending on the arrangement, collateral requirements. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup>

Consider a wholly hypothetical example. An investor exchanges **JPY15,000 for USD100**, then buys a bond paying **USD105 in one year**, assuming contractual repayment. The investor hedges the entire USD105. At a forward rate of **JPY147 per dollar**, the proceeds are **JPY15,435**, a **2.90%** return before costs. A forward rate of **JPY148.5** produces **JPY15,592.5**, or **3.95%**. A hypothetical yen reference investment returning **3%** falls between the two. These numbers illustrate arithmetic; they are neither October market quotes nor a comparison of equivalent credit risks.

The exact calculation is straightforward when the final payment is known and fully hedged: multiply the dollar return factor by the ratio of the forward exchange rate to the initial spot rate. In the first scenario, **1.05 × 147 / 150 − 1 = 2.90%**. Simply subtracting two percentages would produce a different approximation. Hedging changes the ranking because it changes the amount of yen ultimately recovered.

A thirty-year bond hedged with three-month contracts introduces another complication. The price of the next hedge renewal remains unknown, while the bond continues to respond to changes in yields. Carry calculated today indicates income under current conditions; the outcome over thirty years depends on successive hedges, prices, costs and payments. The currency contract’s term and the bond’s interest-rate sensitivity are separate dimensions of risk. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a>, <a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

## Higher Japanese rates work in two directions

On 18 September, the Bank of Japan set its overnight call-rate target at around **1.25%**, effective from 24 September. The decision helps explain monetary normalisation, but a three-month hedge is priced using market interest rates for the corresponding tenor, financing conditions and the market-specific pricing adjustment known as the [*cross-currency basis*](/en/guides/read-dollar-dxy-cross-currency-basis/). An executable hedge quote therefore requires market prices for the relevant maturities. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s08" aria-label="Source 8">8</a>, <a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup>

This produces an important counterweight. Other things equal, a rise in short-term yen rates narrows the gap with short-term dollar rates and can reduce the cost of hedging dollars. A hedged foreign investment then becomes more rewarding in yen. At the same time, a rise in long-term Japanese bond yields improves their appeal for new investments. The choice depends on the relative size of these changes, the slopes of the yield curves and the basis.

The relationship can be understood without predicting the next central-bank decision. If long-term Japanese yields rise while hedging conditions change little, domestic bonds gain ground. If US short-term rates fall more than US long-term yields, holding dollar bonds and renewing their hedges can become more attractive. Stress in the swap market can instead make protection more expensive even as the interest-rate differential improves. These scenarios follow the pricing relationships discussed by the BIS; whether they occur must be checked against contemporary market quotes. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup>

Investors can also change how much of a portfolio they hedge. BIS research describes episodes in which investors adjusted currency derivatives while keeping their underlying assets, including the tensions of April 2025. Substantial dollar-market activity can therefore reflect a change in protection. Interpreting debt disposals and their currency effects requires examining both the asset portfolio and its hedges. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

## An insurer invests for the date of a promise

A life insurer’s choice also reflects the liabilities on its balance sheet. Its assets fund benefits payable over many years, according to policy terms and policyholders’ behaviour. The task is to have the required amounts in the relevant currencies when payments fall due. [Asset-liability management](/en/glossary/alm/) organises that relationship and measures the mismatches that remain. Japan’s economic value-based solvency framework, applied from **31 March 2026**, assesses assets and liabilities through this economic lens. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a>, <a href="#source-s14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a></sup>

In its April report, the BOJ specifies that some foreign bonds classified as “unhedged” are earmarked for foreign-currency insurance policies. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15" aria-label="Source 15">15</a></sup>

Higher rates have two sides on the balance sheet. They improve returns on new purchases while reducing the market value of existing fixed-rate bonds. Other things equal, they also reduce the present value of fixed future benefits when those benefits are discounted at higher rates. The effect on the economic surplus depends on the amounts, sensitivities and options on both sides of the balance sheet. Looking only at bond valuation losses leaves a substantial part of the calculation outside the frame. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a></sup>

**[Duration](/en/glossary/duration/)** helps describe that sensitivity. Modified duration approximates a bond’s relative change in value for a small change in yield, other things equal. For an insurer, the sensitivities of assets and liabilities must be compared alongside their respective values. A very long bond can help bring the two profiles closer together. Once that adjustment is well advanced, buying progressively more long-dated assets serves the original matching need less directly. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a>, <a href="#source-s15" aria-label="Source 15">15</a></sup>

The FSA’s August 2026 insurance monitoring report provides precisely this qualification. It observes that the accumulation of super-long JGBs ahead of the new framework had eased and that duration gaps had narrowed. It also describes replacements of low-yielding bonds. Regulation had already shaped portfolios before implementation; a fresh, uniform wave of purchases in October would require additional evidence. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a></sup>

Execution remains a management choice. Replacing an old bond with a higher-yielding one can crystallise an accounting loss, whereas holding the original to redemption spreads the adjustment over time. Natural maturities, incoming premiums and benefits payable provide several routes for reshaping a portfolio. Insurers differ in their payment schedules, liquidity resources and willingness to recognise an immediate loss. Company documents reveal these choices more clearly than a sector average. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s11" aria-label="Source 11">11</a>, <a href="#source-s12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup>

## Published strategies lead in several directions

In its June presentation, **Nippon Life** planned to reduce domestic bonds and increase currency-hedged foreign bonds in fiscal 2026, while actively managing older low-yielding securities. These were intentions announced at a particular date; their execution must be checked later. Their direction demonstrates that higher Japanese yields can coexist with a stated intention to expand selected foreign investments. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s11" aria-label="Source 11">11</a></sup>

**Sumitomo Life’s** September presentation describes another route: shifting part of its unhedged foreign bond allocation towards foreign equities. It also expects redemptions to weigh on its JGB portfolio for asset-liability management. A reduction in foreign debt can therefore accompany a change in the risk taken abroad, without an equivalent move into Japanese debt. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup>

More recently, Reuters reported on **21 October** that **Meiji Yasuda** planned purchases of twenty- and thirty-year JGBs while targeting a stable yen bond portfolio during its fiscal second half. Purchases and redemptions can offset one another. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s19" aria-label="Source 19">19</a></sup>

These announcements illuminate possible decisions, distinct from the September transactions in the ministry’s panel. They also distinguish an intention to buy, gross purchases and a net increase in holdings. A manager buying after a redemption can be active without expanding the portfolio. When that manager moves between foreign asset classes, debt loses an investor while the capital remains exposed to other international risks.

## Global prices turn on the next reinvestment


One transmission channel runs through reinvestment. A foreign issuer repays a bond; the Japanese investor compares available investments and may choose to participate less in the next issue. Another source of funding must take its place. If additional buyers require greater compensation, the market price adjusts downwards and the yield increases. That pressure can reach new issues and refinancing. The contractual coupon on an existing fixed-rate bond remains unchanged. Our [analysis of the marginal Treasury buyer](/en/analysis/who-buys-the-bill-deluge-us-treasury-marginal-buyer/) explains how new transactions set the funding price.

This competition is developing as the Bank of Japan reduces its government-bond purchases. A staff study published on 4 August describes rising JGB holdings among several investor groups and the time needed to adjust portfolios. It also notes that insurers’ major phase of super-long purchases in preparation for solvency regulation had largely run its course. Japan’s bond market must attract capital in an environment where each investor faces distinct constraints and alternatives. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s16" aria-label="Source 16">16</a></sup>

Empirical research supports the existence of international transmission. An IMF working paper published in November 2025 examines surprises around **151 BOJ announcements between 2010 and 2023**. It identifies effects on foreign sovereign yields; for asset-purchase shocks, the effects are stronger in markets with greater Japanese investor participation. The method concerns historical monetary-policy surprises and depends on its identification assumptions. Converting insurers’ September 2026 disposals into basis points of future yield increases would require a separate estimation. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s17" aria-label="Source 17">17</a></sup>

The IMF’s April 2026 Global Financial Stability Report also discusses reallocation risk while emphasising the usually gradual adjustments of large investors. The effect depends on the market, its liquidity, the maturities affected and replacement demand. Local investors abroad, international funds or an improvement in hedged returns can absorb part of the adjustment. The risk concerns the price at which that absorption becomes possible and the speed at which it must happen. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source 18">18</a></sup>

The IMF’s October report adds context: sovereign yields also reflect expected monetary policy, [term premiums](/en/glossary/prime-de-terme/) compensating for interest-rate risk over time, and public borrowing needs. Its measures of volatility and bid-ask spreads point to more difficult JGB liquidity conditions, especially for long-dated bonds. Domestic reinvestment decisions also depend on those trading conditions. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s21" aria-label="Source 21">21</a></sup>

The reasoning remains conditional. A persistent decline in Japanese demand for a particular segment, alongside substantial issuance and intermediaries reluctant to hold the risk, can raise the yield required to clear the market. A temporary flow quickly absorbed by other buyers leaves a different footprint. A monthly national aggregate cannot resolve these questions about maturity, counterparties and market depth.

## Following the proceeds to their next investment

The weekly release of **22 October**, covering transactions from **11 to 17 October**, records **JPY161.6 billion in net disposals** of foreign medium- and long-term debt for the full reporting panel. It provides a recent observation with a broader scope than life insurers, without a corresponding investor breakdown. The two releases must retain their respective scopes. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

US statistics provide another perspective with a different limitation. As our [analysis of Asian purchases of US assets](/en/analysis/what-asia-really-buys-in-america/) explains, holdings and transactions answer different questions. The TIC series distributed by FRED puts Japan’s Treasury holdings at approximately **USD1,068 billion at the end of August 2026**. It measures a market-value stock across investor categories, classified by legal residence. Custody through foreign intermediaries can obscure the final holder’s location. Changes combine transactions, valuation effects and other adjustments. Isolating any repatriation by life insurers would require sector-specific transactions. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s20" aria-label="Source 20">20</a></sup>

A more complete demonstration would connect investor-specific transactions to the countries, currencies and maturities of the securities, then to domestic purchases and currency hedges. It would also separate decisions taken before a rate increase from those responding to it and identify other forces moving foreign yields. The available evidence establishes sector-level net disinvestment and plausible allocation choices; much of the chain linking them remains to be documented.

The useful signal is a repeated reinvestment choice. Insurers that persistently favour JGBs after comparing yen income and balance-sheet needs can make it more expensive to find a creditor elsewhere. The documents also describe routes that could slow or redirect that movement: cheaper hedging, sustained international allocations and portfolios already adjusted to prudential requirements.

**Japanese savings can contribute to higher foreign borrowing costs through the price investors require to keep providing finance.** As of 22 October 2026, the evidence documents debt flows and diverse stated allocation plans. A generalised repatriation, followed by a rise in foreign yields attributable to that return, remains a hypothesis to test. The risk lies at this boundary between the observations and their interpretation.
