**On November 20, Anthropic confirmed that it had closed the private placement that included about $10 billion from Nvidia. That same day, Apollo and Goldman Sachs announced pricing on roughly $44 billion in debt to finance Nvidia equipment for SpaceX.** Anthropic already buys computing power from SpaceX’s AI subsidiary, SpaceXAI, which supplies it using Nvidia chips. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03)

## Anthropic completes the share sale

Our [twenty-first installment](/en/analysis/nvidia-h200-china-bis-licenses/) followed the placement as Anthropic began trading. On **November 20**, Anthropic confirmed the closing of its public offering and the accompanying **$26 billion private placement**, naming Nvidia among the seven buyers. The final prospectus allocates **120,481,927 Class N shares at $83 each** to Nvidia, or approximately $10 billion. Class N shares carry no voting rights except as required by law. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

Anthropic plans to spend proceeds from the public offering and the placement on items including operations, computing and infrastructure investment, and working capital. Anthropic confirms the completed sale without publishing the bank settlement for each buyer. Our [analysis of supplier financing at Anthropic](/en/analysis/anthropic-supplier-financing-ipo/) describes the earlier agreements that overlap with some subscriptions. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

## The lease schedule starts at delivery

On **October 24**, SpaceX signed a binding commitment to enter into a [finance lease](/en/glossary/finance-lease-us-gaap/) with an Apollo-managed entity. The lessor would buy the equipment and lease it to SpaceX. Under the terms disclosed in October, delivery of each batch starts a separate payment schedule lasting about five years. SpaceX plans to pay rent quarterly in advance. Payments generally include part of the financed cost, interest on the outstanding balance and fees. [S04](#source-S04)

On **November 20**, Apollo and Goldman Sachs priced approximately **$44 billion of senior and subordinated notes** to fund Vera Rubin chips and related equipment. SpaceX’s October estimate of **$40 billion** referred to aggregate principal it expected to repay under the lease schedules. Apollo expects deliveries and the first lease schedules to start in the **first half of 2027**. [S03](#source-S03) [S04](#source-S04)

## SpaceX rent is designed to repay the debt

In its November 16 preliminary rating, KBRA identifies **Saturn Funding Trust** as the issuer of several classes of secured notes. Saturn is indirectly owned by WHCO, the planned lessor. The quarterly rental payments are designed to pay down the debt. KBRA says the senior notes rank ahead of subordinated classes in claims on collateral proceeds. If creditors need to recover money from the equipment, obsolescence and resale values will matter. [S05](#source-S05)

The November 20 release confirms that the notes were priced. At the public filing check on **November 22 at 21:54 Paris time**, settlement and entry into the finance lease still needed confirmation. The disclosures describe the lessee’s obligations and the equipment securing the financing. They identify no Nvidia guarantee specific to the transaction. Nvidia’s broader financing initiative, covered in [part XX](/en/analysis/nvidia-credit-outlook-wall-street-anthropic/), appears in separate disclosures. [S03](#source-S03) [S04](#source-S04) [S05](#source-S05) [S06](#source-S06)

## Anthropic is buying computing time

Anthropic’s prospectus describes contracts entered into or amended between **May and October 2026** for access to SpaceXAI computing capacity powered by Nvidia GPUs. The agreements could cost as much as **$120.5 billion through 2029**. Charges vary while capacity ramps up. After initial firm periods ending in January or March 2027, depending on the agreement, either party may terminate with **90 days’ written notice**. That option matters when assessing how long SpaceXAI can rely on the customer’s payments. [S02](#source-S02)

Anthropic expected full capacity under these contracts in **December 2026**. Apollo expects the equipment financed under the new transaction to arrive in 2027. The documents disclose no allocation of Nvidia’s subscription to SpaceXAI bills and identify no Saturn equipment as backing the existing service contracts. The structure KBRA describes relies on unconditional rent once the lease starts. SpaceX would still owe that rent if one of its customers stopped buying computing power. ## Limits

The announcements confirm Anthropic’s completed share sale and pricing of the SpaceX-related notes. The notes’ settlement, final lease documentation and the date each batch arrives require separate records. The credit disclosures cited here predate the deployment of the new equipment.
