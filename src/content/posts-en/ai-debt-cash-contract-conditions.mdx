*AI’s creditors · 5/6. [Previous instalment: insurers’ investments and guarantees](/en/analysis/ai-insurers-guarantees-off-balance-sheet-risk/). Explore the series in our [AI financing dossier](/en/topics/ai-financing/). Documentary investigation with an information cut-off of 18 November 2026.*

To obtain surety cover for its electricity obligations, CoreWeave placed **$154 million in a trust for the surety providers**. The deposit appears in its accounts at 30 September, filed on 12 November. Retrieving those assets requires the providers’ approval: the company retains an asset whose use it has restricted. [S01](#source-S01)

That allocation matters for a business buying equipment, building facilities and servicing debt at the same time. Money can appear on its balance sheet while being committed to a particular obligation. Assessing available cash requires following the rights that govern where it can go.

Since [the Jupiter campus investigation](/en/analysis/jupiter-hsbc-european-banks-ai-financing/), this series has identified banks, funds and insurers involved in financing AI. The fifth instalment examines their rules for accessing money. Agreements tie funding to physical progress, arrange interest payments during construction and can reserve receipts for creditors. They describe several ways a delay or a change in a customer relationship could affect financing.

## Cash committed behind a surety

In August, CoreWeave bought **$275 million of surety bonds** securing obligations to an electricity provider over four years. The $154 million trust deposit supplies assets supporting that protection and is recorded as non-current restricted cash. The surety providers determine the collateral required, can ask for more and can withdraw trust assets to meet covered losses. [S01](#source-S01)

The electricity supplier gains protection for its customer’s obligations, while the surety providers receive resources backing their promise. CoreWeave commits some of its assets to that security: this is the role of [collateral](/en/glossary/collateral/). Any request for additional collateral would absorb further resources, with the cash effect depending on the assets accepted and how they were financed.

The $275 million bond amount and $154 million deposit describe two parts of the same arrangement. The protection amount needs to be tracked separately from the assets committed to support it. The providers’ identities and the thresholds for additional collateral remain undisclosed. [S01](#source-S01)

A bank’s promise to pay can also use up borrowing capacity. At 30 September, CoreWeave’s **$2.5 billion revolving facility** supported $1.2 billion of drawn loans and $546 million of [letters of credit](/en/glossary/lc/), leaving $754 million available. A letter of credit commits the bank to pay on specified terms and, here, consumes capacity before a corresponding bank cash payment. These instruments primarily support lease obligations. [S01](#source-S01)

The electricity deposit and letters of credit show separate demands on the company’s resources. Assets or bank support can strengthen protection for a creditor. The borrower also needs enough funding to meet its other payment dates.

## Cancellation rights change the accounting

During the third quarter, CoreWeave reclassified **$1.9 billion of deferred revenue as customer liabilities** because contracts had become cancellable. The disclosure records a change in contractual rights. The customers, reasons for the change and any associated refunds remain unidentified. [S01](#source-S01)

CoreWeave records deferred revenue when it invoices ahead of providing the service, as its annual accounting policy explains. Depending on payment terms, that invoice may still be outstanding. Translating the reclassification into cash movements would require the payments received, services still owed and each party’s precise rights. [S02](#source-S02)

A customer commitment supports expectations of future receipts, and cancellation rights change the conditions behind those expectations. The first quarter had already included a **$1.3 billion reclassification into customer liabilities**, without a reason specified in that passage. These are movements in different periods. The public disclosures provide no reconciliation from which to calculate a cumulative balance at 30 September. [S03](#source-S03)

Financing agreements offer another way to examine this dependence on customers: they set out possible consequences when a project’s commercial commitments change. The following case concerns those contractual mechanisms, whose connection to the reclassified amounts remains unestablished.

## Electricity supply governs drawdowns

The **DDTL 5.5** agreement, signed by a CoreWeave subsidiary on 7 August, links project progress directly to financing. A [delayed-draw term loan](/en/glossary/ddtl/) allows borrowing in stages during a defined period. The [second](/en/analysis/european-banks-ai-loans-exposures/) and [third](/en/analysis/private-credit-ai-funds-bank-financing/) instalments identified banks and funds around this transaction. [S04](#source-S04)

The public conditions include delivery of equipment to the data centre or an accessible nearby location, the start of power supply in the relevant halls and arrangements covering the required power for the specified term. Other requirements concern security and insurance. Parts of the filing are redacted. The agreement also allows the agent to waive conditions on instructions from the required lenders. [S04](#source-S04)

A cash forecast therefore needs to schedule borrowing when the conditions can be met or waived. A power delay could postpone access to funds while other bills keep arriving. This scenario follows from the agreement, rather than documenting a funding blockage at an identified site.

## Receipts directed towards creditors

The same agreement provides for a [*cash trap*](/en/glossary/cash-trap/). At a monthly payment date, one possible trigger is a continuing material breach of a customer agreement that gives the counterparty a termination right, unless the breach has been waived in writing. Another is a projected contract-value ratio below **2.40 to one**. [S04](#source-S04)

The ratio compares a contractually defined value of agreements with outstanding loan principal. The detailed numerator definition is redacted. The 2.40 threshold is public, but CoreWeave’s reported backlog is insufficient to reconstruct this contractual measure. [S04](#source-S04)

The mechanism reserves some receipts and also makes new borrowing conditional on the absence of a continuing cash-trap event. One event could therefore leave less money freely available while making further funding harder to obtain. The payment waterfall retains its priorities: eligible operating costs, senior fees, interest, scheduled principal and a liquidity reserve come before the cash trap, which ranks sixth. [S04](#source-S04)

<AiCashFlowFigure lang="en" kind="trap" number={2} sourceHref="#source-S04" />

If the event continues across **three consecutive monthly payment dates**, a [*cash sweep*](/en/glossary/cash-sweep/) provision requires **100% of the cash in the relevant collateral accounts** to be applied to early repayment within **five business days**, limited to their balances. It specifies a pro forma ratio strictly above 2.40 after repayment. This mechanism can also follow a customer breach when the ratio is already above the threshold. If the trap ends earlier, money in the dedicated account may return to the available-cash account under the agreement’s terms. [S04](#source-S04)

These provisions keep receipts close to the claim they protect and can reduce outstanding principal. Their effect depends on the trigger and the payment sequence. At 30 September, CoreWeave reported compliance with all its loan [covenants](/en/glossary/covenant/), the undertakings governing a borrower’s conduct. The documents identified provide no confirmation of this cash trap being activated. [S01](#source-S01)

## Reserves pay for construction time

On 10 March, **APLD ComputeCo 2**, an Applied Digital subsidiary, issued $2.15 billion in principal of 6.75% notes, funding, among other uses, computing capacity at Polaris Forge 2 in North Dakota leased to Oracle. At an issue price of 98%, the gross proceeds were less than the principal to be repaid. [S05](#source-S05)

The indenture provides for **$121 million of interest during construction** and **$148 million in a debt service reserve**. If the power condition is unmet at issuance, the accounts are to be funded when escrow is released. They allow creditors to be paid while the project prepares to earn revenue. After the interest account is exhausted, using the reserve generally requires replenishment within 15 business days, subject to a specified exception while a terminated lease is being replaced. [S06](#source-S06)

The issue proceeds had been placed in escrow pending a certificate addressing, among other things, a power agreement compatible with the leases’ requirements and milestones. Applied Digital’s quarterly filing says the release condition was satisfied on **18 June**. That requirement is therefore a completed step in the financing’s history. [S06](#source-S06) [S07](#source-S07)

<AiCashFlowFigure lang="en" kind="reserves" number={3} sourceHref="#source-S06" />

Under the payment timetable, scheduled principal repayments begin after each initial lease has first commenced. That milestone differs from completion of every construction phase. Interest accrues from issuance, and the parent also provides a commitment to fund completion under the announced terms. These provisions allocate the cost of waiting among the parties. [S05](#source-S05) [S06](#source-S06)

The tenant supports the business model through its lease commitments. The documents separately allocate obligations to the issuer, its guarantors and its parent. Oracle’s role consequently needs to be assessed through the agreements to which it is a party. [S05](#source-S05)

The CRE Finance Council’s November primer distinguishes the stages between securing power and billing: connect and test the facility, then meet the lease requirements. Reserved capacity can subsequently earn rent even when the customer makes little use of it. The landlord’s income depends on the terms governing that capacity as well as the commissioning timetable. [S08](#source-S08)

## Liquidity at each payment date

These protections reserve resources for debt service and tie funding to project development. Their presence can make financing possible. Applied Digital has satisfied the escrow release condition. Establishing the two reserve accounts’ current balances or any recourse to the completion commitment would require further information.

CoreWeave also reported **$4.122 billion of cash and cash equivalents at 30 September**, separately from restricted cash. Liquidity analysis needs to match that consolidated balance against future payments and the rules governing cash movement between entities. [S01](#source-S01)

Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr offered a broader counterpoint on 16 November. He said he was relatively less concerned about direct losses given the debt accumulated so far, highlighting large banks’ strong capital positions and the apparent resilience of rental rates for older chips. His analysis also considers forced selling under certain leverage configurations as a conditional risk. [S09](#source-S09)

Monitoring projects requires following their transitions: draw conditions satisfied, billing started, receipts allocated and refinancing due. On the last point, the CREFC primer shows how unchanged income can support less new debt when funding costs rise. The gap then requires an equity contribution, repayment or an alternative financing structure. [S08](#source-S08)

The creditor map acquires a timetable. A bank can lend to a project, issue a letter of credit or finance a fund holding the loan, as [the private-credit instalment showed](/en/analysis/private-credit-ai-funds-bank-financing/). Identifying the balance sheet that might be called upon means connecting each agreement to its guarantees and resources available on the relevant date.

The documents establish several mechanisms that maintain or restrict access to funding. The final instalment will need to connect those rights with the identified risk holders, keeping an instrument and a date for each link. That approach can identify which creditors might be called upon at different stages of the same project.

## Method and limits

This investigation uses SEC-filed agreements, issuer financial statements, an industry primer and a central-bank speech, checked on 18 November 2026. Interim financial statements are unaudited. Company publications document their disclosures and accessible contractual terms, with partial visibility into execution. The investigation uses public records, without interviews or access to confidential agreements.

CoreWeave’s electricity deposit, letters of credit and DDTL are separate arrangements. Their amounts remain separate, and no identified document connects the $1.9 billion reclassification to the DDTL cash trap. The $154 million deposit is already included in the $877 million of non-current restricted cash. Deferred-revenue reclassifications concern contractual rights, with individual collections and refunds unknown.

Applied Digital’s $121 million and $148 million are allocations required by the indenture at escrow release, with their individual current balances undisclosed. On 18 June the condition was satisfied and the certificate requesting release was delivered. The exact transfer date remains unestablished. The identified documents confirm neither exhaustion of the reserves nor a call on the completion commitment.

The figures separate the deposit observed at 30 September, the loan’s conditional mechanism and the allocations required for the note issue. The social-card artwork is an AI-generated conceptual illustration. Accounts, commitments, guarantees and scenarios are used to examine financing channels, without calculating aggregate losses or net bank exposure.
