Around **14 May 2026**, a Backward Hat Capital employee asked Phillip Williams to approve a **10.01%** return for a fund marketed as aggressive. Williams asked for the previous month’s figure. The employee said **9.51%**. According to the federal criminal complaint, Williams then picked **9.91%** and wrote that he had bumped the figure up a bit. A Homeland Security Investigations agent recounts the exchange in a sworn statement. [S05](#source-S05)

Around April 2025, Devin Johnson told a prospective investor that the group used an artificial intelligence algorithm to exploit differences in currency prices, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) alleges. US authorities are trying to establish where the reported gains and the money paid to investors came from. Their filings bring together internal messages, the investors’ online portal and bank transfers. [S03](#source-S03) · [S05](#source-S05)

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and CFTC filed civil complaints on **17 November 2026**. The next day, federal prosecutors in Manhattan announced the arrests of Williams and Johnson and the unsealing of the criminal complaint. A federal court in Indiana ordered an asset freeze on **18 November** and appointed Kevin B. Duff temporary receiver on **19 November**. The allegations remain to be decided in court. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02) · [S03](#source-S03) · [S04](#source-S04) · [S06](#source-S06) · [S07](#source-S07)

## Steady returns on the screen

Backward Hat began raising money in **2022**, according to the SEC. The marketing described in its complaint laid out an apparently simple process. Subscriptions would go into bank accounts, be converted into cryptoassets and provide capital at brokerages. An algorithm would identify and trade small differences in currency prices. One presentation cited by the SEC put the stated exposure at **2% to 3%** of capital per trade and **25% to 30%** in total. [S02](#source-S02)

The criminal complaint says that, from **October 2023 to September 2026**, the portal generally showed monthly returns of **6.01% to 7.21%**. Prosecutors draw on internal messages to dispute where those returns came from. To see the scale of the promise, an investment that earned exactly **6%** each month and reinvested every gain would more than double over twelve months, before fees and with no losing months. That is the arithmetic of raising 1.06 to the twelfth power. [S05](#source-S05)

## Separate funds, shared accounts

The SEC describes **25 purported funds**. Its complaint says payments to many of them were pooled in two main bank accounts. Investors signed subscription agreements and promoters brought in new clients. Some promoters stood to receive a share of the performance fee on profits attributed to the clients they referred. The SEC says at least **five clients** of Williams’s former employer invested. [S02](#source-S02)

The private vehicles could file a **Form D**, the notice used for certain securities offerings exempt from registration. On EDGAR, the filing makes the offering and the issuer’s disclosures publicly available. The filing records a notice. Investors would need other evidence to assess the strategy and its returns. [S02](#source-S02) · [S09](#source-S09)

## Following the subscriptions

The SEC says approximately **$560 million** was raised from more than **2,000 investors**. The CFTC alleges more than **$556 million** from at least **2,100 participants**. The criminal complaint puts the figure above **$600 million**. Each estimate belongs to the case that produced it and to its underlying records. The figures measure money collected. The CFTC separately alleges losses of at least **$390 million** across more than **1,800 participants**, after some repayments. Final losses will depend in part on recoveries. [S01](#source-S01) · [S03](#source-S03) · [S05](#source-S05)

Across the three bank accounts it examines, the CFTC identifies **at least $303 million** that it characterises as Ponzi payments to participants. It also reports transfers of roughly **$92.9 million** to KMAP, **$41.4 million** to Allen Johnson Group LLC and **$27.3 million** to William Harvey Group, LLC. These are transfers reported by the regulator. Establishing where the money ended up and how much each recipient kept would take further records. [S03](#source-S03)

The Coinbase figures come from separate records. The SEC puts the money that passed through the group’s account between **July 2022 and September 2025** at about **$109 million**. The CFTC identifies about **$91 million** in its own account of the transfers. The public complaints leave those figures unreconciled. The SEC says the group received another **$350 million** or so from **October 2025 to 9 October 2026**, with no more investor money sent to the group’s Coinbase account for conversion. [S02](#source-S02) · [S03](#source-S03)

## A month in the bank accounts

The CFTC reconstructs **September 2026** from an opening balance of about **$3 million**. It puts subscriptions at **$15.1 million** and other receipts at **$0.7 million**. It reports **$13.4 million** in payments characterised as Ponzi payouts, **$4.9 million** in transfers to related entities and **$0.1 million** in other expenses. The closing balance was approximately **$0.4 million**. The figures are rounded. [S03](#source-S03)

<BackwardHatFigure lang="en" kind="september" number={2} />

An investor whose withdrawal went through received a real wire transfer. The CFTC alleges that other participants’ money funded some of those payments. That is how a [Ponzi scheme](/en/glossary/chaine-de-ponzi/) can work: the payout arrives, while the money depends on new subscriptions. The recipient’s bank statement is the start of the trail. Establishing where the cash came from requires the records of the business that sent it. [S03](#source-S03) · [S10](#source-S10)

## The one-dollar account cited by the SEC

Reports presented as independent audits bore the name of the Institute for Process Excellence (IpX). The SEC says IpX had no financial audit division. The regulator identifies Joseph Anderson as the author and says he was not an accountant. It describes his ties to Williams and payments of about **$2.5 million** through KMAP. The CFTC also recounts an intervention in how one report presented returns to investors. [S02](#source-S02) · [S03](#source-S03)

Two reports claimed to verify **$246 million in assets under management** for BH Capital Fund I LP as of **31 July 2025**, relying in part on bank statements. According to the SEC, the relevant account showed **one dollar** that day. A bank balance measures cash at one institution, while the reported fund assets covered a much wider claim. The dispute is over the verification that the reports claimed the bank documents supported. For a separate look at how private fund valuations are established, see [our investigation into private fund valuations](/en/analysis/zombie-funds-private-valuations/). [S02](#source-S02)

## Withdrawals slow and the pitch gets a new name

The criminal complaint dates the withdrawal problems to the **summer of 2026**. The messages it quotes mention bank checks and a change of broker. An October draft email would have extended the quoted withdrawal time from **10 to 15 business days** to **15 to 25**. Prosecutors compare those explanations with the transfers they say they traced. [S05](#source-S05)

The SEC also describes the promotion of **AI Capital** and later **Traders Point Capital**. AI Capital’s material reused the performance history attributed to Backward Hat and cited daily trading above **$120 million**. Traders Point applied for registration as an investment adviser on **11 September 2026**. An amended Form D filed on **9 October** reports a **$300 million** offering, **$4.482 million** in securities sold and **23 investors**. The first figure is the declared offering size. The second is the dollar amount the issuer reported selling in that filing. [S02](#source-S02) · [S08](#source-S08)

## Limits of the evidence

This investigation draws on public documents consulted on **23 November 2026**: the complaints, agency announcements, a signed order and the Form D. The conduct and flows attributed to the defendants are allegations. The asset freeze and appointment of a receiver aim to preserve assets while the proceedings continue. The parties can contest the allegations, and the criminal defendants are presumed innocent. [S01](#source-S01) · [S06](#source-S06) · [S07](#source-S07)

The agencies cooperated in the investigation. Reconciling their totals would take detailed bank statements, records for each fund and the claims accepted in the proceedings. The receivership order calls for a report on the assets. How much investors will recover remains open. The opening image is a digitally generated editorial illustration.
