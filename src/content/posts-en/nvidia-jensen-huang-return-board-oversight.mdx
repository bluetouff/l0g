**Nvidia’s board had said it did not intend to bring back Jensen Huang. Five days later it voted unanimously to restore him as president, chief executive and director.** The company dates the decision and its immediate effect to November 22. Its public announcement followed on November 23. The directors say the input they received from senior US officials gave them confidence that Huang’s return posed no national security risk. Donald Trump says he persuaded them. Nvidia says its independent investigation will continue, with Huang back in charge of the company it is examining. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03) [S07](#source-S07)

## Huang regains his three roles

On November 11, the directors accepted Huang’s resignation from all three posts, effective immediately. The next day’s filing named Colette Kress interim chief executive alongside her existing duties as finance chief. During the November 17 earnings call, board chair Harvey Jones said Huang had resigned after disclosing personal conduct inconsistent with Nvidia’s standards. Jones said the board did not intend to bring him back and would look at candidates inside and outside the company. Arm chief executive René Haas was named in coverage of the search. His roles at Arm and SoftBank made the prospect of a move a governance question in its own right, as we examined in [part IX](/en/analysis/nvidia-china-ai-credit-us-controls-rene-haas/). [S03](#source-S03) [S10](#source-S10) [S11](#source-S11)

The November 23 statement gives the directors’ stated reasons for reversing course. After a series of meetings and discussions with Huang, they say they were satisfied with his candor and ability to lead. Jones says the decision serves shareholders, employees, partners and customers. He remains chair of the board, so Huang returns to the board as a director with Jones still presiding. Kress continues as executive vice president and chief financial officer. The filing places the reinstatement on November 22 without an hour. Nvidia issued its statement at 8:55 a.m. Eastern, or 14:55 in Paris, on November 23. EDGAR accepted the filing at **8:57:54 a.m. Eastern**, or **14:57:54 in Paris**. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

Arm announced on November 23 that Haas had agreed to stay on through **2033**. SoftBank said he would also become vice chair of its board on December 1. [S10](#source-S10)

## Trump’s intervention and the directors’ vote

At **21:54 Eastern on November 22**, or **03:54 in Paris on November 23**, Trump wrote that he hoped the board would rehire Huang. The record leaves open whether the vote came before or after that post. At **9:07 a.m. Eastern on November 23**, Trump welcomed the decision. He said the federal government saw no reason to believe Huang’s return threatened national security and attributed to Attorney General Todd Blanche an assessment that the government had no evidence of unlawful conduct by Huang. In the same post, Trump called on Nvidia to continue its internal review of compliance with export controls. His account concerns Huang’s personal conduct and the risk of reinstating him. Nvidia’s own statement says the company will continue to cooperate with government inquiries. [S01](#source-S01) [S05](#source-S05) [S06](#source-S06)

In a phone interview with CNBC the same morning, Trump said he had spoken to Huang on Sunday night, had been in direct and indirect contact with Nvidia’s directors and was represented at a board meeting. He claimed his intervention persuaded the board. Nvidia’s own account confirms that senior US officials provided input during its deliberations. It leaves their identities, the substance of their advice and when the board received it undisclosed. Delaware corporate law places the company’s affairs under the board’s direction. Nvidia’s bylaws give the board authority over officer appointments and board vacancies. The filing records the directors using that authority. Any appointment or veto right for the government would need its own legal instrument, as our [analysis of public control rights](/en/analysis/ai-governance-anthropic-state-ownership/) explains. ## Three mandates inside Nvidia

A committee of independent directors is examining reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort and the circumstances behind them. It has retained independent counsel. The board says the investigation will run to completion and Huang has committed to cooperating. The reinstatement statement leaves the committee’s findings open. As chief executive, Huang can lead Nvidia while he remains a person from whom the investigators may need documents or testimony. The committee’s access to those records and its ability to pursue questions about the chief executive will matter more than the wording in the announcement. [S02](#source-S02)

A separate standing committee oversees national security, export compliance and global trade risk. Nvidia announced it on November 17, with Suzanne Nora Johnson as chair and Stephen C. Neal and John O. Dabiri as members. Its announced schedule calls for meetings at least quarterly. Nvidia said the two existing teams responsible for national security and trade compliance would report directly to the committee, and to Kress on day-to-day matters while she was interim chief executive. Nvidia reaffirmed the committee on November 23 and said it was engaging a lawyer with national security experience who would retain specialist advisers. The release leaves the teams’ day-to-day reporting arrangements under Huang unspecified. We covered the committee’s initial mandate and the trade rules it oversees in [part XVIII](/en/analysis/nvidia-board-national-security-export-controls/). [S02](#source-S02) [S04](#source-S04)

The announcement also creates an executive role. Nvidia has begun looking for a [chief compliance officer](/en/glossary/chief-compliance-officer/) to oversee compliance across the company and report to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee. That reporting line gives the new officer a route to the board through a body distinct from both the investigators and the national security committee. The remit beyond that description will matter in practice. For instance, who decides whether a shipment is held when the sales team thinks it can proceed? Who hears about an unresolved compliance objection, and how quickly? The wording of the announcement leaves those decisions open. In May, Nvidia told shareholders it had no chief compliance officer. Its report already described risk reviews led by the chief executive and other senior officers and regular compliance updates to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee. The new job gives that committee a named counterpart with a company-wide remit. [S02](#source-S02) [S19](#source-S19)

For directors, the distinction is practical. The investigators are examining events that have already taken place. The national security committee can ask for changes to how the business handles its trade risks. The new compliance chief will have responsibility across the company. A dispute that cuts across all three mandates will test whether independent directors can get the same information that management sees, and whether staff know where to raise a concern. ## Congress and prosecutors have their own questions

Elizabeth Warren, the senior Democrat on the Senate Banking Committee, asked the administration on November 23 to identify the officials who advised Nvidia and explain what they told the directors. She also called on Huang, the other executives and the board to answer for the company’s export controls. Three House committees had already confirmed voluntary, transcribed interviews with Huang on December 1 and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter on December 2. The Senate Banking Committee now lists Huang as president and CEO for a public hearing on export controls on December 3 at 10 a.m. in Washington. The interviews and the public hearing give lawmakers different ways to pursue the records and put questions to Nvidia. [S08](#source-S08) [S14](#source-S14) [S18](#source-S18)

California Attorney General Rob Bonta’s office told Reuters after the announcement that its investigation remains open. On November 16, the federal Justice and Commerce departments described their investigation into Singapore-based Megaspeed over potential export-control violations. Their account says Commerce requested records from Nvidia in May 2025 and that Nvidia put Megaspeed on its own no-sell list in June. The Justice Department then took the lead on the Megaspeed criminal investigation. The California inquiry and the federal Megaspeed case each have their own scope. [S09](#source-S09) [S13](#source-S13)

## Two deals moved ahead during the interim

The change at the top came after two milestones covered in [part XXII](/en/analysis/nvidia-anthropic-spacex-chip-financing/). Anthropic announced on November 20 that it had closed the private placement in which Nvidia was allocated approximately $10 billion of shares. On the same day, Apollo and Goldman Sachs announced pricing on approximately $44 billion of notes for equipment to be leased to SpaceX. The SpaceX commitment dates back to October. Its terms tie payment schedules to batches of equipment as they are delivered. We found no fresh confirmation of settlement or of a signed definitive lease in the public company records checked for this article. [S01](#source-S01) [S16](#source-S16) [S17](#source-S17) [S20](#source-S20)

These two transactions show that business continued while Kress was interim chief executive. They also explain why Nvidia’s new oversight structure matters well beyond one appointment. Nvidia can supply hardware and take stakes in customers, while financing arranged by other companies helps those customers buy or use equipment. The rights and obligations sit in individual contracts. If a return to the founder’s leadership changes the company’s approach to guarantees, sales or export-risk checks, that change should show up in those contracts, in operating decisions or in disclosures to investors. A unanimous vote tells us who will lead Nvidia. The next test is how the board responds if management and compliance staff disagree over a decision with real money at stake. ## Limitations

The public record establishes the date of the vote and the reinstatement. The board minutes, the substance of the US advice and the internal inquiry’s findings remain undisclosed. Nvidia has described the oversight mandates. The compliance chief’s powers and the operating teams’ reporting lines after the interim remain unspecified. Statements on Huang’s personal conduct are attributed to Trump. The ongoing inquiries each have their own scope.
