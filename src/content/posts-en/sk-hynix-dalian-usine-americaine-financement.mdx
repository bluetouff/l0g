A new American factory can begin with permission to send machinery to China. That sounds awkward for a reshoring strategy. It makes more sense when the investment is broken into stages: an established plant keeps supplying customers while its successor consumes capital, learns to manufacture reliably and earns customer approval. The complications multiply when several governments can change the terms of that transition.

On October 15, Reuters reported that SK hynix linked a possible US factory to additional equipment permissions for Dalian. The company disputes a capacity expansion at Mod B and says installation is authorised. No agreement or earmarked financing has been disclosed. <sup><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-S02" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a></sup>

That disagreement sets the boundary of this investigation. There is a credible economic explanation for why keeping Dalian competitive might make a US project more attractive. The available evidence does not establish that Chinese operations must pay for it. Indeed, the accounts show a group with substantial financial assets, and management explicitly reserves the choice between different sources of capital. The questions concern the terms on which SK hynix is willing to diversify production, the timing of that move and the investors who would bear its risks. <sup><a href="#source-S03" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-S14" aria-label="Source 14">[14]</a></sup>

## Two American projects hidden inside one word

The word “memory” conceals a crucial distinction. [NAND](/en/glossary/nand/) retains information when power is switched off and provides the storage inside an [SSD](/en/glossary/ssd/). High-bandwidth memory, or [HBM](/en/glossary/hbm/), stacks working-memory chips to feed data rapidly to processors. A computing system can need both, but they perform different jobs and follow different production routes. An investment in memory for artificial intelligence is not necessarily a NAND fabrication plant. <sup><a href="#source-S20" aria-label="Source 20">[20]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-S06" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a></sup>

The announced project in West Lafayette, Indiana, is an [advanced HBM packaging](/en/glossary/packaging-avance/) and testing facility. Its groundbreaking took place on August 27, 2026, an event also documented by Purdue University. SK hynix describes a production route in which wafers made in South Korea travel to Indiana for the final packaging and testing stages. This project has an identified location and a publicly described industrial role. <sup><a href="#source-S07" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-S06" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a></sup>

A potential US NAND fab would operate further upstream, producing the circuits on silicon wafers. Reporting about Dalian cannot turn that proposal into an already agreed extension of the Indiana project. On September 16, SK hynix had also stated that no specific arrangements had been finalised concerning reported discussions with Intel about US memory production. <sup><a href="#source-S19" aria-label="Source 19">[19]</a></sup>

The distinction matters when assessing industrial policy. Establishing a local packaging or testing operation can build valuable expertise, bring production closer to customers and remove particular dependencies. It does not automatically relocate the fabrication of the underlying chips. A useful measure of resilience asks which operation becomes locally available and which other operations still require a border to remain open.

It also changes the meaning of a supply guarantee. Securing the last manufacturing stage may protect delivery schedules while leaving access to wafers dependent on a different jurisdiction. A NAND plant and an HBM packaging facility therefore cannot be entered as interchangeable items in a tally of American semiconductor capacity. Their contribution has to be assessed within the production system they actually serve.

## Dalian and Solidigm have distinct legal boundaries

The Chinese plant entered the group through a transaction completed in stages. The first closing of Intel’s NAND and SSD disposal occurred on December 29, 2021. The final closing followed on March 27, 2025, transferring the remaining elements and ending the transitional manufacturing and sales agreement. During part of that interval, ownership of assets and responsibility for production did not line up in a simple one-company structure. <sup><a href="#source-S05" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-S04" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a></sup>

The subsidiary list at June 30, 2026 adds another distinction. Manufacturing entity SK hynix Semiconductor (Dalian) is controlled directly by SK hynix Inc.; Solidigm Inc. sits under SK hynix NAND Product Solutions Corp. A separate company with both “Solidigm” and “Dalian” in its name is classified as a sales business. A city in a company name is not sufficient evidence of factory ownership. <sup><a href="#source-S03" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup>

None of this prevents the businesses from working together. It makes the contracts between them important. One entity can manufacture chips, another develop SSD software or sell the finished product, and a third finance the equipment. Purchases, service agreements and payment terms then distribute margins and cash across the structure. The profit of a commercial subgroup is not, by itself, a measurement of cash generated at a particular manufacturing site.

“Dalian pays for America” could consequently describe several different relationships. Chinese operations might supply volumes that support group sales, release investment capacity elsewhere, or improve the terms on which partners are willing to contribute capital. An explicit allocation of dividends or intercompany loans would establish a more direct connection. The documents reviewed do not identify an amount earmarked for a new American factory.

This boundary is especially important for a possible Solidigm listing. Before assigning factory earnings to prospective shareholders, an investor would need to know the proposed asset perimeter, supply commitments and rights retained by the parent. Industrial continuity can survive a change in ownership. The price and durability of the contracts supporting that continuity then become central to the value being sold.

There is also a practical difference between owning a productive asset and having unrestricted access to its cash. A consolidated balance sheet combines entities for accounting purposes; it does not eliminate contractual obligations or the need to decide how funds move between them. An assessment of the US project would require the financing arrangements, rather than a convenient arrow from a Chinese factory to an American construction site.

## Equipment has to pass three different capacity tests

An empty cleanroom offers the possibility of production. Turning it into profitable shipments requires the right tools, a stable manufacturing process and products accepted by customers. In three-dimensional NAND, etching must penetrate increasingly complex stacks with adequate precision. Lam Research’s technical description helps explain why a machine is not an instantly interchangeable unit of productive capacity. <sup><a href="#source-S18" aria-label="Source 18">[18]</a></sup>

Wafer starts, memory content per wafer and the share of usable circuits describe different dimensions of output. Better manufacturing yield can increase saleable production with the same number of wafers. More bits per circuit can also change commercial output. Conversely, process trials and a difficult ramp can absorb equipment without producing a matching increase in shipments. These manufacturing relationships do not reveal what is happening inside Mod B. They explain why “installation”, “upgrade” and “expansion” require precise definitions.

Washington’s first control concerns equipment subject to US export rules. A rule published on September 2, 2025 removed three Chinese entities from the Validated End-User programme, including the entity then identified as Intel Semiconductor (Dalian) Ltd. The change took effect on December 31, 2025. It replaces a regulatory facilitation with licensing requirements for the relevant transactions. It does not, by itself, instruct the company to turn off every machine already installed. <sup><a href="#source-S10" aria-label="Source 10">[10]</a></sup>

The Bureau of Industry and Security announced an intention to support existing operations rather than expansion or technology upgrades. That leaves the decisive questions at licence level: which tools, for which uses, under what conditions? The relevant licence texts and technical scope are not public in the material assembled here. A general BIS policy statement cannot settle the disagreement between the company’s account and the reporting about Mod B. <sup><a href="#source-S09" aria-label="Source 9">[9]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-S02" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a></sup>

A second set of constraints accompanies funding under the federal CHIPS semiconductor-incentive programme. US guardrails restrict material expansion in covered countries for ten years. The NIST description of a greater-than-5% threshold refers to a contractual capacity baseline and defined changes, including additional cleanroom space or production lines. It is neither an annual allowance to grow by 5% nor an export permit. The guidance also distinguishes ordinary efficiency improvements from material expansion. <sup><a href="#source-S11" aria-label="Source 11">[11]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-S12" aria-label="Source 12">[12]</a></sup>

A third framework comes from China. The December 2021 merger approval imposed rising output for specified enterprise SSD categories over five years, alongside supply obligations to the Chinese market. It did not require an expansion specifically at Mod B. Published on December 22, the decision allows an application to remove conditions after five years, subject to approval. The anniversary does not automatically cancel them. <sup><a href="#source-S13" aria-label="Source 13">[13]</a></sup>

The same investment can thus be examined as an equipment shipment, a change in industrial capacity and a commitment to customers. Passing one test does not resolve the others. This is the regulatory setting in which an industrial bargain would have to work, and several of the project-specific contracts remain unavailable.

It would be equally premature to conclude that these obligations are necessarily incompatible. Output can change through product mix or manufacturing efficiency; supplies can come from more than one site. Determining whether a particular plan complies requires the actual equipment, production baseline, agreements and permissions. The public texts identify the questions. They do not establish either a violation or an exemption for the proposed arrangement.

## The balance sheet changes the financing story

A general shortage of money is difficult to reconcile with the published figures. At June 30, the group reported nearly KRW 88 trillion of cash and short-term financial assets against roughly KRW 18.6 trillion of borrowings. The net balance under that measure was close to KRW 69.4 trillion. <sup><a href="#source-S03" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-S21" aria-label="Source 21">[21]</a></sup>

Composition matters as much as the total. The reconciliation below separates cash and cash equivalents from short-term instruments and investment assets. Those categories do not necessarily have identical availability or valuation characteristics. Nor does a consolidated amount certify Solidigm’s bank balance or the unrestricted transferability of every asset into an American investment. The accounting perimeter prevents an indicator of financial strength from being mistaken for an immediately drawable project cheque.

On August 19, the board nevertheless found room to announce a KRW 40 trillion share repurchase and cancellation programme. That decision cannot be subtracted mechanically from the June balance to calculate October liquidity: the dates differ and completion of the entire programme has not been verified here. It does demonstrate that the debate concerns competing uses of capital as well as access to funding. <sup><a href="#source-S15" aria-label="Source 15">[15]</a></sup>

In its October 1 clarification, SK hynix defended the proposition that a company with internal funds might still prefer to raise money elsewhere. Management has an interest in preserving negotiating flexibility. The argument nevertheless reflects a genuine economic distinction: being able to finance a project and accepting its commercial terms are separate decisions. <sup><a href="#source-S14" aria-label="Source 14">[14]</a></sup>

A group might demand that existing plants remain competitive before agreeing to a more expensive or slower investment elsewhere. Such a condition could protect expected returns, insure against lost sales during the transition, or preserve capital for other projects. The public record does not allow any one of these motives to be identified as the exclusive explanation. It does make a confident narrative of an American fab blocked by empty coffers untenable.

That does not make the financing choice trivial. Internal money has alternative uses; equity funding gives new investors a claim on future earnings; debt introduces repayment obligations. The relevant comparison is the full economic cost of each option and the risks attached to it. The factory’s geographical location is only one part of that calculation.

## Funding the overlap between existing and new production

The most tangible financing exposure lies between the first equipment payment and dependable commercial receipts. Suppliers must be paid and teams recruited while saleable production is still being established. An existing plant can buy time by preserving customer relationships and cash inflows during the new asset’s ramp. That is the plausible mechanism connecting Chinese operations to a US investment, even without a dedicated transfer of Chinese profits to the construction budget.

Indiana offers a public illustration of the length of this interval, provided its HBM scope is kept clear. The August timetable targets cleanroom availability in October 2028 and mass production in the second half of 2029. These are company objectives. They show why groundbreaking cannot be treated as the immediate replacement of foreign production. <sup><a href="#source-S06" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a></sup>

Government support has a timetable of its own. The award announced in December 2024 provided for up to $458 million of direct funding and up to $500 million in loans for the Indiana project, with payments linked to milestones. Adding the ceilings together as a subsidy already received, or transferring them to a different NAND fab, would invent financing. <sup><a href="#source-S08" aria-label="Source 8">[8]</a></sup>

A group that builds before reducing output elsewhere can temporarily support two sets of costs. One that cuts first risks lost sales and a harder start for the replacement facility. Outside financing transfers part of that exposure to lenders or new shareholders, who will ask for compensation and protection. Public assistance can ease particular payments while imposing conditions on the activities the recipient intends to retain.

The useful policy question is therefore which dependencies remain acceptable during the transition, for how long, and with what exit arrangements. Verifiable investment commitments, qualification milestones and supply obligations would help establish whether maintaining Dalian genuinely supports diversification. Without them, support described as temporary might instead prolong the original concentration. Either outcome is consistent with the mere existence of negotiations.

A credible transition plan also needs to define what happens when schedules slip. The old plant’s permissions and the new plant’s delivery obligations may run on different clocks. If the bridge expires before the replacement is ready, a manufacturing delay can become a supply disruption. If permissions keep being extended without corresponding construction progress, the transition loses its measurable endpoint. Neither sequence has been established in this case; both belong in the assessment of a future agreement.

## Memory prices can move before the construction bill does

The bridge-financing argument still assumes that existing operations generate a surplus. Selling more bits does not guarantee more cash. Prices, product quality, costs and payment terms together determine what can be reinvested. A customer agreement can make volumes more predictable without removing every one of those risks. The terms needed to quantify that protection at Dalian are not available in this evidence set.

A deliberately simple example makes the mechanism visible. Assume an identical period and constant volume, with 100 units of sales, 60 of variable cash costs and 20 of fixed cash operating costs. The remaining 20 comes before investment, tax, financing and changes in working capital. If prices fall by one quarter and costs are unchanged over the period, sales decline to 75 and the operating cash surplus becomes a shortfall of 5.

This calculation is not calibrated to SK hynix or Dalian. It isolates a price decline when costs cannot immediately adjust. In an actual plant, lower unit costs, a different product mix, contractual protection or output reductions would alter the result. The chart identifies a variable that matters; it does not predict a reversal in the memory market.

The connection with a US construction project is still clear. To the extent that the new investment relies economically on surpluses from existing operations, it also depends on commercial conditions during construction. Committed payments for the new asset may remain due while expected cash inflows weaken. Refinancing exposure can increase without any government changing a licence. Conversely, strong demand and good manufacturing yields can make the overlap much easier to support.

Geographical diversification does not remove the industry cycle. It can temporarily increase the amount of capital tied up before delivering resilience benefits. That transition cost needs to be set against the protection being purchased, using explicit price assumptions rather than an extrapolation of the most recent quarter.

The same logic explains why more physical production and more financial independence are not synonymous. Additional equipment may require spending before it improves yields or expands sales. An assessment based only on installed tools misses the intervening cash demands and the conditions under which customers will accept the output. The financing bridge must be tested in cash terms, across time, rather than inferred from a capacity headline.

## Raising money also requires a route through Seoul

On October 19, Reuters reported that Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) Governor Lee Chan-jin viewed a potential US listing of Solidigm as conflicting with duplicate-listing rules, a position he also attributed to the Financial Services Commission (FSC). In a clarification reported by Edaily later that day, he said no formal request for a legal interpretation had yet been submitted. The scope of domestic securities-registration requirements therefore remains to be clarified. These statements establish the supervisor’s objection without establishing a final prohibition or a completed listing structure. <sup><a href="#source-S16" aria-label="Source 16">[16]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-S23" aria-label="Source 23">[23]</a></sup>

The issue reaches beyond the exchange on which shares might trade. An investor in the parent already owns an indirect interest in the group’s businesses. Opening a subsidiary’s capital can bring in new money while changing claims on its future earnings. The outcome depends on the price, whether shares are issued or sold, control retained and the assets actually included. A transaction attractive to the subsidiary can have a different effect on the parent’s shareholders.

The FSC’s July proposal focused on those shareholder interests, protective measures and board duties, including for listings overseas. That publication describes proposed rules; it cannot alone establish the final legal treatment of a particular October transaction. It nevertheless explains the governance concern behind the current examination. <sup><a href="#source-S17" aria-label="Source 17">[17]</a></sup>

New shares that raise funds for a company must also be distinguished from existing shares sold by a shareholder, who receives the proceeds. Confusing the two creates imaginary investment capacity. The documents used here contain neither a definitive listing structure nor a final US financing plan. Consequently, no IPO proceeds have been inserted into the analysis.

The company plans to present third-quarter results on October 28. That provides a dated opportunity to look for updated financial resources and commitments, without assuming that the licences or a NAND project will be detailed at the event. <sup><a href="#source-S22" aria-label="Source 22">[22]</a></sup>

Dalian emerges from the investigation as a possible source of industrial continuity and bargaining power. Its role in a specific American financing arrangement remains to be documented. The accounts make the group’s other options impossible to ignore, while the regulatory texts show that each arrives with its own conditions. The most informative development will be the conversion of commercial intent into a verifiable commitment, with an industrial perimeter, identified funding and milestones that can be tracked.

Reshoring is ultimately measured by usable capacity and dependencies actually reduced. Between ordering tools and making the first deliveries, a company may have good reasons to preserve the old production system. The difficult question is what that continuity secures for the next one.
