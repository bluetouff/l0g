In May 2026, Britain accused the Russian payment network **A7** of handling proceeds from oil sales. In a separate filing, US financial intelligence agency FinCEN identifies **at least 179.1 billion dollars in A7A5 transactions** between February 2025 and June 2026. That total adds up token transactions whose commercial purpose has yet to be established individually. Once oil is sold, the cargo and its payment leave different records. [S04](#source-S04) · [S10](#source-S10)

We first traced [ownership of the tanker Arrow](/en/analysis/shadow-fleet-arrow-tanker-ownership/), then [a loan raised in Moscow for Lidoil in Dubai](/en/analysis/shadow-fleet-rouble-finance-lid-capital-lidoil/). This instalment follows a separate case. An oil sale abroad generates proceeds in foreign currency. The seller still needs money where its own bills fall due. A7 markets payment services to both importers and exporters. [S03](#source-S03) · [S04](#source-S04) · [S25](#source-S25)

## Liquidity on both sides of the border

Britain’s **National Crime Agency** describes offshore pools of money and payment intermediaries in its August 2026 alert. The economics come from matching different needs. An exporter wants roubles after receiving money abroad. An importer has roubles and needs to pay a foreign supplier. An intermediary can bring these demands together using money already available on either side. The service depends on how much cash it can mobilise when payments fall due. The NCA presents this as a typology based on a limited level of review. [S03](#source-S03) · [S04](#source-S04)

<ShadowFleetPaymentFigure lang="en" kind="clearing" number={1} />

A7 also describes the use of a *veksel*, a promissory note that records an obligation to pay. The FAQ describes a payment note denominated in roubles. The terms of the separate interest-bearing note with foreign-currency face value also specify repayment in roubles. For the customer, the note’s value depends on the issuer’s ability to honour that obligation. Tracing one of those notes to a paid invoice would require records from the issuer and the customer. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02)

## Oil proceeds in the public record

**The Big Shor**, published in June by the Open Source Centre with TRM Labs, documents offshore pools using internal records. Its authors treat funding from energy proceeds as a lead supported by partial evidence. The NCA and FinCEN cite that report, so part of their account draws on the same material. A separate investigation by the Centre for Information Resilience describes intermediaries in the network. FinCEN says it also drew on information outside the public record. [S03](#source-S03) · [S04](#source-S04) · [S05](#source-S05) · [S08](#source-S08) · [S21](#source-S21)

Attributing a payment to a specific cargo would require the invoice, proof of payment and shipping documents. FinCEN’s October alert says trade documents can disguise the real customer and that financial institutions can handle transactions without knowing the underlying arrangement. A bank’s role in a transfer establishes one stage of processing. Establishing what the bank knew would require other evidence. [S05](#source-S05)

## Token volume and the value of invoices

The **A7A5** project says that its [stablecoin](/en/glossary/stablecoin/) aims to track the rouble and is backed by bank deposits. In its proposal published on **5 October 2026**, FinCEN identifies **more than 17 billion dollars** in dollar-denominated transactions processed by the network’s sub-agents from January 2025 to June 2026. The minimum of **179.1 billion dollars** covers A7A5 transactions over a different period. Each figure measures its own category of activity and time window. [S04](#source-S04) · [S11](#source-S11)

The same tokens can enter a transaction total several times. If **100 tokens** change hands **three times**, the recorded volume is **300 tokens**. Supply remains **100** in the absence of issuance or destruction. Transfers may represent payments, movements between accounts or treasury operations. Sorting them requires the economic context. [S04](#source-S04) · [S12](#source-S12)

TRM Labs estimated that about **34% of the A7A5 trading volume** studied for its annual report was likely artificial. This applies to the trading activity TRM covered. FinCEN counted a different set of transactions over a different period. Applying TRM’s percentage to FinCEN’s total would combine incompatible measures. [S04](#source-S04) · [S12](#source-S12)

## The issuer’s powers

A7A5’s public source code provides for a pause in transfers and a blocklist. It also allows tokens held at a blocked address to be destroyed, subject to administrative roles. For the **Ethereum** address named by the project, the repository file and the source published by Blockscout match byte for byte. Blockscout marks the contract as partially verified. The current holders of the administrative keys and deployments on other networks fall outside this check. [S15](#source-S15) · [S16](#source-S16) · [S23](#source-S23)

**Decurity**’s January 2025 code audit excludes centralisation risks at the client’s request. The project’s documentation also lists custody, liquidity and redemption risks. The public address for an earlier reserve report now returns an application page. Verifying current reserve balances would require further records. [S14](#source-S14) · [S17](#source-S17) · [S20](#source-S20)

## The token’s decline and A7’s reported activity

**Elliptic** estimates that A7A5 transfers averaged **24.3 million dollars a day** in June 2026, a **96%** fall from the July 2025 peak. Its analysis, published on 29 July, cites possible drivers including sanctions and access to exchanges. [S13](#source-S13)

<ShadowFleetPaymentFigure lang="en" kind="volume" number={2} />

On **18 November**, an A7 executive told Reuters that activity across the network had risen and that customers were mostly in agriculture and metals. His description covers the company’s wider services over a different period from Elliptic’s study. A comparison of the network over time would require a consistently defined measure of payments actually completed. [S13](#source-S13) · [S19](#source-S19)

## US sanctions and the proposed rule

The US Treasury announced the network’s designation by [OFAC](/en/glossary/ofac/) on **1 October 2026**. The [Financial Crimes Enforcement Network](/en/glossary/fincen/), or FinCEN, separately proposed a measure for certain transfers involving the network’s sub-agents by covered institutions. On **19 November**, FinCEN’s official index still lists the measure as a proposal. A case involving **nearly 140 million dollars** in the US documents concerns entities FinCEN links to the evasion of **Iranian** sanctions. A7 disputed any links to Iran, Reuters reported. [S04](#source-S04) · [S06](#source-S06) · [S07](#source-S07) · [S24](#source-S24)

In its annual results published in March 2026, **Rosneft**’s management said barriers to international payments had pushed up the cost of settlement and currency conversion. It listed several factors affecting its results. The statement gives a commercial stake to the investigation. Every additional delay widens the gap between a sale and the cash the company can spend. Measuring that cost or delay would require records for individual transactions. [S18](#source-S18)

## Scope of the investigation

The investigation covers documents available on **19 November 2026**. We attribute official allegations, commercial estimates and company statements to their authors. The records describe a payment network and its reported links to oil proceeds. The evidence stops short of bank statements, matched cargoes or a payment attributable to Arrow, Citrine or Lidoil. Company responses are drawn from the cited publications. The cover image is an AI-generated illustration.
