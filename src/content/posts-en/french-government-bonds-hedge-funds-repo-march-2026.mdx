On 2 April 2026, France [reopened a bond](/en/glossary/reouverture-obligataire/) it had auctioned four weeks earlier. The bond carried the same identifier, FR0014012II5, the same November 2035 maturity and the same annual [coupon](/en/glossary/coupon-obligataire/) of €3.50 per €100 of face value. In March successful bidders had paid a weighted average of €100.90 for that promise. In April they paid €98.16, both prices excluding accrued interest. France was raising less money per bond for the same future payments. [S05](#source-S05) · [S06](#source-S06) · [S03](#source-S03) · [S15](#source-S15)

The Wednesday before the April [auction](/en/glossary/adjudication/), another number stood well below its February level. Across the European repo transactions visible to the ECB, hedge funds, investment vehicles able to borrow or sell securities short, had borrowed **a net €22.38 billion in cash against French government bonds**, down from €33.15 billion on 25 February. One figure records bids at a Treasury auction. The other comes from the market where investors raise cash against securities. How much of the change in France’s borrowing costs can we explain by following that money? [S02](#source-S02)

[The September investigation](/en/analysis/french-debt-hedge-funds/) used a numerical example to explain how a ten-year bond can be financed with borrowing due within days. On 18 November 2026, the ECB published data that allow that mechanism to be examined alongside actual funding positions during a period of market stress. The spring pullback was larger against Spanish and Italian bonds. By the autumn, as borrowing costs in France and Germany drew further apart, net borrowing against French bonds was rising again. On 4 November it reached **€55.13 billion**, the highest French reading in all 96 observations of the ECB data. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02)

## The second life of a French bond

At auction, the money goes to the French Treasury. Once the bond reaches its buyer, it can raise cash for someone else. The buyer sells it to a bank and agrees to buy it back on specified terms. Economically, the bank advances cash and holds the bond as security. At maturity the investor returns the cash plus the agreed interest and gets the bond back. This is a [repurchase agreement, or *repo*](/en/glossary/repo/). The two parties can complete the transaction years before France owes the principal on the underlying bond. [S07](#source-S07) · [S17](#source-S17)

Consider a fund that finds a [French Treasury bond, known as an OAT](/en/glossary/oat/), cheap relative to the price at which it could be delivered under a futures contract. In this [cash-futures trade](/en/glossary/basis-trade/), it buys the bond, finances the position in repo and sells the future to hedge some of its interest-rate exposure. The fund hopes to earn the price difference as the two prices converge, after paying for funding. But the bank rolling the repo and the clearing house settling the futures margin keep different accounts. A gain on one leg can arrive too late to meet a cash demand on the other. The fund can draw on reserves, post more collateral or close the position. Closing it may mean selling the bond and buying back the future. [S08](#source-S08)

<FrenchRepoCircuit2026 lang="en" />

It matters which way round the trade runs. When ECB researchers matched repo and futures positions for a sample of Cayman-domiciled funds through April 2024, the pattern they found often ran in the opposite direction. The funds bought futures and borrowed the underlying bonds, presumably to sell them. In June 2025 the Banque de France also reported broadly balanced average long and short positions in French, German and Italian government bond futures. Cash borrowing against a French bond is compatible with several strategies. To establish what the manager is betting on, one would need the rest of the book. [S08](#source-S08) · [S09](#source-S09)

## The spring withdrawal, one Wednesday at a time

By February 2026, before the March pullback, hedge funds had built up roughly **€640 billion in gross repo positions** against euro-area sovereign bonds. Gross counts both sides of the business. A fund borrowing cash from one bank and lending it against securities elsewhere contributes to both. In March, gross hedge fund positions fell 13.3%. Banks’ positions grew 8.6%, while those of the institutions the ECB classifies as dealers fell 3.5%. Banking activity was therefore expanding as the funds pulled back. Each category’s percentage change has its own starting base in the ECB table. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02)

Follow the cash instead and a different calculation is needed. For each collateral issuer, the ECB publishes the amount of cash borrowed less cash lent. We added the four weekly balances. Together they accounted for net borrowing of €104.89 billion on 25 February, the last Wednesday of the month. The total actually rose to €109.99 billion on 4 March before falling to €54.52 billion on 1 April. [S02](#source-S02)

Of the **€50.37 billion decline** between 25 February and 1 April, Spanish collateral accounted for €17.65 billion, Italian for €15.36 billion and French for €10.77 billion. Germany accounted for €6.58 billion, through a net lending balance that grew larger: funds were supplying more cash against German bonds than they were borrowing. For each issuer, a decline in net borrowing can reflect either repaid borrowing or additional cash lending. Both changes move the published balance in the same direction. [S02](#source-S02)

The choice of Wednesday also changes the size of the move. Stop on 25 March, before quarter-end, and the four-issuer decline from 25 February was already €38.42 billion, of which €5.60 billion was French. The pullback was under way before the reporting date, although the size of the French move depends on the window. A fund has time to cut a position, rebuild it or switch collateral between successive weekly readings. [S02](#source-S02)

## Three auctions put a price on the same bond

Take the November 2035 bond again. Its weighted-average auction yield **fell from 3.45% on 5 February to 3.39% on 5 March**, then **rose to 3.73% on 2 April**. The extra 34 basis points between March and April amount to 0.34 percentage points. The purchase price was lower and the auction yield higher, while the coupon and maturity were unchanged. In April the primary dealers authorised to bid at the competitive auction submitted bids for €11.644 billion in face value, of which France awarded €6.185 billion. Bidders were still submitting substantial orders, at a price per bond that had become less favourable to the Treasury. [S04](#source-S04) · [S05](#source-S05) · [S06](#source-S06) · [S16](#source-S16)

<FrenchOatPricing2026 lang="en" />

There is a straightforward route from secondary trading to the state’s next issue. A dealer that can buy the identical security more cheaply in the market has little reason to bid much higher at auction. If several funds have to sell together and other investors demand a price concession to absorb their bonds, that concession can reach the Treasury. [S05](#source-S05) · [S06](#source-S06) · [S01](#source-S01)

The German benchmark broadens the comparison. Between February and March, France’s benchmark ten-year yield rose from a monthly average of 3.40% to 3.64%. Germany’s rose too, from 2.745% to 2.910%. The gap widened by **7.5 basis points**. In its June review the Banque de France attributed part of the broader rise in yields to expectations of tighter monetary policy after the energy shock. [S10](#source-S10) · [S11](#source-S11) · [S12](#source-S12)

## French collateral stands out by autumn

Between 1 April and 4 November, net borrowing against French government bonds increased by **€32.75 billion**. Across all four issuers the increase was only **€22.04 billion**. The arithmetic tells us where to look. The Italian series recovered modestly, Spanish net borrowing fell a little further and net cash lending against German bonds increased by €12.27 billion. The hedge fund sector can raise cash against French collateral while supplying cash against German collateral. These are sector-wide net balances for each issuer’s collateral. [S02](#source-S02)

The yield gap between France and Germany had its sharpest monthly widening much later. It averaged 96.6 basis points in September and 147.4 in October, **up 50.8 basis points**. Compare the same two months in the repo workbook. Net borrowing against French collateral increased from €35.71 billion on average over September’s five Wednesdays to €45.88 billion over October’s four. The autumn therefore combined a wider yield spread with more net funding against French bonds. Short-lived sales, derivative hedges and trades between maturities could all occur between Wednesday observations. [S10](#source-S10) · [S11](#source-S11) · [S02](#source-S02)

## How far does the American trail go?

On 10 March, French MP Nicolas Sansu asked the government how much influence hedge funds wielded over public debt. The Economy Ministry’s reply, published on 23 June, stressed how actively funds traded and how varied the ultimate holders were. That is the issuer’s view. The ECB data now offer a measure of how concentrated the financing is. In January 2026, **the five largest hedge funds accounted for 40.7% of their sector’s repo positions** against euro-area government collateral. Across all participants’ gross positions, the entire hedge fund sector accounted for 13.8%, rising to 27.3% in the bilateral segment alone. Across the observed government-collateral repo market, the ECB’s domicile breakdown attributes 95.6% of hedge fund activity to Cayman-domiciled vehicles. A vehicle’s country of registration is a different piece of information from the location of its investment decisions. [S13](#source-S13) · [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02)

The other side of the trade is concentrated too. In June, the Banque de France reported that **five institutions provided nearly two-thirds of hedge funds’ repo financing** in the euro area. The concentration is on both sides of the financing relationship. Establishing a strategy run from the United States would require the vehicles’ managers, bank relationships and hedges. Reporting coverage matters too: a derivatives trade between a Cayman fund and a counterparty outside the EU can fall outside the European data. [S07](#source-S07) · [S01](#source-S01)

Rate changes reach the public budget as new bonds are issued and old debt is refinanced. On 20 October, Antoine Deruennes, the head of Agence France Trésor, gave the French Senate a measure of that transmission. His scenario assumes a permanent one-percentage-point rise in short- and long-term rates from 1 January 2027. The extra interest charge reaches €3.4 billion in 2027 and €8.1 billion in 2028. The figures are annual additions to the baseline interest bill for each year. [S14](#source-S14)

French debt prices also depend on the funding conditions faced by bondholders. An investor short of cash can cut a position it still regards as sound. In March, hedge funds reduced their repo activity. By early November, they were raising more net cash against French bonds than at any other point in the series. The decisive next step would be to connect an identified sale, its funding conditions and the price bid at a new government auction. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02) · [S06](#source-S06)

## Limits

The repo series are Wednesday snapshots aggregated by collateral issuer. Each net balance combines cash borrowed and cash lent; changes can also reflect a switch in collateral. The workbook’s latest observation is 4 November 2026. [S02](#source-S02)

The auctions cover one bond on three dates. OECD/FRED yields are monthly averages for ten-year government benchmarks. The comparison uses different frequencies and securities to put the movements in context. Establishing a causal effect from hedge funds would require identified trades and a credible comparison scenario. Concentration measures change denominator between markets. The vehicle’s domicile is distinct from the manager’s location. Derivatives trades between two non-EU counterparties are partly outside EMIR reporting. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02)
