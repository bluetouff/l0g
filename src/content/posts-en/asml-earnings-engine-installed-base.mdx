An ASML lithography machine can keep generating revenue long after it has been delivered. Its light source needs maintenance, its precision has to be sustained, parts wear out and customers may buy upgrades to raise throughput. In the third quarter of 2026, services and field options generated €3.289 billion. The earnings attached to machines already in factories now deserve as much attention as new equipment sales. [1](#source-1), [2](#source-2)

The opportunity goes beyond customer loyalty. When chipmakers need more capacity, improving a tool that is already connected and qualified can be valuable. ASML gets another opportunity to monetise technology around which a customer has organised production. The practical question is how much of that incremental value the chipmaker keeps, and how much the equipment supplier can capture.

The accounts offer an initial answer, with an important qualification. In the first half of 2026, under European IFRS, services and field options accounted for almost all of the increase in gross profit. That comparison is also affected by higher amortisation of capitalised development spending. The figures show an accounting shift; standalone installed-base operating profit and contract-level returns remain undisclosed. [5](#source-5); l0g calculations

## A machine has more than one commercial life

Lithography projects circuit patterns onto silicon wafers. It is used at several stages in manufacturing. [Extreme ultraviolet, or EUV, systems](/en/glossary/euv/) serve particularly demanding layers; deep ultraviolet, or DUV, is also used for other layers and processes. A production tool must combine resolution, alignment precision and throughput while remaining available on the factory floor. [13](#source-13)

Its usefulness can improve without replacing the entire machine. Upgrades can change wafer handling, light-source power, software or process precision. ASML describes these levers in its product and support documentation. An installed system is therefore a platform on which additional capabilities can be sold. [12](#source-12), [13](#source-13)

The relationship can last a long time. ASML's 2025 annual report says roughly 95% of the systems sold over the preceding thirty years remain in use. That is a statement about equipment longevity from the manufacturer. Utilisation rates and the value of current service contracts require separate evidence. An older tool can remain useful in a less advanced process while earning very different annual revenue from a recently installed EUV machine. [3](#source-3), p. 18

Two forces shape the economics. Shipments expand the fleet that may require services. Technical improvements expand the products ASML can sell to certain generations of that fleet. Revenue also depends on how intensively customers operate the machines and when they choose to upgrade them. Counting tools is a starting point, rather than a formula for automatic annual income.

In Q3 2026, service and field-option sales rose 67.6% year on year, compared with 50.8% for systems. They represented 28.2% of quarterly sales. Their increase contributed €1.327 billion to a total revenue gain of €4.146 billion, or about 32%. Systems remained the larger source of incremental revenue in absolute terms, while the installed base became more important. [2](#source-2); l0g calculations

## One revenue line, several contractual schedules

ASML calls the business [Installed Base Management](/en/glossary/parc-installe/). In the financial statements, the relevant line is net service and field-option sales. It combines activities with different revenue patterns. Only some of them fit a straightforward recurring-revenue description.

A maintenance agreement may pay for staff and parts to be available over a defined period. Revenue is generally recognised over time as that stand-ready service is provided. Performance clauses can introduce variable remuneration. Billable parts and one-off work follow their own delivery or service milestones. [4](#source-4), note 2

Other revenues track usage. Under the OnPulse model described by ASML, light-source maintenance payments depend on pulse counts. A fab running its tools harder may generate more of this revenue. The link to customer activity is more immediate than under a simple annual subscription. [4](#source-4), note 2

Upgrades introduce another pattern. Some options are recognised when delivered. Work requiring substantial installation may be recognised over the installation period, according to the contractual performance obligation. A strong wave of upgrades can therefore create an exceptional quarter without turning into an identical subscription in the following quarter. [4](#source-4), note 2

<AsmlFigure id="02" lang="en" />

That distinction helps explain Q3. In the prepared remarks for the 14 October earnings call, management attributed the installed-base business's roughly €400 million guidance beat to strong demand for EUV and DUV productivity upgrades. It also linked the group's 57.5% gross margin to particularly high-margin components within the installed-base business. Those comments identify a driver, rather than disclose the margin of the entire business. [6](#source-6), p. 1

The different revenue streams can respond differently to a downturn. A maintenance retainer can provide visibility. Usage-linked components may weaken when production falls. A major upgrade can be advanced, deferred or grouped with other interventions. Treating the aggregate as one recurring revenue stream would conceal much of the mechanism investors and customers need to understand.

## Where the gross-profit mix shifts

The statutory interim report provides a breakdown absent from the quarterly US GAAP table: cost of sales is separated between systems and services/options. That makes it possible to calculate [gross profit](/en/glossary/profit-brut/) for each category. Gross profit is the revenue left after costs classified as cost of sales; research, selling expenses and other charges still have to be covered. [5](#source-5), pp. 5 and 12

Between the first halves of 2025 and 2026, gross profit from services and options rose from €2.185 billion to €3.164 billion. Systems remained close to €6.04 billion. The total increase of €982 million comprises €979.1 million from services/options and €2.9 million from systems. The installed-base category thus explains 99.7% of the increase in this accounting framework and period. [5](#source-5); l0g calculations

<AsmlFigure id="03" lang="en" />

The accounting matters. ASML says the fall in its overall IFRS gross margin, from 53.3% to 50.9%, was mainly caused by higher amortisation of capitalised development spending. Some expenditure was initially recorded as an asset and is subsequently charged to earnings. The amortisation schedule can affect comparisons between periods and activities. The interim report itself has neither been audited nor reviewed by an external auditor. [5](#source-5), pp. 4-7

US GAAP treats this spending differently. Extending the IFRS chart with the US quarterly margin, or transferring the 99.7% attribution to US GAAP, would produce a misleading series. The calculation describes the change in reported IFRS gross profit. It does not measure the whole operational improvement, cash generation or net earnings contribution.

Levels also matter. In H1 2026, services and options contributed about 34.4% of IFRS gross profit on 29% of revenue. Systems still generated nearly two-thirds of gross profit. Across the full 2025 year under US GAAP, calculated gross margins were 50.9% for services/options and 53.5% for systems. Superior installed-base profitability is therefore sensitive to product mix, timing and accounting, rather than an immutable feature of the business. [3](#source-3), p. 54; 5; l0g calculations

## Selling more output inside an existing fab

An upgrade's value is easiest to understand from the customer's side. A faster machine can handle more wafer passes in the same floor space. That additional capacity can avoid some of the constraints associated with installing another tool. Its actual value depends on the upgrade price, downtime, demand and the way the production line is organised.

A recent example shows the scale. The 2025 annual report describes a PEP-E software upgrade raising NXE:3800E productivity from 220 to 230 wafers per hour. That is a 4.5% increase in throughput. It is a vendor-reported capability, not an independent measurement of finished-chip output across customers' fabs. [4](#source-4), Product sustainability; l0g calculation

This physical distinction is crucial. A wafer passes through many operations, including repeated lithography steps. A faster lithography tool raises saleable-chip output only where the rest of the process allows it. A saturated inspection step, a slower downstream operation or poor manufacturing yield can constrain the benefit. Wafers per hour measure capacity at a particular step, not the number of finished chips leaving the fab.

For ASML, the value created gives room to negotiate. Pricing can reflect incremental capacity and costs the customer avoids. The customer still has choices: accept existing performance, postpone the upgrade, reorganise output or prioritise another investment. ASML's technical role strengthens its position, while the contract and the customer's realistically achievable gains shape the bill.

Pricing has a timeline, too. In the management video accompanying Q3 results, ASML says it has concluded new price negotiations with major customers. Their impact should be partly visible in 2027 and fully effective from 2028 onward. The comments concern group pricing discussions, without a split between machines and upgrades. Those future terms cannot simply be used to explain the margin already reported in Q3 2026. [7](#source-7)

## Downtime changes the answer

A substantial upgrade can temporarily remove valuable production capacity. The transition cost helps explain why the decision is more complicated than comparing two throughput specifications.

At its September 2021 Investor Day, ASML presented an upgrade from NXT:1960 to NXT:1980: six weeks of work to raise throughput from 230 to 295 wafers per hour. This is a historical example. It is neither a universal installation time nor a description of current commercial terms. It is nevertheless a useful basis for isolating the production trade-off. [8](#source-8), slide 22

Assume the original machine could keep running without interruption, while the upgraded tool immediately reaches its stated speed after forty-two days offline. Availability and loading are held equal before and after the work. Under those assumptions, cumulative wafer passes catch up about 191 days after the upgrade begins, or nearly 149 days after the tool restarts. This is an illustrative calculation, not a payback period published by ASML. [8](#source-8); l0g scenario

<AsmlFigure id="04" lang="en" />

During the shutdown, the no-upgrade path keeps producing. After restart, the extra speed gradually closes that gap. The catch-up date measured from the start of the work equals downtime multiplied by the new throughput, divided by the throughput improvement. The formula compares volumes. It does not price the upgrade, the customer's margins or the cost of capital.

Over 365 days, assuming constant 100% availability and loading, the model produces roughly 2.287 million wafer passes with the upgrade, compared with 2.015 million without it. The cumulative gain is 13.5%, below the 28.3% improvement in steady-state throughput. A lower common availability or loading factor reduces both volumes but leaves this model's catch-up date unchanged. A gradual ramp or different operating rates would change the answer. ASML's 2021 presentation already noted that periods of lower utilisation can make lengthy hardware upgrades easier to schedule. This creates an interesting cyclical effect. A customer may use a production lull to improve its tools, whereas a fully loaded fab must attach a high value to every week offline. Usage-based services and upgrade demand can consequently react differently to the same industry cycle. [8](#source-8), slide 21

## A former 2030 range enters the 2026 outlook

In November 2024, ASML presented installed-base revenue scenarios for 2030 ranging from €11 billion to €13 billion. The three cases reflected a growing fleet and additional services sold to customers. They were conditional long-term scenarios built at that time. [9](#source-9), slides 15-16

The first nine months of 2026 have already delivered €8.538 billion of realised revenue. Adding the approximately €3.7 billion that management expects for Q4 gives an implied full-year figure of about €12.2 billion. That falls inside the former 2030 range. It still depends on the fourth quarter being delivered and is not a reported annual result. [2](#source-2), [6](#source-6); l0g calculation

<AsmlFigure id="05" lang="en" />

The comparison captures the pace of the acceleration, subject to two qualifications. A scenario presented in 2024 is not automatically the appropriate 2030 forecast today. And 2026 revenue may include a wave of upgrades whose repeatability has yet to be established. The level reached and the sustainable rate of activity answer different questions.

Future disclosures will need to be read for continuity in revenue, commentary on the mix and the associated cost trajectory. Sustained demand for upgrades could increase the fleet's earnings potential. It could also bring forward work that customers would otherwise have undertaken later. Available public accounts do not quantify those effects separately.

## Export controls can reach the service visit

A machine's presence at a customer site does not ensure that every future part, software update or technical intervention can be supplied freely. Dutch strategic export-control policy also covers transfers of technology and certain services, including repair and maintenance. The treatment of an intervention depends on the product, destination, end user and applicable rules. [10](#source-10)

For example, the Netherlands announced an extension involving measurement and inspection technology on 15 January 2025, effective from 1 April that year. Its notice describes case-by-case licensing, rather than a blanket ban on all exports. Both the distinction and the two dates matter when political announcements are translated into revenue assumptions. [11](#source-11)

<AsmlFigure id="06" lang="en" />

The economic exposure extends over the life of the relationship. If a necessary authorisation is delayed or refused, a supplier may lose or defer work, and the customer may face maintenance or upgrade constraints. The impact will depend on spare-parts inventories, permitted alternatives and operating requirements. None of the public documents reviewed supports a precise estimate of revenue lost across ASML's entire installed base in China under such a scenario.

Assessing that exposure requires a map of equipment and service types. The geographic mix of new-system sales alone does not reveal maintenance-revenue exposure. Long-lived machines can support sales for years while extending the period over which regulatory decisions may affect the relationship.

## A stronger earnings engine, with economic rent still to be measured

The installed base gives ASML a powerful opportunity: sell the machine, support its operation and capture part of the value of improving it. Better use of capital already committed by the customer can create meaningful value, particularly when adding industrial capacity is difficult.

The figures support the growing importance of that engine. Its H1 IFRS gross-profit contribution is substantial; Q3 confirms accelerating revenue, and management identifies upgrades as a margin driver. Systems nevertheless remain the larger source of gross profit in absolute terms, and comparisons must preserve their accounting basis. [2](#source-2), [5](#source-5), [6](#source-6)

A strict claim of economic rent would require measuring returns after all relevant costs, against the capital and risks needed to deliver the business. Public disclosures do not isolate that calculation. Intervention pricing, allocated research costs, field-service economics and negotiated contractual terms remain largely unavailable.

The supported conclusion is more specific. Machines already sold have become a major source of revenue growth and, in some periods, gross-profit improvement. Their value depends on use, remaining technical potential and permission to supply it. Understanding ASML now requires looking beyond shipments to the work and revenue each installed generation can still support.

## Method and limitations

This document-based investigation is current to 1 November 2026. Monetary figures are nominal euros. The US GAAP third quarter ended on 27 September 2026; the EU-IFRS first half ended on 28 June 2026. Comparisons retain their respective accounting frameworks. Contribution percentages and category margins are calculated from reported figures, while retaining the relevant periods and scopes.

Equipment performance, fleet longevity and operational explanations come from ASML. No independent customer-fab measurements, private contracts or interviews were obtained. Government sources establish the regulatory mechanism without determining individual equipment licences. Prepared remarks and the management video were reviewed; the prepared document does not contain the full question-and-answer session.

The workshop compares theoretical volumes at one lithography step. It is neither a complete production model nor an estimate of an upgrade's financial return. Historical inputs are sourced and the simulator’s assumptions can be changed locally.
