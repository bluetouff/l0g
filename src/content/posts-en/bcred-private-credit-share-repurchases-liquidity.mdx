On 31 August 2026, the repurchase offer from **Blackstone Private Credit Fund**, or **BCRED**, closed with more than **181 million shares** tendered. The fund accepted just over **90 million** and paid for them around **28 October**, using its end-of-September valuation. Nearly half the shares tendered went through in that round. The remainder could be tendered again in a later offer. [S04](#source-S04) · [S06](#source-S06)

Shareholders receive a monthly [net asset value](/en/glossary/nav/), or NAV, based on the estimated value of the fund’s assets after liabilities, expressed per share. In a filing on 20 November, the fund reported NAV of **$23.38** per share at **31 October**. That provides a reference value. The payment date, number of shares accepted and amount paid depend on the terms of each repurchase offer. [S04](#source-S04) · [S07](#source-S07)

## Two quarters of requests above the cap

BCRED is a [non-listed business development company](/en/glossary/bdc/), a type of US vehicle that finances businesses. Under its regular programme, the board decides whether to open quarterly offers, typically for up to **5% of shares outstanding**. The fund holds mostly private loans. Borrowers repay them at maturity or earlier under the terms of their contracts. To sell a loan in the meantime, the fund needs a buyer. [S01](#source-S01) · [S04](#source-S04) · [S09](#source-S09)

For the **second quarter**, Blackstone puts requests at about **$4.5 billion**. The final result shows that the fund accepted **48.54%** of shares validly tendered and paid **$2.20 billion** around 29 July. For the **third quarter**, the manager puts requests at about **$4.3 billion**. The fund accepted **49.88%** of shares tendered and paid **$2.13 billion** around 28 October. The 5% cap is measured against all outstanding shares. The two rates near half are measured against the shares put up for repurchase. [S03](#source-S03) · [S06](#source-S06) · [S10](#source-S10)

The manager estimates that a significant share of unfilled second-quarter requests may have been resubmitted in the third. The filings give aggregate results for each offer. Counting individual investors or measuring new requests would require separating first-time requests from those submitted again. [S02](#source-S02) · [S03](#source-S03)

## The 90 days start with the first payment

Blackstone puts the amount recovered at about **75%** of the requested capital in some **90 days** for a shareholder who tendered all their shares in the second quarter and all the remaining shares in the next. In that illustration, about half goes through in the first round and about half of the balance in the second. The 90 days run between the payments in late July and late October. The first offer had already closed on 29 May. [S02](#source-S02) · [S06](#source-S06) · [S10](#source-S10)

The offer that opened on **2 November** closes at **11:59 p.m. New York time on 1 December**, unless extended. The price is based on the **31 December** valuation unless that date is moved. Payment comes after the valuation is finalised. If demand exceeds capacity, the fund generally accepts shares pro rata, with priority in some circumstances. The balance can be tendered again in a subsequent offer, without automatic priority. Subject to exceptions, the offer also applies a **2%** reduction when the valuation date falls within twelve months of the shares’ initial issuance. [S04](#source-S04) · [S09](#source-S09)

## An outside offer at $20.65 per share

In September, Cox Capital offered **$20.65** for a limited number of **Class I** shares. The published NAV at 31 August was **$23.60**, putting the bid **12.5%** below that reference. BCRED’s board advised shareholders to reject the offer. The bid shows the discount this buyer sought. The filings examined establish the offer terms and the board’s response. The volume actually bought remains unknown. [S05](#source-S05)

## The fund can borrow to help pay for repurchases

At the end of September, Blackstone reported more than **$17 billion** in available liquidity. The accounts break it down into **$0.8 billion** of cash and equivalents excluding restricted cash and **$16.7 billion** available to borrow after collateral-based limits. The fund also has commitments to borrowers to finance. Loan repayments and subscriptions can bring in cash on a different schedule and in different amounts from repurchases. The cap gives the fund time to collect repayments and avoid selling loans in a hurry. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02) · [S08](#source-S08) · [S11](#source-S11) · [S12](#source-S12)

In its **13 November** stability report, the Federal Reserve says requests at perpetual non-listed BDCs eased in the third quarter and remained elevated. It says limits on repurchases helped contain outflows. At BCRED, the time it takes to exit completely still depends on which offers open, how many shares are tendered in each round and how many are accepted. These are the terms we trace in our [guide to private credit risk](/en/guides/read-private-credit-risk/). [S04](#source-S04) · [S08](#source-S08)
