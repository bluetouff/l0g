A company needs financing for an acquisition. Its lender is still willing to back the deal, but asks for a larger equity contribution, reduces the loan size and tightens the contractual protections. The transaction may go ahead. The fund's loan portfolio may even keep growing. Yet something has changed: the next borrower faces a higher hurdle.

This is an illustrative example. It captures the difference between measuring a stock of loans and observing the decisions that replenish it. A central bank needs both to understand how financing reaches businesses.

The pilot opened by the Dallas and New York Feds on November 9 addresses that decision-making process. Responses are being collected through November 18, 2026, with initial aggregate results expected in the first quarter of 2027. [S01](#source-S01) Its questionnaire focuses on direct lending: debt negotiated with a nonbank lender or a small group of lenders, rather than placed across a broad lending market. It asks about changes in lending terms, inquiries from potential borrowers or their sponsors, and lenders' reasons for adjusting credit conditions. [S03](#source-S03)

The exercise has a useful purpose. Assessing its contribution requires some care. The information in this field comes in several forms: reconstructed balance sheets, confidential survey responses and public indicators whose methodology has yet to be detailed. Each reveals a different part of the financing process.

## Private credit was already entering the official accounts

The chronology matters. The Fed's Financial Accounts, published as the Z.1 release, added new sectors and instruments covering private credit in September 2026. A note published on October 16 explains how they were constructed. It advances the issues examined in our investigation of [gaps in private credit data](/en/analysis/private-credit-risk-begins-where-data-ends/). The November pilot therefore follows a substantial improvement in the measurement of outstanding credit. [S06](#source-S06)

The statisticians' task is to connect investors, the vehicles holding the loans and the businesses that must repay them. They draw on confidential Form PF filings, disclosures by registered vehicles and commercial datasets, among other sources. Bringing those records together allows more financial relationships to be tracked within a common framework. It also requires estimates and imputations where observations are missing or reported less frequently. [S06](#source-S06)

This can make a considerable difference to the reported composition of corporate debt. A loan that is classified more accurately appears under the appropriate instrument. The measured total for that instrument changes even though the contract was already in place. Understanding a statistical series requires attention to both the economic activity and the way it has been measured over time.


Terminology adds another complication. “Private credit assets” can refer to the holdings of specialist vehicles, the loans on their balance sheets or claims on businesses in a particular country. Those categories overlap, but their boundaries differ. The Fed's November 9 note provides an opportunity to separate them. [S07](#source-S07)

At the end of 2025, the four groups of US-domiciled vehicles shown in our chart held **$1,857 billion in financial assets**. Their private loan holdings amounted to **$1,118.1 billion**, including loans to foreign borrowers. The remainder comprised other assets, including securities, equity investments and cash. All these figures come from the same Z.1 release, dated September 11, 2026. [S08](#source-S08) [S09](#source-S09) [S10](#source-S10) [S11](#source-S11)

The mix varies considerably. Private loans account for roughly 57% of the assets of private debt funds, 80% of those held by business development companies, or BDCs, and 26% of the assets of interval and tender offer funds. Interval funds make periodic repurchase offers; tender offer funds can offer repurchases at their discretion. Middle-market CLOs, vehicles that pool loans and issue securities against them, consist entirely of private loans within the statistical perimeter used here. [S07](#source-S07) [S08](#source-S08) [S09](#source-S09) [S10](#source-S10) [S11](#source-S11)

Those proportions affect what a headline market-size figure actually means. Using total assets as a measure of lending would bring in other kinds of holdings. Switching from the domicile of the vehicle to the location of the borrower would change the perimeter again. Our comparison uses year-end 2025, the reference period in the November 9 note; some series in the same tables already extend to the second quarter of 2026. It describes a documented historical structure, with an explicit date.

## Five pages focused on the lending decision

The questionnaire narrows the scope considerably. It covers senior secured first-lien direct loans, including unitranche facilities, to businesses assessed as below investment grade that are organized in the United States or have their principal place of business there. The credit assessment can rely on an internal or private credit rating. Distressed debt strategies, subordinated loans and broadly syndicated lending fall outside the stated scope. [S03](#source-S03)

From the borrower's perspective, a unitranche combines different components of financing in a single facility. The loans are secured by collateral; seniority describes a creditor's contractual priority. The amount ultimately recovered will still depend on the assets, competing claims and the company's circumstances. These features give the survey a more consistent lending universe, while leaving parts of private credit to other measures.

Participation criteria require at least $50 million in assets under management devoted to the eligible activity. The questionnaire then groups borrowers by annual EBITDA: below $30 million, $30 million to $100 million, and above $100 million. EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization; here it serves as a measure of business size. [S04](#source-S04) [S03](#source-S03)

The distinction matters. A large company able to approach several financing markets may secure different terms from a smaller business dependent on a few lenders. An overall average can conceal those different experiences. Tracking them separately helps identify which companies are encountering constraints.

The form also asks for three confidential amounts: assets under management devoted to the defined activity, new-money loans extended during the previous quarter, and refinancings during that same quarter of loans previously made by the respondent's own firm. That last qualification sets a specific boundary around the refinancing measure. Our analysis of [borrower exits and refinancing](/en/analysis/private-credit-borrower-exits-reinvestment-risk/) examines how these movements affect lenders. The three fields describe different things; interpreting them together requires consistent definitions. [S03](#source-S03)

Most of the remaining questions concern changes. The detailed questions on loan terms cover loans a firm was willing to approve in the previous quarter, whether or not they ultimately closed. A loan's spread is the margin charged above its reference interest rate. A covenant is a contractual undertaking by the borrower. A [payment-in-kind, or PIK, provision](/en/glossary/pik/) allows interest to be added to principal under the terms of the agreement, deferring its payment in cash. Changes to these provisions can affect both access to finance and the lender's protection.

The economic value of the exercise lies in observing how the terms of new lending evolve. An existing portfolio can contain several generations of contracts. Recent decisions give a more immediate indication of what borrowers face, subject to the period covered and the respondent's customer base.

## Reading the answers alongside other evidence

Tighter conditions can originate with the lender, its own funding providers or its borrowers. The form asks respondents to assess the importance of several reasons, including bank and nonbank financing conditions, competition, the economic outlook and portfolio performance. It separately asks how the number of inquiries from potential borrowers or their sponsors has changed, covering additional or increased credit lines and excluding refinancings. This measures inquiry activity, distinct from the dollar amounts requested or the number of loans approved. [S03](#source-S03)

That separation makes the responses potentially more informative than a single lending total. If inquiries are rising while lending standards tighten, a supply constraint is a plausible explanation. If inquiries fall while terms remain unchanged, businesses' financing needs or investment plans may be playing a larger role. These combinations help frame hypotheses. The customer mix may also change, a respondent may withdraw from a sector, and several forces may act at once.

The survey records the lender's assessment. Establishing a cause would require comparing those responses with contracts, similar transactions and borrower data. The questionnaire provides direction and reported explanations; the work of testing them remains.

The same applies to capitalized interest. A response indicating more flexible PIK provisions may signal a change in the terms on offer. Establishing how much interest has actually been added to debts, how much cash has been received and what was lost after a restructuring requires portfolio-level follow-up. The Financial Stability Board's May 2026 report specifically highlights the importance of examining amendments and restructurings beyond a narrow definition of default. [S03](#source-S03) [S15](#source-S15)

The economic outcomes can differ sharply. A concession may give a viable company time to recover. It may also extend financing whose repayment has become less likely. Subsequent cash flows and recoveries reveal which outcome materializes. The same contractual feature can appear in either case.

## Who responds will shape the result

The survey relies on voluntary participation and confidentiality. The FAQ states that individual responses will be accessible only to a limited number of people at the two Reserve Banks. They will not be shared with the Fed's supervisory teams or other regulators. Anonymous comments can be published only with consent. The stated purpose concerns financing and the economy; the pilot rules out supervisory use of the responses. [S02](#source-S02)

That commitment may encourage participation. It also sets a boundary between what the researchers can analyze and what a reader can reproduce from the published results. Documentation will therefore be central to confidence in an aggregate indicator.

Voluntary participation alone says little about the eventual quality of the data. The Fed's Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey on Bank Lending Practices, or SLOOS, is also voluntary. Its public documentation allows users to assess its coverage. The relevant questions concern recruitment, continuity of the panel and the treatment of responses. [S12](#source-S12)

Consider **a hypothetical example**, unrelated to the pilot's eventual results. Ten lenders respond. Six tighten their standards; four ease them. Giving each lender equal weight produces a net tightening balance of +20 percentage points: 60% minus 40%.

Now suppose the six lenders that tighten account for 30% of a reference lending volume, while the other four account for 70%. Weighting by that volume produces a balance of −40 percentage points. The responses are unchanged. The calculation now answers a different question: how many lenders are changing their position, or how much lending is represented by those that do?

Weighting by outstanding loans would primarily reflect the existing portfolio. Weighting by new originations would give more influence to recent activity. Size categories could help address some imbalances. Each choice has a purpose that needs to be explained.

Nonresponse creates a further question. A panel can include many firms and still miss a particular segment. Large platforms, smaller specialist lenders and vehicles aimed at retail investors have different structures. High coverage by dollar value can coexist with limited coverage of a business model. Conversely, a panel with relatively low coverage may still provide a useful signal if its stability and relationship with other data can be established.

As of November 11, the public documents reviewed do not establish the final number of respondents, the share of the market covered or the chosen aggregation rule. The survey remains open. These are questions for the publication of results, without prejudging the quality of responses being collected.

## Banks enter the picture through the lender's funding

The relationship between private lenders and banks becomes clearer when the financing of the lender itself is examined. A fund can raise capital from investors and borrow to supplement it. The bank then holds a claim on the vehicle, while the vehicle holds claims on businesses. Risk passes through different contracts and balance sheets. Our investigation of [NAV loans](/en/analysis/nav-loans-the-hidden-fund-level-leverage/) follows one specific form of fund-level leverage; it does not describe every bank loan to an investment vehicle.

The Z.1 tables measure part of that connection. At the end of 2025, bank loans to US-domiciled private debt funds stood at $150.3 billion; those to BDCs at $180.8 billion; and those to interval and tender offer funds at $18.4 billion. The published components sum to $349.5 billion, or roughly $350 billion. These are outstanding loans to vehicles in the three groups. Undrawn commitments and other possible exposures are outside that measure. [S08](#source-S08) [S09](#source-S09) [S10](#source-S10)

This helps explain why bank funding conditions appear in the questionnaire. A more expensive credit line, higher collateral requirements or reduced borrowing capacity can affect the next loan a fund offers. Transmission can begin through the price or availability of financing, well before a loss is ultimately realized.

Tracing the process in the other direction requires more information. Deterioration in a corporate loan first affects the value of the portfolio holding it. The consequences for a creditor of the fund depend on the equity available to absorb losses, the priority of its claim, the collateral and the relevant contractual terms. The Fed's August 7 study of BDCs documents banking relationships and their pricing using historical supervisory data. It helps explain the channel without providing a comprehensive picture of November 2026. [S18](#source-S18)

Adding a bank's loan to a fund and the fund's loan to a company would count two links in the same chain as independent sources of financing for the final borrower. Measuring a concentration of risk instead requires matching counterparties and tracing who absorbs losses at each stage.

## Information also follows a regulatory timetable

The survey arrives as another reporting framework is undergoing an explicit reassessment. [Form PF](/en/glossary/form-pf/) is a confidential filing covering certain private funds. Some of its data already feed into the Fed's statistical work. On April 20, 2026, the SEC and CFTC proposed changing its requirements, including raising the general reporting threshold from $150 million to $1 billion in private fund assets under management. As of November 11, the SEC's website still lists the measure as a proposal. [S13](#source-S13) [S06](#source-S06)

The proposal also sought comments on information tailored to private credit, including its definition, leverage, funding, loan quality and investor liquidity. The official comment deadline was June 23; the record also includes later contributions. The final design of any such reporting remains to be confirmed. [S13](#source-S13)

A separate decision has already taken effect. A rule issued on August 31 and effective September 3 moved the compliance date for the 2024 amendments to Form PF from October 1, 2026, to July 1, 2027. Existing reporting continues; the postponement concerns those new requirements. [S14](#source-S14)

The agencies' explanation sets out the tradeoff without requiring any inference about intent. Adapting systems to questions that may subsequently change can create avoidable costs. Their analysis also acknowledges that the extension delays the benefits of additional information and may leave some data unavailable if market events occur in the meantime. [S14](#source-S14)

Timing determines part of the value of information. Detailed data become useful when they arrive early enough to support comparisons, connect exposures or inform a decision. A faster, lighter survey offers a different contribution. The approaches can complement one another; treating them as substitutes would require showing that they meet the same needs.

The Dallas–New York pilot has a distinct role, focused on lending conditions and expressly separated from supervision. Its launch therefore provides no basis for concluding that prudential information needs have been met.

## How the safeguards affect the analysis

Private credit's varied structures provide important reasons to question any account of uniform fragility. A closed-end fund with capital committed for several years can hold a loan and negotiate a restructuring. Shares in a listed BDC can change hands on an exchange without requiring the company to repay the sellers. A vehicle offering periodic repurchases must manage those withdrawals within the limits set out in its documents. These arrangements affect how quickly pressure can spread. The [repurchase windows of semi-liquid funds](/en/analysis/semi-liquid-private-credit-gating/) illustrate the importance of contractual limits and timetables. [S07](#source-S07)

In its July 2026 report, the Bank of England observes that repurchase limits at certain vehicles had largely worked as designed, while also examining the possible incentive for investors to request an exit early. A contractual restriction can preserve cash for the fund as a whole and alter investor behavior. Both effects warrant attention. [S16](#source-S16)

The ECB offers another counterpoint. Its May study finds observed direct exposures sufficiently limited to make systemic instability in the euro area arising from private credit alone unlikely at that time. It also examines broader valuation losses in an adverse scenario and identifies gaps in the data. The conclusion is conditional on its scope, assumptions and date. [S17](#source-S17)

These findings call for examining the sources of resilience as carefully as the points of vulnerability. A confidential contract can coexist with close monitoring of the borrower. Patient capital can absorb volatility that other structures struggle to accommodate. The cost of credit, a vehicle's liquidity and the ultimate loss are distinct outcomes that need to be tracked separately.

## The first results will need a clear guide to interpretation

The next meaningful milestone is the publication of results expected in the first quarter of 2027. Assessing them will require a clear picture of the panel, an explanation of how responses are weighted and a way to distinguish changes in activity from changes in the composition of respondents. If the quarterly survey envisaged after the pilot goes ahead, tracking those features over time will make the series more useful. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

The most useful comparisons will then bring several sources together: reported lending terms, inquiries from borrowers or sponsors, new contracts, changes in outstanding credit and borrower outcomes. Tightening reported by lenders could precede slower credit growth. An end to that tightening could reflect improvement or a different customer mix. Comparing the evidence will establish how much weight those explanations deserve.

The Fed is seeking a closer view of a decisive moment: when a lender agrees to finance a deal, asks for more protection or passes on it. The public value of that view will depend on the strength of the signal and the clarity of the framework around it. With that context, the first results can provide a sounder account of how credit is reaching the economy.


## Method and limitations

Research is dated November 11, 2026. FEDS Notes present their authors’ analyses. Pilot results are still to come; no interviews or access to confidential responses are claimed. Regulatory documents are examined in the sections cited, without claiming an exhaustive reading of the 328-page Form PF proposal. [S01](#source-S01) [S06](#source-S06) [S13](#source-S13)

The financial figures use end-2025 stocks from the September 11, 2026 Z.1 release, matching the November 9 note’s reference period. Some series already extend to the second quarter of 2026. The vehicles are US-domiciled; their loans include foreign borrowers. Uncalled capital is excluded. Vehicle assets, loan holdings and funding describe different layers. [S07](#source-S07) [S08](#source-S08) [S09](#source-S09) [S10](#source-S10) [S11](#source-S11)

Reproducible calculations, in billions of US dollars: assets = 938.7 + 572.2 + 292.4 + 53.7 = 1,857.0; private loans = 532.2 + 455.9 + 76.3 + 53.7 = 1,118.1; bank loans = 150.3 + 180.8 + 18.4 = 349.5. Other assets are calculated by subtraction; percentages use published amounts before rounding. Undrawn commitments are distinct from outstanding loans. [S08](#source-S08) [S09](#source-S09) [S10](#source-S10) [S11](#source-S11)

The weighting example is wholly hypothetical: ten respondents, six tightening and four easing, give 60 − 40 = +20 percentage points at equal weights. With assumed volume shares of 30% and 70%, the balance is 30 − 70 = −40 points. These calculations describe neither pilot observations nor its future method. The form refers to the “previous quarter” without explicit calendar dates and does not specify the reporting date for requested AUM. No further timetable is assumed. [S03](#source-S03)
