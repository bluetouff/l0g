On May 19, 2025, the Pentagon moved $447 million out of a fund that pays for US Navy operations. The money passed through a Defense Department counterdrug account. Military officials told the Government Accountability Office (GAO), Congress’s auditor, that most of it was intended to support migrant detention by Immigration and Customs Enforcement, or ICE. The rest was to reimburse military departments for detention costs at Guantánamo. [S01](#source-S01)

Here the records identify both the account the money came from and its intended use. With DOGE’s claimed savings or funds frozen by the White House, finding out where the money went is often harder. [The first story in *The Trump Clan’s War Chest*](/en/analysis/trump-doge-federal-spending-controls/) examined DOGE’s Treasury access and the loss of a public budget register. This time the question is how much room the administration gains when it cancels, holds or moves funding.

## Cancel the order and the funding may remain

Suppose an agency cancels an order before delivery. Allowing for termination fees, it owes the vendor less. If the [appropriation](/en/glossary/credits-budgetaires-americains/) is still available, the agency can use the remainder for its original purpose. To move budget authority from one account to another, it needs authority under the law. [S03](#source-S03) [S04](#source-S04)

<TrumpBudgetFigure lang="en" kind="contract" />

DOGE claimed $1.7 billion in savings on an IT contract at the Defense Health Agency, which runs military health services. The GAO found the contract intact, with the same scope and funding. Its estimate of the savings from that contract was zero. [S10](#source-S10)

At the Department of Homeland Security, the GAO looked at contracts the department had actually terminated. DHS projected more than $10.5 billion in potential avoided costs, largely from IT contracts with ceilings that covered several years. By the end of March 2026, DHS had released just over $92 million in existing commitments. The department still needed to meet some IT requirements through other contracts. To estimate the net savings, you would need to know which orders DHS would actually have placed. [S11](#source-S11)

A freeze can hurt long before it saves the government money. In 2025, payment holds at the same department delayed work to protect communities from disasters. The GAO found that some projects slipped by nearly a year. For recipients, the delay could disrupt work they had already started planning and staffing. [S07](#source-S07)

## Running down the fiscal year

In Washington, funding for a new contract can expire on September 30, the last day of the federal fiscal year. When the president asks Congress to [cancel funding](/en/glossary/rescission/), the administration can hold it temporarily while lawmakers consider the request. The law gives Congress 45 days of continuous session. The GAO says the money still has to be made available in time for an agency to commit it before it expires. [S13](#source-S13) [S20](#source-S20)

Donald Trump sent Congress a request to cancel $810 million on September 25, 2026. The money was due to expire five days later. The White House budget office blocked agencies from committing it even though, according to the GAO, the congressional clock would run at least until November 9. After a fixed-period appropriation expires, agencies can generally still pay bills they already owe out of it. The deadline matters for new contracts. [S19](#source-S19) [S20](#source-S20) [S21](#source-S21)

<TrumpBudgetFigure lang="en" kind="expiry" />

On September 30, a federal judge suspended the expiration of $57.5 million for housing counseling, including advice for households at risk of losing their homes. The proposal targeted nearly the entire program budget. The court preserved the funds while it considered the nonprofits’ request for relief. The final disposition of the full $810 million remains unclear account by account. [S19](#source-S19) [S35](#source-S35)

## The money available for detention

The $447 million from the Navy fund did change accounts. The Defense Department told the GAO that $442 million was intended to support ICE detention operations and $5 million to reimburse military costs tied to Guantánamo. A separate audit identified $312.5 million transferred to ICE from other parts of the Department of Homeland Security. The GAO gives the destination and the total for that transfer. The details of the originating accounts remain unclear. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

<TrumpBudgetFigure lang="en" kind="transfer" />

In Florida, auditors found another route. The Federal Emergency Management Agency, or FEMA, drew on its Shelter and Services Program to set up a grant for detention in Florida. It awarded the state $608.4 million in September 2025. The first payments arrived in May 2026. The report identifies the source program. Any link to grants FEMA separately terminated would have to be established from the accounts. [S02](#source-S02) [S07](#source-S07)

The biggest commitment came directly from Congress. In July 2025, lawmakers gave ICE $45 billion for detention capacity, available through the end of fiscal year 2029. ICE used it to fund existing facilities during partial government shutdowns, although operators still faced payment delays. The funding can keep one priority operating when annual appropriations for other services lapse. [S02](#source-S02) [S34](#source-S34)

## The price of admission to a national park

A ticket at a national park can help pay for work somewhere else. Sites generally keep at least 80% of their own entrance and certain pass fees. The relevant secretary can lower that share to 60% if collections exceed the site’s reasonable needs. Federal law limits what the fees can pay for, including services for visitors. [S25](#source-S25) [S26](#source-S26)

In June 2026, *The Washington Post* reported that at least $90 million in fees had been redirected to projects in Washington. Eleven senators asked the Interior Department for the contracts and a breakdown of revenue and projects. Assessing the move requires knowing where the fees came from and what the work was for. Park fees, donations and standing funds are the focus of the next story. [S27](#source-S27) [S28](#source-S28)
