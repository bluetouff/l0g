At 3:45 in the afternoon, New York time, one of the doors closes on a product advertised as continuously accessible. The documentation for *Ondo Intelligent Portfolios* generally allows tokens to be minted and redeemed between 9:30 a.m. and 3:45 p.m. on regular US trading days. The final fifteen minutes before the stock-market close are reserved for completing trades in the underlying basket. The token can continue changing hands on supported secondary markets throughout the weekend. <sup><a href="#source-s08" aria-label="Source S08">[S08]</a></sup>

That timetable marks a financial boundary. On Saturday, a seller may find someone willing to take over the exposure. Access to redemption with the issuer follows the primary dealing calendar. Keeping the token’s price close to the value of its backing requires someone to hold the position, supply money and wait for redemption to reopen. The reliability of that service depends on the buyer’s resources and the limits it places on using them.

The tokenization chapter of the IMF’s **October 2026** *Global Financial Stability Report* is a useful starting point for examining that boundary. The statistics have their own reference periods. The chapter describes a growing but fragmented market that remains small, with limited systemic risk at its current scale. The more interesting question concerns how that market will be organized as it expands. <sup><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">[S01]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source S02">[S02]</a></sup>

**Continuous trading needs funding.** A programmable ledger can make an exchange more efficient, connect operations and reduce some sources of error. Where conversion into the assets or money ultimately required by the customer follows a different timetable, someone must still finance the interval. This investigation follows that commitment: who makes it, what can interrupt it and how a funding problem might become a loss.

## The claim beneath the token

Tokenization records an asset’s representation, or the security itself, on a programmable ledger. Transfers can then be governed by rules executed through the system: checking whether a recipient is eligible, exchanging a security for payment or restricting particular movements. The legal claim conveyed by the token depends on the structure. Similar-looking screens can display a share issued directly on a ledger, a claim on securities held in custody, or a certificate tracking their economic performance. <sup><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">[S01]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source S18">[S18]</a></sup>

These differences matter when the holder wants to exit. A share confers rights against a company. A certificate may instead create a claim against a vehicle that owns other instruments. The documents determine whom the holder can approach, what rights attach to collateral, how transfers are restricted and how repayment works. Reconstructing that chain of obligations requires reading the contracts as well as the transfer record.

Ondo is a useful example because its documentation spells out the distinction. Its *Intelligent Portfolios* take the form of tracker certificates. BLKDIGon uses an allocation model provided by BlackRock, but the model provider does not issue or administer the tokens and has no obligation to their holders. Investors receive economic exposure to the basket rather than direct shareholder rights in its constituents. The analysis therefore begins with the obligor and the redemption terms. Access is for eligible investors outside the United States; in the EEA and the UK it is restricted to qualified investors, and in Switzerland to professional clients. Minting and redemption require onboarding with the platform. <sup><a href="#source-s08" aria-label="Source S08">[S08]</a></sup>

A second distinction runs through these structures: **a secondary sale and a redemption by the issuer draw on different resources**. In a sale, the buyer supplies the money and becomes the new holder. In a redemption, the issuer or its agent processes the withdrawal under the product’s rules. A token can change hands without any underlying security being sold at that moment.

Circle illustrated this arrangement in its April 11, 2024 announcement of a facility exchanging BUIDL fund shares for USDC. Its description expressly identifies a transfer of shares **to Circle in the secondary market**. In that announcement, Circle takes the shares and delivers USDC. Converting those tokens into a bank deposit follows a separate route. Assessing current capacity requires documented commitments. <sup><a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source S10">[S10]</a></sup>

For a customer, both routes can look much the same: press a button and receive a balance. Financially, the exposure has moved along a different path. It may sit with a secondary buyer, remain tied to the fund during a redemption procedure, or become an exposure to the issuer of the payment instrument. The chain has to be followed as far as the form of money the customer actually needs.

## Conversion windows

Weekend trading in tokenized assets is already a commercial service for selected products. On December 3, 2025, Kraken announced 24/7 trading for a selection of xStocks on Kraken Pro. The scope matters: selected instruments, eligible customers and eligible jurisdictions, with market depth still to be established. This is a defined operator offering, with instrument-specific access and trading conditions. <sup><a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source S09">[S09]</a></sup>

Bank payments follow their own schedules. According to the published operating hours for **Fedwire Funds**, the US wholesale funds-transfer service normally closes at 7 p.m. on Friday and resumes at 9 p.m. on Sunday for Monday’s business day. That leaves a nominal **50-hour** gap over an ordinary weekend without a daylight-saving transition. Customer transfers face an earlier 6:45 p.m. cutoff, and individual banks may impose still tighter access windows. <sup><a href="#source-s05" aria-label="Source S05">[S05]</a></sup>

For the Ondo portfolio discussed above, the interval between Friday’s 3:45 p.m. primary-market cutoff and Monday’s 9:30 a.m. reopening is **65 hours and 45 minutes**, using its published timetable. The calculation applies the ordinary schedule to a weekend without a public holiday or daylight-saving transition. Primary dealing requires every constituent market to be open; Ondo also allows pauses for platform or security reasons. A secondary-market exit during that interval needs funding that does not rely on immediate execution of a primary redemption. <sup><a href="#source-s08" aria-label="Source S08">[S08]</a></sup>

Other dollar payment routes operate during that interval. **FedNow** supports round-the-clock instant payments through participating financial institutions. A bank’s internal network or another available payment service can also alter the route of a particular transaction. The analysis must establish the actual connections between customer, platform, bank and issuer, together with the balances each can use. <sup><a href="#source-s06" aria-label="Source S06">[S06]</a></sup>

Schedules are changing. In October 2025, the Federal Reserve announced its intention to expand Fedwire Funds and the National Settlement Service to six operating days, with implementation expected in 2028 or 2029. That timetable concerns a future change to the service. Current constraints should be reassessed as implementation proceeds. <sup><a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source S07">[S07]</a></sup>

The useful unit of analysis is a **conversion chain**, with its own permissions and timetables: security token into tokenized money, tokenized money into a deposit, then a deposit transferable over a system the recipient accepts. The first leg may finish in seconds while the others remain pending. Waiting becomes financially important when the intermediary carrying the position must finance a large balance or meet an obligation before the next window opens.

## Asset value and exit capacity

The IMF puts public tokenized asset value within its measured perimeter at **$65.10 billion on July 31, 2026**, excluding unobserved private tokenization platforms, stablecoins and repurchase agreements. Credit accounts for about $30.4 billion, money-market funds for $17.5 billion and equities for $2.3 billion. These figures locate the market’s main activities. They are calculated from token supply and issuer-reported unit values, rather than the executable prices a large seller could necessarily obtain. <sup><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">[S01]</a></sup>

The chapter’s **$303 billion** repo figure measures something different: average daily tokenized repo activity over thirty days. A repurchase agreement provides financing against securities with an agreed reverse transaction. A position can be renewed, generating repeated transactions. Adding this activity flow to the asset stock would produce a total with no coherent economic meaning. <sup><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">[S01]</a></sup>

Assessing exit capacity requires moving from market totals to the balance sheet of an individual arrangement. Consider an **entirely fictional** vehicle, unrelated to the reserves of Ondo or BUIDL. It owns $100 million in assets. Eight million is immediately usable in the payment instrument promised to withdrawing investors. The other $92 million consists of securities whose value is assumed unchanged, but which cannot be converted during the window being examined. A committed credit line supplies another $4 million and can genuinely be drawn and delivered within that window.

If withdrawal requests reach $20 million as conversion is about to resume, initially available resources amount to $12 million, leaving an $8 million funding need. The chart compares cumulative requests with that initial funding envelope, measured before payments are made. The assets may still cover the vehicle’s obligations in value. **The first problem is when the required money becomes available.** Someone must find an additional buyer or lender, defer withdrawals where the contracts allow, or accept a price that compensates another party for waiting and bearing the risk.

The credit line deserves as much scrutiny as the cash reserve. To count toward the hypothetical $4 million, it must deliver the correct instrument, through an operational counterparty, with the necessary permissions and without a condition that can no longer be satisfied. A commercial assurance, a business-hours facility or funding dependent on resources already counted elsewhere cannot be treated as a second independent buffer.

The same reasoning applies to a market maker. Buying tokens from departing investors uses up its payment balances and adds positions it will later seek to hedge or redeem. As its capacity fills, it may offer a lower price for the next trade. A discount can therefore represent the cost of committing more balance sheet even while the underlying asset’s most recent valuation is unchanged.

The FSB had already described, in 2024, the danger of treating a token as more liquid than the asset it represents, alongside the pressures created by prefunding. Its analysis describes a conditional risk mechanism. Assessing the reserves of a named product requires evidence specific to that product. Establishing inadequacy would require its obligations, funding agreements and redemption commitments, followed by a documented withdrawal scenario. <sup><a href="#source-s04" aria-label="Source S04">[S04]</a></sup>

## The payment currency has a price too

The asset received in payment introduces another source of risk into comparisons between a security and its token. A stablecoin is designed to track a reference value, such as one dollar. Its traded price also depends on the buyers and sellers present at each moment. In terms updated on October 15, 2026, Circle distinguishes its conditional redemption commitment from the price quoted by third-party platforms. <sup><a href="#source-s11" aria-label="Source S11">[S11]</a></sup>

Start with a security worth $100 and a stablecoin worth $1. The security is quoted at 100 units of the stablecoin. Now suppose, **solely for illustration**, that the stablecoin falls to $0.98 while the security remains worth $100. The quote becomes approximately **102.04 stablecoins**. The number on the screen has risen, although the security has generated no dollar gain. More units of the weaker payment currency are needed to purchase the same asset.

The reverse illusion is just as important. If the security declines from $100 to $98 while the stablecoin falls from $1 to $0.98, the quote remains **100 stablecoins**. A screen displaying only that unit can conceal the dollar loss. Comparing a token with its underlying therefore requires consistent treatment of the payment currency, trading hours and transaction costs. A quoted gap can originate in the security, the money used to buy it, or both.

Conversion rights also depend on the holder’s jurisdiction. The USDC terms just discussed apply outside the European Economic Area and make direct redemption conditional on access to an eligible Circle Mint account. Circle Europe has a separate redemption policy for the EEA. Its retail process describes bank payment after verification, within a procedure that should take no more than five business days unless discrepancies arise. This describes that redemption route, not the duration of every token transfer or every institutional transaction. <sup><a href="#source-s11" aria-label="Source S11">[S11]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-s12" aria-label="Source S12">[S12]</a></sup>

For a financial structure, the danger lies in equating receipt of a transferable claim with immediate possession of the money a creditor requires. A seller able to discharge its own obligations in stablecoins has different needs from a treasurer facing a payment into a bank account. Two customers holding the same token can therefore value the same offered exit very differently.

## When a quote becomes a liquidation trigger

In an IMF Note expressing its author’s views, Tobias Adrian examines programmable collateral and its risks. The schedule mismatch takes on a further dimension when a tokenized security supports a loan. That use depends on acceptance by the relevant contract or protocol; each arrangement sets its own admission criteria. Once accepted, however, its price can determine how much debt the system considers adequately secured. <sup><a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source S03">[S03]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-s13" aria-label="Source S13">[S13]</a></sup>

The information often arrives through an **oracle**, a mechanism that supplies the program with external data, such as a price. Selecting that input involves a consequential choice. Should the system use the underlying’s last official valuation, the token’s secondary-market quote, or a combination of sources? The first can become stale when the reference market closes. The second can move in response to trades in a shallow market. Neither necessarily equals the amount recoverable from selling a large position. <sup><a href="#source-s04" aria-label="Source S04">[S04]</a></sup>

Aave’s documentation provides a straightforward way to understand the mechanism. Its health factor is collateral value, weighted by the liquidation threshold, divided by debt value. A reading below 1 makes a position eligible for liquidation. A liquidator must then submit a transaction, provide repayment funds and receive collateral under the protocol’s rules. Execution also depends on the funds a liquidator can supply. <sup><a href="#source-s13" aria-label="Source S13">[S13]</a></sup>

Use **fictional parameters**, without assuming that any particular security is listed on Aave. Collateral worth 100, an 80% liquidation threshold and fixed debt of 78 produce a health factor of approximately **1.026**. If the selected collateral price falls to 97, the factor drops to **0.995**, below the boundary of 1. A 3% decline changes the position’s status because its starting safety margin is narrow. The exact boundary is reached at a collateral value of 97.50.

<TokenisationWeekendFigure lang="en" kind="collateral" number="05" sources="13" caption="Fictional l0g scenario: fixed debt of 78 is backed by an asset worth 100, with an 80% liquidation threshold. The health factor falls below 1 when collateral drops to 97.">
<details><summary>Method and scope</summary><p>HF = collateral value × threshold / debt. Initially: 100 × 0.80 / 78 = 1.025641…; after a 3% decline: 97 × 0.80 / 78 = 0.994872…. Exact boundary: 78 / 0.80 = 97.50, where HF = 1. Liquidation eligibility begins strictly below 1; a liquidator must still finance and execute the transaction. One collateral asset, fixed debt and threshold; no actual Aave listing is assumed.</p></details>
</TokenisationWeekendFigure>

What follows depends on market depth and execution rules. A liquidation can be absorbed smoothly where buyers have money and a viable hedge. In a thinner market, selling collateral may depress the price, weaken other borrowers’ coverage and make further positions eligible for liquidation. Alternatively, retaining an outdated and excessively high price may delay recognition of an actual loss. These are different risks that have to be managed together.

Exposure caps, conservative haircuts, execution limits and fallback procedures can reduce propagation. A pause may protect users from an erroneous price while preventing them from exiting or delaying the sale of collateral that is genuinely losing value. Its effectiveness depends on who can invoke it, the criteria used and the conditions for restarting. An emergency switch is a governance arrangement, complete with trade-offs and accountable decision-makers. <sup><a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source S03">[S03]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-s04" aria-label="Source S04">[S04]</a></sup>

## Instant settlement has a funding requirement

Part of tokenization’s appeal is **atomic settlement**: the components of an exchange are linked so that they complete together or fail together. The seller can avoid delivering a security while remaining exposed to non-payment of the corresponding cash. This property describes the conditional connection between operations. Speed, batching and netting are separate architectural choices. <sup><a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source S03">[S03]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source S18">[S18]</a></sup>

That distinction changes the liquidity calculation. Imagine two cash obligations between the same parties, in the same currency and at the same settlement date, legally eligible for netting. A owes B 100 and B owes A 90. Netting leaves A paying 10. If instead the arrangement requires 100 to leave A before the 90 can return, A must have 100 at the first instant. Once B receives the payment, it can reuse 90 to pay A.

Gross payments total 190; **the initial funding requirement in that sequence is 100**, because received funds can be reused. Execution order and the ability to reuse funds matter alongside the amounts. This example isolates the cash leg; it does not model collateral requirements, credit exposure or every feature of a securities-settlement system.

<details><summary>Method and scope</summary><p>Same parties, currency and maturity, with legally and operationally effective netting: 100 − 90 = 10. A gross payment of 100 followed by reuse of 90 produces total transfers of 190 and an initial funding requirement of 100. This depends on order and reuse. Only the cash leg is modelled; collateral, credit and other costs are outside the example. Atomicity, immediacy and netting are distinct dimensions.</p></details>
</TokenisationWeekendFigure>

The designs embody a trade-off. Netting saves liquidity where the necessary legal and operational conditions hold. Immediate settlement can shorten the time during which a counterparty might fail to perform, but may require more money to be available in advance. Fragmenting activity across platforms can also force participants to divide their balances, leaving money idle in one place while another faces an urgent shortage. <sup><a href="#source-s04" aria-label="Source S04">[S04]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source S18">[S18]</a></sup>

An architecture can be designed to retain liquidity savings by batching selected operations, implementing netting or coordinating available balances before triggering settlement. The IMF discusses such possibilities, including in tokenized repo. Prefunding therefore depends on these design choices as well as on ledger speed. <sup><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">[S01]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source S03">[S03]</a></sup>

This provides a better basis for discussing cost. Lower administrative charges can coexist with more capital committed to funding. The overall result depends on the service delivered: fewer failed transactions, better-matched hedges, shorter delays or more usable collateral. Comparing only the visible transaction fee leaves out the cost of keeping money available to support the promised access window.

## Volatility depends on the observation window

The IMF’s transaction evidence is informative only if its construction remains attached to the results. Its sample covers five liquid US exposures associated with the S&P 500, Nasdaq, Tesla, Google and Nvidia across eleven trading venues. It is not the entire tokenized asset market. More than half of observed volume takes place outside regular US hours, while about 80% of transactions involve less than one share. One statistic describes volume; the other counts trades. <sup><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">[S01]</a></sup>

**Outside-hours activity includes nights, extended sessions and weekends.** The aggregate share above 50% combines those periods; IMF Figure 3.7 also distinguishes weekends. The aggregate indicates demand beyond the regular US session, with many small transactions. <sup><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">[S01]</a></sup>

The chapter reports realized volatility for tokenized equities at roughly **1.5 times that of their conventional counterparts**. Its notes specify that the conventional series cover US market hours while the tokenized series use all available hours. Daily volatility is constructed from five-minute price returns and summarized using medians. The observation windows are therefore different. <sup><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">[S01]</a></sup>

The ratio describes an observed difference across distinct measurement windows. A causal estimate of the technology’s effect would require matched trading hours, exposures, trade sizes and comparable liquidity conditions. A price can move more because it is incorporating information while the reference market is closed, because its venue is shallow, or for several reasons simultaneously.

The IMF also finds close pricing during US market hours, when a conventional reference price is available, with better tracking at more active venues. That result matters as much as the volatility finding: intermediaries can connect these markets effectively. The next question is whether the connection withstands larger flows or a reduction in those intermediaries’ own funding capacity. <sup><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">[S01]</a></sup>

The commercial transaction datasets used for those calculations were not available in the public materials reviewed for this investigation. The article therefore reports the published findings without claiming to have reproduced them. It estimates neither a probability of weekend disruption nor a global funding shortfall. Those quantities would require information on balance sheets, contractual commitments and the concentration of liquidity providers that transaction volumes alone do not reveal.

## Safeguards are moving onto the ledger

The market is developing within an evolving regulatory framework. On September 17, 2026, the SEC issued temporary, conditional relief for certain venues trading tokenized US equities. The arrangement limits eligible activity and requires equivalent rights to those of the corresponding conventional share class. This route covers securities satisfying the order’s conditions. Certificates tracking performance through a separate legal claim require a different legal assessment. <sup><a href="#source-s14" aria-label="Source S14">[S14]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-s15" aria-label="Source S15">[S15]</a></sup>

The order also requires a trading stoppage to be synchronized with one in the underlying stock on its primary listing exchange, including a halt or suspension. This concerns interruptions such as regulatory halts and circuit breakers. Ordinary operating hours are a separate issue: the order requires venues to disclose their schedules, including whether they offer 24/7 trading. Systems must be capable of enforcing those rules. <sup><a href="#source-s15" aria-label="Source S15">[S15]</a></sup>

On the money side, the Eurosystem launched **Pontes on September 21, 2026**, enabling wholesale tokenized-asset transactions to settle in central-bank money. The announcement describes a core set of services followed by a gradual expansion of features and operating hours, with full implementation expected by 2028. Access to central-bank settlement money addresses a concrete source of payment-asset risk. It does not settle every question about access, credit or operating schedules at once. <sup><a href="#source-s16" aria-label="Source S16">[S16]</a></sup>

The **Agorá** prototype presented by the ECB on May 27, 2026 demonstrated cross-border, multi-currency atomic settlement using tokenized central-bank reserves and commercial-bank deposits. At that publication date, real-value testing was described as the next step. The result applies to that prototype and to the testing programme described at that publication date. <sup><a href="#source-s17" aria-label="Source S17">[S17]</a></sup>

These developments provide concrete responses to the identified risks. Better-coordinated infrastructure can narrow timing gaps, reduce delivery risk and improve the use of collateral across poorly connected systems. Products that restrict primary redemption to hours when backing assets can actually be converted may also avoid overpromising liquidity. Any remaining cost falls on a secondary seller negotiating with available buyers and accepting their price.

The arrangement becomes more vulnerable when several protections deteriorate together: a small group of market makers, the same payment currency, a shared oracle and funding secured on related assets. Several interfaces may still lead back to the same balance sheet. Identifying that economic concentration matters more than counting the number of platforms displaying the product. <sup><a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source S03">[S03]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-s04" aria-label="Source S04">[S04]</a></sup>

## The real test of an always-open market

An investigation into weekend liquidity should begin with the amounts a buyer commits to absorb, in a specified currency and during a specified window. It would compare those commitments with potential withdrawals, margin needs and obligations already made elsewhere. It would then examine enforceable rights over collateral, price-updating rules and the procedures used when conventional conversion channels reopen.

Reopening can itself concentrate adjustments. An intermediary that has held tokens for two days may want to sell assets received through redemption, restore a hedge or repay the money it advanced. If several participants share that plan, their funding needs converge at the same time. This is a risk scenario to test; the data examined here concern other observations. It is a reason to include Monday morning in a stress test rather than check only that a ledger continues operating on Sunday.

The service can be robust and useful where its commitments are realistic. Known and eligible customers, reserves in the payment instrument actually required, independent funding providers and appropriate pricing rules can allow the system to absorb demand. A somewhat lower secondary selling price may also compensate a buyer fairly for advancing money. Continuous availability does not necessarily imply a standing commitment to buy at the last official valuation.

Conversely, presenting a transferable asset as immediately available cash hides an assumption about a balance sheet the customer may never see. Fragility grows when other obligations are built on that assumption: on-demand repayment, debt close to a liquidation boundary or a bank payment due at a fixed time. A local disruption can then propagate through funding requirements even if the original security has not defaulted.

**The documents establish the different operating windows, mechanisms that could amplify their effects and several responses already under way. The IMF assesses current systemic risk as limited at these markets’ present scale.** The IMF still describes a limited-size market, and the structures are too varied to assign them a single risk profile. <sup><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">[S01]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source S02">[S02]</a></sup>

The promise should ultimately be evaluated like any financial service: through its price, capacity, settlement currency and verifiable commitments. A token moving on Sunday shows that its ledger is operating. The ability to pay still depends on the institutions that have agreed to finance the exit.

## Method and limits

Evidence cutoff: October 16, 2026. The IMF’s asset stock is dated July 31; its October report and October 8 blog are distinct publications.
