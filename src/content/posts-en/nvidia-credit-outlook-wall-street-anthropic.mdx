Nvidia still carries an **Aa1 senior unsecured credit rating** from Moody’s. On **November 18**, the agency changed its outlook from positive to stable, bringing the uncertainty over the company’s next leader into its credit assessment. Meanwhile, the *Wall Street Journal* reports that the financing platforms announced with six Wall Street firms are still working toward their first funded deal. [S01](#source-S01) [S05](#source-S05)

Another commitment is approaching execution. Anthropic has priced its public offering, which is expected to be followed by a separate private placement involving approximately **$10 billion from Nvidia**. Nvidia’s leadership has to weigh its own borrowing needs against the support it provides to customers and the stakes it takes in companies that use its chips. [S06](#source-S06) [S07](#source-S07)

Our [nineteenth installment, on Aolani’s proposed loan](/en/analysis/nvidia-financing-guarantees-aolani-loan/), followed the banks assessing a customer’s project. Here, the attention turns to the manufacturer’s credit and the terms on which it helps finance demand.

## Moody’s reassesses the prospects for an upgrade

A [rating outlook](/en/glossary/perspective-de-notation/) describes the possible direction of a rating over the medium term. Bloomberg reports that Moody’s linked its revision to Huang’s departure, the absence of a formal succession plan and uncertainty over strategy and financial policy. The agency considers an upgrade unlikely over the next **12 to 18 months**. Its **Prime-1** short-term rating was also affirmed, according to the same report. [S01](#source-S01)

The immediate significance for borrowing is easier to understand through an existing contract. Nvidia’s June prospectus covers **$25 billion of senior unsecured bonds in seven fixed-rate tranches**. Their coupons and payment dates are written into the terms. November’s outlook change leaves those coupons intact. For a future issue, investors can demand different compensation for lending, taking account of the company’s finances, the maturity and prevailing interest rates. [S02](#source-S02)

Existing bondholders face a different decision if they want to sell before maturity. The prospectus explains that changing credit assessments and market conditions can affect the bonds’ resale value and liquidity. A fixed coupon gives certainty about the contractual interest payment while the holder remains exposed to Nvidia’s ability to pay and to the price obtainable on a sale. [S02](#source-S02)

That ability to pay also depends on the obligations Nvidia takes on elsewhere. Its financial commentary reports **$118 billion of maximum gross guarantee exposure as of October 25**. Nvidia says it had made no guarantee-related payments as of the commentary. The exposure combines contracts with different triggers. SB Energy’s guarantees, for example, become effective in phases as the relevant buildings are completed and ready for operation. [S03](#source-S03)

## A $500 billion target needs its first funded deal

The August announcement brought together **Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR** around independent financing platforms intended to mobilize **more than $500 billion of third-party capital over time**. Nvidia’s quarterly filing describes preliminary memorandums of understanding that may fall short of definitive agreements. The capital providers would independently underwrite the financing. [S04](#source-S04)

Jensen Huang’s explanation of the initiative added an optional form of support. Nvidia could protect residual value for **up to 25% of an opportunity**, considered project by project. Residual value is the equipment’s remaining value. For a lender taking chips as collateral, the question becomes how much could be recovered if the borrower defaults and the hardware has to be sold or redeployed. [S04](#source-S04)

The terms of a guarantee would determine how much Nvidia covers and when it must pay. That matters especially if the borrower’s computing contracts expire before its debt is repaid. A financier then has to assess the prospect of finding new customers as well as the resale market for the equipment. The manufacturer’s support can help make that risk acceptable, with the amount and conditions settled for each transaction.

The *Journal*, read through its licensed Mint edition, reports that **no capital has yet been deployed under the initiative**. Anonymous sources describe continuing negotiations over the first chip-backed financing, with some expecting completion **in 2027 at the earliest**. Investors are reportedly seeking greater residual-value protection. The report adds detail about the program’s progress to the preliminary status already disclosed in August. [S05](#source-S05)

For a customer planning a new facility, those negotiations can determine how much equity it needs and how quickly it can build. If financiers insist on stronger protection, Nvidia must decide whether the additional exposure is worth the equipment sales and wider business the project could generate. Existing guarantees continue to follow their own contractual timetable. [S03](#source-S03)

## Anthropic moves from pricing toward settlement

Anthropic has priced **980 million Class A shares at $83 each**, including shares sold by existing stockholders. Its announcement schedules the start of trading for **November 19** and expects the offering to close on **November 20**, subject to customary conditions. Pricing establishes the offering terms. Settlement is the later exchange of money and shares. Our [analysis of Anthropic’s supplier financing](/en/analysis/anthropic-supplier-financing-ipo/) traces the computing contracts behind those subscriptions. [S06](#source-S06)

Alongside the public offering, seven strategic investors have individually agreed to purchase **$26 billion of Class N shares**, including approximately **$10 billion for Nvidia**. The shares are priced at the IPO price. The November 17 prospectus provides for these purchases immediately after completion of the IPO, subject to conditions including the requisite stockholder approval. Anthropic can complete its public offering independently of the private placement. [S07](#source-S07)

The filed model purchase agreement illustrates the settlement mechanics. Payment is made by wire against evidence that the shares have been entered in the investor’s name. If an investor sends money and closing fails on the scheduled date, the form provides for its return. The template leaves the investor details blank. **As of 08:43 UTC on November 19, the public filings reviewed did not confirm Nvidia’s payment or issuance of the shares to Nvidia.** [S06](#source-S06) [S07](#source-S07)

For Nvidia, buying shares creates an ownership exposure to Anthropic’s future business. Providing a guarantee creates a potential payment obligation under a separate contract. Both can support the computing ecosystem, but they consume financial capacity differently. The prospectus and pricing announcement put the subscription on a defined schedule. Confirmation of settlement will establish its execution.

The next test of the Wall Street initiative will be the terms of its first completed financing: who supplies the money, which customer repays it and how much risk Nvidia accepts. Those decisions will give the new leadership’s financial policy a concrete form.
