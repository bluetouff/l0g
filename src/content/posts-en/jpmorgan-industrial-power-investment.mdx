When a JPMorgan affiliate invested in Perpetua Resources on October 28, 2025, it obtained more than shares. Its agreement provided qualified access to technical data, development reports and the people working on the mining project, subject to confidentiality. An investment in American antimony came with a closer view of the operation expected to produce it. <sup><a href="#source-S05" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a></sup>

Nearly a year later, on October 19, 2026, JPMorgan reported **$5 billion of equity investments** and **more than $230 billion financed or facilitated** in the first year of its Security and Resiliency Initiative. Reuters covered the announcement. The significance extends beyond the headline amounts: one financial group is connecting businesses, investors and customers whose decisions may depend on Washington’s industrial priorities. <sup><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-S19" aria-label="Source 19">[19]</a></sup>

The available contracts and disclosures reveal a more specific strategy than buying a collection of national champions. The bank takes equity interests, arranges transactions and supports projects that government may help through lending, procurement or investment. This combination can unlock useful capacity. It also makes the allocation of risk important: who pays through construction, who carries a disappointing production ramp, and which revenues survive a change in political priorities? <sup><a href="#source-S02" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-S04" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-S07" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-S12" aria-label="Source 12">[12]</a></sup>

<h2 id="equity-and-financing-totals">Equity and financing totals cover different activities</h2>

The initiative launched on October 13, 2025 set a **$1.5 trillion target over ten years**. JPMorgan said it had already expected approximately **$1 trillion** of activity in the relevant industries. The intended increase was therefore **up to $500 billion**, measured against the bank’s own baseline. An allocation of up to $10 billion for direct equity investments sat within the wider initiative. <sup><a href="#source-S02" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a></sup>

That structure matters. It does not describe a bank taking $1.5 trillion of cash out of its vault and setting it aside for American factories. Facilitating a transaction can bring in money supplied by other investors. Buying shares puts the group’s own capital at risk, with a different claim on future returns from that of a lender. A decade of financing activity is a flow measured across time, rather than a dedicated cash balance at a particular date.

October’s update requires another distinction. The release describes the $5 billion as *deployed* in its opening and as *committed* further down. It does not reconcile commitments with cash disbursements or explain possible overlap with the broader financing total. **Adding five to 230 to produce a $235 billion figure would impose an accounting boundary the source does not provide.** <sup><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup>

Industrial effectiveness requires a further comparison: what would have happened without the initiative? A project made possible, a factory expanded earlier and a deal that would have found the same financing anyway create different amounts of additional capacity. All three may contribute to an activity total. Separating them requires evidence about individual projects, timing and realistic financing alternatives, rather than transaction volumes alone.

<h2 id="stibnite-antimony-and-gold">At Stibnite, antimony depends on gold economics</h2>

Perpetua provides a first view of the financial structure. The equity transaction announced in October 2025 combined **$75 million from JPMorgan** with **$180 million from mining group Agnico Eagle**. Perpetua’s subsequent quarterly filing records its completion on October 28. The transaction announcement also identifies **J.P. Morgan Securities as Perpetua’s adviser on Agnico’s investment**. Different entities within the same group were acting as investor and adviser. <sup><a href="#source-S04" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-S06" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a></sup>

For a developer facing a large construction bill, equity has an important characteristic: unlike a loan, it does not arrive with a comparable schedule of repayments. It can absorb early setbacks and give lenders greater confidence that shareholders have resources at risk if costs rise. The investors receive a claim on future value in return, while exposing their investment to a decline in that value.

Government support changes the scale of the proposed financing. On May 21, 2026, Perpetua announced that the board of the US Export-Import Bank, EXIM, had approved a **$2.9 billion [senior secured loan](/en/glossary/pret-senior-garanti/)**. The structure contemplated a $2.4 billion upfront facility, with the balance covering capitalised construction-period interest and EXIM’s exposure fee. It superseded the $2.7 billion proposal still under consideration in March. <sup><a href="#source-S07" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-S09" aria-label="Source 9">[9]</a></sup>

Approval and available cash remain separate milestones. The quarterly report signed on August 14 still made closing conditional on definitive documentation and conditions precedent. The public material reviewed as of October 20 did not provide confirmation of completed closing and an actual drawdown. The supported conclusion is an approved, conditional financing arrangement, rather than cash already received by the project. <sup><a href="#source-S08" aria-label="Source 8">[8]</a></sup>

The construction update published on September 28 describes progress on the access road, worker housing and power infrastructure without announcing loan closing. <sup><a href="#source-S21" aria-label="Source 21">[21]</a></sup>

Public lending retains a repayment requirement. EXIM’s Make More in America programme calls for reasonable assurance of repayment. A senior secured loan gives the lender a ranking and collateral rights; it is not a blanket promise that taxpayers will cover every construction loss. Assessing the public exposure would require the final security package, draw conditions and remedies available if the borrower runs into trouble. <sup><a href="#source-S10" aria-label="Source 10">[10]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-S07" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a></sup>

The strategic account of Stibnite also leaves part of its commercial economics out of the picture. Perpetua’s risk disclosures emphasise the project’s dependence on the **gold price**. Precious-metal revenues matter to an operation promoted for its antimony. The filing also reports put options bought in July and August to protect part of forecast gold production in 2031; that partial hedge reduces exposure without eliminating it. National importance may improve access to financing without stabilising the market price of the commodity expected to pay part of the bills. <sup><a href="#source-S08" aria-label="Source 8">[8]</a></sup>

That is both a useful complement and a dependency. A profitable co-product can help make a strategically important mineral available. If its price deteriorates, the project’s financial cushion may shrink. Shareholders, lenders and the authority seeking a secure supply then view the same operation through different interests. The strategic need can remain unchanged while the commercial case becomes less comfortable.

<h2 id="opening-a-factory-is-only-the-start-of-its-production-ramp">Opening a factory is only the start of its production ramp</h2>

Hadrian makes the link between investment and public demand particularly visible. On August 6, 2026, it announced a **$1.37 billion equity round**, with JPMorgan’s initiative taking a lead role. The bank’s individual contribution was not disclosed. Treating the entire round as a JPMorgan investment would erase the other investors from the transaction. <sup><a href="#source-S11" aria-label="Source 11">[11]</a></sup>

Months earlier, Hadrian had presented its Cherokee, Alabama, facility within a partnership bringing together **more than $1.5 billion of private capital and $900 million of Navy funding**. The body of the announcement describes three facilities, including Factory 4. Two tempting calculations should be resisted: assigning the whole partnership to one building, then automatically adding August’s round to the private capital announced in March. The potential overlap between those funding sources is not disclosed. <sup><a href="#source-S12" aria-label="Source 12">[12]</a></sup>

The industrial timetable explains why the structure matters. In its March 20 statement, read through its republication by *Seapower*, the Navy described an **18-to-24-month** path to full-rate production. That is a target, not an observed production rate. Between installing equipment and delivering reliable output, a factory has to pass qualification and organisational milestones. <sup><a href="#source-S13" aria-label="Source 13">[13]</a></sup>

Automation does not eliminate that interval. Components have to satisfy the customer’s requirements, materials must arrive on time and individual production stages must work together. Investment can increase theoretical capacity while leaving the bottleneck that determines final delivery largely intact. The relevant test is usable output arriving when needed, rather than the number of machines purchased.

A correction issued on March 21 complicates any easy assumption that government simply protects private investors. The added official statement describes private capital going in first, followed by performance-based public support. The Navy claims both downside protection and participation in the upside. Those claims need to be tested against the full contractual terms, which are not included in the release; they cannot be replaced by an assumption that all losses are public and all gains private. <sup><a href="#source-S12" aria-label="Source 12">[12]</a></sup>

The definition of performance remains central. Installing a machine, obtaining acceptance for the first component and maintaining a delivery rate are different achievements. A financing arrangement can be protective in design and demanding in execution, especially when several factories depend on common suppliers or scarce skills. The useful questions concern payment milestones and recovery rights if the industrial schedule slips.

<h2 id="contract-rights-and-order-books">Contract rights determine the value of an order book</h2>

Castelion illustrates another financial distinction. Its August 19 announcement combines **$800 million of equity with a $250 million committed revolving credit facility**. The components total $1.05 billion beneath a headline rounded to $1 billion. JPMorgan is named among the equity round’s co-leads. The release does not establish that it provided the entire revolver, or how much of that facility had been drawn. <sup><a href="#source-S14" aria-label="Source 14">[14]</a></sup>

A [revolver](/en/glossary/credit-renouvelable/) provides borrowing capacity that can be reused under its terms. In a conventional production cycle, it can pay for materials and wages before delivery, then be repaid when the customer pays. It finances the interval between spending and collection. Persistent losses on each unit create a different problem: drawing on the facility prolongs the funding of a deficit rather than supporting a cycle capable of paying the debt back.

Having a public-sector customer does not settle every question. A contract may include options, acceptance conditions or termination rights. For fixed-price contracts terminated for the government’s convenience, section 49.202 of the US Federal Acquisition Regulation distinguishes profit on work performed from anticipated profit on unperformed work in a termination settlement. The published overhaul retains that distinction. The applicability of these provisions to the particular corporate contracts discussed here has not been established. <sup><a href="#source-S17" aria-label="Source 17">[17]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-S18" aria-label="Source 18">[18]</a></sup>

A lender therefore has to examine the rights created by the order: firm quantities, delivery dates, payment for work in progress and any compensation entitlement. A receivable owed by a creditworthy customer may be sound once it exists. Expected sales before products have been accepted represent a different exposure. The customer’s standing cannot substitute for analysing the contract.

<h2 id="a-schedule-problem-eventually-reaches-cash-flow">A schedule problem eventually reaches cash flow</h2>

A hypothetical factory makes the transmission easier to see. Assume annual revenue of $100 million, variable costs equal to 55% of sales, fixed operating costs of $20 million and annual debt service, interest and principal, of $12 million. The simplified business retains **$13 million after those payments**. This calculation excludes tax, capital expenditure and changes in working capital.

At revenue of $60 million, variable spending falls, but fixed costs and debt service stay unchanged under the model’s assumptions. The resulting balance is **negative $5 million**. Revenue of approximately **$71.1 million** is needed to cover just those specified cash outflows. None of the inputs is calibrated to Hadrian, Castelion, Perpetua or JPMorgan. The example shows why a financed factory can keep consuming cash when expected volumes arrive late.

[Working capital](/en/glossary/bfr/) can deepen the gap. It is the money tied up between paying to produce goods and collecting the sales proceeds. If components have already been bought for a programme that is subsequently delayed, fewer deliveries may not release the cash already committed. The business can possess inventory, equipment and trained employees while having less liquidity to fund the wait.

At portfolio level, holding more companies does not necessarily diversify this exposure. Suppliers of different mechanical parts, electronic systems and assemblies may depend on the same budget, programme or qualification schedule. A shared delay can reduce their revenue at the same time. This is a mechanism worth testing, not a numerical correlation that public disclosures already allow us to estimate for JPMorgan.

The same dependency can also support the case for patient investment. Without sufficient confidence in future demand, no supplier wants to be the first to build capacity needed by the rest of the chain. Procurement, equity and lending can jointly address that coordination problem. Their value should be judged by the additional capacity produced and its cost, rather than solely by whether the money came from a public or private balance sheet.

<h2 id="information-rights-at-perpetua">Information rights and influence at Perpetua</h2>

Perpetua’s documents distinguish access to information from management authority. Its investor agreement includes a notification mechanism around a **4.99% voting threshold**, with provisions to restructure the investment to avoid or remedy a breach. That contractual arrangement is not proof of control over the mine, nor a universal cap applicable to every JPMorgan transaction. <sup><a href="#source-S05" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a></sup>

The economic roles nevertheless remain distinct. An equity stake can appreciate, while an advisory engagement pays for a service. Combining them may improve transaction preparation and the understanding of a project. It also creates interests that need careful management when recommending financing, negotiating its price or deciding whether to invest again. The documented dual role does not by itself establish misconduct. <sup><a href="#source-S04" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a></sup>

The bank also explicitly advocates changes to permitting, research and procurement policies relevant to these industries. This raises a familiar industrial-policy question: the people who understand a constraint best are often among those who stand to benefit from removing it. Their expertise may be useful, but the intended outcomes should be open to assessment independent of their earnings. <sup><a href="#source-S02" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a></sup>

The initiative also extends beyond a strictly domestic interpretation. Its European expansion was announced in April 2026. By October, JPMorgan placed almost a third of activity outside the United States, without defining the denominator sufficiently to translate that share into a dollar figure. Allied production may contribute to supply security, while each country still needs to examine the skills required and its dependence on outside financing. <sup><a href="#source-S03" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup>

Perpetua’s public support crosses political periods as well. Reuters documented an initial EXIM process in April 2024, before the banking initiative existed. That history does not establish that every later decision would have been the same. It does prevent the origin of government support from being attributed solely to JPMorgan’s programme. <sup><a href="#source-S20" aria-label="Source 20">[20]</a></sup>

<h2 id="the-decisive-balance-sheet-is-still-on-the-factory-floor">The decisive balance sheet is still on the factory floor</h2>

An external review offers a useful way to judge the outcome. In April 2026, the Government Accountability Office examined fifteen selected submarine industrial-base investments from fiscal years 2023 to 2025. Baseline performance was documented for **five**, expected outcomes for **twelve**, and tracking of those outcomes over time for **three**. The sample was non-generalizable and was not an audit of JPMorgan’s portfolio. <sup><a href="#source-S15" aria-label="Source 15">[15]</a></sup>

These gaps limit what the assessment can establish. A baseline helps attribute improvements, while a defined outcome prevents spending from becoming its own measure of success. Follow-up must also show whether early progress accelerated deliveries or merely moved the bottleneck. The investments may have been useful; the available record leaves part of their effectiveness unproven.

These questions sit within longstanding constraints on skills, capacity and demand visibility, already identified by GAO in 2025. Capital can help address them, but its arrival does not reveal how long each constraint will take to resolve. A company can complete a funding round before it has solved the organisation of production. <sup><a href="#source-S16" aria-label="Source 16">[16]</a></sup>

Resilience also requires a more demanding test than profitability alone. Spare capacity may have considerable public value while generating little income for its owner. Conversely, a highly profitable plant can become so important that it represents another single-source dependency. Aligning security with returns requires clarity about what the public is trying to obtain: more output, faster delivery, an alternative supplier or capacity held ready for a crisis. Those objectives imply different contracts and costs.

The documents establish equity investments, advisory roles and several financing arrangements associated with American priorities. They do not yet establish the additional industrial capacity attributable to the initiative as a whole, or its complete allocation of risk. **JPMorgan is organising strategic capital whose returns will also depend on the continuity of demand and successful industrial execution.** The next decisive evidence will be the contractual rights exercised and the deliveries achieved, at an observable cost and on a verifiable schedule.
