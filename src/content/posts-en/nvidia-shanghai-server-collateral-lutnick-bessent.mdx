**The servers in this story perform two jobs. They provide computing power, and they can secure a creditor’s claim. A Bloomberg investigation published on 13 November follows the second role to Shanghai, just as Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly directs questions about Megaspeed to the department run by Howard Lutnick.** [S01](#source-S01) [S06](#source-S06)

According to filings reviewed by Bloomberg and details attributed to its investigation by TaiSounds, **193 servers**, assigned a collateral value of **485 million yuan**, were pledged in January 2025 by a Chinese company linked to Alice Huang’s network. The reported inventory includes Dell and Supermicro machines fitted with Nvidia GPUs subject to US export restrictions. The filing opens a financial trail alongside the investigation into the equipment’s movement. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

This eighth instalment follows our examination of [guarantees behind AI financing](/en/analysis/nvidia-ai-financing-guarantees-asian-investigations/) and [Congress’s demands for answers on export controls](/en/analysis/nvidia-congress-teter-kessler-export-controls/). Its central question is tangible: when a server becomes collateral, who identified it, who claimed rights over it, and which rights did the lender obtain?

## In Shanghai, computing equipment becomes pledged property

Bloomberg’s investigation describes Shanghai Longteng Intelligent Computing, founded by Alice Huang, and its subsidiary Shanghai Longjie Yaoteng. According to the reporting, Huang transferred her Longteng shares in 2017. Her personal role in the pledge remains unestablished. TaiSounds’s account identifies the subsidiary as the collateral-filing company. The published breakdown comprises **22 Dell servers containing H800 chips**, **52 Supermicro servers with H100 or H200 chips**, and **119 Gigabyte servers whose GPUs remain unidentified**. These counts refer to servers. An individual server can contain several chips. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

<NvidiaCollateral2026 lang="en" figure="collateral" />

The 485 million yuan is the declared equipment value. Establishing the loan principal, disbursements and credit terms will require further documents. For this article, we examined the Chinese registry’s rules, Bloomberg’s publicly available passages and identified accounts of its reporting. Access to the individual record requires an account or its certificate identifier, which remains unavailable. The filing details are therefore attributed to Bloomberg. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03)

The registry is operated by the People’s Bank of China’s Credit Reference Center. Its rules provide for public notice of security interests over movable property. Filing parties supply the information and bear responsibility for it. The regime in force since February 2022 places verification of the assets with the secured creditor. The center registers declarations without substantive review. A registration preserves a dated declaration identifying parties and assets. Establishing that a particular chip was physically present, or legally delivered, requires further verification. [S03](#source-S03)

That declaration can still matter to a prospective lender searching for existing rights over the equipment. The economic protection depends on ownership, priority and the ability to recover or sell the assets under the contract and applicable law. This is the purpose of [collateral](/en/glossary/collateral/): property from which a creditor expects to recover money after a default.

## Following Aperia means keeping the legal entities distinct

Bloomberg reports, citing a person familiar with the matter, that some servers were originally sold to Singapore’s **Aperia Cloud Services**. It says serial numbers were supplied to a source to trace Dell’s customer. Dell’s on-record response names **Aperia International**: the manufacturer says it stopped sales and support in February 2025 because of concerns about unauthorized diversion. The two names therefore appear at different points in the supplier’s account and the investigation. [S01](#source-S01) [S04](#source-S04)

Singapore police use a further defined group in their 1 July statement. It includes Aperia International, A-Speed Infotech and Aperia Cloud Services **(II)**. The suffix “II” identifies an additional company in the police statement. [S05](#source-S05)

Supermicro says its sales complied with US export and re-export rules and disputes having sold directly to the named Chinese companies. Nvidia declined to comment on the new findings. Bloomberg reports that Megaspeed maintains its general denial, while several questions to the Chinese companies remain unanswered. [S02](#source-S02) [S04](#source-S04)

## Bessent directs the questions to Commerce

On CNBC on 13 November, Bessent said Treasury, Defense, State and the US trade representative had favored restrictions on Megaspeed for months. He directed questions about the process to Commerce. Politically, the Treasury secretary is distancing himself from Lutnick’s handling of the issue and leaving his colleague to explain the outcome. [S06](#source-S06)

The timing matters. CNBC reported that Commerce had not immediately responded to Friday’s remarks. A spokesperson’s denial quoted in the same article dates from Wednesday, 11 November, and concerns a reported Situation Room conversation. That earlier response addresses a different account from Bessent’s new public intervention. [S06](#source-S06)

The Entity List imposes the export-licensing requirements specified in each listing. An interagency **End-User Review Committee**, chaired by Commerce, manages the process. Additions require a majority vote. State, Defense and Energy participate, with Treasury where appropriate. The US trade representative was among the supporters named by Bessent, but the rule does not list USTR as a committee member. [S07](#source-S07)

Chairing the committee gives Commerce a central role. The rules also provide for appeals that can reach the president. Reconstructing the alleged obstruction will require the proposal actually circulated, the votes and any appeals. Bessent highlights a gap between the support he describes and the outcome. Votes and internal communications would help explain responsibility for that gap. [S06](#source-S06) [S07](#source-S07)

## Political pressure converges on Lutnick

Republican criticism is adding pressure. Jim Banks wants answers and accuses Commerce of leaving loopholes. John Kennedy wants to hear from the officials involved. Thom Tillis calls for hearings before Democrats can set the narrative. His language openly identifies a political interest in protecting the president’s party from the fallout. [S09](#source-S09) [S10](#source-S10)

**The pressure makes a scenario increasingly plausible: the administration could sacrifice Lutnick to contain the political damage.** Concentrating blame on Commerce could let the administration replace an official, present the change as a response to the crisis and draw attention away from presidential decisions. This is a scenario, extending the questions explored in our [fifth instalment on state leverage](/en/analysis/nvidia-trump-tim-cook-lutnick-state-leverage/). Testing it requires tracing instructions as carefully as public statements.

The evidence also complicates a coordinated-abandonment theory. On 12 November, Kennedy reserved judgment on whether Lutnick should lose his job, while Brian Mast expressed confidence in him. Reuters reported that same day discussions among White House officials about Lutnick’s future, citing two sources, alongside an official’s reaffirmation of Trump’s confidence. Presidential approval for removing Lutnick remains a hypothesis. Public positions show an administration under strain and Republicans alert to the political cost. [S08](#source-S08) [S10](#source-S10)

## Trump and Lutnick have a long history and intersecting family interests

At a House interview in May 2026, Lutnick said he had known Trump for more than thirty years. At his Commerce swearing-in in February 2025, Trump had already emphasized their long acquaintance and Lutnick’s central role in his trade agenda. That personal history matters when assessing a potential rupture. [S11](#source-S11) [S12](#source-S12)

Their children’s interests also intersect through a chain involving strategic minerals. Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump are shareholders and advisers at Dominari. The group controls the management companies of American Ventures, a vehicle involved in taking control of Skyline. Skyline became involved with Cove Kaz, which is developing tungsten projects in Kazakhstan. ASP Isotopes also owns an interest in Skyline through its subsidiary Quantum Leap Energy. [S13](#source-S13) [S15](#source-S15)

Cantor Fitzgerald, led by Brandon Lutnick with Kyle Lutnick as executive vice chairman, jointly managed an ASP Isotopes share offering with Lucid in October 2025. Cantor’s banking role connects this chain to the Lutnick family. The records distinguish equity interests, advisory positions and securities placement services. Together, they establish a network worth scrutinizing when Washington negotiates access to strategic resources. [S14](#source-S14)

The project’s recent changes deserve equal attention. Skyline and Cove Kaz announced the termination of their merger on 2 November. Reuters subsequently reported that US public lenders were not actively pursuing the proposed financing. The letters of interest were preliminary. Cove Kaz says it is completing technical studies and expects to apply for construction financing after their planned completion in 2027. That development weighs against an assumption of consistently privileged access to public money. [S16](#source-S16) [S17](#source-S17)

## The Epstein connections belong in the scrutiny of public accounts

Both men also had separately documented ties to Jeffrey Epstein. In 2002, Trump told *New York* he had known Epstein for fifteen years and spoke approvingly of his company. He later said they had fallen out. [S18](#source-S18)

Lutnick acknowledged a family lunch on Epstein’s island in December 2012. House questioners confronted him with his earlier account of a rupture, which he called incomplete. He described their interactions as limited. [S11](#source-S11)

These histories warrant scrutiny of the men’s public accounts and transparency. A connection to Nvidia policy would require evidence specific to this case. Any presidential protection should be investigated through instructions, communications and decisions concerning exports.

## Nvidia’s succession now meets a question of credit

The board appointed Colette Kress as interim chief executive and began a search for internal and external candidates, with Harvey C. Jones as chairman. The next leader will inherit customers whose expansion depends, for some projects, on equipment financing and the allocation of risk with Nvidia. Our [seventh instalment](/en/analysis/nvidia-ai-financing-guarantees-asian-investigations/) explains those arrangements. [S19](#source-S19)

The Shanghai story exposes a further aspect. A server’s value to a secured lender also depends on tracing it and on the legal ability to operate or resell it. A dispute over ownership, a trade restriction or difficulty recovering the equipment could reduce proceeds after default. That is a channel of risk to examine in the relevant contracts.

An internal successor could preserve commercial relationships while tightening checks. An outsider might seek a broader review of commitments before renewing them. Under a scenario of increased Washington influence, licenses, procurement and guarantees could become negotiating levers around identified activities. Board decisions and signed agreements will distinguish these paths. The Shanghai filing and Bessent’s remarks provide two complementary leads: follow the rights attached to the machines, and the responsibility attached to the decisions.
