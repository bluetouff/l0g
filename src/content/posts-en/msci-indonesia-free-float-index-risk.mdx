On 15 October 2026, MSCI proposed moving Indonesia from Emerging Markets to Standalone status, a category of markets tracked separately. The company says it expects to proceed unless there is evidence of material improvement. As of 22 October, the process remains a consultation: feedback is due by 30 April 2027, a decision by 30 June, and implementation timing is still open. That distinction defines the risk. Some portfolios could need to change even if the economic prospects of the Indonesian companies they hold remain the same. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Understanding the proposal means following a share from its ultimate owner to the mandate of the fund that owns it. The quality of ownership information influences how many shares an index considers accessible. That quantity feeds into an index weight, which then becomes a portfolio target. A rule applied at any of these stages can alter demand for the shares. The documents assembled for this investigation show how that transmission works, and where its effects could be amplified or absorbed.

## Ownership behind the shareholder register

A new regulatory disclosure provides a concrete example. On 21 October, Indonesia’s Financial Services Authority, OJK, announced that it had delivered notices of administrative sanctions decided on 16 October. In the BREN and CUAN initial public offerings, OJK said it had found that parties related to the controlling shareholders obtained large allocations while obscuring their identities, concentrating ownership and restricting the shares in circulation. This is the administrative authority’s finding concerning the transactions it examined. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup>

The mechanism helps explain why [free float](/en/glossary/flottant/) matters. A company may have many shareholder accounts while several of them ultimately reflect the same economic interests. If those accounts operate within the controlling shareholder’s sphere, treating every holding as independent can overstate the pool of shares available to outside investors. A manager seeking to build a meaningful position may encounter fewer willing sellers than the apparent ownership distribution suggested.

MSCI’s free float methodology addresses the distinction between strategic holdings and shares available to investors. It includes an examination of beneficial ownership behind certain holding structures. The number of entries in a register and the legal label attached to an account therefore provide only part of the evidence needed to assess the shares’ economic availability. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s06" aria-label="Source 6">6</a></sup>

The authorities have already changed the information framework. Their joint statement of 2 April describes disclosure of shareholders above 1%, an expansion to 39 investor categories, and a system for flagging high shareholding concentration, known as HSC. Beneficial-owner information for shareholders holding 10% or more is also collected, with access available on request under the exchange’s procedures. That arrangement has a narrower scope than general public disclosure of every chain of control. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

These reforms provide better tools for checking ownership. Their practical value then depends on whether accounts can be connected, relationships established and the rules applied consistently. Discovering and sanctioning a problem can consequently support two readings at once: the finding documents a weakness, while the enforcement action demonstrates an ability to address it. A fair assessment of the market needs both.

## Free float becomes a portfolio weight

A global index weighted by free float adjusted market capitalisation has an operational purpose: to represent securities that investors can reasonably hold. Under MSCI’s methodology, the amount recognised for a security depends on its price, its number of shares and its Foreign Inclusion Factor, or FIF. The factor reflects the portion accessible to international investors, taking relevant restrictions into account. The security’s weight is then its adjusted capitalisation divided by the total for the index. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a></sup>

This construction connects confidence in ownership data to the size of an index position. If a holding previously treated as available turns out to be strategic, the factor may fall. The company retains its business, assets and earnings, while its place in the reference portfolio shrinks. The methodology is concerned with the ability to reproduce the index reliably.

Country classification operates one level above the individual security: it determines which groups a market belongs to. MSCI’s glossary defines Standalone markets as being tracked separately from the World, Emerging Markets and Frontier Markets composites. Shares can remain listed and available for investment; their presence in broad international baskets changes with the eligible universe. The economic effect comes through the investment arrangements that use those baskets. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source 18">18</a></sup>

The tension was already clear in MSCI’s June review. It linked ownership and price formation concerns to difficulties in assessing free float and replicating portfolios. MSCI acknowledged the direction of the reforms while seeking evidence of lasting results. That assessment belongs to the index provider and the investor feedback it reports; it needs to be read alongside the measures and enforcement actions presented by the Indonesian authorities. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

## A smaller index footprint before October

Indonesia’s representation had contracted substantially before the consultation opened. Between 31 December 2025 and 30 September 2026, the MSCI Indonesia Index went from 18 constituents to nine. Its free float adjusted market capitalisation fell from $119.14 billion to $53.36 billion, a calculated decline of 55.22%. Its weight in MSCI Emerging Markets moved from 1.16% to 0.43%. These figures concern the Standard index covering large and mid cap companies. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup>

Each measure describes a narrower benchmark presence, with a different meaning. Adjusted capitalisation depends on share prices, exchange rates, share counts, inclusion factors and index membership. A country’s global weight also depends on developments elsewhere in the denominator. The difference between the two capitalisation figures therefore measures a change in the capitalisation represented in the index. Establishing an amount of money leaving the country would require evidence of transactions and their counterparties.

The chronology provides context, although it does not give a numerical attribution. On 27 January, MSCI froze increases in FIF and shares counted, additions to Indonesia IMI, and certain upward size migrations. In April, it maintained those restrictions while providing for deletions associated with HSC flags and the use of new 1% ownership disclosures to adjust free float. Some routes to increased representation were restricted, while reductions could continue. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a>, <a href="#source-s19" aria-label="Source 19">19</a></sup>

This asymmetry matters. An improvement in a security’s circumstances may await recognition in the index, while adverse information can reduce its representation. That is why the meaning of the freeze requires care. Prices still move, weights still change and constituents can still leave the reference portfolio.

## When a rule creates a trading requirement

Consider a deliberately hypothetical company with 100 shares, each worth $10. With a FIF of 30%, its adjusted index capitalisation is $300. If the price falls to $8 with all other inputs unchanged, that amount becomes $240. A portfolio holding the same share quantities simultaneously experiences the same proportional decline in the value of its position.


With full replication, fixed index composition and no flows or other adjustments, portfolio weights move with index weights. The existing share quantities therefore keep the portfolio aligned with the index. The Bank for International Settlements (BIS) describes this property of [passive investing](/en/glossary/gestion-passive/) in its study of indexation and securities markets. A trading requirement comes from other changes, including investor flows, index revisions or adjustments to replication inputs. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s16" aria-label="Source 16">16</a></sup>

Now return to the hypothetical company at $10 a share and reduce its FIF from 30% to 20%. Its adjusted capitalisation becomes $200. The rule has changed the number of units recognised in the reference basket. A fund must recalculate its target weights and the share quantities corresponding to its assets. The precise adjustment also depends on the other constituents and the portfolio’s size: the ten units removed describe the construction of the index basket, while an actual fund’s trade requires those additional inputs.

Country exclusion operates through the same chain on a larger scale. If adopted and implemented, the decision removes the affected securities from the specified composite indexes. Funds seeking to track those composites must adjust their exposure. They may need to change their holdings even when their own investors retain every fund share. This is where a methodology decision becomes a transaction requirement. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a>, <a href="#source-s05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a></sup>

The same distinction between theoretical weight and actual buying appears in [bond indexes](/en/analysis/does-borrowing-more-make-bond-index-buy-more/#from-index-weight-to-actual-buying), under their own eligibility and replication rules.

## The mandate determines the pressure

Prospectuses for exchange-traded funds, or [ETFs](/en/glossary/etf/), show the mechanism in actual products. EEM, the iShares MSCI Emerging Markets ETF, tracks MSCI Emerging Markets. IEMG, the iShares Core MSCI Emerging Markets ETF, tracks the IMI version, which also includes small caps. A change to the emerging market composites would therefore affect their respective benchmarks, with different security universes. Indonesia’s published September weight of 0.43% in the Standard index concerns EEM’s benchmark; IEMG’s IMI universe requires its own calculation. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a>, <a href="#source-s13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a></sup>

EIDO presents a different case. The iShares MSCI Indonesia ETF tracks MSCI Indonesia IMI 25/50, a country equity index with concentration limits. Its separate country index mandate helps explain why Standalone status alone would not require the fund to liquidate. Its exposure would remain subject to its own benchmark methodology, management decisions and any subsequent product changes. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a>, <a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source 18">18</a></sup>

Passive management also allows choices in execution. These prospectuses provide for representative sampling: the manager can assemble holdings intended to reproduce the index’s characteristics without owning every constituent in precisely the same proportion. Eligible asset provisions provide additional operating flexibility. A benchmark change creates a substantial portfolio management consequence, but following it through to individual orders requires knowledge of the positions, instruments and implementation choices. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a>, <a href="#source-s13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a>, <a href="#source-s14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a></sup>

For active funds, the question is both contractual and economic. A mandate may restrict eligible markets, limit departures from the benchmark or allow broader discretion. Depending on those terms, retaining Indonesia after a benchmark exit could become a deliberate active position, consume more of the manager’s risk budget or require a portfolio change. The relevant mandate determines which situation applies.

## Two markets behind an ETF share

ETFs add a further distinction. When an investor sells a fund share on an exchange to another investor, ownership changes; the transaction can settle without changing the fund’s assets. Creations and redemptions instead take place through authorised participants, intermediaries that exchange large blocks of fund shares with the fund for securities, cash or a combination of the two. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15" aria-label="Source 15">15</a></sup>

Those arrangements create several possible transmission routes. Cash redemptions may require the use of liquidity or asset sales. An in kind redemption transfers a basket to the intermediary, which can subsequently hold, hedge or sell it. Local transfer and settlement conditions matter in choosing the route. The illustration shows the general ETF architecture; it makes no assumption about the contents of an Indonesian redemption basket on a particular day. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a>, <a href="#source-s16" aria-label="Source 16">16</a></sup>

An index rebalance is a separate event again. The fund may need to change its portfolio while the number of shares outstanding remains stable. Conversely, heavy trading in fund shares may largely reflect transactions between investors. Tracking potential sales of Indonesian equities therefore requires connecting benchmark changes, actual holdings, creations and redemptions, and subsequent trading in the underlying securities.

The [guide to ETF creation, redemption and arbitrage](/en/guides/read-etfs-creation-redemption-arbitrage/#connect-the-secondary-and-primary-markets) follows fund shares and underlying assets through the two markets.

## Where sellers meet buyers

The market risk becomes clearer when several portfolios receive a similar instruction. If their benchmarks remove the same securities within a short period, their need to reduce exposure can become concentrated. Active investors may also anticipate the event or withdraw because of uncertainty. This is a transmission scenario inferred from mandates and index construction. Measuring its scale would require the future orders of the affected portfolios.

The crucial variable is other investors’ capacity to absorb the shares at the offered prices and within the required time. Free float describes potential availability; market depth concerns what can actually trade around a price. Shares classified as available may be held by investors who prefer to keep them. Buyers with liquidity and a different investment horizon can, in turn, cushion a reallocation. A concentration of sellers turns these preferences into execution conditions.

Anticipation also makes the timetable more complicated than a single date. One manager may prepare a reduction, another wait for the final implementation terms, while a prospective buyer seeks a discount before the deadline. These decisions can bring some trading forward or spread it over time. They can also shift risk towards intermediaries willing to hold positions temporarily. The eventual effect depends on timing, hedges and counterparties as well as the nominal value of the exposures.

The [KOSPI analysis](/en/analysis/kospi-concentrated-liquidation-samsung-sk-hynix/#the-daily-mechanics-of-2x) follows another source of concentrated orders: the daily rebalancing of leveraged ETFs. Contracts and calculation rules likewise determine the trades required.

It is tempting to produce a large selling estimate from assets associated with MSCI. Its emerging market index page reports more than $2.2 trillion benchmarked to the family, using data as of 30 June 2026. The methodology note includes actively managed assets as well as ETFs. That total concerns a family of indexes and a different date from Indonesia’s September Standard index weight. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s17" aria-label="Source 17">17</a></sup>

Turning those figures into a flow estimate would require identifying the funds actually affected, their holdings when the change takes place, their constraints and adjustments already completed, then removing double counting. Multiplying benchmarked assets by a weight can indicate an exposure when the dates and definitions match. A forecast of net selling requires the additional steps. The aggregate amount of future selling therefore remains unknown.

The same discipline applies to foreign exchange. An equity sale may be followed by local reinvestment, a cash holding or conversion into another currency. Pressure on the rupiah would depend on those decisions and the associated hedges. Corporate financing would be affected if persistently weaker demand raised the cost of future equity issuance. These are possible extensions of the mechanism, whose occurrence needs to be established separately.

## Reform deadlines extend beyond the MSCI consultation

Indonesia is moving towards a 15% minimum free float through transition periods. Rule I-A, published on 7 October and effective from 9 October, retains separate deadlines based on capitalisation and free float. For companies with capitalisation of at least IDR5 trillion, the position on 31 March 2026 determines the deadline: float from 12.5% to below 15% must reach 15% in March 2027; below 12.5%, the steps are 12.5% in March 2027 and 15% in March 2028. Companies below the capitalisation threshold have until March 2029. Deadlines may be adjusted with OJK approval or instruction. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s08" aria-label="Source 8">8</a></sup>

MSCI’s consultation timetable leads to a decision before several of those transitions are complete. Putting the two calendars together reveals a practical difficulty: a reform can be legally in place while its economic implementation remains unfinished when its effectiveness is assessed. Authorities and companies may need observable evidence of progress ahead of some regulatory deadlines. The figure compares the deadlines. MSCI’s assessment also concerns transparency, market functioning and enforcement.

The local threshold and the FIF also serve different purposes. Meeting a listing requirement establishes compliance with that rule; index recognition follows an investability methodology and the available information. Increasing free float can improve market depth, but its effect depends on the buyers, their relationships and their behaviour. That passage from the rule to its practical effect deserves continued scrutiny. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a>, <a href="#source-s06" aria-label="Source 6">6</a>, <a href="#source-s08" aria-label="Source 8">8</a></sup>

## Reforms and buyers can change the risk

The authorities’ publications also document progress. Disclosure requirements have been strengthened, sanctions imposed, and on 21 October OJK reiterated its commitment to further reform and engagement with international market participants. These documents establish decisions taken and the regulator’s position. Lasting effectiveness across the market then requires verifiable results. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a>, <a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a>, <a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

Another index provider offers a useful comparison. In its review published on 6 October, FTSE Russell retained Indonesia as Secondary Emerging and kept it off the reclassification Watch List. It also recalled postponing certain index changes and said it continued to assess the reforms. That assessment predates MSCI’s 15 October consultation. It shows how classification decisions and timetables can diverge while providers share concerns about investability. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s11" aria-label="Source 11">11</a></sup>

The next phase will depend on evidence precise enough to influence the decision and the portfolios tied to it: ownership that can be verified, genuinely available shares, consistent enforcement and explicit transition arrangements. Assessing the market risk also requires identifying affected funds and buyers able to take over positions. Better transparency can improve the first part of this chain; available demand can cushion the second.

Indonesia’s case reveals a particular form of index power. Benchmarks connect the description of a market to the commitments managers make to their investors. When that description becomes uncertain, the provider can revise the reference universe. When the universe changes, a methodology decision can create a need to trade. As of 22 October 2026, that chain is documented, while the eventual scale and execution timetable remain to be established. The risk lies in the combination of rules already operating and decisions still open.
