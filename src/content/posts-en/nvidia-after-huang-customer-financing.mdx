**Colette Kress takes charge of a company that helps finance demand for its own chips.** Remaining chief financial officer while serving as interim chief executive, she inherits investments, contracts and negotiations whose commercial effects extend beyond each individual deal. [S02](#source-S02) [S04](#source-S04)

A new *Wall Street Journal* report, published on **16 November**, offers a view into those decisions. Its three cases involve different types of transaction. The economic question is which projects Nvidia will continue to support, on what terms, and how much risk it will retain while partners build a customer base of their own. [S01](#source-S01)

This fourteenth instalment complements [our examination of Washington’s pressure on the succession](/en/analysis/nvidia-succession-washington-ai-champions/). The [twelfth](/en/analysis/nvidia-after-huang-contracts-guarantees-crisis-costs/) followed contracts and guarantees. Here the focus is how a leadership transition affects the selection of future commitments and the execution of existing ones.

## Three deals at different stages

According to a person cited by the WSJ, **Perplexity’s** chief executive told employees on **30 October** that a **$3 billion** Nvidia investment had closed. That dates the internal announcement. At **Reflection**, the paper describes a licensing and hiring offer, with no agreement reached before Huang’s departure. An investment in **Thinking Machines Lab** was still being finalized. [S01](#source-S01)

Each case calls for a different set of rights to be examined. An equity investment exchanges capital for securities. A licence pays for access to technology, while hiring establishes relationships with individuals. Completing a negotiation depends on the terms the parties accept. Treating these projects as one pool of customer funding would obscure the decisions facing Nvidia’s new leadership.

Thinking Machines provides a useful primary reference. On **10 March 2026**, the lab and Nvidia had already announced a significant investment, without disclosing its value, and a partnership to deploy **at least one gigawatt of Vera Rubin systems**, with deployment targeted for early 2027. The March announcement establishes an earlier relationship. How it connects contractually to the investment being finalized in the November report remains unclear. The gigawatt describes the power scale of the planned computing deployment. [S03](#source-S03)

The WSJ also places earlier reassessments in August and discussions in Berlin in October. The succession arrives during a review already under way. [S01](#source-S01)

## Investing can also support equipment demand

[Vendor financing](/en/glossary/vendor-financing/) brings two commercial interests together. An investment can appreciate if an AI lab succeeds. It can also help the lab pay for computing from clouds that buy Nvidia equipment. The chipmaker’s filings describe the connections between ecosystem investments, direct customers and companies purchasing cloud services. [S04](#source-S04)

<NvidiaClientCapital2026 lang="en" />

Other investors and the labs’ commercial revenue bring money into the system as well. Assessing its resilience means examining the lab’s ability to sell its services and the cloud’s ability to put its machines to work. A funding round can accelerate the start. Recurring revenue supports the business beyond that initial reserve of capital.

Nvidia’s potential returns come through investment value, equipment sales and wider adoption of its architectures. They can arrive at different times. A promising equity holding may keep absorbing capital after the associated hardware sales have already generated revenue. Management has to assess the combined return while keeping a funded order distinct from the customer’s eventual profitability.

The 10-Q supplies a historical measure of these choices. At **26 July 2026**, Nvidia reported **$25 billion of future equity investment commitments**, subject to contingencies, including commitments to model makers and infrastructure financiers. That figure describes obligations at that date. Deals announced later need to be reconciled against subsequent financial reporting. [S04](#source-S04)

## Reserved computing capacity can find another buyer

Support can also take the form of service contracts. In the model Nvidia describes with selected AI clouds, the partner buys Nvidia infrastructure and the chipmaker commits to purchasing cloud services, typically over six years. The cloud can instead sell that capacity to third parties at more advantageous rates. Nvidia’s commitment decreases as third parties, or Nvidia itself for research and development, use the capacity. Revenue sharing applies if specified criteria are met. [S04](#source-S04)

<NvidiaCapacitySupport2026 lang="en" />

For the cloud, an existing commitment from a capacity buyer can help launch expensive infrastructure. Finding other users then expands its commercial options and reduces Nvidia’s remaining commitment through the disclosed mechanism. Machine utilization becomes one of the variables determining the chipmaker’s exposure.

The arrangement explains why management may scrutinize prices, cash collections and equipment usage. Reserved capacity helps establish a computing service. Its economic value then depends on finding uses with customers able to pay. A more selective approach would become measurable through newly signed terms and capacity actually sold.

## Management and the board retain distinct responsibilities

An organization remains in place after Huang’s departure. Nvidia’s governance policies, effective since **3 March 2025**, assign management responsibility for developing strategic and financial policies, running operations and managing risk. The board oversees decisions and directly supervises strategic risks. [S06](#source-S06)

The bylaws set out executive functions, delegation and the designation of people authorized to bind the company. They provide an institutional framework during the interim period. Individual investment authorizations and internal approval thresholds remain unavailable. [S07](#source-S07)

On **12 November**, the board said it expected operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue. That states its public direction. Execution of a particular deal depends on the contract’s terms and timetable. [S02](#source-S02)

A new transaction can require choices about the stake to buy, payment dates or the exposure accepted in return for a commercial opportunity. An existing agreement calls for examination of its obligations, closing conditions and amendment rights. Kress’s combined roles put those financial judgments at the centre of the transition, within the board oversight described by the governance documents.

## Nscale gives the funding question a date

Nscale’s latest prospectus illustrates the distinction. It describes an initial **$1 billion** Nvidia subscription within issued convertible notes and a further **$1 billion** expected **on or around 16 November**. At the SEC check on **16 November at 12:56:41 UTC**, or **13:56:41 in Paris**, its latest filing remained the prospectus dated **12 November**. No additional confirmation of closing or receipt was identified in the primary sources checked. [S05](#source-S05)

“On or around” allows timing flexibility. An issuer confirmation or subsequent filing would establish the next stage. Our [analysis of Nscale’s financing needs](/en/analysis/nvidia-fed-nscale-ai-guarantees-credit/) examines the orders and obligations that make this funding significant.

For Nvidia’s partners, the transition ultimately has to produce decisions they can use: money available on a given date, accepted terms and capacity they can deliver. The new leadership’s task is to choose transactions whose commercial success can justify Nvidia’s capital and commitments over time.
