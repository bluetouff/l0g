In Madrid, a developer can start certain works while the city is still examining the technical file. An initial permit settles the key planning requirements. The review continues alongside the work and, if the final permit is refused, the developer may have to reverse work already done. The arrangement raises a practical question. How much does an extra month cost when the land is already financed and the project is waiting to break ground? [S09](#source-S09)

On **16 November 2026**, the European Commission presented a study it commissioned from Intellera Consulting and Finland’s VTT research centre. It plans to use the findings in a housing simplification package due in the second quarter of 2027. Planning decisions remain largely in the hands of national and local authorities. The study identifies ways to coordinate approvals and give applicants a more predictable timetable. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02)

## The formal review starts with a complete file

Under the standard French rules, the review period is **two months** for a permit to build a single-family home and its ancillary structures and **three months** for other building permit applications. The period starts when the administration receives a complete application. Preparing the file, some exchanges with the planning authority and steps after the decision sit outside that period. Different deadlines can apply depending on the project and its location. [S06](#source-S06)

Applicants in the Commission’s study reported a median of **about 13 weeks** for formal review of a new building. For new buildings, the median for the full administrative process was **about 43 weeks**, including steps after the decision. Work can already be under way at that stage. The two medians cover different parts of a process that varies from one project to the next. [S01](#source-S01)

<HousingPermitsFigure lang="en" kind="timeline" number={1} />

Survey coverage is uneven. Portugal provided **137 of the 226 responses from authorities**, while Italy accounted for **88 of the 204 applicant responses**. The surveys give an insight into the participants’ experience. Ranking national performance would require comparable projects and a more balanced sample. For developers, uncertainty also matters. A late request for documents can disrupt a financing schedule or a booking with a contractor. [S01](#source-S01)

## Interest keeps accruing while a project waits

[Property carrying costs](/en/glossary/cout-de-portage/) start once capital is committed. Take a project that has already drawn **€1 million** at a hypothetical annual rate of **5%**. If the balance stays constant, six months cost **€25,000** in simple interest and a year costs **€50,000**. This illustration leaves out fees, repayments and compounding.

<HousingPermitsFigure lang="en" kind="carrying" number={2} />

Land, studies and deposits may all be financed long before a sale. Calculating the actual interest bill requires knowing how much the developer borrowed at each stage. Some of the Commission study’s simulations estimate the time saving using the eventual value of the project. Those estimates are a model of opportunity cost under assumed financing conditions. [S01](#source-S01)

For a project with little margin for error, a few more months of expenses can force a change in the financing plan. For lower costs to show up in home prices, developments still need to start, builders need to compete and homes need to reach the places where people want them. Where buildable land is scarce, some of the gain can end up in the land price. [S01](#source-S01) · [S12](#source-S12)

## Madrid lets part of the work start earlier

Madrid’s *licencia básica* has a statutory decision period of **one month**. Once granted, it allows the specified work to proceed while the city reviews the rest of the project. The developer bears the risk that the final licence will be refused. The reform lets work and assessment run in parallel while retaining checks before the building can be used. [S09](#source-S09)

For cases processed without a collaborating body, the city’s evaluation shows average processing time falling from **177 to 139 days** between the two cohorts it examined. The city attributes most of the fall to adding faster initial licences to the average. In the route involving a collaborating body, processing time rose from **40 to 49 days**. Following comparable projects through to completion would show how much time residents actually gained. [S10](#source-S10)

The Commission study also looks at how information moves between departments. A PDF can be filed remotely. A digital building model, known as BIM, makes the information reusable. Staff can check floor area and levels against one version of the project, provided that the model, applicable rules and public databases can work together. The study’s scenarios illustrate how much that might help under those conditions. [S01](#source-S01) · [S02](#source-S02)

## French permits and starts follow different timetables

From October 2025 to September 2026, France’s statistical service for sustainable development estimates that **377,834 dwellings were authorised** and **318,419 were started**, excluding Mayotte. The totals use monthly series adjusted for seasonal and working-day effects. A start may follow a permit issued before the period. Counting the projects still waiting would require tracking each permit through to the start of work or cancellation. [S04](#source-S04) · [S05](#source-S05) · [S13](#source-S13)

<HousingPermitsFigure lang="en" kind="france" number={3} />

In October, developers surveyed by Insee were more pessimistic about buyers’ access to finance and the outlook for housing starts. Across the euro area, the ECB reports that residential investment fell **1.3%** in the second quarter of 2026 from the previous quarter. Permits continued to recover, partly because permits had been registered early in the Netherlands ahead of a change in legislation. Buyer financing still matters after a project has permission to proceed. Our investigation into [how French mortgages are funded](/en/analysis/who-bought-4-billion-french-mortgages/) follows that side of the market. [S07](#source-S07) · [S11](#source-S11)

## Completion is the test that matters

In Auckland, Ryan Greenaway-McGrevy and Yun So estimate that, in 2024, rents on new private tenancies were **23% below the level they would have reached without the reform** in the authors’ preferred model. Observed rents rose over the period, and the result depends on the choice of comparison cities. Auckland had broadened building rights across large areas, changing the scope for new housing on a different scale. [S08](#source-S08)

Europe could measure the benefits of reform more directly by tracking each project from the complete application to completion. An initial licence shows when certain work can begin. The time to the start of construction, and then to occupancy, would show whether simpler procedures change the date a home becomes available. [S01](#source-S01) · [S13](#source-S13)
