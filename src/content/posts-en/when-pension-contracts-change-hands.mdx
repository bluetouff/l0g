*When investment firms buy insurers · 1/8 · The contract changes hands*

**In 2023, roughly 96,000 participants and beneficiaries in AT&T’s pension plan came under annuity contracts issued by Athene, part of Apollo. The transaction preserved their promised benefit amounts. Behind the payments, however, the debtor changed, along with the assets supporting the promise and the system protecting it. U.S. filings and a British precedent reveal how those changes work and where the public record ends.**

Starting with the August 2023 payment, two Athene insurance companies took responsibility for the benefits AT&T had transferred. The filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission names them: **Athene Annuity and Life Company** and **Athene Annuity & Life Assurance Company of New York**. Their commitments to pay the covered benefits are irrevocable. Approximately 96,000 plan participants and beneficiaries were included in the transaction. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

For someone receiving a pension, the amount and the reliability of the payment understandably come first. The right to receive it also rests on a legal entity, a contract and financial resources. All three can change while the recipient continues to collect the expected sum. Following asset managers into the insurance business therefore begins with a tangible claim: **which company can be required to pay, and what resources stand behind its obligation?**

AT&T’s transaction provides a first answer that can be checked against the documents. Prudential’s dealings with Rothesay in the United Kingdom then reveal a further complication: economic risk, legal responsibility and payment administration can move separately.

## Three dates in the transfer of a pension obligation

On April 26, 2023, AT&T and State Street Global Advisors Trust Company, acting as the plan’s independent fiduciary, entered into an agreement with Athene’s subsidiaries. The purchase of the group annuity contracts closed on May 3. The agreement assigned the August payments onward to the insurers. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

The benefits came from a **defined-benefit pension plan**, which owes payments calculated under the plan’s rules. Its investments are held in a trust to fund those entitlements. For the people covered by the transaction, single-premium group annuity contracts took over: assets were provided upfront in exchange for the insurers’ commitment to make the scheduled payments, including those due for life. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

This is a [*pension risk transfer*](/en/glossary/pension-risk-transfer/). The plan settles the benefits included in the transaction, and the insurer becomes responsible for the corresponding payments. U.S. Department of Labor guidance on selecting an insurer explicitly describes that substitution. AT&T’s transfer was partial; the plan retained other pension obligations. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

The recipient holds a right to benefits against the insurer. Ownership of the investments belongs to a separate legal relationship. The relevant contract or certificate identifies the issuing insurance company against which that right can be enforced. AT&T’s filing names two companies for the transaction as a whole without publishing the allocation of individual beneficiaries between them. Establishing any joint and several liability requires the contracts and guarantees applicable to each beneficiary. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup>

Acquisition announcements often blur the different roles within a group. The shareholder directs strategy and receives the returns on its investment. The insurer carries the contractual obligation. The asset manager selects investments under an agreed mandate. One organization can bring these activities together, but an annuitant’s rights depend on the entities and guarantees specified in the legal documents.

## The price of a retirement promise

AT&T’s final accounts give a more precise picture than the initial announcements. For 2023, the company recorded the settlement of **$8.067 billion in pension obligations** and the transfer of **$7.704 billion in plan assets**. It recognized a **pre-tax settlement gain of $363 million**. The purchase was funded from the plan’s assets, without an additional contribution by AT&T for the transaction. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup>

The first two figures measure different things. The pension obligation is a present value: expected future benefits are converted into an amount today, using financial and demographic assumptions. The transferred assets are the property delivered to purchase the contracts. The $363 million difference appears in AT&T’s earnings. Apollo’s fees and the amount of pension payments require different calculations. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup>

Why would an insurer agree to pay benefits whose value on the seller’s books exceeds the assets it receives? It prices the obligation using its own assumptions about investment returns, the timing of payments, life expectancy, expenses, required capital and expected profit. A pension plan’s accounting valuation and an insurer’s commercial price are shaped by different methods and constraints. The purchase price tells us something about the transaction without establishing its eventual profitability. That is why the rules governing insurer selection require scrutiny of investments, financial resources and contractual protections. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

A reconstruction of the revenues earned along the way would require the fees for selecting the insurer, investment management charges attributable to this portfolio, the cost of its reinsurance and results over subsequent years. **A revenue breakdown specific to the AT&T annuities remains to be obtained.**

## Following ownership back to Apollo

Athene became a subsidiary of Apollo Global Management through the merger effective January 1, 2022. The ultimate owner in this case is therefore the publicly traded Apollo group. The shorthand “fund” covers a corporate organization designed to last beyond the life of any one investment vehicle. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s27" aria-label="Source 27">27</a></sup>

Athene’s 2025 annual report shows the economic attraction of the arrangement. Apollo manages nearly all of Athene’s net invested assets, with compensation that depends in part on their composition and value. Investments in certain affiliated vehicles can carry additional fees. The report also acknowledges potential conflicts of interest. These disclosures describe relationships across the group; they do not provide a separate bill for managing AT&T’s pensions. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s26" aria-label="Source 26">26</a></sup>

Our [investigation of life insurers, private credit and Bermuda](/en/analysis/life-insurers-retirement-savings-private-credit-bermuda/) follows the financial organization of these groups. This article starts with the beneficiary’s rights. Integration brings premium income, investment management and the manager’s compensation into the same organization. A durable pool of assets can be directed toward financing opportunities the group knows how to originate or select. For the annuitant, the test is whether the resulting income can meet the promised benefits after fees and investment losses. Assessing that requires the portfolio’s assets, payment schedules and protections.

Athene itself draws a legal boundary in the notes to its retirement services website. Each insurance subsidiary is responsible for its own obligations; Apollo Global Management and Athene Holding do not assume those payment obligations. Published capital maintenance agreements provide another form of support to subsidiaries. An undertaking to supply capital under specified conditions gives different rights from a guarantee that each annuitant can enforce directly. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s26" aria-label="Source 26">26</a></sup>

Our [guide to reading a life insurer’s financial strength](/en/guides/read-life-insurer-health/) explains how to place each figure in context. That distinction matters when a group presents figures for equity, liquidity or assets under management. They describe the resources of an economic organization. Establishing which resources actually support a pension requires a return to the issuing company’s balance sheet, the assets assigned to the benefits and any contractual commitments to provide support.

## A separate account deserves a closer look

The AT&T portfolio includes a protection central to the analysis: a **separate account**. A report by U.S. Magistrate Judge Paul Levenson, dated August 29, 2025, identifies it in litigation brought by beneficiaries. The initial complaint had not adequately addressed that feature. The report nevertheless distinguishes the account’s existence from AT&T’s claim that approximately $8 billion was fully insulated from other liabilities. That broader claim had not been established on the merits at that stage. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s06" aria-label="Source 6">6</a></sup>

A separate account can reserve assets for specified obligations and protect them from creditors of other parts of the business. Its precise effect depends on the governing law and the contracts. The Iowa insurer’s 2025 statutory accounts describe its group annuity separate accounts as legally insulated from the general account. The general account must still support the contractual payments if the separate account falls short. The New York subsidiary’s accounts describe a similar arrangement for its group annuities. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s08" aria-label="Source 8">8</a></sup>

This structure adds protection for the assets and another source of support for the payments. The assets themselves still carry risk: a borrower can miss a payment, a loan can lose value, and an investment can prove difficult to sell. An assessment specific to AT&T would require the full contract, its provisions assigning assets to the benefits, and a current investment inventory. The insurer’s financial statements aggregate several transactions and cannot replace that examination.

The litigation illustrates the distinction. **On September 28, 2026**, the federal court in Massachusetts dismissed the claims against AT&T while allowing a claim for breach of the duty of prudence to proceed against State Street and the executive named alongside it. Relative risk and the protection afforded by the separate account remained disputed. In deciding the motion to dismiss, the judge treated the plaintiffs’ factual allegations as true, with their merits still to be tested against the evidence. The decision was neither a judgment against Athene nor a finding that it had failed to pay benefits. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

The obligation to pay the pension and the duty to select the insurer prudently consequently follow separate legal paths. Department of Labor guidance requires the selection process to consider investment diversification, capital, the size of the transaction and available guarantees. Credit ratings form only one part of that assessment. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

## The protection system changes as well

The Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation, the U.S. federal pension insurance agency, states that its protection ends for benefits covered by irrevocable commitments purchased from an insurer. After a transfer, state insurance guaranty arrangements become relevant, with their own eligibility conditions and limits. Changing the company responsible for the payments also changes the system protecting them. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

Comparing the two systems requires more than placing two coverage limits side by side. Depending on the arrangement, a limit may apply to periodic benefits or to the present value of future entitlements; age, the form of the annuity and jurisdiction also matter. The available record does not establish the protection applicable to each of the roughly 96,000 people covered. AT&T’s filings state that benefit amounts would remain unchanged. Demonstrating equivalent protection for every individual would require further work. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a>, <a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

The United Kingdom offers a useful comparison. An eligible annuity provided under a long-term insurance contract can qualify for Financial Services Compensation Scheme protection at **100%, with no cap**. An occupational pension scheme itself operates under a different framework, potentially involving the Pension Protection Fund. The presence of an overseas reinsurer behind a British insurer therefore does not, by itself, mean the policyholder has lost the protection attached to the insurance contract. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s23" aria-label="Source 23">23</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s24" aria-label="Source 24">24</a></sup>

## At Prudential, risk moved before the policies

A British case shows how these distinctions unfold over time. On March 14, 2018, The Prudential Assurance Company Limited, known as PAC, entered into both a business transfer agreement and a collateralized reinsurance agreement with Rothesay Life plc. Assets moved to Rothesay, together with much of the portfolio’s economic risk and reward. **The contractual obligations to policyholders remained with PAC.** The Court of Appeal explained the position in its December 2, 2020 judgment. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s11" aria-label="Source 11">11</a></sup>

The distinction became particularly clear in August 2019, when the High Court initially refused the legal transfer while the reinsurance stayed in place. The companies had already reorganized the portfolio’s financing; changing the direct insurer still required judicial approval. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup>

Following the appeal and final court approval on November 24, 2021, the transfer took effect on **December 15, 2021**. Rothesay became responsible for approximately **350,000 policies**. Around **33,000 others** remained with Prudential and continued to be reinsured. Prudential also temporarily administered the transferred policies on Rothesay’s behalf. These figures describe the final position reported for 2021 and are not directly comparable with the counts announced when the transaction began. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a></sup>

The Rothesay sequence is historical: Blackstone sold its stake on December 1, 2020, before the legal policy transfer in December 2021. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s30" aria-label="Source 30">30</a></sup>

The company handling an inquiry or administering a payment is therefore not necessarily the company that legally owes the money. Ownership can also change without replacing the insurer named in the contract. Athora’s acquisition of Pension Insurance Corporation Group, completed on March 27, 2026, provides a current example: PIC says the legal entity insuring its policyholders’ pensions remained the same after the change of shareholder. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15" aria-label="Source 15">15</a></sup>

Athora should also be distinguished from Athene. Athora reports that Apollo, including Athene, held a 24.51% economic interest as of December 31, 2025, and describes its investment management relationships with Apollo. That dated ownership figure does not make Athora a wholly owned subsidiary or establish that any particular PIC policy has been reinsured. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s16" aria-label="Source 16">16</a></sup>

Even the point at which insurance enters a British pension arrangement matters. In a [*buy-in*](/en/glossary/buy-in-buy-out/), a scheme purchases a policy to fund benefits, while its trustees remain responsible to members. In a *buy-out*, the insurer assumes direct responsibility for the covered pensions. The Pensions Regulator describes both structures: an announcement that a scheme has reached an agreement with an insurer can refer to materially different legal arrangements. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s17" aria-label="Source 17">17</a></sup>

## Reinsurance adds a second contract

Bermuda draws attention because these arrangements can combine international transfers, different regulatory frameworks and affiliated companies. The first task is to identify the agreement itself. In an ordinary reinsurance arrangement of this kind, the insurer retains its obligation to the annuitant and acquires a claim against a reinsurer for agreed payments. The contract between insurance companies supports the annuity contract. The Prudential Regulation Authority explicitly states that insurers remain responsible for their promises to annuitants under both longevity and funded reinsurance. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source 18">18</a></sup>

With [**funded reinsurance**](/en/glossary/reassurance-adossee-a-l-actif/), an agreement may cover both investment risk and the cost of paying benefits for longer than expected. An upfront premium funds the obligations assumed, while collateral arrangements protect the ceding insurer if its counterparty fails to perform. The legal ownership of the assets, their custody and the allocation of their economic returns can differ across structures. Saying that “the money has gone to Bermuda” leaves those essential questions unanswered. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s19" aria-label="Source 19">19</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s20" aria-label="Source 20">20</a></sup>

Athene Annuity and Life Company’s accounts do describe a treaty with **Athene Annuity Re Ltd** in Bermuda, effective January 1, 2023, covering an agreed proportion of certain new group annuity business. They also describe the insurer’s continuing responsibility to its customers if the reinsurer does not perform. **The published treaty disclosure covers a category of group-annuity business.** An allocation of AT&T’s portfolio, assets or liabilities to that treaty remains to be established from the documents reviewed. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

When an arrangement breaks down, [*recapture*](/en/glossary/recapture/) may follow: the insurer takes back risks it previously ceded and recovers assets under the contractual terms. Solvency triggers or other breaches can activate this process before insolvency. Throughout, the obligation to the policyholder has remained with the insurer. The additional challenge is to assemble the resources, eligible assets and liquidity needed to keep honoring that obligation. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source 18">18</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s20" aria-label="Source 20">20</a></sup>

## The economic case remains open

There is a sound economic reason to match some annuity liabilities with investments that are difficult to sell. Predictable payments allow an investor to hold assets for long periods and collect their income without relying on a quick sale. Corporate and infrastructure financing can benefit from that horizon. The PRA recognizes the potential advantages while requiring insurers to examine concentrations and the quality of the match between assets and liabilities. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source 18">18</a></sup>

This [asset-liability management, or ALM](/en/glossary/alm/), matches expected resources to promised payments. Research by Ralf Meisenzahl, Jackson Overpeck and Andy Polacek at the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago, revised on **April 27, 2026**, finds evidence of better matching of maturities and cash flows. Its scope is specific: the results are concentrated in *indexed annuities*, U.S. savings products whose returns depend partly on market indexes. They cannot be used to establish the quality of the assets supporting the lifetime benefits transferred by AT&T. The authors also identify liquidity risks and growing financial interconnectedness. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s22" aria-label="Source 22">22</a></sup>

British regulators are examining a separate issue: the incentives created by their rules. In **CP8/26, published on April 29, 2026**, the PRA proposed changing the valuation adjustment applied to funded reinsurance claims to better reflect counterparty risk and bring their treatment closer to that of economically similar assets. The consultation closed on July 31, with a proposed implementation date of July 1, 2027. **The PRA pages reviewed on October 23, 2026 still present CP8/26 as a consultation.** <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s19" aria-label="Source 19">19</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s28" aria-label="Source 28">28</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s29" aria-label="Source 29">29</a></sup>

The available stress tests also call for a measured interpretation. In the exercise published in November 2025, using end-2024 balance sheets, the PRA found that the participating insurers could absorb the recapture assumed in its exploratory scenario. It nevertheless reported a material effect on solvency and highlighted the exercise’s limitations. The result indicates capacity to withstand that particular scenario; exposures and collateral still need to be examined as they evolve. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s21" aria-label="Source 21">21</a></sup>

## The company that must pay in twenty years

The documents answer the first question in this series. For the transferred AT&T benefits, payment is owed by the issuing insurer identified in the applicable contract. Apollo controls the corporate group. An asset manager earns income from investing its assets. A reinsurer may assume some of the risks. These relationships help determine the insurer’s ability to pay while leaving each party with its own responsibilities.

The next task is to examine the resources sustaining that ability: asset quality, fees, available capital, guarantees and the terms on which ceded risks can return. For AT&T, a complete reconstruction would require the full group annuity contracts, the separate account’s investment inventory and the reinsurance treaties attributable to the portfolio. An asset manager’s place in the ownership chart does not, on its own, settle whether the arrangement is efficient or fragile.

The legal principle has an explicit counterpart in France: in the version dated June 11, 2026 consulted on Légifrance, Article L.111-3 of the Insurance Code preserves an insurer’s responsibility to its policyholder when it buys reinsurance. Products, pension arrangements and guarantee systems must then be compared within their respective legal frameworks. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s25" aria-label="Source 25">25</a></sup> For someone who will still be collecting a pension in twenty years, the practical question remains the one that began this investigation: which company owes the money, and which resources will it actually be able to draw on when payment falls due?
