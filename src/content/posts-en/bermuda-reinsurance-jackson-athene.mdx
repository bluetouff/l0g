*When investment firms buy insurers · Article 3/8 · The Bermuda connection*

**In 2020, Jackson reinsured the risks on a portfolio of American savings and retirement contracts with Athene Life Re. Jackson retained the investments, the Bermuda reinsurer received their economic return, and Apollo took on their management. The published treaty, its public amendment and recent financial statements trace that division of responsibilities through to the collateral and the terms on which Jackson can take the risks back.**

The [previous investigation into insurer acquisitions](/en/analysis/why-investment-firms-buy-insurers/) examines the rights acquired by Apollo, KKR, Brookfield and Blackstone. This third article follows one reinsurance treaty through to the assets and collateral.

On 18 June 2020, Prudential announced that Jackson National Life Insurance Company would reinsure a portfolio with Athene Life Re in Bermuda. The announcement described **$27.6 billion of liabilities and $28.2 billion of assets**, measured under IFRS at the end of the previous March. The geography of the transaction needs a little more explanation than those figures suggest: under the treaty, Jackson retained ownership of the investments backing the reinsured liabilities. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a>, <a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Customers still held their contracts with Jackson, an insurer domiciled in Michigan. Athene Life Re, or ALRe, undertook obligations to Jackson under a second contract. The treatment of benefits, investment returns and periodic settlements changed between the two companies, while the insurer owing money to the customer remained the same. Jackson's financial statements at 30 June 2026 continue to describe this arrangement. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

That distinction offers a way into understanding reinsurance in Bermuda. Assessing the protection behind an insurance contract requires following three things together: the obligation to pay, ownership of the securities and entitlement to their returns. In the Jackson–Athene transaction, those three lines separate, then meet again in the provisions governing collateral and exit.

## Two contracts behind each benefit payment

The treaty was signed on 18 June 2020 and took economic effect on **1 June 2020**. It covers an existing block of fixed annuities and fixed-index annuities: American savings and retirement products that provide a guaranteed interest rate or credit interest under a formula linked to an index. It also provides for contracts that will later be converted into an income stream, including the supplementary contract issued at that stage. The block therefore follows savings through to their payout phase. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a>, <a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup>

The reinsurance covers **100% of the liabilities within the treaty's defined scope**. Jackson continues to administer the policies and pay benefits, while ALRe owes Jackson the amounts specified in the agreement. Section 18.06 confines rights under the treaty to the parties and their permitted successors. Policyholders therefore retain their claim against Jackson; the reinsurance agreement itself gives them no direct contractual claim against ALRe. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

The International Association of Insurance Supervisors, or IAIS, describes the general mechanism in the same terms: reinsurance transfers risk economically while leaving the insurer's obligations to its customers in place. In exchange for the protection it buys, the original insurer becomes exposed to its reinsurer's ability to perform. This is **counterparty risk**: a claim on another company brings the possibility of late payment, a dispute over the amount due or insufficient resources to pay. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s20" aria-label="Source 20">20</a></sup>

The word *coinsurance* in this document refers to a form of proportional reinsurance. The treaty combines a funds-withheld component with a coinsurance component. The central mechanism is easier to see by looking at the **[funds withheld](/en/glossary/funds-withheld/)**: the assets allocated to the portfolio remain Jackson's property, while ALRe receives their economic performance under the agreed rules. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

## Keeping the securities, passing on the return

Section 6.02 provides for two separate custody accounts at **The Bank of New York Mellon**. Investments are deposited in those accounts or, for certain categories specified in the agreement, allocated to them. Jackson must retain ownership and control. Alongside those assets, it records a funds-withheld liability to the reinsurer, which changes with the return on the underlying investments. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

A bond held under this arrangement can continue to pay its coupons into an account associated with Jackson. Yet the treaty allocates the benefit or cost of its performance to the reinsurer: income, gains, losses, defaults and impairments. The accounting makes this separation visible. Jackson keeps the investments on its balance sheet and recognises the corresponding effects in its liability to ALRe, using the methods explained in its reinsurance note. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a>, <a href="#source-s08" aria-label="Source 8">8</a></sup>

The cash needed to service the policies follows a related path. Jackson can withdraw cash from the account to pay benefits or reimburse itself for benefits already paid. The companies then reconcile the quarter's movements and settle the balance required by the treaty. Adjustments reflect changes in reserves, payments and investment performance, among other items. A net amount can therefore pass between the companies while the bulk of the portfolio remains in the custody accounts. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Protection also depends on how the assets are set aside. In its June 2026 financial statements, Jackson says that investments in the segregated account are available exclusively for policies covered by the agreement and cannot be used to settle its other obligations to policyholders or general creditors. This describes an important protection for the reinsured block. Establishing how it would work in an insolvency also requires examining the security documents and applicable law. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

Legal ownership gives Jackson a practical advantage: much of the financial backing is already under its control. The quality of the securities, their value when needed and their ability to generate cash then determine how much economic protection that ownership provides.

## Apollo invests within an agreed mandate

The contract names **Apollo Insurance Solutions Group LP** as the account's initial investment manager. Jackson appoints it, but the agreement with ALRe constrains Jackson's freedom to change the mandate or investment limits. Section 6.02 requires the reinsurer's prior consent in specified circumstances and sets out a particular replacement procedure, including when a material breach of law threatens Jackson or the account, or in cases of fraud, gross negligence or wilful misconduct in performing the mandate. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Here the distinction between owning the securities and managing them becomes especially useful. Jackson owns the assets, Apollo holds the investment mandate, and ALRe bears the agreed economic result. Under the treaty, the reinsurer bears the investment manager's and custodian's fees. ALRe consequently has a direct interest in the return earned after those charges and in how well it matches the benefits to be financed. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

At 30 June 2026, Jackson reported that Apollo managed **$10.5 billion of cash and investments** under this relationship. The same report disclosed **$11.9 billion of contract liabilities reinsured with Athene**. These figures use different definitions and valuation methods. Assessing the backing for the liabilities requires reconciling all the treaty's components and their accounting treatment, including the financial hedges. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

Those hedges serve a specific purpose. Where a contract credits interest by reference to an index, movements in that index can change the amounts owed. Section 6.01 makes the reinsurer responsible for hedging this risk and provides, subject to specified conditions, for rights over derivatives and their collateral to be assigned for Jackson's benefit. Protection therefore includes instruments whose maturity dates and exercise terms matter alongside their reported value. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Jackson also received compensation at inception. Its 2025 statutory accounts record a **$1,196.3 million ceding commission** associated with the 2020 agreement. That payment forms part of the transaction's initial economics; Apollo's management charges follow a separate agreement. The detailed investment management agreement appears among the exhibits identified but omitted from the public filing reviewed here, preventing a reconstruction of its precise fee schedule. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s08" aria-label="Source 8">8</a>, <a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

The audited accounts of **Athene Co-Invest Reinsurance Affiliate 1A Ltd**, or ACRA 1A, reveal another stage in the transaction. They state that, effective 1 June 2020, ACRA 1A entered into a ModCo agreement with ALRe covering **100% of the risks ALRe had assumed on the Jackson block**. Reinsurance purchased by a reinsurer is called *retrocession*. It adds another claim to ALRe's side of the chain, while ALRe's commitment to Jackson remains governed by the original treaty. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s25" aria-label="Source 25">25</a>, <a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

This second transaction shows how a co-investment platform can work: partners' capital supports the risk behind the reinsurer that signed the original agreement. The Jackson treaty permits retrocessions to affiliates without applying the volume restriction imposed on transactions with outside parties. ACRA's accounts document this stage in 2020. The detailed retrocession treaty was not among the documents obtained, limiting what can be established about its own collateral and exit terms. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s25" aria-label="Source 25">25</a></sup>

## Each form of collateral has its own conditions

The funds-withheld account provides the first layer of protection. ALRe also supplies additional assets or commitments for Jackson's benefit. At **30 June 2026**, Jackson reported **$1.1 billion in letters of credit** and a **trust with a carrying value of approximately $57 million** supporting obligations under the treaty. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

A trust sets assets aside for a specified purpose under the responsibility of a trustee. A letter of credit commits a bank to pay when a demand meets the agreed conditions. The trust thus relies on assets already placed in a collateral arrangement; the letter of credit adds a bank's payment commitment. To establish how either can be used, the relevant withdrawal rights, required documents and deadlines must be checked. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s17" aria-label="Source 17">17</a></sup>

The treaty also establishes an order for using the collateral. Section 9.05 permits a draw on a letter of credit when non-payment continues for ten calendar days after notice and the resources of the overcollateralisation account, measured under the contract's rules, have been exhausted. That sequence explains why a reported collateral amount and immediately available cash answer different questions. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

The treaty details permitted uses of withdrawals, rules for substituting assets and adjustments to an overcollateralisation account called the *OC Account*. It defines a required balance through a formula that takes several parts of the arrangement into account. A first amendment, signed on **30 September 2020**, added a component for excess derivative collateral to that formula and replaced the relevant investment guidelines. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a></sup>

These adjustments help explain why simply adding the published amounts would give an incomplete picture. Letters of credit represent a payment commitment that may be drawn upon, the trust is reported at carrying value, and the managed portfolio contains assets with different characteristics. A meaningful reconciliation requires the values specified by the contract, the resources actually available and the obligations they cover, all measured at the same date.

The Bermuda Monetary Authority, or BMA, itself emphasises asset quality, concentration limits and the ceding insurer's ability to manage a portfolio after recapture. Illiquid securities or structures that are difficult to value can complicate the use of collateral even where the legal right to access it is established. That makes the investment guidelines a central document in the investigation. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s17" aria-label="Source 17">17</a></sup>

## The treaty allows several stages of intervention

Jackson has rights designed to strengthen its protection before a possible full recapture. The treaty tracks two measures of the reinsurer's capital: an **ECR ratio**, comparing available statutory economic capital with the relevant Bermuda requirement, and an **RBC ratio**, calculated for ALRe using Athene's published methodology referenced in the agreement, on the US *company action level* basis. Each has its own definition, and their percentages belong on different scales. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Under the published terms, an ECR ratio below **135%** or an RBC ratio below **250%**, recorded at the end of a monitoring period and persisting at the end of the following quarter, triggers stronger protections. If the statutory book value of permitted assets in the OC Account is then below the balance required by the contract, ALRe must make up that shortfall within **five business days** of the trigger. The amount due therefore depends on the shortfall at that point. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

A further threshold gives Jackson the option of **[recapture](/en/glossary/recapture/)**, taking back the economic risks it previously reinsured: an ECR ratio below **120%** or an RBC ratio below **200%**, subject to the same persistence condition. The reinsurer's insolvency, certain failures to pay and other events defined in the treaty can also establish this right. These thresholds come from the published contract, which includes a provision for adapting them if the calculation frameworks change. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Jackson can then decide whether to exercise its right. If it proceeds under section 13.03, recapture covers **the whole block**, with closing scheduled between **five and thirty business days after notice**. The treaty then governs the closing accounts and settlement between the companies. The right to take the risks back and the work required to complete that settlement are distinct stages. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

At settlement, the treaty compares the statutory book value of the retained assets with an exit amount derived from reserves and specified adjustments, including the unamortised ceding commission. ALRe must make up a shortfall with cash or permitted investments valued at fair value; any surplus must be returned to it. The funds-withheld account then ends, supplementary collateral is released under the settlement provisions, and the accounts remain subject to a further adjustment. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

From the policyholder's perspective, Jackson already owed the benefits. Recapture chiefly increases the economic risks Jackson must carry directly and the resources it must devote to managing them. It can retain the assets already in its possession while having to reconsider the hedges, investments, capital and liquidity needed to support the policies. The quality of that preparation determines the practical value of the exit provision. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s20" aria-label="Source 20">20</a>, <a href="#source-s14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a></sup>

## Bermuda brings together several forms of reinsurance

The Jackson structure belongs to a well-documented family of arrangements. In its report published in September 2025, the BMA examined **657 treaty records submitted by 86 entities**, generally using year-end 2024 balance sheets, with a few different reporting dates. Some records combine several small treaties. Funds-withheld and *[modified coinsurance](/en/glossary/modco/)*, or ModCo, arrangements together accounted for **$429.8 billion of reserves out of a published total of $601.3 billion**, approximately **71.5%** by our calculation. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s21" aria-label="Source 21">21</a></sup>

Both structures leave the assets with the ceding insurer; under the mechanism described by the BMA, ModCo also leaves the reserves there. Their combined share of reserves is much greater than their **33.3%** share of the treaty records. Pure coinsurance shows the reverse pattern: about **49.6%** of records but **9.2%** of reserves. The report explains this partly by the inclusion of traditional mortality and longevity covers, which require fewer backing assets. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s21" aria-label="Source 21">21</a>, <a href="#source-s17" aria-label="Source 17">17</a></sup>

These figures direct attention towards rights over assets and the conventions used to value them. They measure reinsurance reserves within a defined data collection; they do not locate every security held across the companies concerned. The method below the figure sets out the numerators, denominators and rounding difference in the original table so that the percentages can be reproduced. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s21" aria-label="Source 21">21</a></sup>

The public treaty between American General Life and Fortitude Re provides a useful comparison. Its 2020 version also leaves assets with the ceding insurer, in this case in a ModCo account. Several financial thresholds are redacted. The broad structure can be compared, while the precise recapture conditions must be established separately for each agreement. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s26" aria-label="Source 26">26</a></sup>

## Assessing the protection also means finding the missing documents

The strongest argument for these arrangements lies in their financing capacity. A specialist company can provide capital, organise the management of long-term investments and share risk with other investors. In the June 2020 Jackson announcement, Athene described financing through ACRA, its co-investment platform. The capital to be deployed was announced as **63% funded by third-party investors and 37% by Athene**. This was the stated funding split for the transaction and its various components. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a>, <a href="#source-s04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

The authors of a Bank for International Settlements paper describe several possible motives for funded reinsurance: pooling management resources, accessing capital, drawing on investment expertise, or benefiting from differences in tax and prudential regimes across jurisdictions. These motives can coexist. Determining their relative contribution to the eventual return on the Jackson treaty would require a breakdown that the public documents assembled here do not provide. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s30" aria-label="Source 30">30</a></sup>

Supervisors now scrutinise these relationships closely. An April 2025 BMA notice describes prior approval for block transactions by the commercial long-term reinsurers concerned, a process in place since January 2023. The review covers contracts, collateral, investment mandates and a reconciliation of asset requirements across jurisdictions. This procedure covers new block transactions and amendments to existing blocks; its timetable must be distinguished from the signing of the Jackson deal in 2020. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s19" aria-label="Source 19">19</a></sup>

In the United Kingdom, the PRA expects insurers to have an executable recapture plan setting out legal steps, asset movements and the ability to manage the recovered portfolio. Its consultation of **29 April 2026** also proposes revising the treatment of the claim on the reinsurer. The consultation closed on **31 July 2026**; as of this investigation's cut-off date, I found no final decision in the official sources consulted. This British work helps explain the risk under examination while remaining separate from the framework governing the American contract. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a>, <a href="#source-s12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup>

For Jackson–Athene, the available documents establish the division of responsibilities, the existence of the reported collateral and the main recapture conditions in the published text. The **complete investment guidelines, trust agreement, investment management agreement and detailed collateral statements at a common date** would allow the investigation to go further. Several exhibits were expressly omitted from the public filing, and the September 2020 amendment shows that some have changed. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-s05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a>, <a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup>

Following the savings therefore means following a chain of rights. Jackson remains responsible to the policyholder, ALRe commits to Jackson, Apollo manages investments, and collateral supports that second promise. Assessing the strength of the arrangement requires reading the place of incorporation alongside the contract, the composition of the assets and the practical ability to use them when payment falls due.
