The first shipment of 250 Argentine gold bars reached the Bank of England on June 21, 2024. Each bar had been weighed, photographed and recorded by serial number. The custodian accepted 248. They showed up on Argentina’s statement as **99,496.615 fine troy ounces**. On its account with the BIS, the central bank now had a claim to a quantity of gold. A serial number would no longer help it claim a particular bar. [S01](#source-S01)

There were four shipments in all, carrying 1,000 bars. A [performance audit approved on November 26, 2026](https://agn.ar/informes/Informe-220-2026) traces the metal to Argentina’s account with the Bank for International Settlements, or BIS. Using the ledger, it also reconstructs a derivatives loss of just over a billion dollars in 2025. To understand the bill, start with the reason the bars left Buenos Aires. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03)

## A vault in London and an account with the BIS

On March 15, 2024, the reserves department wrote that keeping some gold abroad could help it raise dollars faster and at lower cost. It also saw scope to earn a return. The backdrop mattered. At the end of May, the [IMF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2024/english/1argea2024002.pdf) put gross international reserves at $29.2 billion and net reserves, after its chosen deductions, at about $300 million. That narrow margin helps explain the appeal of gold the bank could put to use more quickly. [S01](#source-S01) [S08](#source-S08)

The Bank of England received the metal as custodian for the BIS and holds identified bars in its vaults. Argentina’s [unallocated gold](/en/glossary/or-non-alloue/) account with the BIS gives it a right to a quantity of metal under the terms of the account. IMF rules allow such a claim to count as monetary gold when it meets the conditions on delivery and availability. The price of gold still matters to Argentina. Access to the metal depends on the account and the counterparty. [S01](#source-S01) [S09](#source-S09) [S10](#source-S10)

<ArgentinaGoldFigure lang="en" kind="custody" />

London rejected bars with cracks, cavities or imperfect stamps. In all, 115 had to be recast. Argentina’s public audit office, the AGN, put the economic cost at $487,185, including shipping, insurance, recasting and the value of metal lost in the process. After the shipments, **31.6% of the bank’s ounces** sat in the BIS account, up from **11.3%** before the first delivery. Its total gold holdings stood at about 61.7 tonnes at the end of both 2024 and 2025. The form of part of the holding had changed. [S01](#source-S01) [S04](#source-S04) [S07](#source-S07)

In September 2026, Argentina’s presidential spokesperson said the gold was in Switzerland, citing the central bank governor. The audit traces the bars to London in 2024 and records the credit to the BIS account. Establishing where the bars are held on November 27, 2026 would take a recent custody record. [S01](#source-S01) [S17](#source-S17)

## A price floor that costs money when gold rises

By autumn 2024, the central bank was also looking to protect the value of its gold. Its [monetary policy report](https://www.bcra.gob.ar/publicaciones/informe-de-politica-monetaria-ipom-tercer-trimestre-de-2026/) explains how it buys a put option to establish a floor. It can finance that protection by selling an option promising the counterparty any rise above a cap. This is a [collar](/en/glossary/collar/). When the premiums offset one another at signing, the structure has zero upfront cost. The cash settlement still depends on the price of gold. [S01](#source-S01) [S06](#source-S06)

<ArgentinaGoldFigure lang="en" kind="collar" />

Take an illustrative ounce whose price rises beyond the cap. The central bank gains on the metal. For the amount hedged, it gives up each dollar of upside beyond the cap through the option it sold. The metal can stay in a vault while a cash payment falls due. In 2025, auditors observed dollar settlements. Net exposure on the options reached 1.792 million ounces at one point, well above the roughly 626,432 ounces in the BIS account. The amount under option therefore exceeded the balance on the foreign account. [S01](#source-S01) [S03](#source-S03) [S06](#source-S06)

## A billion-dollar derivatives loss

The AGN calculates a **realised loss of $1.023 billion** on gold derivatives in 2025, including premiums. Its financial audit attributes $960.2 million to negative options results and $62.8 million to premiums. The performance audit breaks down the same loss by strategy and counterparty. The BIS and Deutsche Bank appear in the ledger. It records the outcome for Argentina’s central bank. Any profit for either counterparty also depends on its own trades. [S01](#source-S01) [S03](#source-S03) [S04](#source-S04)

<ArgentinaGoldFigure lang="en" kind="payments" />

For the same year, the central bank [reported a $2.505 billion net gain](https://www.bcra.gob.ar/noticias/resultado-neto-positivo-oro-2025/) on its entire gold position, including hedges. The gold rose in value while the options lost money. The precise reconciliation is harder. The difference between the year-end gold balances, net of provisions, was about $3.380 billion. Subtracting the derivatives loss leaves about $2.357 billion. That compares two balance-sheet values. Annual earnings can use different valuation and currency conversion conventions. A schedule linking those conventions would be needed to reconcile the two dollar figures. [S01](#source-S01) [S04](#source-S04) [S05](#source-S05)

## The terms the auditors could not read

The shipments were insured. When auditors asked to see the full policy, they received a letter from the broker. The bank points to the carrier’s umbrella policy. The AGN says the letter leaves practical questions open, including who would receive any payout and on what terms. It asks for a full certified copy before future shipments. A record of the premium payment reveals much less than the contract behind it. [S01](#source-S01)

The central bank says the derivatives settled in foreign currency and it did not pledge its gold under the contracts. The AGN verified settlements, while the contracts themselves were unavailable for checking counterparties’ rights. In a separate case, on December 19, 2025, the federal administrative court ordered the bank to provide information requested by the banking union or justify each refusal. The bank told Congress that its extraordinary appeal was admitted for Supreme Court review on April 1, 2026. That proceeding concerns access to the records. [S01](#source-S01) [S11](#source-S11) [S12](#source-S12)

The vote comes amid a slowdown. Argentina’s statistics office, [INDEC](https://www.indec.gob.ar/Nivel4/Tema/3/9/47), recorded a 0.6% fall in GDP in the second quarter of 2026 from the previous quarter. Our average of its [seasonally adjusted monthly activity index](https://www.indec.gob.ar/uploads/informesdeprensa/emae_11_26C3D902DB86.pdf) fell about 1.8% in the third quarter. It is an indication of activity ahead of the GDP release scheduled for December 16. The shipment dates to the 2024 contraction, amid the [external financing pressures documented by the IMF](/en/analysis/argentina-the-imf-largest-ever-exposure/). [S08](#source-S08) [S13](#source-S13) [S14](#source-S14) [S15](#source-S15) [S16](#source-S16)

The records trace the rejected bars, their weight and the dollars the bank paid. Judging the commitments takes the terms that set the price of protection, the dates cash was due and the rights that come with gold held on account. Those are the terms that matter when reserves have to be turned into spendable dollars in a hurry. [S01](#source-S01) [S03](#source-S03)
