**The economic cost of a backdoor · Part 4 of 7**

An encryption option becomes unavailable in one country. Elsewhere, an account’s region determines the provider and the contract. Following Apple’s documents and French cloud rules reveals the value of privacy guarantees and the work involved in changing services.

The same service name can cover two different promises of confidentiality. Apple’s documentation, consulted on November 7, 2026, still states that users in the United Kingdom who do not already have Advanced Data Protection for iCloud enabled can no longer turn it on. Known as ADP, this option extends end-to-end encryption to categories such as backups, photos and files. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q01" aria-label="Source Q01">Q01</a></sup>

The customer can continue using iCloud. Yet one feature of the product available to them has changed: the ability to entrust more content to the service while keeping access to the keys on trusted devices. The economic effect begins here, in the quality of the available service. It may then affect a confidentiality commitment, a purchasing decision or a migration project. That chain depends on how the service is used; the sources assembled here cannot put a general price on it. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q01" aria-label="Source Q01">Q01</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q02" aria-label="Source Q02">Q02</a></sup>

This fourth installment traces how a change in protection can affect the customer’s costs. Apple’s case illustrates the constraints a U.S. provider faces abroad. The Chinese documents show how an account’s region can change the operator and the contract. French and European rules then help explain how a technical safeguard becomes a purchasing requirement and a practical undertaking when leaving a service.

## Following the keys

A decryption key is used to turn encrypted content back into readable data. Assessing the protection means identifying the people and systems able to use that key. Apple describes two configurations. Under standard data protection, the categories concerned are encrypted in transit and at rest, with keys held in Apple’s data centers. The provider can therefore help recover them. With ADP, trusted devices retain sole access to the keys for the majority of the data. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q02" aria-label="Source Q02">Q02</a></sup>

The UK restriction concerns that extension. In its UK notice, Apple distinguishes **15 categories protected by end-to-end encryption by default**, which retain that protection, from **10 additional categories** for which new activation of ADP is unavailable. These figures count categories: each square in the diagram represents a type of data, regardless of its size. iMessage and FaceTime also remain end-to-end encrypted. For existing ADP users, Apple describes a process requiring users to disable the feature themselves and says it cannot do so automatically. The actual status of all those accounts is unknown. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q01" aria-label="Source Q01">Q01</a></sup>

<EncryptionBordersFigure lang="en" kind="categories" />

This distinction may matter to someone storing sensitive documents. If their requirement is to restrict reading to their devices, they need to examine the protection of the category they use, the recovery arrangements and any copies. Shared content and some metadata have their own rules. The choice turns on that level of detail, well beyond the padlock displayed on a screen. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q02" aria-label="Source Q02">Q02</a></sup>

The comparison also needs the right scope. The iCloud terms exclude Managed Apple Accounts from eligibility for ADP. The British case illustrates a change in the offering for eligible accounts; applying it to a business infrastructure requires a separate examination of the service and contract involved. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q03" aria-label="Source Q03">Q03</a></sup>

## A visible product change, a partly secret dispute

The public evidence serves different purposes. The Investigatory Powers Tribunal’s judgment of **April 7, 2025** establishes that Apple brought proceedings concerning the powers to issue *Technical Capability Notices*, orders relating to technical capabilities. It addresses whether the litigation should be public. Paragraph 20 states that the tribunal neither confirms nor denies the accuracy of press reports about the demand made of Apple. The full text of a notice is therefore a different document from the public judgment on secrecy surrounding the proceedings. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q04" aria-label="Source Q04">Q04</a></sup>

The litigation continued in 2026. In submissions signed on **September 11**, Privacy International and Liberty state that Apple filed a second claim on **July 13, 2026**. Liberty reports that a case management hearing took place on **September 17**. These documents come from claimants in the proceedings: they describe their actions and arguments. Their account of a revised demand remains subject to the British government’s position of neither confirming nor denying it. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q05" aria-label="Source Q05">Q05</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q06" aria-label="Source Q06">Q06</a></sup>

In the United States, Ron Wyden and Warren Davidson called for greater transparency. Their **September 11, 2026** press release describes the full scope of the current demand as unknown to the public and to Congress. This is an attributed congressional position, distinct from a judicial finding. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q07" aria-label="Source Q07">Q07</a></sup>

For the economic analysis, the observable effect is narrower and better established: Apple’s documentation continues to describe a territorial difference in the service it offers. A diplomatic announcement or procedural development would require a separate check of product availability before concluding that the feature had returned. The material reviewed provides neither a rate of UK customer departures nor lost sales attributable to the restriction. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q01" aria-label="Source Q01">Q01</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q05" aria-label="Source Q05">Q05</a></sup>

The contract sets out some ways customers can respond. In the iCloud terms revised on **September 14, 2026**, Apple provides for notice of material adverse changes, with exceptions including circumstances arising from legal or governmental action. The terms also provide, in the specified situations, for termination and a prorated refund of certain prepayments. These clauses describe contractual rights; they establish no compensation actually paid in connection with ADP. Territorial provisions and mandatory legal rights must also be considered. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q03" aria-label="Source Q03">Q03</a></sup>

A subscription refund and a digital move address different problems. The first concerns the service paid for. The second may require choosing a solution, transferring data and restoring a working arrangement. The gap between them can leave the customer with a cost even when the refund has been calculated correctly.

## Contracts and product availability in China

Apple describes iCloud in mainland China as a service operated by **GCBD, AIPO Cloud (Guizhou) Technology Co. Ltd.**, within China’s regulatory framework. Its support page, dated April 15, 2025, directs the users concerned to specific terms. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q08" aria-label="Source Q08">Q08</a></sup>

The version revised on **September 14, 2026** provides for a change of operator and contract when a change of region moves an account between Apple and GCBD. It also contains **Section II.J on ADP**, extending end-to-end encryption and setting out recovery arrangements that rely on the user. The existence of that clause is a documented fact. Establishing who actually holds each key and how the feature is deployed on each account would require further technical verification. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q09" aria-label="Source Q09">Q09</a></sup>

The comparison with the United Kingdom calls for examining each feature separately. Apple lists France and the United States for *Private Relay* in its macOS availability catalog; mainland China is absent. Private Relay and ADP are different features. The catalog describes availability without assigning a precise legal cause to every absence. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q12" aria-label="Source Q12">Q12</a></sup>

GCBD’s privacy policy adds a revealing detail. Its **April 12, 2024** text provides for storage in China of personal information GCBD processes or generates there. The same section envisages transfers or access from abroad, including by Apple and its affiliates, for the purposes specified. Storage in China can therefore coexist with access from outside it. This is the provider’s description; no independent audit was conducted for this article. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q10" aria-label="Source Q10">Q10</a></sup>

Chinese rules themselves require the flow to be classified. Article 40 of the Personal Information Protection Law confines its local storage duty to certain categories of actors. The law provides several routes for transfers and includes encryption among the security measures set out in Article 51. The regulation of **March 22, 2024** adds conditional exemptions from certain outbound transfer formalities while retaining applicable protection duties. Inferring a general ban on transfers from the word “China” alone would misread these provisions. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q13" aria-label="Source Q13">Q13</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q14" aria-label="Source Q14">Q14</a></sup>

## Four dimensions of a cloud service

A buyer can clarify this landscape by following four dimensions: the entity with which it signs a contract, the location where data is stored, the teams and systems authorized to administer it, and the actors able to use the keys. The diagram separates these relationships to show where they intersect.

<EncryptionBordersFigure lang="en" kind="relations" />

A data center’s address answers a physical question. The contracting party helps identify the obligations governing the commercial relationship. Administrative permissions specify who can act on the service. Control of the keys determines the ability to read content within the architecture concerned. Administrative access can be extensive without automatically providing access to every item in plaintext; its reach depends on permissions and encryption mechanisms. France’s data protection authority, the CNIL, stresses precisely the distinction between possession, control, access and use of keys. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q16" aria-label="Source Q16">Q16</a></sup>

Consider a buyer assured that its files will remain in a particular country. It still needs answers to the other questions: which entity can receive a demand, which provider can administer the systems, and which operations require a key? This is an illustrative example. It organizes the examination of an offering without attributing to a real provider an architecture that public documents do not describe.

The account’s region adds another layer. Apple’s **October 30, 2026** support article describes steps to take before changing a country or region, including matters involving the account balance, subscriptions, pending transactions and a payment method. These requirements document potential work for the user. They belong to the commercial ecosystem; their entire burden cannot be attributed to an obligation to provide access to content. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q11" aria-label="Source Q11">Q11</a></sup>

## Confidentiality becomes a purchasing requirement

For a French organization, the assessment starts with the function it needs. Keeping an encrypted archive and running a process that must read its contents raise different questions.

The European Data Protection Board illustrates this in its final recommendations on transfers of **June 18, 2021**. Hosting backups may benefit from an effective supplementary measure where encryption takes place before transmission and meets demanding conditions, including those concerning its strength, implementation and control of the keys. A provider that needs to process the data in plaintext presents a different difficulty where disproportionate access powers in a third country actually apply to the transfer. The Board’s technical assessment dates from 2021 and depends on those assumptions. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q15" aria-label="Source Q15">Q15</a></sup>



The commercial question follows: which functions is the buyer willing to entrust to the provider, and with what access? In the common architectures described by France’s data protection authority, the CNIL, [client-side encryption](/en/glossary/chiffrement-cote-client/) can preserve control over content while limiting some collaboration or indexing functions. The choice requires aligning the confidentiality sought with the service actually needed. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q16" aria-label="Source Q16">Q16</a></sup>

**[SecNumCloud](/en/glossary/secnumcloud/)** qualification adds another set of requirements. France’s cybersecurity agency, ANSSI, describes criteria that include governance, operational autonomy and relationships with third parties subject to laws outside Europe. Qualification applies to a specific offering. An application hosted on a qualified service still needs examination within its own scope; the status does not pass to it automatically. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q17" aria-label="Source Q17">Q17</a></sup>

A French document from 2026 shows how these issues enter a concrete purchasing process. The government’s Legal Affairs Directorate has published **model clauses for public digital procurement**; the PDF examined was updated on **August 21, 2026**. It brings together safeguards, audit findings, notice of certain changes and termination options. [Reversibility](/en/glossary/reversibilite/), the organised ability to take back or transfer the service, also depends on access to the necessary resources and, for the bespoke developments covered, delivery of code and documentation. These are templates to be incorporated and adapted, with distinct scopes of application. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q19" aria-label="Source Q19">Q19</a></sup>

The economic mechanism becomes tangible. If a characteristic required by the buyer changes, its compatibility with the buyer’s needs and the contract must be assessed. Additional evidence, a correction or an exit may become necessary. A new access capability does not, however, automatically entail loss of qualification or termination: that connection would have to be established in the case at hand.

For the provider, a documented safeguard can therefore be a condition of access to certain markets. For the buyer, it has value because it reduces a specific uncertainty. An effect on sales remains a hypothesis until a purchasing or termination decision attributable to the change is observed.

## The work involved in switching services

[Portability](/en/glossary/portabilite/) enables data to be transferred in a usable form. Exporting data is an initial stage of the transfer. The steps that follow depend on the use case: locating the expected files, checking their formats, restoring the necessary connections and making sure authorized users can work. An organization may need to run both environments temporarily. This is a project model whose stages must be adapted to the actual situation.

Guidelines issued by the French regulator Arcep on **July 2, 2026** give this distinction practical substance. They separate duties to facilitate switching from additional support services. Examples drawn from its work include export assistance, integrity checks, planning, script adaptation and training. How a service is classified depends on its purpose; a required action retains that status even when the customer asks for it. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q20" aria-label="Source Q20">Q20</a></sup>

<EncryptionBordersFigure lang="en" kind="migration" />

A team’s time has an opportunity cost: while it reconfigures a service, it has less availability for other work. Estimating that cost would require knowing the scope, the people involved, the hours and the alternatives. This article assigns neither an average duration nor a fictional hourly rate to that work. The examples of tasks establish a mechanism without measuring its scale.

Preparation also changes the decision. Documented exports, usable formats and a tested recovery procedure can reduce the work that has to be improvised when leaving. An integrated service may, in turn, offer enough useful functions to justify staying. The economic decision concerns the complete service, its protection and the available exit options.

## Zero euros within a defined scope

The regulatory timetable corrects a common assumption about exit fees. **In France, the covered data transfer fees incurred when switching providers have already been capped at zero euros since December 1, 2025.** The order is dated November 17, was published on November 30, and provides for this rule to apply until January 12, 2027. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q21" aria-label="Source Q21">Q21</a></sup>

**January 12, 2027** marks a separate step: the prohibition on switching charges under Article 29 of the **Data Act**, within its scope. The regulation has generally applied since September 12, 2025 and provides for exit rights, assistance and information about exportable data. Special rules apply, among other things, to certain custom-built services and temporary trials. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q18" aria-label="Source Q18">Q18</a></sup>



The two charging scopes need to remain clear. Data transfer is one component of switching; the customer’s project may also require internal work or additional services. Arcep explains that services genuinely going beyond the exit obligations can be charged for, including after January 2027, where they are requested and their price is agreed in advance. Ordinary service charges also follow their own rules. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q20" aria-label="Source Q20">Q20</a></sup>

This framework reduces some barriers to switching. Its practical effect will depend on interfaces, exportable data and the work needed to resume operations. A zero-euro line on the invoice can make it easier to decide to leave; the need to restore a working service remains. European exit rules therefore provide a real counterweight to dependence, within a scope that the contract and architecture must allow customers to exercise. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q18" aria-label="Source Q18">Q18</a></sup>

## The difficulty of valuing trust

At what price would a customer accept a weaker confidentiality guarantee? How much effort would they make to retain a stronger one? Answering these questions could turn the mechanism into an amount. Product documentation and legal texts do not, on their own, supply the necessary data.

A **study published in 2021 by Law and Economics Consulting Associates (LECA)**, commissioned by the **Internet Society**, illustrates the difficulty. Examining Australia’s Telecommunications and Other Legislation Amendment (Assistance and Access) Act, known as TOLA, it combines nine interviews with a questionnaire that received 79 responses, including 54 from companies headquartered in Australia. It reports economic concerns but acknowledges that the sample cannot support a reliable aggregate estimate. Its targeted recruitment, self-reported responses and the commissioning organization’s interest in strong encryption must inform the reading. Individual companies’ reported amounts are not treated here as verified costs. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q23" aria-label="Source Q23">Q23</a></sup>

The **Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) and the World Trade Organization**, in their study published in February 2025 and revised in August, offer a broader framework for rules governing data flows. Their model combines trade and compliance costs with a trust effect on willingness to pay. This channel rests on assumptions and calibration; it does not measure an observed bill for a backdoor. It nevertheless highlights an essential counterargument: credible safeguards can support use and trade even when they impose obligations on providers. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q22" aria-label="Source Q22">Q22</a></sup>

The economic comparison should therefore concern specific configurations. A requirement can increase the compliance burden while making an offering acceptable to some buyers. A restriction on a feature can reduce a product’s usefulness for some customers without prompting all the others to leave. Adding a hypothetical migration cost to an assumed loss of trust could also count the same response twice.

Measuring the effect of compelled access would require tracking changes in the offering, customer decisions, expenditure actually incurred and a credible comparison scenario. The cost of a move already planned for other reasons could not be attributed in full to that obligation. A stated intention to leave would have to remain distinct from an observed departure.

## Exit options are part of the purchase

Withdrawal of a protection can have an economic effect before any access to content is publicly documented. In the British case, the restriction on availability is verifiable. The costs that follow depend on the role that guarantee plays in the customer’s use of the service. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q01" aria-label="Source Q01">Q01</a></sup>

The Chinese documents require a precise reading of the contract and the features. The French sources show how to specify the evidence expected and prepare for reversibility. Together, they describe a market in which confidentiality is negotiated through technical capabilities, obligations and opportunities to leave. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q09" aria-label="Source Q09">Q09</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-Q19" aria-label="Source Q19">Q19</a></sup>

For a buyer, the useful question becomes concrete: **if this safeguard changes, can it continue operating with the level of protection it needs, and what means will it have to switch services?** The answer lies in access to the keys, the contract terms and the quality of the transition. This is where the debate over backdoors meets a product’s value.

## Method and scope

This documentary investigation is current to **November 7, 2026**. Sources distinguish publication dates, versions and events. Apple and GCBD documents are providers’ statements; claimants’ filings and congressional positions are attributed. Chinese legal texts describe rules without proving how they are implemented on a particular account. No interviews, infrastructure tests or independent audits of key custody were conducted for this article. The diagrams are explanatory models; category counts and charging dates are sourced. No global cost or measured commercial causality is claimed.
