**Washington authorized hundreds of thousands of advanced chips for Chinese companies. Congress has now opened part of the paperwork.** On November 19, the House Foreign Affairs Committee released a Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) record covering Nvidia H200 and AMD MI325X applications. It lists **3,444,160 chips requested** and **879,868 approved across 21 applications**, all listed as unshipped. The record describes the position on **May 11**, six months before publication. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

The disclosure arrives as a new US rule takes effect, extending controls to certain forms of remote access to computing power. The machines can stay in Europe while a company elsewhere uses their computing power. That puts a Norwegian data center in the same debate. Bloomberg reports that Nscale’s service for ByteDance now faces a licensing question. [S03](#source-S03) [S05](#source-S05)

## Congress opens the licensing record

Export applications normally receive statutory confidentiality protection. Under the Export Control Reform Act, a committee with jurisdiction can obtain them, and the full committee can authorize disclosure after determining that withholding the information would run against the national interest. Ranking Democrat Gregory Meeks says the committee agreed by unanimous consent. Republican chairman Brian Mast announced the release that afternoon in Washington. [S01](#source-S01)

The committee’s definition of Chinese companies includes businesses whose ultimate parent is headquartered in China. Some applications therefore concern equipment located in another country. [S01](#source-S01)

Among the approved entries, the location column names **China as the final chip destination in all 21 cases**. Our arithmetic separates **17 Nvidia entries, covering 579,868 H200s**, from **four AMD entries, covering 300,000 MI325Xs**. Together they reproduce the committee’s published total. [S02](#source-S02)

Applications elsewhere in the table have other outcomes, including pending and returned cases. The committee also holds applications incompletely reflected in the May summary, singling out a separate Nvidia request for **445,000 H200s for Tencent**. That supplemental request needs to remain separate from the published totals until its relationship to the other entries is established. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

## Following an approval through to shipment

Our [earlier analysis of US controls](/en/analysis/nvidia-china-ai-credit-us-controls-rene-haas/) examined the opening for eligible chips under a case-by-case licensing policy. This record shows how far approvals had progressed in the disclosed series. Following the physical equipment requires shipment dates, quantities dispatched and the license used for each consignment.

Nvidia’s August quarterly filing records some shipments under the program. The company reported shipping a fraction of the quantities permitted under the H200 program. Those shipments contributed **less than 1% of Data Center revenue in the quarter ended July 26**. Nvidia reports the revenue share at the division level. Matching shipments to the May decisions would require quantities and individual license records. [S07](#source-S07)

## A Norwegian contract meets a wider rule

The rule examined in our [seventeenth installment](/en/analysis/nvidia-bis-remote-access-cloud-controls/) became effective on **November 19**. Alongside destination-based equipment controls, it covers certain exports or transfers when the party knows the destination data center provides, or will provide, remote access exceeding **12 million aggregate [TPP](/en/glossary/tpp-agrege/)** for companies headquartered, or with an ultimate parent headquartered, in Macau or Country Group D:5, which includes China. TPP is a regulatory performance measure, assessed across a facility or linked facilities. Covered existing services wishing to continue have **90 days to obtain a license**. A conditional reporting exception addresses computing used exclusively for commercial recommender systems. [S03](#source-S03)

Nscale’s **Glomfjord** facility illustrates the exposure. Bloomberg’s November 19 report, read through E24, says its ByteDance service could require a license. Nscale declined to comment, citing its planned listing. A February draft prospectus named ByteDance as its largest 2025 customer and left the percentage blank. The November 12 amendment gives the largest customer’s share of 2025 revenue as **73%** and omits the name in that passage. [S04](#source-S04) [S05](#source-S05)

## Glomfjord’s loan relies on project revenue

Macquarie provided a **$105 million facility** in June 2025 to finance equipment at Glomfjord. Nscale reports drawing **$70.1 million on August 1, 2025**, and **$34.9 million on January 30, 2026**. Monthly principal repayments are tied to project cash flows. The public loan agreement specifies **2,304 Nvidia B200 GPUs** and names the cloud services contract signed with **Spring (SG) Pte. Ltd.** on May 15, 2025. The *Financial Times* identified Spring as a ByteDance subsidiary. [S04](#source-S04) [S05](#source-S05)

The agreement’s [covenants](/en/glossary/covenant/) show the lender’s stake in the customer relationship. Customers must pay their fees into a collections account. The operator must monitor usage within the limits of its technical access and customer terms, and notify the agent in writing of anomalies indicating a possible breach of export controls. Amending or terminating the Spring contract requires the agent’s prior written consent. If the contract is terminated, the agreement calls for a qualifying replacement within **two months**, on a contract at least as long as the remaining term. [S04](#source-S04)

The [proposed Aolani loan](/en/analysis/nvidia-financing-guarantees-aolani-loan/) came under review while lenders were still deciding whether to participate. At Glomfjord the facility has already been drawn. The availability of a license, the service to Spring and the status of the loan obligations each need their own confirmation. The additional **$1 billion Nvidia tranche** for Nscale, expected around November 16, is a separate investment. Public filings reviewed for this edition have yet to confirm its closing. [S03](#source-S03) [S04](#source-S04) [S05](#source-S05)

## Anthropic’s placement approaches settlement

Our [twentieth installment](/en/analysis/nvidia-credit-outlook-wall-street-anthropic/) followed Nvidia’s prospective investment in Anthropic, which began trading on **November 19**. The final prospectus specifies **120,481,927 Class N shares for Nvidia at $83 each**, approximately **$10 billion**. Public-offering settlement is expected on or about **November 20**. The private placement is scheduled to follow completion, subject to its conditions. Anthropic says shareholder approval has been obtained. At the filing check at **00:41 Paris time on November 20**, payment and issuance of Nvidia’s shares were still unconfirmed in the public record. [S06](#source-S06)
