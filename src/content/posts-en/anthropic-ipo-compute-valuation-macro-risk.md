**A possible valuation above $2 trillion.** The figure reported by Reuters for Anthropic’s proposed IPO captures attention. Behind it lies an industrial financing question: who puts up the money between ordering computing capacity and customers paying enough to fund the operation? [Reuters, September 28, 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/anthropics-ipo-prospectus-shows-sweeping-ai-vision-surging-costs-2026-09-28/)

The company behind Claude can have a product in strong demand, rapid growth and a continuing need for capital. All three can coexist. For investors, the technology matters alongside the entry price, shareholder rights and payment schedule.

## Preparing for a public offering

On **June 1, 2026**, Anthropic announced that it had confidentially submitted a draft Form S-1 to the SEC, the U.S. securities regulator. The announcement left the price and share count open, with the offering subject to market conditions. [Official announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)

The late-September financial disclosures come from a prospectus reviewed by Reuters. **We could not independently examine Anthropic’s complete S-1.** Figures from that document remain attributed to the agency. This analysis uses no unverified ticker, final offer price or definitive fundraising total.

An initial public offering, or IPO, can combine newly issued shares with sales by existing shareholders. New issuance funds the company, net of fees; secondary sales pay the selling shareholders. The value of all the equity and the cash the company receives therefore measure different things. [SEC, IPO guidance](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-17)

A large offering may prompt investors to sell other assets to participate. Others may use cash, while selling shareholders may reinvest their proceeds. The size of those reallocations depends on the actual offering and where subscriptions come from. A $2 trillion headline valuation cannot establish an equivalent withdrawal from Nvidia, bonds or the rest of the market.

The securities being compared also matter. Conversion provisions, economic preferences and protections in a private funding round may differ from the rights attached to a future publicly traded share. Two successive valuations are insufficient to calculate every investor’s return.

## From accounting losses to cash requirements

**Reuters reports approximately $4.6 billion of revenue and a $42 billion net loss for 2025, including roughly $34 billion of non-cash charges** from revaluing financing convertible into shares. It also describes **$518 billion of future cloud, compute and infrastructure obligations**. Their detailed payment schedule still needs to be examined in the prospectus. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/anthropics-ipo-prospectus-shows-sweeping-ai-vision-surging-costs-2026-09-28/)

Revaluation can increase an accounting charge as financing rights become more valuable. Those rights may cause future dilution without an equivalent bank payment at that moment. Measuring cash consumption requires reconciling receipts, operating payments, investment and advances. Simply subtracting 34 from 42 does not reconstruct the cash-flow statement. [SEC, financial statements guide](https://www.sec.gov/about/reports-publications/investorpubsbegfinstmtguide)

A customer may pay before revenue is recognized; a supplier may require an advance well before capacity becomes usable. Such timing differences, including changes in working capital, alter how much financing a growing business needs.

Resources have also changed since the 2025 year-end. In May, Anthropic announced a **$65 billion funding round at a $965 billion post-money valuation**. It includes $15 billion of previously committed hyperscaler investments, including $5 billion from Amazon. Adding them again would inflate the total. [Anthropic, Series H](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)

Estimating financial runway would require a recent cash balance and payment schedule. Dividing an old balance by an accounting loss would produce a misleading answer.

## Annualized revenue measures a pace

Anthropic said its annualized revenue had exceeded **$47 billion in May**. The New York Times, in a report syndicated by DT Next, described an **expectation of more than $100 billion by the end of 2026**, citing people familiar with the matter. That second figure is a projected annualized pace. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h) · [New York Times / DT Next, September 21](https://www.dtnext.in/amp/story/news/business/growth-slowdown-dynamics-anthropic-pursues-ipo-amid-ai-safety-debate)

A [run rate](/en/glossary/run-rate/) extrapolates a recent period: in the simplest example, one month’s revenue is multiplied by twelve. It describes the business’s annual scale if that pace persists. Revenue already earned since January and purchases contractually guaranteed for the future have different definitions.

A useful comparison keeps periods aligned. The last completed financial year may understate the current size of a fast-growing business. An expected year-end pace can overstate realized revenue if treated as a collected annual total. Retention, recurring customer spending, discounts and payment terms would help establish the durability of growth.

The Financial Times also reported that Anthropic was telling some investors it expected a second consecutive quarter of positive adjusted operating income. These are investor communications reported by the newspaper, read in its Irish Times republication. The adjustment excludes stock-based compensation, among other costs. Net income and cash remaining after investment still need to be reconciled with that measure. [Financial Times / Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/09/14/anthropic-tells-investors-it-will-be-profitable-for-second-straight-quarter/)

## Suppliers also finance their customer

In April, Anthropic announced **more than $100 billion of AWS spending over ten years**, securing up to five gigawatts of capacity. Amazon separately announced a $5 billion immediate investment and up to $20 billion more tied to commercial milestones. Announced capacity and conditional investments must be distinguished from delivery and cash already received. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute) · [Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-invests-additional-5-billion-anthropic-ai)

The November 2025 Microsoft and Nvidia partnership also combines purchasing and financing: a $30 billion Azure capacity commitment and investments of up to $5 billion and $10 billion respectively. [Partnership announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/microsoft-nvidia-anthropic-announce-strategic-partnerships)

The same organization can sell compute, provide capital to its customer and participate in that customer’s future equity value. This can accelerate a real industrial expansion. Understanding the accounts requires following each flow: equity subscriptions finance the laboratory; compute purchases pay for a service; final demand comes from users willing to pay.

The critical asymmetry concerns flexibility. Customers may reduce usage quickly while the laboratory remains committed to capacity purchases. Conversely, delivery requirements before billing leave some delay risk with the supplier. Minimum purchases, advances, guarantees and termination clauses determine who funds the interval.

The $518 billion total reported by Reuters describes future obligations. It supplies neither a financial-debt balance nor an annual budget. AWS and Azure agreements may already be included: adding them without reconciling the scope would create double counting. Our investigation into [AI debt, contracts and guarantees](/en/analysis/ai-slowdown-6-debt-contracts-guarantees/) examines this problem.

## Akamai: investment before receipts

On **September 24**, Akamai announced an **$11.6 billion, seven-year Anthropic commitment** for general-purpose processor, or CPU, workloads. It estimates **$5.5 billion of related capital expenditure**, including **$1.7 billion of additional 2026 capex**, with no increase in that year’s revenue guidance. A possible $9 billion expansion remains optional. [Akamai](https://www.akamai.com/newsroom/press-release/akamai-announces-11-6-billion-multi-year-agreement-with-anthropic-to-support-growing-demand)

[Akamai’s Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1086222/000119312526401048/d288154d8k.htm), reviewed directly, says the commitments depend on delivery and availability conditions. Each seven-year project term starts on its respective service commencement date. Termination rights include uncured breaches and, under specified conditions, a material outage. The complete master agreement is scheduled for a later filing.

The financing mechanism is tangible: securing components ties up money before all the expected receipts arrive. A delivery delay can extend the period during which equipment needs financing. A major customer reduces some commercial uncertainty while leaving an industrial timetable and performance conditions to manage.

Akamai also granted a **warrant**, a right to buy securities at an agreed price, potentially representing about 5% of its common shares on an as-converted basis. Vesting depends on payments and additional commitments; exercise requires cash payment. [Akamai](https://www.akamai.com/newsroom/press-release/akamai-announces-11-6-billion-multi-year-agreement-with-anthropic-to-support-growing-demand) · [SEC, 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1086222/000119312526401048/d288154d8k.htm)

The arrangement brings supplier and customer interests closer together. Any gain depends on the exercise price, future stock price and contractual conditions. The total value of the underlying shares is not Anthropic’s profit. Nor can Akamai’s published terms be assumed to apply to other suppliers.

In this **entirely fictional example**, receiving 100 in ten years is worth 46.32 today at an 8% discount rate, 38.55 at 10%, and 32.20 at 12%. Moving from 8% to 10% reduces present value by roughly **16.8%**. The future payment is unchanged; only the rate moves. These assumptions do not estimate Anthropic’s cost of capital.

Improving earnings prospects can offset that effect. Conversely, revenue growth accompanied by weaker margins or larger advance payments can increase shareholder risk. Our installment on [Japan, interest rates and the cost of waiting](/en/analysis/ai-slowdown-5-japan-rates-cost-of-waiting/) explores these channels.

## Why the reason for a rate cut matters

Monetary easing alongside resilient activity could facilitate financing. An emergency cut responding to economic deterioration would have other effects: some customers would cut budgets, lenders might seek stronger protections and investors might require a higher risk premium.

In that scenario, the risk-free rate would decline while expected cash flows weakened. Long-term yields could also react differently from policy rates, depending on inflation expectations and compensation for lending over long horizons. A Fed cut alone therefore cannot determine a company’s value.

This is a possible mechanism, without a forecast of monetary action or a crash. For an offering in preparation, the exposure is to a financing window that may change between filing documents and placing shares.

Japan adds another variable. An investor financing dollar assets with yen resources must consider currency hedging, liabilities and the asset’s own risk. Higher Japanese rates can alter allocation decisions. Their effect on Anthropic would require information about actual holders and flows.

## Compute costs meet selling prices

The IEA’s September report describes oil flows still disrupted by conflict and delayed normalization, with inventories being drawn down. In the United States, the BLS reports annual inflation of **16.3% for energy**, compared with **3.8% for electricity**, in August. [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-september-2026) · [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_09112026.htm)

A data center’s electricity cost depends on location, generation mix and contracts. Household electricity inflation does not establish Anthropic’s industrial tariff. Energy can also affect component transport, construction or the persistence of higher rates. Each channel requires its own assessment.

Meanwhile, prices charged to users evolve. For **Opus 5.5, released September 22**, Anthropic lists **$4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens**, 20% below the corresponding Opus 5 prices. Tokens are units of content processed by the model. List prices do not establish average realized revenue across contracts. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5)

Greater efficiency can lower costs faster than prices and enable new applications. But volume and revenue grow at different rates. In a fictional example, 50% more volume with a 20% reduction in unit price increases revenue by 20%: 1.50 × 0.80 = 1.20. The profit margin still depends on unit costs.

For agents performing successive tasks, value depends on completed work, including errors, retries and human supervision. [Competing models and their prices](/en/analysis/ai-slowdown-3-chinese-models-price-competition/) can influence customers’ choices. Total migration costs and service quality determine how far that competition reaches.

## Refinancing can transmit a slowdown

A laboratory whose growth disappoints may reduce new reservations or decline an optional expansion while paying all its invoices. The supplier then loses future orders. If infrastructure was financed in anticipation of those orders, shareholders and lenders must reconsider expected receipts.

Transmission depends on debt actually drawn, maturities, guarantees and the identity of the borrower. A firm contract performed with a solvent customer can protect the supplier: the customer then bears the cost of unused capacity. Releasing equipment to another customer depends on an available buyer and an acceptable price.

A falling share price could make another financing round more dilutive: raising the same amount at a lower issue price requires more shares. That could constrain new compute purchases and subsequently affect supplier financing. This chain is conditional. A stock-price move triggers a margin call only where an applicable contract provides for one.

An adequately funded IPO can buy time to bridge the gap. Its contribution will appear in net capital received and subsequent cash flows.

## The expectations behind $2 trillion

Take **$2 trillion of equity value as an assumption**, with a fictional multiple of 40 times annual cash flow available to shareholders. That implies $50 billion a year: 2,000 / 40 = 50. At an assumed 20% conversion of revenue into that cash flow, corresponding revenue would be $250 billion; at 30%, roughly $166.7 billion.

These divisions illustrate sensitivity to assumptions. They are neither a price target nor a complete discounted cash-flow valuation. The cash flow described comes after the requirements of the business and its financing; it differs from gross margin and adjusted operating income.

If those flows arrive years later, the calculation must include time, interim earnings or losses, fresh capital and dilution. Comparing them directly with the expected year-end run rate would mix horizons and definitions. A complete valuation requires consistent paths for recognized revenue, costs, investment and the share count.

## The power attached to the shares

Reuters describes a proposal under which **Founder LLC**, initially comprising the seven cofounders, would hold a Class F share carrying **50.1% of voting power on certain matters**. The agency says the Long-Term Benefit Trust would elect four directors, with the other three elected by Class F and Class A holders. Governance therefore cannot be reduced to the voting percentage alone. These remain the prospectus terms reported by Reuters. [Reuters, governance](https://yall1067.com/2026/09/28/exclusive-anthropic-leaders-to-control-ai-lab-via-founder-llc-to-promote-public-good-over-market-forces/)

In 2023, Anthropic presented its Public Benefit Corporation status and Trust as arrangements serving a long-term mission. [Historical account](https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-long-term-benefit-trust) Management insulated from quarterly pressure can fund long research programs or delay a risky deployment. Investors need to understand their ability to challenge decisions and which financial obligations continue during a delay.

Safety concerns can be sincere and supported by evidence. Regulation’s economic effects on entry costs should be assessed separately from allegations of anticompetitive intent. Our investigation into [documented security incidents](/en/analysis/ai-slowdown-2-what-agents-actually-did/) examines that distinction.

## Turning adoption into sustainable funding

The favorable scenario is concrete: customers obtain enough value to increase purchases, the cost of useful work falls and capital raised covers industrial timing gaps. Anthropic gains time to bring receipts and expenditure closer together.

The unfavorable scenario can occur with useful technology: slower commercial growth, price pressure, investment paid too early and more demanding financing. A remarkable company can disappoint shareholders who entered at a high price.

The complete prospectus needs to connect revenue with collections, commitments with payment dates and capital raised with post-conversion rights. The primary share of the offering will show how much money reaches the business. Minimum purchases and termination clauses will reveal its flexibility.

**The stock market can buy Anthropic more time. Durable value will depend on what customers are willing to pay during that time.**
