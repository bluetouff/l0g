*Investigation · Financing artificial intelligence · Documentary cut-off: October 24, 2026*

**On October 17, 2026, Nebius signed a $3.5 billion credit agreement with Citi. The press release followed five days later. The money is available under a facility that matures on October 16, 2027, just 364 days after signature. Yet the GPU clusters the company is racing to install are supposed to supply computing power for years.** The important story begins where those two schedules meet. [S01](#source-s01) [S02](#source-s02)

Nebius, headquartered in Amsterdam, has secured major commitments from Microsoft and Meta. Customers can fund part of the buildout in advance. Investors can supply convertible debt or equity. Banks can lend against hardware already deployed and the cash generated by the contracts attached to it. The new Citi facility adds another, shorter instrument to that stack. Its margin steps up every 90 days; its security package reaches across much of the assets of three group borrowers; and it contains a minimum-liquidity covenant whose numerical threshold is absent from the public filing. [S01](#source-s01) [S04](#source-s04)

The first-half accounts reveal why timing matters. They record **$4.50 billion in positive operating cash flow** and **$8.13 billion in cash purchases of property, equipment and intangible assets**. Both figures are real. Understanding their economic meaning requires following the customer's money into the servers, and then looking ahead to the computing service that Nebius still owes. [S03](#source-s03) [S04](#source-s04)

## Short-term borrowing for infrastructure used over several years

An AI cloud provider spends heavily before most new clusters can be billed for productive work. It secures land and electricity, develops a site, orders servers and networking equipment, installs GPUs, tests the system and makes computing capacity available to customers. Construction requires cash immediately. Service revenues are ordinarily recognized over the period for which that capacity is provided, even when some of the customer's payment arrives earlier.

A lender works to a contractual repayment schedule. It advances funds under a contract, collects interest, may take collateral and expects repayment or refinancing on a particular date. Those arrangements can each be commercially sound while leaving a demanding intersection in the middle. A five-year customer commitment gives visibility into future activity. It does not guarantee freely available cash on the day a one-year loan falls due.

The Form 6-K Nebius filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on October 22 identifies three borrowers: **Nebius Group N.V., Nebius Inc. and Nebius B.V.** It describes a senior secured term loan facility with a **$3.5 billion principal amount**, arranged and provided by Citibank, N.A., with Citi affiliates serving in agency and security roles. The company has filed a summary of the bridge agreement rather than its complete contractual text. That summary establishes the size of the facility; it does **not disclose how much had actually been drawn** as of the filing date. A borrowing limit and an outstanding loan balance are different measurements. [S01](#source-s01)

Nebius describes the facility as a way of accelerating construction while arranging other sources of financing. In its October release, management sets out an intended sequence that includes customer prepayments, operating cash flow, convertible securities and asset-backed debt. A bridge is useful when it crosses a period in which permanent funding is still being put together. The public record shows that this bridge exists. It does not yet identify which financing, if any, will replace the Citi balance by October 2027. [S02](#source-s02)

## The lender margin rises every 90 days

At inception, loans bear interest at **Term SOFR plus a 2.75-percentage-point margin**. SOFR is a reference rate based on overnight borrowing secured by US Treasury securities; Term SOFR translates that market into a forward-looking term benchmark. The contract adds a lender margin to the reference rate. The disclosed margin increases by 0.25 percentage points after each 90-day period starting October 17, subject to other adjustments, until it reaches 3.50 percentage points. [S01](#source-s01)

That produces four identifiable steps: **October 17, 2026; January 15, 2027; April 15; and July 14**, followed by maturity on **October 16, 2027**. Changes in the underlying reference rate will affect the all-in interest cost independently of these predetermined margin increases. No forecast of SOFR is needed to understand the incentive built into the contract: refinancing sooner avoids later, higher margins. [S01](#source-s01)

Suppose, purely for illustration, that the entire $3.5 billion were outstanding. Every extra 25 basis points would add **$8.75 million to the annualized margin cost**. At 2.75%, that margin alone corresponds to $96.25 million per year; at 3.50%, to $122.5 million. These are annualized sensitivities, **not reported interest expenses**. They exclude SOFR, fees, possible contractual adjustments, partial time periods and any changes in the amount borrowed. Without a draw schedule, adding the four annualized figures would manufacture a misleading bill. [S01](#source-s01)

The SEC filing also permits voluntary repayment without a premium or penalty. That provision matters. If Nebius finds longer-term funding on attractive terms, it can retire the bridge before its final step. The increasingly expensive margin rewards doing so. Whether attractive long-term credit will be available at the required time is a separate market question. [S01](#source-s01)

## A loan against deployed GPUs, and a loan against a corporate group

The Citi transaction follows another, structurally different financing. In July, Nebius announced approximately **$775 million of secured debt**, maturing **October 31, 2030**, priced at **SOFR plus 2.50 percentage points**. The company said that debt was backed by already deployed GPU infrastructure and cash flow from a contract with a creditworthy customer. MUFG led the syndication, with Citi among the participating lenders. A completed and operating computing asset can therefore support longer-dated credit on the strength of an identified income stream. [S06](#source-s06) The July 6-K names two separate borrowers, Nebius Compute II, LLC and Nebius Compute II Oy, with broad security over their assets and shares. The announcement describes the deployed GPU and customer cash-flow basis; the filing specifies the borrower-level legal scope. [S06](#source-s06)

The October bridge comes with a broader perimeter. The Form 6-K describes security over **substantially all the assets of each of the three borrowers**, including the shares that the parent holds in its US and Dutch borrowing subsidiaries. It also expressly allows excluded assets and permitted liens. Calling the entire financing merely “a loan backed by GPUs” would obscure this distinction. The enforceable ranking of creditors depends on which assets are included, what prior claims exist and how applicable laws and intercreditor arrangements interact. Those details cannot be fully reconstructed from the disclosed summary. [S01](#source-s01)

The October facility additionally contains a **minimum-liquidity financial covenant**. A covenant is a condition a borrower agrees to observe throughout a loan's life. Breaching it can have legal consequences, depending on any cure periods, exceptions and thresholds. The SEC filing discloses the covenant's existence and references customary events of default, but gives neither the required dollar threshold nor the full definition of eligible liquidity. Anyone claiming to know how close Nebius is to a breach from this document alone is claiming a calculation the evidence does not support. [S01](#source-s01)

The distinction also matters to holders of Nebius's convertible bonds. Convertible debt carries creditor claims alongside rights to receive equity under specified conditions. A larger pool of secured borrowing can change the assets available to other creditors in a downside scenario. Estimating that effect would require a current, entity-level debt schedule and a complete collateral register. The public October material does not provide both.

## Customer advances fund construction and future services

The strongest financial evidence comes from the unaudited accounts for the six months ended June 30. Nebius reported **$4,504.1 million of net operating cash provided by continuing operations**. Within the cash-flow reconciliation, the deferred-revenue line contributed **$4,395.0 million**. Deferred revenue arises when a company receives consideration, or has an unconditional right to bill, before it has completed the promised service. It records an obligation to provide that service as revenue is earned over time. [S03](#source-s03) [S04](#source-s04)

The deferred-revenue cash-flow movement is therefore equivalent to approximately **97.6% of reported first-half operating cash flow**, as an arithmetic comparison of the two items. The remaining $109.1 million is the net effect of everything else in that cash-flow statement: other working-capital movements, operating results and non-cash adjustments. It should not be presented as underlying recurring operating profit, or projected mechanically into the second half. [S04](#source-s04)

Over the same six months, Nebius **paid $8,130.3 million** for property, equipment and intangible assets. Subtracting those payments from the operating cash inflow gives **minus $3,626.2 million**. This deliberately narrow subtraction leaves out acquisitions, other investing cash flows and financing transactions; it is not the group's complete change in cash. Net investing cash outflow was $8,288.8 million, while financing activities supplied $9,160.4 million net, notably through convertible notes, treasury-share sales and prefunded warrants. At June 30, **unrestricted cash and equivalents stood at $8,042.1 million**, with another $1,056 million classified as restricted cash. The two balances have different legal and operational uses. [S03](#source-s03) [S04](#source-s04)

An advance from a customer is valuable because it shifts liquidity towards the infrastructure provider early in the buildout. The customer receives a promise in return, and the operator still faces the costs of construction, electricity, maintenance and delivery. At June 30, Nebius carried **$5,975.2 million in total deferred revenue**, current and non-current combined. The notes say some of those balances may be refundable. The cash is genuine; its associated future obligation is genuine too. [S04](#source-s04)

There is another subtlety. Under the accounting policies disclosed by Nebius, a sufficiently material financing component in advance customer payments can generate accounting interest expense as the service is delivered. Its June notes explicitly include **interest recognized on advances from major customers** among the components of interest expense. The company does not separately quantify that amount. A headline suggesting that all customer prepayments are costless financing would therefore outrun the accounting evidence, just as assigning the entire period's interest expense to customers would. [S04](#source-s04) [S15](#source-s15)

## A $37.5 billion order book that has to be earned

In its October announcement, Nebius points to **more than $40 billion in long-term, investment-grade customer commitments**. The June 30 financial statements contain a more precisely defined number: **$37,490.6 million in remaining performance obligations**, or RPO. In US GAAP revenue accounting, RPO is the transaction price assigned to promised services still to be performed. It includes qualifying unbilled commitments and deferred revenue. Nebius omits obligations from contracts originally lasting one year or less under the accounting disclosure exemption. The balance-sheet measurement date and accounting perimeter are different from the commercial claims made in October. [S02](#source-s02) [S04](#source-s04)

Management expects to recognize **36%** of that amount in the following 24 months, **40%** in months 25 through 48 and the rest later. Applied to the June balance, these rounded percentages yield about **$13.497 billion, $14.996 billion and $8.998 billion**, respectively. They are a schedule of anticipated revenue recognition, rather than a bank-account forecast. Variable consideration can change with performance and timing constraints. The $5.975 billion in deferred revenue already sits within the larger RPO number; counting it again would overstate the contractual pipeline. [S04](#source-s04)

The business is indeed expanding. Consolidated second-quarter revenue reached **$582.3 million**, versus $105.1 million a year earlier. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA was **$236.2 million**, a non-GAAP measure requiring reconciliation to ordinary accounting profit. The company nevertheless posted a **$190.4 million net loss from continuing operations** for the quarter. Its cited 50% adjusted EBITDA margin in AI cloud applies to a narrower business unit than the consolidated figures. An improving cloud unit and a still-lossmaking group can coexist. [S05](#source-s05)

RPO gives investors meaningful visibility on the demand customers have already contracted. Its scale says much less, on its own, about eventual margins, financing requirements or the amount of cash available on October 16, 2027. The construction and delivery calendar remains indispensable.

## Microsoft and Meta: delivery and availability commitments

The Microsoft agreement, signed in September 2025, covers dedicated GPU computing capacity at Vineland, New Jersey, deployed across **nine tranches during 2025 and 2026**. The company's annual report describes potential fees of **up to $17.3929 billion through October 2031**, conditional on deployment and availability. Aggregate **contractual upfront payments are approximately $6.9581 billion**, with the remainder billed monthly across the tranches. That upfront contractual amount must not be mistaken for cash already collected at a particular reporting date. [S08](#source-s08) [S10](#source-s10) Subject to the agreed delivery and availability conditions, Microsoft must pay whether or not it actually uses the GPUs. [S08](#source-s08) [S15](#source-s15)

Availability is central to the relationship. Microsoft can receive service credits and, in specified cases of delay or recurrent service failure, terminate an individual tranche. That allocation of risk explains both the attraction and the limitation of lending against a marquee client agreement. Microsoft's financial standing reduces the concern that an otherwise payable bill goes unpaid. It does not manufacture a completed GPU cluster, guarantee on-time delivery, or fix future power and maintenance costs. Nebius's revenue policy recognizes services as they are delivered. [S08](#source-s08)

Meta's agreement announced on March 16, 2026 takes a different form. It commits **$12 billion for dedicated capacity**, beginning in early 2027, and provides for the purchase of **up to $15 billion in additional computing capacity** at certain other clusters. Nebius first intends to market that second set of resources to third-party cloud customers. Under the announced arrangement, Meta purchases the capacity that remains unsold within the specified scope. The widely cited **$27 billion** is therefore a contract value *up to* that amount, shaped by deployment, market demand and the availability of eligible unsold capacity. It is not $27 billion in already recognized revenue. [S09](#source-s09)

These large customers are real, and so are the service-level conditions attached to their contracts. Lumping every advertised maximum into an undifferentiated pile of guaranteed revenue would conceal why the agreements have different values to a lender. [S08](#source-s08)

## Contracted power, grid connections and active GPU capacity

Physical capacity offers another easy source of confusion. Nebius separates **contracted power**, **connected power** and **active power** in its annual reporting. Contracted power is electricity and associated site capacity secured through agreements. Connected power is capacity made available through an actual grid connection. Active power describes the operating infrastructure available for computing work. The sequence represents distinct engineering and commercial milestones. [S08](#source-s08)

In August, the company raised its **end-2026 target for contracted power to five gigawatts**. A February shareholder letter had targeted **800 megawatts to one gigawatt of connected power by end-2026**. The historical figure of approximately **170 megawatts of active power** reported in the annual filing refers to **December 31, 2025**. Those are observations or projections at different dates and on different definitions. Dividing 170 MW by 5 GW and calling the result a project completion rate would amount to a false comparison. [S05](#source-s05) [S08](#source-s08) [S16](#source-s16)

The gap between a power reservation and an operational server is precisely where capital goes. Securing electricity may open the route to construction; customers pay for functioning systems, with uptime and delivery obligations. Nebius projects deployment of more than one gigawatt a year beginning in 2027. Its disclosed contractual commitments for future minimum or fixed payments linked to electricity, power and colocation reached **$5.3241 billion at June 30**, including $541.8 million scheduled for 2027. Those numbers represent the stated commitments and time buckets, not an estimate of all future electricity expenses or a single immediate payment obligation. [S05](#source-s05) [S04](#source-s04)

## NVIDIA supplies the technology, and some of the capital

One distinguishing feature of this expansion is that NVIDIA has supplied **$2 billion of equity-linked financing** to Nebius. The March 11, 2026 SEC filing describes the instrument as a *prefunded warrant* exercisable for 21,065,936 Class A ordinary shares at a residual exercise price of $0.0001 per share, subject to its terms. Nebius included the resulting proceeds among its first-half financing inflows. A prefunded warrant is an equity-linked instrument for which the investor has effectively paid almost the full share price upfront. [S11](#source-s11) [S04](#source-s04)

NVIDIA is also a major technology supplier to the very type of GPU cloud Nebius is building. The investment helps its customer expand the market for computing infrastructure. That raises a productive analytical question: to what extent are suppliers financing customers' demand for their own products? The warrant and its funding are documented. A legal requirement to spend those particular proceeds on NVIDIA chips has not been established from the documents reviewed; a full answer would need purchasing flows and the relevant commercial terms. [S12](#source-s12) [S11](#source-s11)

Nebius closed **$5.75 billion in new convertible notes** on August 24, split across maturities in 2030 and 2034, while separately exchanging $800 million of original principal in older notes for approximately 15.8 million shares. Because these transactions occurred after June 30, the debt and liquidity figures in the interim balance sheet cannot be presented as a definitive October capital structure. The company's ability to raise capital is evident; its current unencumbered liquidity after several more months of construction still needs an updated reconciliation. [S07](#source-s07)

## Refinancing under changing credit conditions

In its October 16 report on CoreWeave, another AI-cloud operator, Reuters interviewed analysts and fund managers about the financing of projects whose revenue develops over several years. Its October 22 report on the Citi facility also placed Nebius's announcement in that context. Those assessments concern financing conditions across companies with different contracts, collateral and capital structures. [S13](#source-s13) [S14](#source-s14)

The channels through which tighter financial conditions could matter are straightforward. Higher reference rates increase the expense of floating-rate debt, while higher credit spreads make refinancing more costly. A late cluster can delay recognized revenue and activate service remedies. Lower resale or collateral values for computing equipment can weaken borrowing capacity. If those pressures coincided, shareholders might face dilution or the company might defer commercially promising projects. These are plausible mechanisms, rather than events established as having occurred together at Nebius.

The strongest counterargument deserves equal weight. Nebius had **$8.042 billion in unrestricted cash and equivalents on June 30**. It has demonstrated access to convertible capital markets, arranged a longer-term GPU financing in July, attracted strategic equity funding and collected major customer advances. Revenue is growing rapidly. The flexible, prepayable Citi bridge may be an instrument for preserving expansion speed, rather than evidence of imminent funding distress. Public documents available on October 24, however, do not reveal a full October liquidity bridge, the facility's actual draw or the threshold for its new covenant. [S04](#source-s04) [S06](#source-s06) [S07](#source-s07) [S01](#source-s01)

## The evidence needed to assess refinancing risk

The missing contractual evidence is specific: the **amount drawn** against the facility, its use over time, the complete liquidity covenant and definitions, the excluded collateral, any intercreditor agreements, and a complete entity-level breakdown of unrestricted and restricted cash. These would allow an analyst to test repayment capacity and collateral availability without pretending that a loan commitment is an outstanding balance.

On the industrial side, the decisive evidence would be actual grid connections, operational clusters delivered on schedule, remaining construction costs and service availability against the Microsoft and Meta commitments. The agreements establish valuable demand. Execution determines when that demand turns into accounting revenue, operating cash and durable funding capacity.

**Nebius provides a documented example of a broader AI-finance problem: customers can pay ahead of service delivery, infrastructure providers can borrow against assets that will earn money for years, and lenders can still demand repayment after only twelve months.** The public record shows substantial financial resources and several ways of funding that interval. Assessing the risk now requires equipment-delivery milestones, remaining costs, customer receipts and creditor repayment dates, with a clear distinction between completed steps and future commitments.
