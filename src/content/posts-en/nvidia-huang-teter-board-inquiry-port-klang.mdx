**In October, Jensen Huang was still describing his interactions with Alice Huang as ordinary business dealings to Nvidia leaders, including general counsel Tim Teter.** Reuters reports that account on November 14, citing two people familiar with the matter and internal messages it examined. Chief marketing officer Alison Wagonfeld was also among the recipients. The messages raise a governance question: how did the company assess the founder’s explanations? [S01](#source-S01)

Huang and Nvidia declined to comment, while Teter and Wagonfeld had not responded and Alice Huang could not be reached. [S01](#source-S01)

This eleventh installment follows the board’s investigation, congressional requests and a separate development in Malaysia. It builds on our examination of [Nvidia’s financing commitments](/en/analysis/nvidia-fed-nscale-ai-guarantees-credit/) and [the succession process](/en/analysis/nvidia-after-huang-singapore-washington-ai-financing/).

## The board’s inquiry has its own mandate

On November 8, Nvidia’s board announced an independent investigation into reporting that Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. A committee of independent directors would oversee the review with outside independent counsel. The board expressed confidence in Huang and said it had no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets had been compromised. Those were its assessments at that stage. [S09](#source-S09)

On November 10, the independent directors named former acting federal prosecutor Joon H. Kim and Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct the investigation. Their brief statement supplied neither a completion date nor a publication schedule. [S02](#source-S02)

The distinction matters for interpreting Reuters’ reporting. Information about a personal relationship and knowledge of a customer’s compliance are separate questions. Investigators must establish the contents, recipients and timing of each communication before assessing how it affected corporate decisions.

Huang has separately retained Paul Weiss, Reuters reported on November 13. The detailed scope of its work remains undisclosed. That personal representation has a different client from the independent investigation commissioned by Nvidia’s directors. [S05](#source-S05)

A congressional document supplies a narrower, verifiable link: the November 12 letter requesting Huang’s interview was addressed to him through Walter Brown at Paul Weiss. Wachtell’s mandate comes from the independent directors. Keeping the clients and mandates distinct helps readers follow whose interests each lawyer represents. [S04](#source-S04) [S02](#source-S02)

## Congress seeks answers from the lawyer as well as the founder

Three House committee chairs requested Huang for a transcribed interview on December 1 and Teter on December 2. Teter’s letter identifies chip smuggling, military uses of Nvidia products and reported interference in export-control policies and investigations, including Megaspeed. Both letters required written confirmation by November 13 at 8 p.m. EST, with subpoenas threatened otherwise. [S04](#source-S04)

That deadline has passed. A published acceptance or an issued subpoena remains the next procedural document to obtain. Our [sixth installment](/en/analysis/nvidia-congress-teter-kessler-export-controls/) explains the requests and their different congressional tracks.

## At Port Klang, an unsigned offer reaches its expiry date

In Malaysia, PKFZ says a proposed land lease for Speedmatrix fell through because the company failed to sign the offer before it expired. According to the statement reported by Malay Mail on November 14, Speedmatrix applied for a parcel in July, the offer expired in early November and formal notification followed on November 13. [S06](#source-S06)

The offer included a condition requiring prior approval from the relevant authorities before exporting computer chips. The land offer lapsed before a lease was executed. Its intended use and Speedmatrix’s reason for leaving it unsigned remain unknown. The original offer and notification would establish the precise terms. [S06](#source-S06)

This remains a separate transaction from the server collateral examined in [our Shanghai investigation](/en/analysis/nvidia-shanghai-server-collateral-lutnick-bessent/). Its relevance is practical: land rights, permits and equipment each come with conditions that must align before a project can operate.

## A successor inherits contracts alongside the inquiry

Nvidia’s SEC filing records the board’s November 11 acceptance of Huang’s resignation and appointment of Colette Kress as interim CEO while she remains CFO. Harvey Jones became chair. The November 12 announcement says the permanent search will consider internal and external candidates. [S03](#source-S03)

On November 13, the board told CNBC it was selecting a search firm, had not formally engaged any candidates and considered individual speculation premature. That statement and the reported informal approach to Rene Haas, covered in [our ninth installment](/en/analysis/nvidia-china-ai-credit-us-controls-rene-haas/), concern different stages of a search. [S07](#source-S07)

The financial commitments continue meanwhile. Nscale’s November 12 prospectus describes additional funding needed for projected 2027 obligations, without quantifying the requirement. Its further billion-dollar Nvidia investment remains expected around November 16 in the documents reviewed. These are milestones for incoming leadership to monitor alongside the investigation. [S08](#source-S08)

The successor will also have to assess customer finances alongside the risks taken to support chip sales. A [residual value guarantee](/en/analysis/residual-value-guarantee-ai-infrastructure-credit/) can help a buyer borrow by protecting an agreed future equipment value. This [credit enhancement](/en/glossary/rehaussement-de-credit/) brings another party’s financial resources into the arrangement. Reliable information reaching the board becomes essential to evaluating those commitments.

## Washington’s influence will depend on identifiable decisions

An internal successor could prioritize continuity while reviewing approval procedures. An outsider could seek a broader reassessment of customer relationships and future financial support. Either would inherit signed agreements and an unfinished inquiry.

As our [analysis of state leverage](/en/analysis/nvidia-trump-tim-cook-lutnick-state-leverage/) explains, the crisis gives Washington opportunities to press for changes through licensing decisions and negotiations over any proposed public support. Trump could seek greater influence over a strategic supplier. Evidence of that scenario would come from instructions, financing terms or negotiated rights. A presidential preference for a candidate and an appointment by Nvidia’s board carry different authority.

The immediate test is the quality of the information reaching those decision-makers. Reconstructing Huang’s assurances, establishing the board’s findings and tracking contracts as they become effective would show how the company moves beyond its founder’s personal authority.
