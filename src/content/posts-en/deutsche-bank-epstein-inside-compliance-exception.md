*The Inside Deutsche Bank investigation, published on 17 November 2026, puts a larger figure and new internal records behind the bank's relationship with Jeffrey Epstein. The consortium reports $581.9 million in transactions subsequently identified as suspicious. Its accounts of internal exchanges follow the permissions that allowed a known risk to become an enduring client.* [S06](#source-S06)

l0g addressed that mechanism in [our analysis published on 9 August](/en/analysis/deutsche-bank-epstein-compliance-exception/), included in the [Epstein's Money collection](/en/publications/epsteins-money/). The French article and EPUB existed **100 days before the consortium's first publications**. Their central argument was already explicit: a succession of approvals and compromised controls had made an exceptional client increasingly ordinary inside the institution. [S01](#source-S01) [S14](#source-S14) [S15](#source-S15)

The groundwork came from the New York regulator's 2020 findings, Reuters' investigation of the protracted exit in February 2026 and Senator Ron Wyden's August banking report. These sources made our earlier analysis possible. November brings the work of an international consortium with access to internal material. The new findings belong to those investigators, and their evidence provides a more detailed basis for testing our earlier argument. [S02](#source-S02) [S04](#source-S04) [S05](#source-S05)

This analysis is current to **18 November 2026**. We have examined the public investigations and official documents cited below. Access to the underlying leak remains with the consortium and its research infrastructure. [S09](#source-S09) [S10](#source-S10)

## A known risk becomes reusable permission

Commercial ambition and a known risk shared the same acquisition file. In April 2013, it paired Epstein's criminal history with prospective annual revenue of $2 million to $4 million. The decisive May email invoked conversations with legal and anti-money-laundering officials. The NYDFS found no separate record of those discussions or initial meeting of the regional reputational-risk committee. [S02](#source-S02)

Anti-money-laundering controls, usually abbreviated to [AML](/en/glossary/#aml), help banks identify and report potentially unlawful activity. Know-your-customer checks, or [KYC](/en/glossary/#kyc), establish who the client is and the expected purpose of the relationship. A high-risk designation should affect the scrutiny applied to actual transactions. Its value depends on the decisions it changes.

In our reading, that approval became a substitute for asking whether particular activities were justified. A senior commercial officer had represented that the relationship could proceed. Staff taking over the client could refer back to his email, treating the sensitive issue as settled. The organisation had produced an answer that could travel more easily than its underlying reasoning.

Responsibility follows the surviving record. The email establishes the commercial executive's account of a conversation, with the legal and AML officials' agreement reported through him. A directly recorded approval by those officials would be a different piece of evidence. Keeping that distinction visible allows the reconstruction to rest on the documents actually available. [S02](#source-S02)

The NYDFS described conditions imposed after a 2015 review that failed to reach all the relevant staff. Transaction monitoring also came to assess unusual activity against Epstein's own previous activity. In its 2020 findings, the regulator identified a 2017 alert closed on that basis. [S02](#source-S02)

Repeated exceptions can gradually acquire the appearance of normal behaviour. Each earlier approval supplies context for the next alert, making a transaction less surprising because something similar has happened before. The original concern may remain unresolved throughout. Reassurance then comes from a history of activity the bank itself has permitted.

This is especially dangerous when the concern lies in the purpose of a payment. Comparing transactions with past behaviour helps to detect sudden changes. A stable practice poses a harder problem: repetition can look reassuring even when that practice fits the risk already identified. Monitoring has to keep the original concern in view as the history of authorised activity grows.

## The larger total changes the measurement problem

The consortium attributes its $581.9 million figure to an internal US Financial Crimes Intelligence Unit document covering 21 suspicious activity reports and approximately 3,000 transactions between October 2013 and May 2019. A suspicious activity report, or [SAR](/en/glossary/#sar), alerts authorities to activity a financial institution considers suspicious. [S06](#source-S06)

The August Wyden report described more than $250 million in wire transfers retrospectively flagged by Deutsche Bank. Its account included an example of 1,140 wires worth $147 million. Those were findings attributed to investigators who had reviewed records at the Treasury. [S05](#source-S05)

Reconciling these totals requires the underlying populations of payments and their selection rules. The earlier figure was a floor. The new aggregate expands the public picture, but the apparent gap also depends on which transactions were included and how they were counted.

A bank's financial-intelligence record is organised around reporting obligations. Reconstructing the economics of a network means tracing the money from its entry to its eventual use or retention. A collection of suspicious transactions may contain incoming payments, outgoing payments and successive transfers of funds already counted earlier in their journey. Those movements have to be connected before the total can describe the resources available to the account holder.

FinCEN's guidance on completing SARs discusses the treatment of flows and the calculation of reported amounts. It reinforces the need to read the reporting method before turning an aggregate into an economic measure. [S16](#source-S16)

Suppose the same funds move from one account to another and then to a third recipient. Each step may matter to an investigator. Counting the steps describes movement through the system. Identifying the original resources available to the account holder requires a reconstruction of those links. The two exercises can both be useful while producing different totals.

Read as the value of transactions covered by the suspicious-activity reporting described by the consortium, the November figure has a defined purpose. It measures reported movements. A fortune is a stock of assets at a particular date, while criminal proceeds require evidence about the origin of the funds. Preserving those distinctions gives the larger figure its analytical value.

Our earlier [cross-bank investigation](/en/analysis/epstein-money-what-the-banks-saw/) made this accounting discipline central to the dossier. Different institutions can observe separate legs of the same transfer. A larger number becomes analytically useful when readers understand the perimeter, rather than simply the scale.

Timing changes the usefulness of that record. A retrospective investigation can recover a valuable trail for authorities, sometimes years after the money moved. Preventive monitoring has to work while the relationship and its payments are still active. The Wyden report's criticism concerns that delay. Reconstructing the history reveals the distance between a risk that could have been recognised and the bank's eventual response. [S05](#source-S05)

## An exit that kept supplying reassurance

The distinction between deciding to leave and completing the exit was already part of our August analysis. Deutsche Bank notified Epstein on 21 December 2018 that it would stop servicing his accounts. Reuters subsequently documented activity after the transfer deadline, including a reference letter in March 2019 and accounts still holding balances in May. [S02](#source-S02) [S04](#source-S04)

A bank's exit can involve settling securities positions, moving cash and finding another institution willing to accept the assets. Someone must control the instructions the departing client can still give during that process. Describing the relationship as “terminated” can conceal this interval, allowing the date of a decision to acquire the appearance of a complete operational break.

Following the services gives a more useful chronology. It identifies the transfers still permitted, the staff with authority to approve them and the point at which each account stopped functioning. Until those permissions have been withdrawn, the exit remains an active exercise in managing the client's risk. An announcement supplies its intended direction, while the available services show how far the bank has actually travelled.

The reference letters belong to that operational history. The NYDFS had already recorded letters issued to two institutions despite the offboarding decision. Reuters added reporting on their timing. The November investigations give the episode a clearer internal context. [S02](#source-S02) [S04](#source-S04)

According to the consortium, a March 2020 email copied to senior executives acknowledged the lack of a policy for these references. Der Standard also reports that the employee writing them knew about problematic activity and had participated in the exit decision. [S06](#source-S06) [S08](#source-S08)

Alongside the assets, information was moving to another institution that could use it to decide whether to accept the client. A letter carries the authority of the bank's name while compressing a complicated relationship into a short statement about account operation. Its wording can influence the confidence with which the recipient approaches the incoming business.

An orderly transfer may suit the commercial relationship manager who knows the client. For the receiving bank, the value of a reference depends on its accuracy in light of the risks known to the sender. An exit procedure must determine who can speak for the institution and how that person's assurances are checked against the reasons for the departure.

Where the sender's own concern helped trigger the departure, reassurance warrants particular scrutiny. The assets are leaving because the relationship has become unacceptable, yet the accompanying language may help another bank to welcome it. Reconstructing the wording and approval of the reference follows the risk across the boundary between the two institutions.

Der Standard dates the closure of Epstein's last accounts to 9 July 2019, following his arrest. The ZDF account similarly places final closure after the arrest. This extends the operational history beyond the December decision. [S07](#source-S07) [S08](#source-S08)

## Germany's public record has also moved on

Germany's supervisory record also requires an update. Our August account described the government's public position then available. Parliamentary replies published before the leak have since clarified which American documents BaFin examined and the limits of the information released to the public.

In replies dated 5 October and published on 9 October, the German government stated that BaFin had examined both the NYDFS order and the Wyden report. It emphasised the US authorities' responsibility for the American banking relationship and referred to earlier answers, including classified material. A question about the joint BaFin and financial-intelligence task force also received a partly classified answer. [S12](#source-S12)

Part of the supervisory response therefore exists within protected parliamentary procedures. Public readers have the government's confirmation that the documents were examined and its account of the competent authorities. Access to the substance of some supervisory work remains restricted, shaping how far outside scrutiny can follow it.

Another answer, published on 21 September and summarised by parliament on 12 October, clarifies two requests sent earlier in 2026. They concerned possible Epstein connections of significant owners of German banks and their reliability under ownership-control rules. The government said the information reviewed provided no indication that those owners were unreliable. The scope concerns bank ownership and must be distinguished from a review of the handling of Epstein as a customer. [S13](#source-S13)

An assessment of a bank owner and a review of customer monitoring address different conduct. A criminal investigation has its own evidentiary requirements. Following each institution's mandate makes it possible to identify where findings overlap and which authority can act on a particular failure.

Publication and regulatory access also have separate chronologies. Establishing when an authority received a particular record would help to assess the scope of its earlier work and any response to the November reporting. The competent institutions hold part of that history. Further disclosure could reveal how the evidence travelled from the bank to its supervisors.

## Reform has to change the decision

Deutsche Bank's 17 November statement says the matters reported are historical and points to €7 billion of direct investment in its control environment between 2019 and 2025. It describes a transformed institution with stronger risk management and acknowledges continuing remediation. These are the bank's own statements about its reform programme. [S11](#source-S11)

Spending measures the resources committed to controls. Their effectiveness depends on how people use them. Following an actual case from the first concern to the resulting action would show whether staff received a restriction they could apply and whether it changed the services available to the client. That is where an investment programme reaches the commercial relationship.

The ZDF reports that independent reviewers feared the customer base might contain other cases comparable in scale to Epstein. That warning concerns the possibility of a broader control weakness. It gives a reason to test the system across customers, with the findings and the review's limits kept visible. [S07](#source-S07)

Independent monitoring already formed part of the NYDFS order's response to the failures it described. The order also distinguished that administrative settlement from the responsibilities of other authorities. Assessing subsequent reform means following what happened under those arrangements, including the findings that returned to supervisors and the changes made in response. [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03)

Our interpretation of the accumulated record is that an exception becomes durable when each part of the institution can point to an earlier decision as sufficient authority to proceed. The challenge of reform is to make a new concern capable of reopening that decision. A customer file can contain the right facts for years while the practical permissions around it keep expanding.

The internal accounts add weight and specificity to the problem l0g analysed in August, especially the production of reassurance during an exit. Further documents could trace who had authority to stop the activity and how the relevant warnings reached them. The result would be a closer account of the point at which knowledge of the risk finally changed the services the client could obtain.
