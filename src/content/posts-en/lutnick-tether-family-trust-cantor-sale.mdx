**US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick sold his Cantor Fitzgerald interests to trusts benefiting his four children. Bloomberg reports that one trust borrowed from Tether, already a Cantor business partner. Public contracts shed light on the sale; the loan’s amount, terms and use remain undisclosed. The financial ties raise questions about impartiality, while a political quid pro quo remains unestablished.**

*Document-based investigation, information cut off on November 9, 2026. Statements by lawmakers and companies are attributed. No requests for comment were sent as part of this work.*

On October 6, 2025, Howard Lutnick signed a filing for the Securities and Exchange Commission. He disclosed that the sale of his interests in Cantor Fitzgerald and its controlling general partner had closed. The commerce secretary had formally severed an ownership link to the business that had defined his career. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s03">S03</a></sup>

The following day, according to Bloomberg's investigation published on March 18, 2026, a credit filing in New York identified Tether as a lender to a trust benefiting his four children. Senators Elizabeth Warren and Ron Wyden repeated the finding in an April 29 letter: the security interest reportedly covered all the assets of Dynasty Trust A, including more than half of Cantor's equity. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01">S01</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02">S02</a></sup>

A sale and a loan can be entirely routine estate-planning transactions. What makes this one worth examining is the reported lender’s overlapping commercial ties with the business being sold. Tether issues dollar-linked tokens, including [USDT](/en/glossary/usdt/), maintains substantial reserves and has a custody relationship with Cantor. Lutnick, meanwhile, joined an administration reshaping the rules for digital assets. His family’s reported credit relationship sits at the intersection of those interests. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s05">S05</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s08">S08</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s10">S10</a></sup>

The useful question is straightforward: **which economic ties survive the sale, and which government decisions could affect them?** Answering it requires following the contracts, their dates and their legal scope. A sale price, a loan and a reserve backing tokens are different kinds of money. Combining them would obscure the mechanism that needs explaining.

## The missing document is at the center of the story

The disposal is supported by a signed regulatory filing. The loan is described in an original press investigation subsequently cited by lawmakers. Warren and Wyden's letter points to Bloomberg for the trust's name, its beneficiaries and the extent of the collateral. Those repetitions are one source chain, rather than several independent confirmations. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01">S01</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02">S02</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s03">S03</a></sup>

Neither the loan agreement nor the original Uniform Commercial Code financing statement was obtained for this investigation. A UCC filing gives public notice of a security interest by identifying the debtor, secured party and collateral. New York law allows a financing statement to be filed before a security agreement is made. The reported October 7 filing date therefore cannot, on its own, establish when the loan was negotiated or funded. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s07">S07</a></sup>

Its principal, interest rate, maturity, precise lending entity and default provisions remain unknown. Whether it supplied money for the children's purchase of their father's interests is itself one of the senators' questions. Their letter also cites a Cantor spokesperson saying that the disposal involved several sources of financing. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02">S02</a></sup>

These gaps have consequences for the interpretation. A properly secured, market-rate loan arranged without the secretary's involvement could have an ordinary commercial explanation. Unusually generous terms, a personal guarantee from the seller or exceptional rights for the lender would require a different assessment. The public material examined does not resolve those alternatives.

A [trust](/en/glossary/trust/) holds and administers assets for designated beneficiaries. Benefiting from it, managing it and directly owning the shares are distinct roles. Similarly, a pledge of shares ordinarily protects a creditor in the event of default; it does not necessarily transfer voting rights immediately. To understand the power Tether might have acquired, the list of collateral must be read alongside the agreement's operative clauses.

## Before the loan, an established commercial relationship

In written answers associated with his January 29, 2025 confirmation hearing, Lutnick describes an investment made in April 2024 and says he participated in the negotiations: Cantor acquired convertible debt in the holding company that owns Tether’s stablecoin business. He distinguished that instrument from an existing equity stake and said its amount was private. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s05">S05</a></sup>

That distinction matters. [Convertible debt](/en/glossary/dette-convertible/) can become shares when specified conditions are met. Until then, its rights, expected returns and risks differ from those of ordinary equity. Congressional documents in 2026 refer to a 5% stake. We did not find a document in the reviewed material establishing when or on what terms the earlier instrument converted. Presenting 5% as an independently verified snapshot of the current ownership would exceed the evidence. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s05">S05</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s16">S16</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s22">S22</a></sup>

Custody adds a separate relationship: safeguarding or administering assets for a client in return for fees. The commercial customer, the investment exposure and the reported lender to the owners' family sit within the same network. Each connection nevertheless has its own contract and legal counterparties. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s05">S05</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s10">S10</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s22">S22</a></sup>

That concentration is the economic core of the investigation. When a large customer is also linked to a firm's investment value and the financing of its owners, a government decision affecting the customer can travel through several channels. Those channels identify a potential conflict to examine. Their existence does not show that anyone used them to influence the secretary.

## May, July and October tell different parts of the exit

The economic separation preceded the legal closing, according to the White House’s account. A limited waiver signed by the White House counsel on July 8, 2025 describes fixed-price sale agreements executed on May 16. According to that memorandum, subsequent changes in value and economic benefits belonged to the buyers, while legal ownership remained with the seller pending regulatory approvals. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s04">S04</a></sup>

The family’s May agreements also leave a trail in EDGAR. A May 16 contract for a CFGM voting share acquired for RGL Management Trust assigns the intervening dividends with the share and leaves the seller with the agreed consideration alone. Its definitions expressly refer to a separate agreement signed the same day with Dynasty Trust A. These records connect the May and October stages of the family transaction while leaving the reported Tether loan and the use of its proceeds unresolved. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s29">S29</a></sup>

The waiver temporarily allowed participation in certain matters of general applicability. It excluded specific-party matters involving Cantor, BGC or Newmark, domestic regulatory approvals for the sales, and meetings with representatives of those businesses. Its duration and scope were limited to the interests it described. It was not blanket approval of future dealings between the family and Tether. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s04">S04</a></sup>

This is substantive evidence against a simple account of uninterrupted personal enrichment. It documents the reasoning adopted by the White House. It does not, by itself, show how officials screened every sensitive matter in practice.

Newmark's accounts add a detail that could otherwise produce a spectacularly misleading headline. They disclose **$200,000 for the voting shares of CFGM**, Cantor's controlling general partner, acquired by trusts at the October 6 closing. That amount concerns a particular control instrument. It is not the aggregate purchase price of Cantor's economic interests. Nor does the reference to cash on hand identify the ultimate source of every dollar used by the buyers. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s06">S06</a></sup>

Assessing the separation therefore requires reading the May agreements, closing documents, credit agreement and trust instruments together. An ownership filing answers who holds the asset. Financing documents reveal whom its buyer owes and on what terms.

## Impartiality extends beyond the shareholder register

Federal ethics rules address several different situations. The criminal financial-conflict provision covers, among other interests, those of an official, a spouse and minor children, as well as certain professional relationships. Adult children's financial interests are not automatically attributed to the official in the same way. Other rules address apparent impartiality in matters involving specific parties, including cases involving a close relative, and provide for recusal or authorization. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s23">S23</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s26">S26</a></sup>

Those distinctions prevent both overstatement and premature reassurance. A benefit to adult children does not, by itself, establish a criminal conflict. An ownership transfer also does not settle every question about impartiality, especially where a close family relationship and a substantial commercial exposure overlap.

The documents that would clarify the position are practical ones: ethics advice, the precise scope of recusals, and records showing how they were applied. Personal guarantees, retained claims or other contractual rights would also matter if any existed. We have not established that Lutnick retained such interests. Their possible existence is a question for the contracts, not a fact to insert into the narrative.

## Why Washington has an interest in Tether

A dollar-linked [stablecoin](/en/glossary/stablecoin/) is a token whose issuer seeks to maintain a redemption value near one dollar. Issuance creates a liability to token holders while money received supports the reserve portfolio. Interest-bearing reserve assets can generate earnings for the issuer and service fees for its providers. Holding a token does not, in itself, make the customer an owner of the underlying Treasury bills. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09">S09</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s11">S11</a></sup>

At September 30, 2026, Tether International reported **$191.451 billion in assets and $183.496 billion in liabilities**. The excess was **$7.955 billion**, or **4.34% of liabilities**, calculated from the reported figures. Its October 30 release also reported $132.4 billion of direct and indirect exposure to U.S. Treasury bills. That last figure does not measure the assets entrusted solely to Cantor. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s11">S11</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s12">S12</a></sup>

<TetherLutnickFigure lang="en" kind="reserves" />

The scale helps explain the strategic interest. Tokens can extend dollar use beyond conventional banking and create demand for U.S. public debt. Supporting the industry therefore has an explicit economic-policy rationale, reflected in the January 23, 2025 executive order. That rationale belongs in the analysis alongside private interests. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s08">S08</a></sup>

The reserve report does, however, raise a precise question about the family financing. It includes **$14.175 billion of secured loans**, without identifying individual borrowers in the public document. We do not know whether the reported Dynasty Trust A loan falls within that reporting entity, elsewhere in the group or outside the reserve portfolio. The material obtained does not establish that USDT holders' reserves financed the disposal. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s11">S11</a></sup>

The lender's exact legal identity becomes decisive at this point. “Tether” can refer to a brand, a group of companies or the issuer covered by a particular financial report. Moving between those meanings without specifying the reporting boundary would manufacture an accounting connection.

## An audit has a defined job

Tether announced completion of its 2025 financial-statement audit on August 13, 2026. The next day, Reuters obtained direct confirmation from a KPMG US spokesperson that the firm had issued an unqualified opinion, under American Institute of Certified Public Accountants standards, on Tether International’s statements for the year ended December 31, 2025. The report had not been made public at the time of that account; we have not obtained the signed report and accompanying statements. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s13">S13</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s14">S14</a></sup>

BDO's quarterly assurance covers a dated snapshot of assets and liabilities. Its report specifies the accounting criteria and limitations; the final notes are outside the assurance scope. It is not a full audit of quarterly financial statements. Reserve backing, anti-money-laundering controls and a cabinet secretary's impartiality are different questions, requiring different evidence. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s11">S11</a></sup>

## The GENIUS Act creates a conditional route

The commerce secretary is a member of the presidential working group on digital assets, giving him a role in policy discussions. Licensing and supervisory powers are distributed across other institutions. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s08">S08</a></sup>

The GENIUS Act, enacted on July 18, 2025, provides a route for certain foreign issuers. It includes a Treasury finding that their home supervision is comparable, registration with the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, and U.S. liquidity requirements subject to arrangements specified in the statute. Compliance with lawful orders is also required. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09">S09</a></sup>

Two separate dates matter. The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), a US banking supervisor, confirms that the Act and its final rule take effect on January 18, 2027. Section 3(b)(1)’s three-year period concerns a particular restriction on offers or sales by service providers, subject to the statutory exceptions. As of November 9, 2026, the new regime’s effective date is still ahead. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09">S09</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s28">S28</a></sup>

Describing the statute as a general immunity for Tether would erase those conditions and the authorities charged with applying them. The investigable question is narrower: who advocated which provisions, relying on what analysis and following which contacts? A commercially favorable rule may reflect industrial policy, a regulatory compromise or favoritism. The benefit alone does not establish its cause.

On January 27, 2026, Anchorage announced that its nationally chartered bank had issued USAT under OCC supervision. Its announcement distinguishes the token from USDT and identifies Anchorage Digital Bank, rather than Tether Operations, as the issuer. Tether names **Cantor as the reserve custodian and preferred primary dealer**. The product has already launched; the new GENIUS framework takes effect later, on January 18, 2027. The announcements also specify the absence of a government guarantee and federal deposit-insurance protection. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s10">S10</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s27">S27</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s28">S28</a></sup>

That business gives concrete form to Cantor's interest in the new framework: additional reserves to safeguard can create additional service revenue. The fee schedule was not disclosed in the reviewed material. Quantifying a family gain, let alone attributing it to a particular intervention by Lutnick, would be premature.

## The Iran findings need their denominator

On September 28, 2026, Richard Blumenthal and the Democratic staff of the Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations published a report on USDT in Iran-linked financial networks. This is a minority-staff investigation with an identifiable methodology and conclusions attributable to its authors. It is not a court finding of criminal liability against Tether. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15">S15</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s16">S16</a></sup>

The dataset comprises **846 unique addresses**, selected through U.S. and Israeli sanctions designations and seizure measures from June 2021 to August 2026. The report says **84%** transacted exclusively or predominantly in USDT. Its detailed methodology defines predominant use as more than 80% of transaction value in dollars, using an approximate valuation approach. The result matters for that selected sample. It does not measure the illicit share of all USDT activity or USDT's share of Iran's entire financial system. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15">S15</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s16">S16</a></sup>

The Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) administers US Treasury sanctions; Israel’s National Bureau for Counter Terror Financing (NBCTF) targets terrorist financing. Both appear among the authorities whose actions informed the sample.

<TetherLutnickFigure lang="en" kind="denominator" />

The most directly testable allegation concerns freezing delays. The report says that late blocks affecting a group of wallets covered by an Israeli measure in June 2023 allowed **$34.6 million in USDT** to leave after the notice. We have not independently reproduced that analysis. Before judging individual delays, the signature, publication and notification dates should be compared with on-chain blocking events. The report itself acknowledges that some measures became public after they were signed. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15">S15</a></sup>

The relationship to Cantor also requires precision. A secondary-market USDT transfer can move existing tokens between wallets without generating a new dollar deposit at the reserve custodian. Claiming that Cantor automatically receives money whenever an illicit payment occurs would confuse the backing assets with token circulation. Relevant risks instead include scrutiny of the commercial partner, issuance and redemption flows, and financial exposure to its business. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s11">S11</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15">S15</a></sup>

The issuer retains technical blocking capabilities, reflected in Tether's publicly announced December 2023 policy. Blockchain circulation therefore does not eliminate every point of intervention. It changes where controls operate and makes the speed of response important. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s25">S25</a></sup>

## Evidence in Tether's favor belongs in the investigation

Cantor and the Commerce Department also defend the divestiture’s compliance. In its August 7, 2026 investigation, the International Consortium of Investigative Journalists (ICIJ) reports that their representatives told Bloomberg that Lutnick had complied with his ethics obligations. This attributed position belongs in the record; financing contracts and records of specific recusals remain necessary to assess it. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s30">S30</a></sup>

On September 28, Tether highlighted nearly **$550 million in Iran-linked USDT freezes it said it had supported during 2026**. That is a company figure we have not reconstructed. It cannot be subtracted from cumulative suspicious transaction volumes: blocked assets and repeated payment flows are different measures. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s17">S17</a></sup>

Evidence outside the company's own communications nevertheless confirms cooperation in some cases. On June 18, 2025, the Justice Department expressly thanked Tether for proactive assistance in a civil forfeiture action targeting more than **$225.3 million** connected to investment fraud. That case is separate from both Iran and the family loan. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s18">S18</a></sup>

In April 2026, Tether announced a freeze exceeding $344 million, followed by an OFAC designation update. More recently, on November 6, Treasury announced sanctions against eight Canadian money-service businesses accused of assisting Iranian networks. Those actions contradict a description of the administration as wholly inactive. They do not establish the adequacy of its particular oversight of Tether. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s19">S19</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s20">S20</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s21">S21</a></sup>

A balanced account must allow both possibilities to coexist: an issuer may cooperate effectively in some investigations and have shortcomings elsewhere; an administration may sanction intermediaries while remaining insufficiently transparent about its own conflicts.

## The evidence a political-protection allegation requires

On October 8, 2026, Blumenthal asked Cantor chairman Brandon Lutnick for documents on the firms' relationship, his father's disposal and possible Tether financing. The requested deadline was October 23. As of November 9, we had not located a complete public response or disclosure of the loan terms. Failure to find a response in open sources does not establish that nothing was provided to the senators. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s22">S22</a></sup>

A cover-up allegation requires additional evidence: intervention to stop an inquiry, an instruction to conceal information, a knowingly false statement, or some other documented obstruction. Congressional materials ask whether Tether may have benefited from political protection. The records examined do not establish that Lutnick ordered an investigation into the company dropped, or that a government decision was exchanged for the loan. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s02">S02</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s16">S16</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s22">S22</a></sup>

The inquiry can still move forward without waiting for a dramatic disclosure. First comes the legal lender and the credit agreement: pricing, collateral, default clauses and any seller guarantees. Next is the purchase financing: transfers, other lenders and Tether's precise contribution. Those dates can then be compared with ethics advice, meetings and regulatory decisions.

A commercially ordinary loan, effective separation of the seller's finances and documented recusals would substantially weaken the favoritism hypothesis. An unusual financial concession linked by specific communications to an official intervention would strengthen it. These are the records that would take the story beyond overlapping names and dates.

**The public record currently establishes a formal disposal, documents interlocking commercial interests and reports family financing that warrants clarification. Evidence of a political quid pro quo remains missing.** The public interest supports disclosure of the contracts and recusal arrangements. Those documents would show how far the announced separation actually went.

## Method and limitations

This investigation combines SEC disclosures, White House records, legislation, confirmation answers, a reserve report, congressional findings and competing public statements. The reported loan comes from a single original source chain, Bloomberg; congressional repetitions are identified as such. The original article was only partially accessible, and the underlying UCC document was not obtained.

All six pages of the waiver were visually checked. The BDO report was read in full, with visual inspection of the conclusion and asset-table pages. The PSI report was read through complete text extractions; its charts were not visually inspected. No figures were taken from automated chart interpretation: the values used also appear in report prose or an associated release. A May family agreement for RGL is public. The signed KPMG report, full Dynasty sale agreement, financing contracts and private trust documents remain unavailable. The Senate's blockchain analysis has not been independently replicated.

This investigation uses public sources. No requests for comment were sent. Established records, attributed statements, interpretation and unresolved questions are distinguished throughout. The opening illustration was created with a generative image tool and is a symbolic composition.
