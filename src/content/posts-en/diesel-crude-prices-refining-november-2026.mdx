Between 30 October and 13 November 2026, the average price of diesel in mainland France fell from **€2.3706 to €2.1229 a litre**. For a 50-litre fill, that meant a saving of **€12.39**. Brent crude fell **24%** in euros over the same reporting window, while diesel at the pump fell **10.4%**. The gap opens well before the filling station. Wholesale diesel delivered to Rotterdam was down **10.3%**. [S01](#source-S01) · [S03](#source-S03)

## Refined diesel holds up as crude falls

The French energy ministry’s weekly figures let us trace all three prices. Spot [Brent](/en/glossary/brent/) fell from **€110.04 to €83.65 a barrel**, while the Rotterdam wholesale diesel benchmark moved from **112.6 to 101.0 euro cents a litre**. French pump prices are surveyed on Fridays and weighted by reported volumes. The market quotes are averages from Friday through Thursday. The figures track the same stretch of the market on slightly different schedules. At the start and end of the comparison, the published exchange rate is the same to the precision shown. Daily EIA prices corroborate weekly Brent averages in dollars for both dates. [S01](#source-S01) · [S03](#source-S03) · [S04](#source-S04) · [S22](#source-S22)

<DieselNovemberFigure lang="en" kind="transmission" number={1} />

A barrel of crude yields several products. Distillation splits the crude into fractions. Conversion and treatment units let refineries adjust the mix and produce fuels that meet specifications. Diesel is a **middle distillate**, as is kerosene. A refinery needs the right crude and spare processing capacity to make more of it. When stocks are low or deliveries slow, the distance from a refinery to its customers also matters. [S06](#source-S06) · [S17](#source-S17) · [S18](#source-S18)

That difference shows up in the International Energy Agency’s October figures. Gulf crude exports excluding Oman had recovered to roughly **82%** of their level before the Middle East conflict. Gulf exports of refined products and liquefied petroleum gas were still around half their pre-war level. The latter covers a basket of fuels and gives a sense of the supply constraints facing refined products. [S05](#source-S05)

## Cheaper crude can widen refining spreads

A [crack spread](/en/glossary/crack-spread/) is the price difference between a refined product and a benchmark crude, expressed in the same units. It can widen when the fuel price falls more slowly than crude. As the European Central Bank points out, transport, insurance and labour costs sit between market prices and the profit a refiner ultimately earns. The mix of products a refinery sells also matters. [S12](#source-S12)

TotalEnergies offers an example from a different period. From the second to the third quarter of 2026, its average Brent price fell from **$103.8 to $97.0 a barrel**, while its European refining margin indicator rose from **$13.5 to $33.2 a barrel**. The company bases the indicator on a basket of crudes, products and variable costs. It covers more than diesel, and the quarters precede the pump-price comparison here. [S10](#source-S10) · [S20](#source-S20)

## The taxes in a litre of diesel

On 13 November, the pre-tax price derived from the mainland French pump survey was **116.16 euro cents a litre**. The excise duty added **60.75 cents**. VAT at **20%** applies to the sum of the two, adding **35.38 cents** after rounding. That brings the price to **212.29 cents** at the pump. [S01](#source-S01) · [S07](#source-S07) · [S16](#source-S16)

<DieselNovemberFigure lang="en" kind="litre" number={2} />

If the pre-tax price falls by 10 cents under the same tax rates, the pump price falls by 12 cents. The extra two cents come from lower VAT. The excise duty remains a fixed amount per litre, raising the starting price against which the saving is measured. In the ministry’s figures, the pre-tax price fell **15.1%**, compared with **10.4%** at the pump. [S01](#source-S01) · [S03](#source-S03) · [S07](#source-S07) · [S16](#source-S16)

The gap between the Rotterdam quote and the French pre-tax price narrowed from about **24.2 to 15.2 cents a litre**. The Rotterdam benchmark already includes cost, insurance and freight to the point of delivery. The French gap includes inland transport, storage, regulatory obligations and distribution. For the retailer, profit is whatever remains after the relevant costs are paid. [S01](#source-S01) · [S03](#source-S03) · [S04](#source-S04) · [S21](#source-S21)

## Emergency stocks change the cost of some deliveries

The French government announced the release of **10 million barrels of diesel** from strategic stocks on **7 October** and launched the programme on **1 November**. It is already refined and still has to be delivered, as we explain in our investigation into [how oil reserves reach the pump](/en/analysis/oil-reserves-crude-diesel-contents-delivery/). Under the 23 October decree, sales take place at weighted average acquisition cost where the sale order specifies those terms. The rules require any pre-tax financial advantage to be passed through in full to consumers by **31 March 2027**. [S08](#source-S08) · [S09](#source-S09) · [S23](#source-S23)

France’s consumer and competition authority notes that a sale of stocks below market prices can bring down the apparent gross margin in its monitoring. Its method differs from the energy ministry’s weekly survey, but both face the same issue. A wholesale quote offers a benchmark, while actual purchase costs depend on where the fuel came from and the terms of supply. The timing allows for the stock release to have contributed to the fall at the pump. How much reached drivers remains an open question. [S01](#source-S01) · [S08](#source-S08) · [S21](#source-S21)

## Wholesale diesel is the next price to watch

If wholesale diesel keeps falling and the French pre-tax price stops following it, purchase schedules, transport costs and competition between distributors are worth examining. A longer series and data for individual filling stations would help distinguish those explanations. The November figures already show where the fall in crude prices lost momentum: in the market for refined diesel, before the fuel reached the pump. Our [guide to the oil market](/en/guides/read-oil-market/) explains how to follow the benchmarks. [S01](#source-S01) · [S03](#source-S03) · [S15](#source-S15)

Sources were consulted on **20 November 2026**. The latest French survey used here covers 13 November and was published on 16 November. Prices are nominal. The cover image is an AI-generated editorial illustration. [S01](#source-S01)
