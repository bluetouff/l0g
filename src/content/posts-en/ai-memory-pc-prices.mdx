A computer bought for homework, email and a family photo collection can share an industrial problem with the machines running artificial intelligence. Both depend on memory. Somewhere between the factories making those chips and the shop selling the laptop, the demand from data centres can reach an ordinary household’s budget.

France offers a useful place to follow that connection. In September 2026, the official price index for computers, laptops and tablets was **6.2% higher than a year earlier**. Yet it was only **0.5% above September 2024**. The rise followed a decline, and keeping both comparisons in view changes the story. [R01](#source-r01)

Insee, France’s national statistics office, links the increase in computer prices during the first half of 2026 to component supply tensions associated with the development of AI. Suppliers’ disclosures and computer makers’ accounts help explain how that pressure travels. They describe production being redirected, capacity promised in advance and selling prices adjusted to meet higher costs. The evidence establishes a route from AI investment to consumer computers. Calculating how much of a particular receipt belongs to that route requires more information. [R02](#source-r02) [R05](#source-r05) [R12](#source-r12)

## The French curve has a dip

A percentage change depends on where the comparison starts. September 2025 was a low point in the French series. Measuring from there makes the subsequent rebound look steep; adding another year shows the ground on either side. The chart retains all 33 monthly observations from January 2024 to September 2026. This is a price index, with the average for 2025 set to 100. September 2026’s reading of 102.38 is a position on that relative scale. The category also includes tablets, a detail worth remembering when comparing the series with news about the PC industry. [R01](#source-r01) [R16](#source-r16) [R17](#source-r17)

Insee aims to track prices at comparable quality. That matters for products whose specifications change frequently. A new version of a laptop may have more memory, a different screen or a larger drive. A buyer can pay more and receive more equipment at the same time. The statistical task is to distinguish the value of those changes from the movement in price, using methods suited to the products being followed. [R03](#source-r03)

This can create a gap between the household’s experience and the inflation measure without either being wrong. A family has a cash budget. If the cheapest suitable replacement offers better specifications at a higher price, the family still needs the extra money. An index asks a different question about the price of comparable goods. A maker’s [average selling price, or ASP](/en/glossary/asp/) answers yet another question about the mix of machines actually sold.

The time window matters too. The average index for January–September 2026 was **2.1% higher** than for the same nine months of 2025. September’s year-on-year increase was 6.2%, while the index fell **1.3% between August and September 2026**. These calculations use the published monthly observations, with no seasonal adjustment applied here. A persistent industrial constraint can coexist with short-term movement in shop prices. [R01](#source-r01)

## Two jobs hidden inside the word memory

Start with a file. While a computer is working on it, the processor needs quick access to the relevant data. Working memory provides that space. In an ordinary PC, this is generally [DRAM](/en/glossary/dram/), or dynamic random-access memory, a form of memory that loses its contents when power disappears. The file also needs somewhere to remain after the machine is switched off. In a solid-state drive, or [SSD](/en/glossary/ssd/), [NAND](/en/glossary/nand/) flash provides that persistent storage. [R15](#source-r15) [R18](#source-r18)

The distinction is familiar once translated into everyday tasks. Working memory is the space available for what the machine is doing now. Storage is the place where documents, photographs and applications are kept. Increasing one solves different problems from increasing the other. Both can appear under “memory” in discussions of the semiconductor market, which makes the industrial story easy to muddle.

Many AI accelerators use a specialised form of DRAM called **[high-bandwidth memory](/en/glossary/hbm/)**, or HBM. Its stacked chips and many connections move large amounts of data to and from the processor. Bandwidth describes how quickly data can move; capacity describes how much can be held. HBM and the DDR memory used in PCs belong to the DRAM family, while taking very different forms inside a machine. [R14](#source-r14) [R18](#source-r18)

The competition emerges earlier, in manufacturing. Chips begin on silicon discs called [wafers](/en/glossary/wafer/). In June 2024, Micron gave a revealing estimate: producing a given number of bits in HBM3E consumed approximately **three times the wafer supply** needed for DDR5, using the same technology node. The comparison specifies a generation of memory, an equal quantity of bits and a common manufacturing generation. [R04](#source-r04)

<PcAiMemoryFigure lang="en" kind="wafers" number={2} caption={"Micron estimates that HBM3E uses roughly three times the wafer supply of DDR5 for the same number of bits on the same technology node."} sources={["R04","R14"]} method={"Source: Micron prepared remarks, 26 June 2024, p. 6. Each drawn disc represents one relative unit of wafer resources; three discs express the approximate 3:1 ratio. Product drawings are schematic and imply no number of modules produced per wafer. This is the manufacturer's estimate for HBM3E versus DDR5. It does not apply to NAND, selling prices or power consumption."} />

Those conditions give the figure its meaning. With a limited pool of production resources, supplying more HBM can leave less scope for other DRAM products. The finished products have different forms and connections; their industrial relationship lies in the resources and choices upstream. The three-to-one estimate describes wafer requirements; converting that into a retail price would require further information about manufacturing yields, assembly, contracts and demand.

Storage follows a separate branch. NAND has its own production processes and markets. Data centres need SSDs as well as working memory, so they can exert pressure on that branch too. SK hynix’s October results described growth across HBM, server DDR5 and enterprise SSDs. Several kinds of memory and storage are being pulled towards data centres, each through its own industrial decisions. The HBM3E–DDR5 wafer comparison applies to its stated pair. [R10](#source-r10) [R15](#source-r15)

## Capacity has customers before the chips arrive

The allocation decisions appear in company accounts. For its fiscal year ended 3 September 2026, Micron reports that quantities shipped by its Mobile and Client business, measured in bits, declined as supply was redirected to other business units. That is a specific change in where output went, giving substance to the broader claim that AI demand competes with other uses. [R05](#source-r05)

Crucial makes the decision more recognisable to anyone who has upgraded a computer. On 3 December 2025, Micron announced that it would leave the Crucial consumer business, with consumer shipments scheduled through the end of its fiscal second quarter, in February 2026. Management cited demand from data centres and its intention to serve larger strategic customers. It also announced continued warranty service and support. The annual report makes clear that Micron still supplies components to third-party module and SSD makers. A familiar consumer brand can disappear while its parent’s chips keep reaching consumers through other companies. [R08](#source-r08) [R05](#source-r05)

The timing of a purchase also runs much further ahead than the order on a retailer’s website. In December 2025, Micron said it had completed price and volume agreements covering its entire calendar-2026 HBM supply. At that point, these were commitments for future supply. A PC maker negotiating months later faces an industry whose production plans already carry promises to other customers. [R06](#source-r06)

Samsung’s results, published on 29 October 2026, describe a similar emphasis on AI-related products under capacity constraints. The accounts point in the same direction, although they come from suppliers with a commercial interest in presenting demand as durable. Their disclosures are most useful when placed beside independent price statistics and the accounts of the companies buying the components. [R09](#source-r09)

This is also why securing a quantity and securing a price are separate achievements. Dell describes long-term supplier arrangements under which some future prices remain subject to negotiation. A computer maker can gain confidence that it will receive components while retaining uncertainty about what it will pay for them. [R12](#source-r12)

## Factories follow a slower calendar

New capacity needs buildings, equipment, production qualification and a gradual increase in output. On 30 September 2026, Micron’s timetable envisaged first wafers from its ID1 plant in Idaho around mid-2027, output from new NAND capacity in Singapore in the second half of 2028, and first wafers from its initial New York fab in 2030. These were company targets, followed by further time to ramp production. [R07](#source-r07)

The delay explains how large investment announcements and tight supply can exist together. Money committed today changes future possibilities. It takes time to change the number of usable chips available for the next computer production run. Long-term purchasing agreements help bridge that gap, giving the supplier a basis for investment and the customer a claim on future output.

The economics can also change while those agreements are in force. In its September presentation, Micron said higher HBM prices agreed for 2027 would narrow the margin gap with conventional DRAM. It also reported that a greater HBM share had weighed on one division’s margin in the quarter just ended. The immediate profitability ranking can therefore differ from the strategic importance of a product. [R07](#source-r07)

A factory’s future rests on existing commitments, customer relationships, production preparation and expected demand, alongside current margins. The financial incentive operates across several time horizons. That helps explain why a shift towards AI-related supply can persist through changes in relative profitability.

Prices themselves have different calendars. TrendForce’s public summary for **specialty DRAM**, dated 30 October, described spot prices retreating from their peaks while contract supply remained tight. This observation concerns the market covered by that report. It illustrates how immediate transactions and negotiated supply can move differently. Assessing PC memory as a whole requires evidence covering that broader market. [R13](#source-r13)

## A component cost passes through several decisions

Between a wafer and a laptop sits a chain of businesses. Memory makers sell chips; other suppliers can turn those chips into modules or drives; computer makers obtain parts and assemble systems; distributors and retailers bring them to buyers. Some links are integrated or bypassed. The important point is that the price crosses several contracts before it becomes a household expense. Micron’s continuing component sales after its Crucial exit provide a concrete example of those intermediate routes. [R05](#source-r05)

At each stage, decisions change the timing and size of the effect. Inventory acquired earlier can delay exposure to a higher replacement cost. A supplier agreement can settle volume ahead of price. A manufacturer can accept a lower margin, raise the price of an unchanged machine, reduce a discount, alter the configuration or devote more of its range to expensive models. These are possible responses, whose actual importance has to be established company by company.

The component also enters a much larger bill. Depending on the machine, that bill includes a processor, screen, battery, casing and other parts, alongside assembly, distribution and commercial costs. The proportion exposed to a particular memory shock varies with the specification and the buyer’s purchasing terms. A percentage increase in the price of memory therefore acts on one part of the total cost.

Dell explicitly links memory cost inflation to strong AI demand and capacity limitations in its filing for the quarter ended 31 July 2026. It describes higher average selling prices and lower unit volumes in its Client Solutions business. This provides evidence from the receiving end of the component market: a computer maker reports the pressure and its commercial response. [R12](#source-r12)

Other influences remain in the picture. HP discusses currency movements and a Windows-based replacement cycle alongside commodity costs and product mix. A buyer in France also encounters the local tax basis, retailer competition and promotion calendar. The mechanism becomes convincing when evidence from different stages fits together. Its numerical precision remains limited by the information published at each stage. [R11](#source-r11)

## The average-price trap

HP offers a particularly clear example. In the quarter ended 31 July 2026, its Personal Systems division reported an **average selling price up 40.8%** from a year earlier, **PC unit volume down 15.8%**, and **revenue up 18.5%**. These are worldwide business figures reported in dollars. HP attributes the average-price movement to pricing actions, currency and a shift towards higher-value products. [R11](#source-r11)

<PcAiMemoryFigure lang="en" kind="average" number={4} caption={"HP Personal Systems: unit volumes −15.8%, average selling price +40.8%, revenue +18.5%. The average combines pricing, currency and the products actually sold."} sources={["R11"]} method={"HP Form 10-Q signed 26 August 2026: Personal Systems, three months ended 31 July 2026 versus three months ended 31 July 2025. Worldwide business, revenue in current US dollars. Reported changes converted to indices, comparable 2025 period = 100. Multiplying rounded factors gives 1.185536; the chart retains the reported revenue increase of 18.5%. Portfolio ASP is not constant-specification inflation and cannot be compared directly with the French index."} />

An average can rise because comparable products become more expensive, because expensive products make up more of the sales, or through both changes together. Consider an imaginary shop selling a €600 computer and a €1,200 computer. Equal numbers of each produce an average of €900. If three-quarters of its sales shift to the €1,200 model, the average becomes €1,050 while both price tags stay unchanged. These figures are chosen solely to illustrate product mix.

The example explains why the 40.8% figure needs its accompanying explanation. It describes what happened to the average amount received per machine across a changing set of sales. Reading it as the price increase for an unchanged laptop would lose the mix effect. Comparing it directly with France’s 6.2% would also erase differences in geography, time period, product coverage and method.

Operating margins also followed different paths: HP’s Personal Systems margin fell year on year, while Dell’s Client Solutions margin rose. These worldwide business segments cover different activities and product mixes. Their accounts show that firms respond differently; isolating how much each passed on to buyers would require a more detailed cost comparison. [R11](#source-r11) [R12](#source-r12)

## Passing a component-cost increase into a PC price

An illustrative calculation makes the transmission easier to see. Imagine a computer selling for **€1,000**, with **€150 of initial component costs** exposed to a price shock. **All amounts in this example exclude tax.** Hold the other inputs fixed and suppose the affected cost doubles, adding €150. If 60% of that extra cost reaches the customer, the price increases by **€90**, to **€1,090**.

Every input in that example is chosen for teaching purposes. The €150 is an assumed component cost, and the 60% is an assumed pass-through rate. The relationship is simple: **extra selling price = initial affected cost × percentage cost increase × fraction passed through**. A doubling is a 100% increase, so the middle factor equals one.

The calculation separates three questions often compressed into a headline. How much of the original cost is exposed? How sharply does that cost rise? How much of the increase reaches the final price? A large shock to a small component share can have a limited effect on the total. A more modest shock to a larger share, passed through fully, can matter more.

Putting real numbers into that relationship would require the manufacturer’s component purchase costs for the particular machine, the timing of replacement stock, the relevant cost changes and the commercial response. The public documents do not reveal that complete record model by model. The calculator below lets readers change the assumptions and inspect the result; it remains a scenario tool.

The same reasoning accommodates changes in configuration. A new generation can contain more memory, raising the bill even if the cost per unit of memory stays constant. A manufacturer can compress its margin. Weaker demand can prompt discounts. Those choices help determine the eventual price alongside the quantity of silicon under the keyboard.

## Industrial choices and the household budget

Putting a precise AI surcharge on French computers would require a detailed cost and price history, together with a credible estimate of what prices would have done without the additional demand. The available documents reveal allocation decisions and commercial responses, while Insee tracks prices at comparable quality. The contribution of each cause remains unquantified. [R01](#source-r01) [R02](#source-r02) [R05](#source-r05) [R11](#source-r11) [R12](#source-r12)

The next readings of the same price series, changes in purchasing conditions and the actual ramp-up of announced capacity will add to that evidence. A spot quotation, a supplier’s average selling price and a household price index each describe a different part of the market. Keeping their units, dates and product boundaries attached is essential to reading them together.

For someone replacing a computer, a precise comparison starts with the work the machine must do, its configuration and the conditions of sale. For someone following the industry, it starts further upstream, with which customers have reserved capacity and which products the factories are preparing to make. AI demand connects those two perspectives. The price beside an ordinary laptop is one place where the decisions meet.
