From springs made in China to a car returned at the end of its lease, the funding changes hands several times. Public accounts and contract terms reveal the handovers and the risks still to be allocated.

A BYD DOLPHIN SURF Boost electric car is advertised in France at €219 a month in November 2026. The offer fits neatly into a headline. Behind it sit several businesses that have already committed money: making components, assembling the car, shipping it, holding inventory and financing just over four years of use. The driver joins a chain of cash advances that began much earlier. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-12" title="S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup>

To understand who finances the car, follow two things: the vehicle and the claim to payment. Sometimes they move together. Often their timetables diverge. A supplier may have delivered its springs while still holding a promise of future payment. A dealer may sell the car to a finance business that leases it to the driver. After the final rental payment, there will still be a vehicle to sell.

Public documents illuminate these handovers. BYD’s accounts at 30 September 2026 show a sharp rise in inventory, changes in payment instruments and positive operating cash flow. A Chinese stock exchange filing offers a view from one supplier. The French advertisement provides a rental schedule. Some of the contracts connecting these pieces remain private. This investigation reconstructs the documented mechanisms. Tracing an identified car from its factory to a French home would require private contracts. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.4,10,12,18" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-04" title="S04, p.29" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-12" title="S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup>

## The journey ties up capital

Financing starts before the sale. A component on a production line, a car at sea and a vehicle waiting at a dealership all represent money committed to the business. [Working capital](/en/glossary/bfr/) is the funding tied up in this operating cycle: inventories and amounts owed by customers absorb resources, while supplier credit and some customer advances provide them. The length of the commercial journey matters alongside the number of vehicles travelling through it.

At 30 September 2026, BYD reported RMB221.6 billion of inventory, up from RMB138.4 billion at the end of 2025. The company attributed most of the increase to its overseas automotive business. Its half-year report had already disclosed inventory turnover of approximately 109 days in the first half of 2026, against 79 days in the first half of 2025, citing international expansion and lengthy sea transport among the reasons. That is the company’s explanation. The 109-day measure covers consolidated inventory under its own calculation method; it tells us little about the crossing time of an individual car. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.4" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.10" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-02" title="S02, p.42" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup>

The scope matters from the outset. BYD’s accounts include activities beyond cars, including electronics. The accounts follow China’s Accounting Standards for Business Enterprises (ASBE). The figures cover the worldwide group in renminbi, and the 2026 interim accounts are unaudited. Applying them directly to the French dealership network would create false precision. They can, however, show the financial effort being made by the group expanding that network. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.20" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-02" title="S02, p.61–62,72–74" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup>

The cash picture contains an instructive contrast. From January to September 2026, operations generated RMB54.5 billion, compared with RMB40.8 billion in the same nine months of 2025. Yet cash and cash equivalents in the cash-flow statement fell from RMB68.4 billion at the start of the period to RMB29.8 billion at its end. Net investing and financing outflows, together with exchange-rate movements, more than absorbed the operating inflow. The waterfall below reconciles the two balances exactly. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.18–20" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

The chart uses a specific accounting category. Other financial assets appear on the balance sheet and require a separate assessment of liquidity, risk and availability. The RMB29.8 billion balance therefore cannot represent the group’s entire financial capacity. Operating cash flow already records collections from customers and payments to suppliers: trade credit is embedded in it. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.10" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.18" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-03" title="S03, p.302" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup>

One conclusion survives a careful reading. BYD is funding an expansion that ties up more capital while continuing to generate cash from operations. To understand how that advance is shared, the next useful perspective is a supplier’s.

## The supplier’s time has a price


An unusually useful account comes from 华纬科技, a Chinese spring manufacturer listed in Shenzhen under stock code 001380. In a response to the exchange released on 30 June 2026, it described BYD’s payment terms: one to two months of commercial credit after invoicing, with settlement through DiChain instruments with maturities of two to six months, or through bank acceptances. Those terms describe stages in the payment process. Simply adding the two ranges would manufacture an average collection delay that the filing does not measure. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-04" title="S04, p.5" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

The filing describes DiChain as an electronic receivable certificate. Its holder has a claim payable on a specified date. The supplier can retain it until maturity, transfer it to another supplier willing to accept it, or obtain financing against it. [Factoring](/en/glossary/affacturage/) turns the receivable into available cash under a contract and at a cost. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-04" title="S04, p.30" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

The choices become tangible when the spring maker buys steel. It says its steel suppliers seldom accept DiChain, preferring cash or bank acceptances. A claim that moves readily between some trading partners can encounter resistance at the next step. A business needing money for steel must then find another source of cash, wait until maturity or finance the claim. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-04" title="S04, p.29" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>


<BydFinancingFigure lang="en" kind="supplier" />


During the first quarter of 2026, the supplier received RMB133.74 million of new DiChain instruments. Its outstanding balance reached RMB183.26 million at 31 March. In the same filing, it reported no overdue unpaid BYD or DiChain receivables. Both observations matter: money remained committed to a claim with a future maturity, while the supplier reported no overdue unpaid receivables within the scope disclosed. This individual case predates the regulatory tightening in September. It documents a mechanism and its use at a particular time. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-04" title="S04, p.30" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-04" title="S04, p.31" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

A receivable confirmed by a large buyer can also help a smaller supplier obtain financing. If a lender considers that claim safer than an unsecured loan to the supplier, it may offer better terms. The outcome depends on the financing price, security, recourse and whether participation is voluntary. The documents reviewed provide no representative DiChain financing rate for BYD suppliers in November 2026.

The instruments themselves need to be distinguished. A bank acceptance commits a bank to pay a bill at maturity; a commercial acceptance commits a company. Discounting can release money before maturity on the financier’s terms. An electronic receivable certificate is a different legal category. These instruments arrange payment at a future date, with different rights and risks. Financing them can bring forward the availability of cash. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-19" title="S19" aria-label="Source 19">19</a></sup>

## Beijing wants settlement to deliver cash sooner

That distinction helps explain the direction of Chinese policy. BYD announced in June 2025 that supplier payment terms would be limited to 60 days. Assessing such a pledge requires three details: when the clock starts, what the supplier receives as payment, and when the money becomes available. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-20" title="S20" aria-label="Source 20">20</a></sup>

Revised rules protecting payments to small and medium-sized enterprises took effect on 1 June 2025. For purchases by large businesses from small and medium enterprises (SMEs), they provide for payment within 60 days of delivery, while allowing a different, reasonable contractual period consistent with industry practice. They regulate acceptance procedures and prohibit forcing SMEs to accept bills or receivable certificates, or using those instruments to artificially extend payment terms. Applying these provisions to a particular contract requires checking the parties’ status and the agreement itself. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-05" title="S05, articles 9–11" aria-label="Source 5">5</a></sup>

A notice signed on 3 September 2026 puts greater emphasis on cash availability. It encourages large companies to commit to paying SMEs in cash within 60 days of delivery. For payments to SMEs by government bodies, public institutions and large companies, cash is required where the contract specifies no payment method. Newly issued electronic certificates are capped at six months, and banks and commercial factoring companies may not provide financing for certificates with longer maturities. A specific requirement for payment entirely in cash applies to state-owned enterprises within its stated scope. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-06" title="S06, paragraphs 1–7" aria-label="Source 6">6</a></sup>

The notice also proposes a financial route: support loans and bond issuance that allow large buyers to replace trade payables and pay SMEs in cash. The intended mechanism is straightforward. The large buyer borrows, the supplier collects, and a lender takes on the funding period. The same industrial activity can then show less supplier credit and more financial debt. That is the policy’s logic; its specific effect on BYD still needs to be established. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-06" title="S06, paragraph 10" aria-label="Source 6">6</a></sup>

Payment indicators demand similar care. A February 2026 survey by the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers reported an average of roughly 54 days across 17 carmakers. Its measure includes settlement by bank acceptance and acknowledges different starting points. This industry-wide result describes the survey’s convention. Measuring the wait for available cash at a particular BYD supplier would require different evidence. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-08" title="S08" aria-label="Source 8">8</a></sup>

## The accounts show a change in the form of credit

Figures released on 29 October 2026 demonstrate the danger of following a single balance-sheet line. BYD’s trade payables fell from RMB186.7 billion to RMB172.7 billion between December 2025 and September 2026. Over the same period, bills payable rose from RMB22.5 billion to RMB78.9 billion. Combined, the two balances increased from RMB209.2 billion to RMB251.6 billion, a rise of RMB42.4 billion. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.12" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>





Bills therefore account for more of the credit visible in this balance-sheet snapshot. BYD itself attributes their growth to greater use of bills in settlement. The quarterly publication leaves the split between bank and commercial acceptances, detailed maturities and individual invoice histories unspecified. Connecting that increase to a conversion of DiChain claims or to the September reform would require further documents. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.4" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.12" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Customers contribute funding too. Contract liabilities reached RMB92.9 billion at 30 September, up from RMB51.5 billion at the end of 2025. This category records obligations to deliver goods or services against payments received or due before the contract is performed. BYD attributes the increase mainly to automotive advance payments. It is a worldwide consolidated balance. The share attributable to deposits from French retail customers is unknown. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.4" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01, p.12" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-03" title="S03, p.266" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup>

The manufacturer thus benefits from different timetables on either side of the business: some suppliers wait for payment, while some customers advance money. Together they help fund the operating cycle. Changes in these balance-sheet amounts do not translate one for one into a cash-flow bridge: currency movements, non-cash transactions and other adjustments can intervene. Adding them to operating cash flow would count some of the same resources twice.

The balance sheet documents a financing structure. Attributing a share of the French rental payment to credit granted by a Chinese supplier would require more: the marginal cost of that funding, the other sources available, and evidence of how any savings reach the selling price. The public documents examined do not provide those inputs.

## The border brings another bill

A car entering the European Union from China then encounters another funding requirement. Under the framework checked on 6 November 2026, new Chinese-origin battery electric vehicles qualifying for BYD’s individual rate face a 17% countervailing duty, alongside the ordinary 10% customs duty on cars. The individual rate requires, among other conditions, a compliant invoice. Coverage depends on the product, origin and manufacturer; the nationality of a brand alone is insufficient to establish the customs treatment. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-09" title="S09, article 1" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-10" title="S10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

An indexed example makes the calculation visible. With a value at the border of 100, the two duties add 10 and 17 respectively. The value after duties is 127. Both rates apply to the same starting value. The example describes that customs assumption and its conditions. It leaves the transfer price, port of entry and amounts actually paid for the advertised DOLPHIN SURF unspecified. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-09" title="S09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-10" title="S10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>





Value-added tax (VAT) introduces a crucial distinction between cost and cash. In France, the import VAT base includes customs duties and certain additional expenses. Those expenses are set to zero in the simplified example, so 20% of 127 gives 25.4. An importer registered for French VAT with full deduction rights can report and deduct that amount simultaneously through the reverse-charge mechanism. The corresponding net VAT is then zero, with no cash advance for this tax. Customs duties remain part of the import cost. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-11" title="S11" aria-label="Source 11">11</a></sup>

The French selling price will also depend on transport, distribution, discounts, margins and commercial decisions. A customs tariff describes a levy on importation. Its eventual impact on the customer depends on those adjustments. The contracts and declarations needed to measure them car by car are outside the public evidence collected here.

## The dealer’s inventory needs funding

Once a car has arrived on the market, it still needs a driver. During that wait, a dealer may use its own money, obtain payment terms or draw on an inventory finance facility, commonly called a [floor plan](/en/glossary/floor-plan/). The facility finances vehicles held in stock. Its cost depends in part on the amount drawn and how long it remains outstanding.

CGI Finance does market funding for new and used vehicle inventories, with interest charged on the portion used. Its documentation establishes that the product exists. The research found no public confirmation that it finances the whole BYD network in France, nor the dealers’ specific credit limits, borrowing periods or security arrangements. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-14" title="S14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a></sup>

Published partnerships nevertheless show the part European banks play in selling the cars. Crédit Agricole Auto Bank and BYD announced a French agreement on 14 April 2025, with offers for individuals and businesses from May. The November 2026 DOLPHIN SURF advertisement, meanwhile, makes the offer subject to approval by CGL, operating under the CGI Finance brand and part of Société Générale. These documents establish particular relationships and products. The distribution of financed volumes between partners remains undisclosed in this evidence. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-15" title="S15" aria-label="Source 15">15</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-12" title="S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup>

When a finance business purchases a vehicle for lease, it changes the seller’s timetable: the sale price can be collected while the driver pays over the following months. The car moves from commercial inventory into a rental fleet. The sale, inventory finance and lease contracts determine the actual dates and each party’s obligations. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-13" title="S13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a></sup>

## The first payment changes the arithmetic

Return to the offer for orders placed between 1 and 30 November 2026. It covers a DOLPHIN SURF Boost on a long-term rental contract, known in France as LLD, for 49 months and 40,000 kilometres in total at participating dealers. The initial rental payment is €1,865 including VAT. A €365 incentive under France’s energy-saving certificate scheme (CEE) reduces the eligible customer’s share to €1,500. There are then 48 monthly payments of €219. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-12" title="S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup>

The eligible customer’s advertised rental payments add up to €12,012: €1,500 + 48 × €219. Before applying the incentive, the sum is €12,377. The initial amount is an increased first rental payment. The advertisement excludes insurance and optional services; running costs and any contractually payable return charges need to be considered separately. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-12" title="S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-18" title="S18" aria-label="Source 18">18</a></sup>


<BydFinancingFigure lang="en" kind="lease" />


The CEE incentive adds another small source of funding. The offer says the dealer advances it towards the first rental payment. France’s energy-saving certificate system places obligations on energy sellers, which fund eligible actions or acquire certificates. For this offer, the advertisement specifies the amount and the dealer’s advance, while leaving the payment timetable between intermediaries undisclosed. Treating the €365 automatically as a direct government budget payment would misdescribe the scheme. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-12" title="S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-17" title="S17" aria-label="Source 17">17</a></sup>

CGL is named as the business approving the application. The public wording identifies that participant; the contract offered to the customer must specify the lessor and the detailed commitments. The €12,012 total measures the advertised rental payments on the example’s terms. At the end of an LLD contract, the customer returns the vehicle according to the agreement. Those payments therefore purchase use over time. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-12" title="S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-13" title="S13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-18" title="S18" aria-label="Source 18">18</a></sup>

## After the final payment, a car remains

Long-term rental separates ownership from use. The owner finances the purchase; the driver pays to use the vehicle. French public guidance describes the institution buying the car and retaining ownership during the lease. Once the rental payments have been collected, the vehicle still has economic value. Its eventual resale is part of the transaction’s economics. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-13" title="S13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a></sup>

Residual value is the estimated value of that vehicle at the end of the contract. All else being equal, expecting higher resale proceeds reduces the depreciation that must be covered during the lease and can support lower payments. The actual calculation also depends on the lessor’s acquisition price, funding, services, expenses and margin. Subtracting rentals from the public retail price would be insufficient to recover either the residual value or an interest rate. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-16" title="S16, p.386" aria-label="Source 16">16</a></sup>





Risk appears when resale brings in less than expected. Ayvens discusses this general exposure and the monitoring of its fleet values in its 2025 universal registration document. That source explains a leasing company’s economics; it provides a framework, without measuring the risk of CGL or the particular DOLPHIN SURF offer. A buyback guarantee or sharing agreement could transfer some or all of the shortfall to another party. The research found no public confirmation of such a commitment for this offer. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-16" title="S16, p.133–134" aria-label="Source 16">16</a></sup>

The driver has separate contractual obligations: rental payments, mileage, return condition and any agreed services. A broad decline in used-car prices and a charge for exceeding the mileage allowance are different risks. Their allocation is set out in the lease and the agreements between the businesses involved. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-18" title="S18" aria-label="Source 18">18</a></sup>

The car has several financial lives. Its manufacturer awaits the commercial proceeds. The supplier waits to collect its receivable. A lender finances a period of time. The lessor relies partly on the value of an asset that will keep being driven. One vehicle connects those positions, each with its own due dates and protections.

## Competitiveness also lives in the contracts

The investigation establishes a shared funding process: trade credit in China, the manufacturer’s resources, possible bank financing of dealer inventory, then rental payments and the vehicle’s future value in France. Recent accounts show rising inventory and a larger bills-payable balance. They also show stronger operating cash generation. The supplier case documents a claim payable in the future alongside a declaration of no overdue unpaid receivables. These findings point towards assessing the quality and cost of the financing arrangements; a single outstanding balance is an inadequate basis for diagnosing a liquidity crisis. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-01" title="S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-04" title="S04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

The strongest qualification to the argument comes from that diversity of funding. A large buyer can improve its suppliers’ access to finance; a bank can make acquisition affordable; a solid resale value can lower the cost of use. Judging how the benefits are shared would require actual financing rates, the time until cash is collected, buyback agreements and prices paid between businesses. Without them, a quantified contribution from Chinese suppliers to the French monthly payment would be speculative.


Future disclosures will be useful if they allow inventory, operating cash flow and payment maturities to be read together. In France, lease and repurchase contracts would clarify the risk each party retains. The central question is practical: who pays for the time spent in inventory, and who absorbs a weaker resale price?
