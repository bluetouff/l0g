China’s exports rose **36.3% year on year in September 2026**, measured in current US dollars. Imports increased by 31%. Released on 14 October, the figures suggest a powerful acceleration in cross-border business. Days earlier, the World Trade Organization had raised its forecast for 2026 merchandise trade volume growth from 1.9% to 3.9%. Investment linked to artificial intelligence is central to its explanation. <sup><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup>

To understand the expansion, an order needs to be followed beyond its first buyer. A chip can cross a border, become part of a circuit board, travel again inside a server and support a computing service sold somewhere else. Several companies earn revenue and several economies record exports. Yet their business may depend on the same investment programme. Diversifying trade routes and diversifying ultimate sources of demand offer different kinds of resilience.

The documents assembled for this investigation illuminate that common exposure without providing an exact global measure. They also contain evidence that complicates a simple concentration story. In NVIDIA’s latest quarterly comparison, fewer direct customers exceed the 10% revenue disclosure threshold. Indirect demand remains much harder to observe. Part of the risk lies in the distance between those two views. <sup><a href="#source-7" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a></sup>

## The sources of trade growth are shifting

The WTO attributes **47% of the increase in global merchandise trade value in the first half of 2026**, compared with the same period of 2025, to goods that enable AI deployment. Trade in those goods grew by 67% year on year. The category includes products such as semiconductors and servers. The 47% figure concerns the increase in trade: it identifies the source of momentum. <sup><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup> The basket represents **14.8% of world merchandise trade value** in that period, a different denominator from its contribution to growth.

A category can explain much of an increase while accounting for a considerably smaller fraction of the total. The contribution also depends on what happens to other products. When some trade flows weaken, the contribution of the fastest-growing group becomes larger. This is evidence about the concentration of the recent impulse, which deserves to be distinguished from the composition of the whole economy.

**Value and volume** need to be kept separate too. Dollar-denominated trade combines prices and quantities, exchange-rate effects and changes in the mix of goods sold. Substituting more powerful, more expensive machines for ordinary equipment can raise the trade bill without a proportional increase in the number of units shipped. The WTO’s 3.9% forecast instead refers to a volume index. Putting those percentages on the same scale would obscure their different meanings. <sup><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-12" aria-label="Source 12">[12]</a></sup>

China’s release provides a practical illustration. The official 14 October briefing transcript reports September export growth of **29.6% in yuan**, whereas the dollar-denominated report gives 36.3%. Both are nominal comparisons with the same month a year earlier. The gap calls for attention to currency conversion; it says nothing by itself about the physical expansion of shipments. <sup><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a></sup>

The AI label also needs a scope check. The WTO revised its product list to focus more closely on deployment, removing some general-purpose goods and infrastructure inputs predominantly used outside AI. The basket remains a selection of customs products, rather than an itemised register of each final user’s purchases. A customs classification identifies the object shipped more readily than its eventual use. <sup><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup>

## Components travel through the new destinations

Chinese customs reports that intermediate goods accounted for **more than half of export value in the first nine months of 2026**. These are goods used in a further stage of production. The same briefing reports increased trade with more than 180 economies, a measure covering imports and exports together. These government statements describe the reach of the network and China’s role in successive stages of production. The distribution of ultimate demand remains a separate question. <sup><a href="#source-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a></sup>

A business shipping components to a Southeast Asian assembler may have changed its customs destination while still serving orders from the same final markets. Another may genuinely have found new local customers. Rising shipments to a region can accommodate both stories. Distinguishing them requires links between products, importing companies, processing activities and subsequent exports.

Taiwan’s figures show why the distinction matters. September exports reached **$87.222 billion**, up 60.9% on the year. Two groups, information, communication and audio-video products, and parts of electronic products, together accounted for **$70.259 billion**, or **80.6% of total exports** after rounding. These categories extend well beyond AI. They nevertheless establish the scale of Taiwan’s electronics specialisation. <sup><a href="#source-4" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a></sup>

A separate breakdown shows the United States taking 31.5% of exports, mainland China and Hong Kong 23.6%, and ASEAN 21.6%. ASEAN is the association of Southeast Asian nations. These are trade destinations: goods can be consumed, installed as productive equipment or processed further there. The attachment does not supply the detailed cross-tabulation needed to connect each product category to each destination. <sup><a href="#source-4" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a></sup>

Our analysis of [semiconductor bottlenecks](/en/analysis/semiconductors-a-stack-of-constraints/) separates fabrication, memory and assembly: rising shipments do not establish that every production stage has equal spare capacity.

This changes the design of a resilience test. A local transport disruption may be bypassed by reorganising routes. A slowdown in one investment category can affect several routes at once. The first question concerns alternative capacity. The second concerns the independence of the demand being served.

## One investment can leave several customs records

Consider a wholly fictional supply chain, using one common monetary unit. A manufacturer in economy A produces a chip with 40 of value added and exports it to B for 40. The business in B adds 30 through labour, services and operating surplus, then exports a board to C for 70. Server integration in C adds another 30. The completed server is sold for 100 to a business in D, which uses it as productive equipment.

Assume that all transactions occur in the same period, with no inventories, taxes or other transactions. Gross exports sum to **210: first 40, then 70, then 100**. Final investment expenditure is **100**. Value added is distributed as 40, 30 and 30 across the three producing economies. Each border correctly records the value of the product passing through it; previously incorporated value travels with the product.

<TradeCycleFigure lang="en" kind="borders" number="03" sources="5 6" caption="A fictional chain turns 100 of final spending into 210 of successive gross exports. The 100 of value added is distributed across three producers.">
<details><summary>Method and scope</summary><p>l0g simulation: four economies, one period, no taxes, inventories, separately priced transport or other intermediate purchases. Export prices are 40, 70 and 100; value added is 40, 30 and 30. The initial producer is assumed to use no purchased input. The final server is investment in economy D. No actual supply chain is quantified.</p></details>
</TradeCycleFigure>

Within the example, every transaction is genuine. Each pays a business for productive activity. The analytical mistake would be to treat the three shipments as three independent final purchasing decisions. A change to the project in D can reach suppliers in A, B and C. The chain distributes income during the expansion, then transmits adjustments when its ultimate market changes pace.

This is the problem addressed by the OECD’s **Trade in Value Added**, or **[TiVA](/en/glossary/tiva/)**, approach. It reconstructs contributions by economies and industries using tables of their purchases from one another and of final demand. Final demand includes consumption and investment in equipment. This provides a view of interdependence that gross shipment records alone make difficult to interpret. <sup><a href="#source-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a></sup>

The 2025 edition described in OECD country notes covers 1995–2022. In April 2026, the OECD also published experimental estimates for 2023 and 2024. These newer data remain aggregated; they do not reconstruct AI users’ September 2026 orders. Estimating a precise fraction of Chinese exports dependent on a few investors would require additional information and an explicit updating method. The fictional chain explains an accounting identity without estimating that fraction. <sup><a href="#source-6" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-13" aria-label="Source 13">[13]</a></sup>

There is also a scope limitation: standard TiVA treats a server purchase as final investment. Tracing the value added embodied in the equipment does not, by itself, identify later users of computing services. A new OECD framework extends the analysis to capital used in production, without identifying private customers in 2026. <sup><a href="#source-6" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a></sup>

Our analysis of the [US trade deficit, from the port to the factory](/en/analysis/us-trade-deficit-imports-august-2026/) follows the same distinction between equipment purchases, inventories and domestic output.

## The visible buyer and the demand behind it

NVIDIA’s quarterly filing provides evidence at another scale. For the three months ended **26 July 2026**, its second quarter of fiscal 2027, the company reports that one direct customer accounted for 16% of revenue. A year earlier, two customers represented 23% and 16%. US accounting rules require information on customers accounting for at least 10% of revenue, without requiring their identities. This is the benchmark used in our chart, rather than a map of every buyer. <sup><a href="#source-14" aria-label="Source 14">[14]</a></sup> The relevant customers are unnamed in this section. <sup><a href="#source-7" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a></sup>

The comparison shows fewer large direct customers in the published shares above that threshold. It does not measure a reduction in overall concentration. The latest disclosure weakens a story based solely on growing concentration among major direct buyers. Shares below the threshold can be distributed in many ways: an “other customers” segment in a chart represents several buyers, never a single one. These disclosures cannot produce a complete concentration index or establish which customers are the same across years.

The filing adds information that ordinary trade accounts would struggle to show. By NVIDIA’s estimate, **one AI research and deployment company contributes meaningfully to its revenue by purchasing cloud services from its customers**. The company thus distinguishes direct purchases from indirect demand. It provides neither an identity nor a usable percentage for this particular customer. <sup><a href="#source-7" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a></sup>

A relationship diagram can explain the mechanism without assigning any role to a real company. One user spreads its computing requirements across several cloud providers. Those providers purchase equipment through manufacturers or integrators, or directly from a processor designer. There are several buyers at the first invoicing layer. Further downstream, part of their business may rely on the same programme. Conversely, one large direct customer can serve thousands of users with unrelated needs. Both possibilities have to remain visible.

NVIDIA’s statement establishes a measurement problem and a disclosed indirect dependency. It leaves the exact share of that demand, its stability and the contractual allocation of adjustments unknown. It cannot establish one universal customer structure for the entire global industry.

A further convention matters when matching sources. **NVIDIA assigns geographic revenue according to the headquarters of its direct customer**. Shipment locations and ultimate users may be elsewhere. Mechanically matching this table to Taiwanese or Chinese customs data would create spurious precision. The sources become useful together when their respective units of observation are preserved. <sup><a href="#source-7" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a></sup>

## Inventories can magnify a change in pace

An order also passes through inventory decisions. Companies buy to meet expected sales and maintain a buffer. When both objectives change, the movement reaching a supplier can be larger than the change observed at the final customer. Daniel Rees and Phurichai Rungcharoenkitkul describe this amplification mechanism, often called the **[bullwhip effect](/en/glossary/effet-fouet/)**, in their 2021 BIS bulletin on bottlenecks. The text expresses the authors’ views. That episode illustrates a logic, rather than supplying a coefficient that can be applied automatically to AI in 2026. <sup><a href="#source-9" aria-label="Source 9">[9]</a></sup>

A second fictional example isolates the arithmetic. An assembler normally sells 100 units per period. It starts with 40 in inventory and wants to finish with 40, so it receives 100 from its supplier. For the next period it expects 90 sales and chooses to reduce its ending buffer to 20. With 40 already available, it needs new purchases of only **70**. Receiving 70 and selling 90 indeed leaves 20.

Sales fall 10%; purchases fall 30%. The difference comes from the additional reduction in desired inventory. Had the business retained its 40-unit target, it would have bought 90. The example therefore contains two explicit decisions: a lower sales forecast and a smaller buffer. No comparable movement is being attributed to an actual company.

<TradeCycleFigure lang="en" kind="inventory" number="05" sources="9" caption="Expected sales of 90 instead of 100, combined with an inventory target cut from 40 to 20, bring new purchases down to 70. The supplier receives an amplified signal.">
<details><summary>Method and scope</summary><p>l0g simulation in homogeneous physical units, over one period, with no lead times, losses, returns or outstanding orders. Closing inventory = opening inventory + purchases received − sales. Baseline: 40 + 100 − 100 = 40. Adjustment: 40 + 70 − 90 = 20. Retaining the original buffer would imply purchases of 90. BIS documents the general mechanism, not these parameters.</p></details>
</TradeCycleFigure>

Each stage can then respond to its own information. Some contracts cushion changes in the short run; others permit deliveries to be deferred. Shipments can continue even after new orders weaken. Customs statistics record goods actually crossing a border, potentially well after the investment decision that set them in motion.

The timing matters when reading industrial announcements. Delivered equipment, reserved capacity and a purchase planned for a future period occupy different places in the chain. Combining them into one headline amount erases the points at which schedules can still change. An investigation of order books needs delivery dates, deferral clauses and inventory movements, rather than an assumption that current revenue fixes future demand.

## From technology orders to macroeconomic exposure

At its 13 October briefing, presented on a page published the following day, the IMF describes the technology boom as a demand impulse, particularly evident in trade and investment. It also highlights supply-chain concentration across countries, industries and companies. That structure allows a localised impulse to travel far, while creating common dependencies. <sup><a href="#source-10" aria-label="Source 10">[10]</a></sup>

The potential transmission can be traced without forecasting a crisis. Suppose several purchasers revise their capacity needs. New orders slow; assemblers then adjust procurement and inventories. Suppliers defer some of their own investment. Depending on the local value-added share, wages, profits or tax receipts become weaker than expected. In an exporting economy, foreign-currency receipts may also slow, with the consequences depending on associated imports, available financing and other export industries.

The important word is **common**. A company can diversify direct customers, and an economy its destinations, while retaining exposure to the same shift in technology spending. The exposure may be large or small; available accounts do not measure it fully. The case of [Crux AI’s chip collateral and bank risk](/en/analysis/crux-ai-google-blackstone-bank-risk-chip-collateral/) examines another connection: contracts and expected revenue supporting repayment of financed equipment. The implication is to test together activities whose revenues depend on the same ultimate decisions.

There is also a path towards broader demand. Different users served by the same infrastructure can gradually stabilise revenue. Better software and organisation can make installed equipment more productive. In his 23 March speech, Philip Lane emphasises the role of AI diffusion in delivering productivity gains. The pace of hardware purchases and the pace of economic benefits can consequently follow different trajectories. <sup><a href="#source-11" aria-label="Source 11">[11]</a></sup>

## Evidence that would change the assessment

The current expansion deserves to be taken seriously. NVIDIA announced $96.2 billion of revenue for its quarter ended in July. Equipment has been sold and capacity deployed; some of that investment may deliver useful services for years. September’s trade increases and the WTO revision are also documented outcomes or forecasts, on their respective scales. <sup><a href="#source-8" aria-label="Source 8">[8]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup>

The favourable scenario would gain credibility from sustained growth in the user base, equipment actually put to work and benefits spreading beyond infrastructure suppliers. Stronger activity in sectors with little connection to the technology cycle would also diversify the expansion. Productivity improvements could support income or reduce prices, depending on competition and the distribution of gains. Those channels can extend growth beyond the initial installation phase. <sup><a href="#source-11" aria-label="Source 11">[11]</a></sup>

A concerning signal would be more specific than one weak export month: converging cancellations or deferrals, inventory accumulating despite weaker expected sales, followed by lower orders at suppliers across several economies. Each observation would require its own scope check. A new hardware generation, price changes or a delivery problem can produce temporary movements quite different from a broad weakening of final demand.

The investigation ultimately proposes a way of reading the expansion. Borders show where goods move. Company accounts show who receives the invoice. Assessing resilience requires following the uses, commitments and decisions that trigger those purchases. **World trade can acquire new routes while remaining tied to the same rhythm of orders.**
