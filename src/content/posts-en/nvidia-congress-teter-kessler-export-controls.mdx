**The new letter to Tim Teter puts Nvidia’s dealings with Washington at the centre of the inquiry. Its chief lawyer is asked to attend a closed-door, transcribed interview on 2 December about chip smuggling, military use by adversaries and reported corporate intervention in [export-control policy](/en/glossary/controle-export/) and investigations. Congress is seeking an account it can put alongside the government’s decisions.** [S03](#source-S03)

Teter’s requested appearance follows one for former CEO Jensen Huang on **1 December**. Separately, House Foreign Affairs chair Brian Mast asks Jeffrey Kessler, the senior Commerce official responsible for industry and security, to testify at a hearing on **3 December**. The letters, dated **12 November**, bring the company’s leadership, legal department and regulator into the same inquiry. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02) [S04](#source-S04)

That changes the centre of attention. Huang’s conduct triggered the crisis, but the testimony now requested concerns how a strategic supplier dealt with the officials overseeing its trade. The question is whether Congress can reconstruct the chain from a concern about a customer to an administrative response, with enough detail to identify who made each decision.

## A deadline to confirm attendance

The Huang and Teter letters are signed by Mast, China Select Committee chair John Moolenaar and Intelligence Committee chair Rick Crawford. Both seek written confirmation by **8 p.m. EST on 13 November**, equivalent to **01:00 UTC on 14 November**. The chairs say a subpoena will follow if the recipients fail to cooperate. Kessler’s separate invitation is signed by Mast and requests a hearing rather than the interview format proposed for the two Nvidia figures. [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03) [S04](#source-S04)

The confirmation deadline should focus attention on the next correspondence. An acceptance could settle attendance while leaving other arrangements to negotiate. A refusal or unanswered request could lead the chairs to take the compulsory step they describe. The letters themselves establish the requests and the threatened escalation, while any subpoena issued would become a further document to examine.

A transcribed interview would create a written record that investigators could compare with correspondence and later testimony. House rules provide powers to obtain evidence, while the Congressional Research Service distinguishes voluntary requests, compulsory subpoenas and enforcement. [S11](#source-S11)

The requests also deserve to be read individually. Huang is asked to explain events during his leadership. Teter’s letter reaches directly into the company’s interactions with export-control policy and investigations, including Megaspeed. Kessler would be questioned from the government side about enforcement, chip diversion and reported interference by private companies. Their different responsibilities could help Congress compare accounts of the same sequence. [S02](#source-S02) [S03](#source-S03) [S04](#source-S04)

## Teter and Kessler connect the company to the regulator

Bloomberg reported on **9 November** that Teter called Kessler in late summer **2025** to ask whether the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) was involved in Singapore’s detention of Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, citing people familiar with the matter. Nvidia said Teter was then unaware of Jensen Huang’s affair and described its contacts as information-sharing and requests for guidance. BIS defended its enforcement as following the rules. [S10](#source-S10)

The useful comparison would start with the request made by the company and the response it received. A phone call could seek information, challenge an allegation or ask for an action. A later change in an investigation might follow that contact, reflect evidence already under review or arise from another decision entirely. Establishing the sequence requires the records showing the purpose of the exchange and how officials handled it.

Investigators could compare correspondence with internal referrals, briefing material and the reasons recorded for subsequent decisions. Dates would show whether an official acted before or after receiving particular information. The identity of the decision maker would establish who had authority to respond. An explanation given at an interview could then be checked against a contemporary document instead of relying entirely on a witness’s recollection.

<NvidiaCongressFigure lang="en" kind="records" />

This is why Teter’s appearance could be consequential beyond the founder’s personal account. A company’s legal department deals with how information is presented to authorities and how regulatory decisions are challenged. Kessler’s testimony could explain what the agency received and how it applied its powers. Hearing both accounts would give Congress a way to test the administrative process described in the reporting.

The letters’ references to smuggling and interference express the concerns driving that scrutiny. Their eventual evidentiary value will come from the answers and supporting records. Our earlier examination of [Singapore’s requests and the US response](/en/analysis/nvidia-after-huang-singapore-washington-ai-financing/) explains why matching those records across jurisdictions is essential to understanding Megaspeed’s role.

## Lutnick agrees to a voluntary appearance

The Senate is pursuing its own questioning. Banking Committee chair Tim Scott has requested Huang’s appearance on **3 December** and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick’s on **10 December**. These dates come from the committee’s public statements. [S05](#source-S05)

According to NBC News, Commerce spokesperson **Kristen Eichamer** has confirmed Lutnick’s agreement to appear voluntarily before Senate Banking. Scott’s request supplies the proposed date of 10 December, while the arrangements for the session remain to be confirmed. [S06](#source-S06)

For Lutnick, testimony would move the debate from competing political statements towards an account of his department’s actions. Senators can ask how warnings were circulated, how export requests were assessed and how enforcement concerns were handled. The overlap with the House requests could allow officials’ explanations to be compared across different settings.

## Trump backed Huang before proposing Cook

The political setting has also become clearer. The New York Times reports that Trump called Huang on **10 November** and encouraged him to fight for his job, citing **two people briefed on the conversation**. Trump and the White House did not respond concerning the call, and Huang’s representative provided no comment. Reuters relayed the Times’ account. [S07](#source-S07)

That account places the president behind the incumbent shortly before his departure. Nvidia’s board accepted Huang’s resignation as President, CEO and director on **11 November**, then announced the transition on **12 November**. Later on the day of the announcement, Trump publicly proposed Tim Cook as his replacement. The public preference and the reported private encouragement belong to different stages of the same week. [S08](#source-S08) [S14](#source-S14)

The sequence complicates a simple theory in which Trump set out to remove Huang. It is consistent with an attempt to preserve a valued corporate ally, followed by an effort to influence the discussion after his departure. The actual selection remains the board’s responsibility under Nvidia’s bylaws. A presidential suggestion can carry political weight while leaving the candidate’s willingness and directors’ decision to be established. [S09](#source-S09)

## New leadership inherits promises and choices

Colette Kress remains interim CEO while continuing as CFO, with Harvey C. Jones chairing the board. Nvidia says its permanent search will consider internal and external candidates. A longer interim, an internal appointment and an external recruitment would each require a workable division of responsibility over technology, customers and finance. The announced search supplies those routes without settling the choice. [S08](#source-S08)

The financing consequences depend on how the next leadership team uses that authority. An operator building an AI site needs equipment and construction funding before it earns revenue from computing services. A lender may take comfort from a major customer’s purchase commitment or a guarantee, then examine the delivery conditions and payment triggers that make the support usable. A more cautious approach to future commitments could mean owners contributing more equity or projects taking longer to secure funding.

Existing commitments have their own contractual lives. Nvidia’s CoreWeave capacity agreement and its SB Energy project guarantees cover different obligations, with different conditions. A change of CEO transfers responsibility for supervising them. Any revision, termination or payment requirement still has to be assessed under the relevant agreement. Our [fourth instalment examines those contracts and their financing links](/en/analysis/nvidia-after-huang-singapore-washington-ai-financing/). [S12](#source-S12)

Export permission has a separate legal basis. **15 CFR 750.8** allows licences to be revised, suspended or revoked. That general power gives officials an administrative lever whose exercise must be checked against an actual decision. For a proposed project, both the commercial promise and the necessary permissions would therefore matter to financing. [S13](#source-S13)

Alongside the interviews, the chairs seek passage of the AI OVERWATCH Act, Chip Security Act, MATCH Act and Remote Access Security Act through the National Defense Authorization Act, the annual defence authorisation bill. [S01](#source-S01) A separate scenario would link public support to a defined Nvidia industrial project. Any public stake would require an identified legal authority, funding and negotiated rights tied to the capacity sought. Our [fifth instalment on state influence](/en/analysis/nvidia-trump-tim-cook-lutnick-state-leverage/) examines those routes, including the difference between backing a project and acquiring rights across a company.

The next documents can make this inquiry more concrete: confirmations of attendance, notices fixing sessions and testimony that can be matched to contemporary records. Those materials could show how the company and the government handled the same concerns. The departure of Nvidia’s founder leaves those decisions, and the officials responsible for explaining them, open to scrutiny.
