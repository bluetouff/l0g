**Anthropic’s national-security agenda also shapes its commercial opportunities. To represent its interests in Washington, the AI developer has turned to a firm whose team includes Marco Rubio’s former chief of staff.** Federal lobbying disclosures put that relationship on the record. Their value lies in the people, institutions and mechanisms they make visible. [S01](#source-S01)

Six quarterly reports from **Continental Strategy** record **$740,000 in declared lobbying income from Anthropic**, covering the second quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. **Alberto Martinez** appears in each one. The latest adds the State Department to the institutions listed. Together, the filings document a continuing mandate on AI development and national security. [S02](#source-S02)

This standalone investigation extends our examination of [Anthropic’s governance and the powers of the state](/en/analysis/ai-governance-anthropic-state-ownership/) and [the financing provided by its suppliers](/en/analysis/anthropic-supplier-financing-ipo/). It follows another strategic resource: the ability to argue for technical and commercial choices before the institutions writing the rules.

## A mandate beginning in April 2025

The initial registration, form **LD-1**, identifies **Anthropic PBC** as Continental Strategy’s client. The form gives **24 April 2025** as the registration’s effective date. Signed on **7 May**, the form names two lobbyists, Alberto Martinez and Paul Calkins Jr., for “AI development and national security.” That is a broad issue description, with no particular bill or individual decision identified in the field. [S01](#source-S01)

Martinez lists his former position as chief of staff to Senator Marco Rubio. The Government Publishing Office’s **February 2016** *Congressional Directory* independently confirms him in that role. Continental’s current biography presents him as its Washington managing partner and describes a long political association with Rubio. The earlier office is thus supported by an institutional record alongside the firm’s account of its experience. [S04](#source-S04) [S05](#source-S05)

A client can find practical value in that background. Former congressional staff understand procedures, policy trade-offs and how to present an issue to public officials’ teams. An intermediary can turn a company’s technical concerns into a brief suited to a department or congressional committee.

<AnthropicLobbyingMandate2026 lang="en" />

The US transparency framework, the [Lobbying Disclosure Act, or LDA](/en/glossary/lda/), requires disclosure of clients, issues and certain former public positions held by lobbyists. Rubio’s name appears in the registration because of that employment-history requirement. Subsequent quarterly reports identify the institutions listed for the mandate, generally at the level of a department or congressional body. [S01](#source-S01) [S03](#source-S03)

## Reported income across six quarters

The total comes from six figures: **$90,000** in the second quarter of 2025, **$90,000** in the third and **$110,000** in the fourth. Continental then reports **$150,000 per quarter** for each of the first three quarters of 2026. That adds up to **$290,000 for 2025** and **$450,000 from January through September 2026**. All six original files were located in the House of Representatives’ official XML archives. [S02](#source-S02)

<AnthropicLobbyingRevenue2026 lang="en" />

LDA guidance gives these figures a specific accounting meaning. A firm reports lobbying income earned or accrued during the quarter, including amounts billed or paid later. The figures are estimates rounded to the nearest **$10,000**. The $740,000 total therefore adds together rounded disclosure values. It describes the firm’s income attributable to this client, without allocating pay among the individual lobbyists. [S03](#source-S03)

The distinction also matters when assessing Anthropic’s overall lobbying effort. A company’s in-house expense disclosures include payments to outside firms under the applicable instructions. Simply adding Continental’s income to Anthropic’s reported lobbying budget could count the same spending twice. Political contributions belong to different disclosures and require a separate assessment. [S03](#source-S03)

The move from $90,000 to $150,000 shows an increase in reported quarterly income. It establishes the mandate’s continuity and financial scale.

## The institutions listed change over time

The **second-quarter 2025** report lists the Senate, House, Executive Office of the President and Treasury. The next quarter adds Commerce and the Office of Management and Budget, the White House budget office. Reports for the fourth quarter of 2025 and first quarter of 2026 list Congress and the Executive Office of the President. [S02](#source-S02)

The **second-quarter 2026** filing lists six institutions: the Senate, House, **National Security Council**, **Treasury**, **Commerce** and **Defense**. The third-quarter report names Treasury, Commerce, the Executive Office of the President and the **State Department**. The issue description remains the same throughout. [S02](#source-S02)

The team expands too. **Carlos Trujillo** joins the two names carried through 2025 in the first quarter of 2026, followed by **Caroline Farr** in the third. The reports disclose Trujillo’s former role as US ambassador to the Organization of American States and Farr’s former advisory role at Treasury. Several institutional backgrounds thus form part of the same client’s representation. [S02](#source-S02)

These lists map the mandate’s administrative reach. Identifying the officials approached and dividing the work among team members requires more detail. The State Department’s appearance in the third quarter gives researchers a specific line of inquiry: the officials involved and the requests they received.

## Anthropic already advocated restrictions on chips

The developer’s public policy program is more detailed than the lobbying forms. In its **6 March 2025** submission to the Office of Science and Technology Policy, or **OSTP**, Anthropic called for stronger controls on advanced chips and model weights, the parameters produced through training. Its recommendations included controlling **H20 chips**, Nvidia products intended for the Chinese market, and increasing resources for BIS, the agency responsible for US export controls. [S06](#source-S06)

The submission also proposed agreements between governments to prevent smuggling and a lower threshold for access without a license under the **AI Diffusion** framework then in place. These were requests made in March 2025. Their date and regulatory setting matter when comparing them with rules published later. [S06](#source-S06)

This submission predates Continental’s mandate. Anthropic had already put its preference for tighter controls in writing. On **23 July 2025**, responding to the US AI Action Plan, the company again criticized the policy reversal allowing H20 exports to China and argued for maintaining controls. [S07](#source-S07)

The position has a clear economic dimension. Access to chips determines the computing resources available to competing labs. Restricting some sales can affect how quickly Chinese developers advance their models while narrowing the market available to US chipmakers. The policy choice therefore cuts across industrial interests within the technology sector. Anthropic explicitly grounds its request in national security.

Our [analysis of BIS and remote cloud access](/en/analysis/nvidia-bis-remote-access-cloud-controls/) follows a different layer of control: the customers using the machines from a distance. Read together, the records show the stakes of these debates for AI labs. The documents examined here establish Anthropic’s positions and its lobbying mandate. Tracing a particular regulatory provision to that work would require the exchanges involved in drafting it.

## National security also creates public-sector demand

Anthropic’s OSTP submission goes beyond restrictions. It recommends closer cooperation between companies and security agencies, greater energy capacity and faster federal adoption of AI. Its July response also welcomes measures supporting infrastructure, procurement, and defense or national-security applications. [S06](#source-S06) [S07](#source-S07)

From the developer’s perspective, these requests form a coherent industrial policy. Government can limit some competitors’ access to critical resources, facilitate infrastructure construction in the United States and become a customer itself. Risk prevention, technological sovereignty and commercial expansion can therefore sit within the same brief.

That convergence makes incentives worth examining. A company can sincerely defend a security objective and benefit from the policy it proposes. Assessing the relationship means following the specific requests, the administration’s responses and the contracts awarded. LDA filings identify the actors representing those interests. Public submissions set out an agenda connecting access to computing resources, security needs and government demand for AI services.

## Political connections need a chronology

Rubio’s support for a tougher technological stance toward China predates this mandate. On **29 February 2024**, he and Democratic Senator Mark Warner called for stronger controls in critical sectors. Their letter focused principally on biotechnology and used semiconductors as a precedent. It establishes an earlier policy orientation, with a scope that did not specifically address the H20. [S08](#source-S08)

The chronology helps place the network in context. An earlier professional relationship makes a route of access plausible. A client funds continuing representation. Public proposals identify the policy choices it wants. Establishing that an intermediary shaped a particular decision requires a dated request, its recipient and the administrative response.

The record is substantial on its own: **a disclosed mandate, six original reports, an expanding team and a public program combining chip controls with federal markets**. It offers a concrete way to examine AI developers’ participation in rulemaking.

## Limits

These are the firm’s disclosures, cross-checked against official files available on the access date. They provide rounded amounts, issues and institutions at a level of detail insufficient to reconstruct every contact. The research obtained no account of a Martinez–Rubio meeting for Anthropic, no Rubio instruction tied to this mandate and no document connecting the firm to Huang’s departure. Anthropic’s texts establish its public program. Measuring its influence on a rule or contract requires the exchanges and decisions belonging to that particular case.
