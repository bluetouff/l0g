In the simulation published by the OFCE on 16 October, households at the bottom of the living-standard distribution lose about €100 on average in 2027, compared with about €500 at the top. Measured against their resources, the ranking reverses: the loss amounts to roughly 0.8% of the first group’s living standard, against 0.35% for the last. These two views of the same budget proposals describe very different experiences of the burden. The economic consequences then depend on adjustments between everyday purchases, saving and postponed expenditure. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Pierre Madec’s analysis estimates a **€8.7 billion reduction in household disposable income in 2027** for the measures it examines, relative to its reference scenario. It provides a useful map of their initial incidence. Assessing the associated risk means following the money through consumption, businesses and public revenue, while also considering how a fragile debt trajectory could raise financing costs. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a>, <a href="#source-S13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a>, <a href="#source-S15" aria-label="Source 15">15</a></sup>

The investigation starts, however, with a timing problem. The simulation covers measures from the government’s original proposals, some of which the National Assembly had already removed before the study appeared. The figures below retain their status as results for that scenario. Parliamentary developments are identified separately, using the documents available on 20 October 2026. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a>, <a href="#source-S06" aria-label="Source 6">6</a>, <a href="#source-S07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

## A figure tied to a particular version of the budget

France’s finance bill and social security financing bill were introduced on 1 October. The finance bill included a €3,000 sub-ceiling on the portion of the 10% income-tax allowance attributable to retirement pensions. On 15 October, MPs adopted amendments deleting Article 3, which contained that proposal. The deletion remains in the Assembly’s provisional consolidated document issued on 20 October, incorporating proceedings through the second sitting of 19 October. The document is provisional, and the legislative process continues. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a>, <a href="#source-S03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a>, <a href="#source-S05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a>, <a href="#source-S06" aria-label="Source 6">6</a></sup>

On 14 October, amendment I-502 and identical amendments had also removed provisions increasing income tax and the CSG social contribution on certain sickness and occupational accident or disease daily benefits. The OFCE discusses these in a supplementary section, outside its main charts. Any account of them therefore needs to identify their place in the original proposal. The main €8.7 billion figure already excludes these daily benefits; it does include the pension tax-allowance ceiling that MPs removed on 15 October. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S06" aria-label="Source 6">6</a>, <a href="#source-S07" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

That chronology matters for the calculation. The OFCE attributes about €1.1 billion of lost disposable income to the pension allowance proposal. Subtracting it from the headline figure and announcing an updated “cost of the budget” would imply more precision than the evidence supports. A new estimate would require another simulation using the relevant text, the interactions between taxes and benefits, and the other changes to the package. The published result describes one configuration; a different configuration requires a fresh calculation. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup>

The scope also needs to remain visible. The study examines several measures affecting households directly, principally through pensions, public-sector pay and benefits. Other budget decisions, including those transmitted through businesses, public services and investment, belong in a broader assessment. This investigation uses the 2027 budget to examine a specific transmission mechanism within a defined set of measures. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a>, <a href="#source-S03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup>

## A freeze opens a gap between two paths

Understanding a freeze requires comparing two trajectories. Along one, a benefit rises according to the uprating assumed in the [counterfactual](/en/glossary/contrefactuel/), the reference path chosen in the absence of the freeze. Along the other, its amount stays unchanged. The shortfall is the difference between those paths, even when the payment reaching a household’s account remains identical to the previous year’s. Nominal income growth, purchasing power and the difference from a reference policy are therefore separate measurements. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

The OFCE assumes, among other things, a 2.3% April increase for the prime d’activité, France’s in-work benefit, and for the monthly family-benefit base (BMAF). It also assumes a 2% October increase in the expenditure parameters used to calculate housing benefits. That last percentage is an assumption made for the study. Article 74 of the finance bill proposes suspending the uprating of the in-work benefit and housing-benefit expenditure parameters, while allowing their restoration by decree. Housing-benefit income parameters reviewed in January follow a separate timetable. For family benefits, the government’s press dossier separately proposes adjusting their calculation base on 1 April to neutralise statutory uprating. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a>, <a href="#source-S04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

The starting month makes a large difference to the first-year amount. A gap beginning in April operates for nine months of 2027; one beginning in October operates for three. The OFCE estimates the in-work benefit freeze at €723 million in 2027 and €965 million over a full year. The corresponding housing-benefit figures are €81 million and €323 million. These amounts describe the same mechanism over different periods. The full-year measure is a twelve-month comparison under the study’s assumptions; treating it as an independent forecast for 2028 would overlook subsequent policy decisions and economic changes. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Public-sector pay introduces a more fundamental distinction. The study compares a frozen salary index point with a hypothetical 1.8% rise, matching its assumed inflation rate for 2027. That pay component has no automatic statutory indexation. The roughly €1.7 billion income shortfall estimated by the OFCE is consequently a gap relative to this assumed increase, a distinction the author explicitly makes. Its interpretation depends on keeping both the chosen pay baseline and the coverage visible: the study includes the three branches of the French public service and measures households’ net income. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

The €8.7 billion total thus combines gaps with different institutional origins. It measures their income consequences under the specified scenario. Translating it into an improvement in the public balance would require reconciling gross spending, taxes, social contributions, transfers between public bodies and reference assumptions across a consistent scope. That reconciliation comes before any [fiscal-multiplier](/en/glossary/multiplicateur-budgetaire/) calculation. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a>, <a href="#source-S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup>

## Cash amounts and the burden on household resources

The French statistical concept of niveau de vie adjusts income for household size. Insee defines it as disposable income divided by consumption units: one for the first adult, half a unit for each additional person aged 14 or over, and 0.3 for a younger child. This recognises that some costs, including housing, can be shared. Households with identical total incomes can therefore have different living standards. In English, the measure is generally called [equivalised disposable income](/en/glossary/niveau-de-vie/). <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S08" aria-label="Source 8">8</a></sup>

The OFCE groups households into twenty ordered bands of living standards, or ventiles. Average losses in euros tend to rise towards the top of the distribution. Their relative burden follows a different profile: around 0.8–0.9% in several lower groups, falling to 0.35% at the top. The more affluent half can bear about 64% of the total cash loss while lower groups experience a heavier proportional burden. Those findings describe different dimensions of the same scenario. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Averages also conceal how widely losses are spread. The study identifies 22.4 million losing households, with an average loss of about €390 among them. The ventile averages in the first chart, by contrast, cover all households in each group. Confusing the two would understate the loss for those actually affected. In the published tooltips, more than 85% of households lose in groups three to eight, compared with about 59% in the top group. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Several measures can affect the same household. A lower-paid household may receive the in-work benefit, family benefits and housing assistance. Protecting the uprating of a minimum-income payment therefore leaves a separate question about its other resources. Similarly, protection for a small pension is defined at the beneficiary level, whereas living standards depend on all household income and household composition. Distributional analysis needs to follow those different units of assessment through the calculation. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a>, <a href="#source-S08" aria-label="Source 8">8</a></sup>

## Different instruments reach different households

A simple division between pensioners and working-age households is too broad to explain the package. In the simulation, the more affluent half bears 96% of the loss from the pension tax-allowance ceiling. For the back-to-school allowance reduction modelled by the OFCE, the direction reverses: the less affluent half bears 96% of the loss. Its share reaches 87% for the in-work benefit freeze and 95% for housing benefits. The profile of the affected households tells us more about a measure’s incidence than its administrative label. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Removing the pension allowance ceiling therefore removes, at this stage, an instrument whose burden was concentrated near the top of the distribution. That establishes the direction of this particular change, holding other things constant. The overall distribution will depend on the rest of the legislation and any replacement measures. The available evidence identifies the instrument that was removed; the complete picture of the final budget remains to be calculated. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a>, <a href="#source-S06" aria-label="Source 6">6</a></sup>

The back-to-school allowance raises a different issue about the scenario’s construction. The government records an intended saving and proposes retargeting the allowance, with parameters to be determined after consultation and attention to children’s ages. To make the measure calculable, the OFCE assumes a uniform 30% reduction, generating a €550 million disposable-income loss within its scope. The 30% cut is therefore a modelling assumption. Targeting by age or changing eligibility thresholds could affect different families, even if the total saving were similar. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

For pensions, Article 35 of the social security financing bill is also more detailed than a single cliff edge at €1,260 a month. It provides for full uprating up to that amount of total pension income, a transition up to €1,281, and the possibility of coefficients set by decree above the threshold, with additional income bands. The derogation concerns basic-scheme pensions; total pension income determines the beneficiary’s position. The assumed freeze and treatment of supplementary pensions must therefore be distinguished from the options the draft legislation allows. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup>

## Where households absorb the shortfall

A reduction in disposable income can lead to lower purchases, reduced saving or the use of money previously set aside. The split depends on the resources that are actually available when the shock arrives, its expected duration and the expenses that are difficult to postpone. A home is an asset, but its value cannot immediately become cash to meet the month’s bills. This distinction between wealth and liquidity is one reason the distribution of the burden matters for the economy. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a>, <a href="#source-S16" aria-label="Source 16">16</a></sup>

Economists use the [marginal propensity to consume](/en/glossary/propension-marginale-a-consommer/) to describe the share of a change in resources reflected in consumption over a specified period. A 2025 European Central Bank (ECB) study estimates different responses across income groups in an analysis of mortgage transmission. Its design, population and type of shock limit its transferability to frozen French benefits. The response to the 2027 measures still needs to be estimated: taking an international coefficient and multiplying it by €8.7 billion would create the appearance of precision without establishing the connection between the two. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S11" aria-label="Source 11">11</a></sup>

Pensioner status alone supplies too little information. One household may have a regular income and very limited liquid reserves; another may temporarily absorb the shortfall by reducing contributions to a savings product. Research by Austria’s central bank (OeNB) on heterogeneous responses to additional income reinforces the need to examine household balance sheets. It uses a hypothetical lottery-windfall question and finds differences across countries, so its conclusions must remain anchored in that survey design. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S16" aria-label="Source 16">16</a></sup>

The national saving rate cannot resolve the question either. Banque de France research shows that the composition of income, particularly financial income, helped sustain high saving in 2023–24. An aggregate average can therefore rise while households affected by a particular measure have little room to adjust. For the budget assessment, the relevant information would be the capacity of those specific households to absorb the shortfall when it occurs. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S10" aria-label="Source 10">10</a></sup>

## From household finances back to the public balance

The next stage depends on which purchases households actually reduce. Part of their value pays for French production; another part reflects imports. Insee’s work on the content of household consumption shows large differences across products. That historical picture helps explain the mechanism, while a 2027 estimate would require identifying the spending cut at the margin. A product’s origin also leaves a separate question about public revenue: its sale can generate French distribution margins and VAT. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S17" aria-label="Source 17">17</a></sup>

Businesses facing weaker demand may then adjust output, investment or employment. The resulting income changes feed into further consumption decisions. Comparisons of the French Mésange and ThreeME models published in July 2026 illustrate these links and their dependence on the policy instrument and time horizon. A transfer to a household works first through its spending decision, whereas a public order creates demand directly. Applying the same coefficient to both would erase that distinction. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a></sup>

The process then feeds back into public finances. Weaker tax bases can reduce receipts, while some spending, including unemployment benefits, can rise. This is one role of the automatic stabilisers described by the ECB. Their operation explains why the yield after the economy responds can be smaller than a measure’s initial yield. Quantifying that difference requires an estimate for the particular scenario, including its tax structure, timing and other economic responses. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a></sup>

This feedback also creates a methodological problem when assessing a budget forecast. An official projection may already incorporate some effects of its measures on consumption, employment and revenue. Adding a separately calculated macroeconomic loss could count the same effects twice. The useful comparison is between two internally consistent paths, with and without the package, under the same external assumptions. The assessment then needs to specify what each path includes and which responses remain outside the model. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a>, <a href="#source-S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a>, <a href="#source-S13" aria-label="Source 13">13</a></sup>

The OFCE microsimulation stops before these reactions. Ines documentation describes its standard use as a static model of immediate monetary effects; behavioural responses and macroeconomic feedback require further work. The €8.7 billion figure consequently informs the initial incidence on income. Corresponding estimates of lost consumption, lost GDP and foregone public revenue remain to be produced. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup>

## Financing costs are another part of the risk

Our analysis of [bond prices and sovereign refinancing](/en/analysis/france-2027-budget-bond-market-risk/#budget2027-en-title) explains the passage from market yields to government interest payments and includes a sensitivity tool.

The counterargument follows a different route. A fiscal trajectory judged less sustainable can raise the return lenders demand. As debt is refinanced, a larger share of public resources then goes towards interest. France’s High Council of Public Finances, the HCFP, reports a government sensitivity calculation: a permanent 100-basis-point increase, equivalent to one percentage point, across all maturities from 1 January 2027 would add €3.4 billion to interest costs in the first year and €8.1 billion in the second. These are annual additional costs under the assumed rate shock. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S15" aria-label="Source 15">15</a></sup>

That sensitivity shows why financing belongs in the analysis. Estimating the rate benefit attributable to a particular benefit freeze or tax-allowance change would require a separate study. Between the choice of a measure and the cost of public debt lie the overall fiscal path, its credibility, inflation, monetary policy and market conditions. This transmission has its own timetable, just as the transmission from income to consumption does. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a>, <a href="#source-S15" aria-label="Source 15">15</a></sup>

An International Monetary Fund (IMF) working paper published in June 2026 examines this trade-off in a model of a highly indebted economy calibrated using Belgium, France and Italy. Its results depend on the assumed path without adjustment and on the financing response. Crucially, its expenditure-led package increases targeted assistance to vulnerable households while reducing other transfers. That composition offers another reason to examine who bears the burden. The exercise provides a theoretical counterpoint under specific assumptions, rather than a forecast of France’s proposals. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a></sup>

The HCFP locates this tension within the government’s 2027 submission. It considers the 1% growth forecast optimistic, with stronger private demand expected despite a larger fiscal adjustment. The credibility of the fiscal effort and the credibility of the activity forecast therefore need to be examined together. An announced saving is more convincing when its implementation, distribution and assumed behavioural response can all be explained. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S15" aria-label="Source 15">15</a></sup>

## Reassessing the package after parliamentary votes

The useful next step would be to reconstruct the package retained after parliamentary consideration, including the decrees and parameters needed for implementation. Deleted measures, restored upratings and the final back-to-school allowance rules would change the starting calculation. The analysis would then need to reconcile household disposable income with spending and receipts across public bodies before connecting those results to a path for consumption and activity. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S02" aria-label="Source 2">2</a>, <a href="#source-S03" aria-label="Source 3">3</a>, <a href="#source-S04" aria-label="Source 4">4</a>, <a href="#source-S06" aria-label="Source 6">6</a>, <a href="#source-S09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a></sup>

That assessment should document the liquid resources of affected households, how long they expect the shortfall to last and which expenses they adjust. It could then compare different distributions of the same adjustment, tracing their consequences for households, activity, financing and the public balance. The models examined here show why those dimensions can interact; applying them to the 2027 package remains a separate task. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S09" aria-label="Source 9">9</a>, <a href="#source-S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a>, <a href="#source-S14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a></sup>

**The distribution of the burden belongs inside the economic assessment of the budget.** It determines the resources available where spending and saving decisions are made. The OFCE’s data make that initial incidence visible, and parliamentary votes have already changed some of its components. Assessing the eventual saving requires following those changes through household responses and financing costs, while keeping the reference scenario consistent at every stage. <sup className="l0g-budget-ref"><a href="#source-S01" aria-label="Source 1">1</a>, <a href="#source-S05" aria-label="Source 5">5</a>, <a href="#source-S06" aria-label="Source 6">6</a>, <a href="#source-S12" aria-label="Source 12">12</a>, <a href="#source-S14" aria-label="Source 14">14</a></sup>
