On an online shop, your clicks, pointer movements and some form entries can be recorded to reconstruct your visit. A member of staff can then watch the journey unfold again. This technique is called **[session replay](/en/glossary/rejeu-de-session/)**. Its capabilities vary with the software, its settings and the safeguards applied. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l01">L01</a></sup>

A visit ends at the sign-in page. In the retailer’s analytics, it becomes another exit from the checkout. With session replay, the team can return to the sequence of actions: the field selected, the button pressed, the message that appeared, then the departure. That second look explains the appeal of the product. It also helps explain how much detail it can collect.

On November 5, 2026, France’s data protection authority, the CNIL, published its final recommendation on these tools, adopted on October 8. It acknowledges their value in diagnosing errors, improving journeys and assisting customers. It also sets out the conditions for consent and calls for collection proportionate to a purpose defined in advance. The document explains the existing framework and suggests ways to comply; it expressly describes itself as non-regulatory guidance. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l01">L01</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l02">L02</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l03">L03</a></sup>

The economic question begins here. A better understanding of a problem may lead to a profitable fix. Between the recorded scene and the claimed benefit, however, lie a diagnosis, design work and a measurement of results. The data may also serve purposes of the supplier’s own. To assess the value of a recorded visit, we need to follow both the business decision and the personal information behind it.

## Rebuilding a page, one event at a time

In the web architecture documented by rrweb, the browser first produces a description of the page. It then records changes and timestamped events. A session player applies them in sequence to reconstruct the visit. The moving page is generated from structured data, without capturing a video stream. Mobile software can use other methods; the description here concerns this particular web architecture. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t01">T01</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t03">T03</a></sup>

The structure being observed is the **DOM**, or *Document Object Model*: the organization of page elements as the browser works with them. It includes headings, forms, buttons and text replaced after an action. A collector can capture an initial state and subsequent changes: a message appearing, a value changing, a pointer moving or the page scrolling. The exact capabilities and exclusions depend on the product and its configuration. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t02">T02</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t03">T03</a></sup>

This gives a team context around an error. A counter may show how often something went wrong; a replay can help explain the circumstances. Perhaps a message was too far down the page, a window covered a control or an unexpected state blocked progress. These are possible uses, each requiring verification on the interface concerned. The CNIL recognizes this contribution of individual observation when aggregate metrics leave important context unexplained. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l04">L04</a></sup>

<SessionReplayFigure kind="mechanism" lang="en" />

Collection can take place while someone is typing. A script observing changes to a field may access its value before the form is submitted, subject to the protections in place. The “send” button triggers a business action. By itself, it seldom marks the boundary of everything that code can read on the page. The rrweb documentation and research on online forms establish this distinction. Understanding collection therefore requires examining the instrumentation and its settings, beyond the visible interface. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t02">T02</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t04">T04</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t11">T11</a></sup>

A replay also inherits the limitations of its construction. Missing resources, unusual components or omitted events can leave gaps. What appears in the player is a representation assembled from the retained elements. Its accuracy deserves checking, particularly when it is being used to explain a rare fault or support an important business decision. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t03">T03</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t07">T07</a></sup>

## Masking and the fate of recorded data

Protection often starts in the browser, with [masking before transmission](/en/glossary/masquage-avant-envoi/). Fullstory explains that, when enabled, its *Private by Default* mode replaces text before transmission unless capture has been explicitly allowed. It also distinguishes excluding an area, which removes more elements and interactions, from masking its text while potentially retaining layout and gestures. Clarity likewise documents masking before transmission. These are suppliers’ descriptions of their mechanisms; the behavior of a particular website depends on how they are integrated. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t06">T06</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t08">T08</a></sup>

The CNIL uses “masking” in a broader framework. Its recommendation distinguishes three arrangements. Data may be collected with unmasking subject to authorized access and internal approval. It may be collected under encryption, with the supplier providing keys after a justified request. Or the data may remain outside collection altogether. These arrangements are labeled M1, M2 and M3. They sit alongside other measures recommended for the relevant purpose, including masking by default and blocking especially risky content. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l02">L02</a></sup>

The distinction changes the exposure. Keeping a value out of collection removes that value from subsequent access to the collected dataset. An encrypted value remains recoverable by whoever holds the means to decrypt it. A value protected through an unmasking procedure depends on access permissions and the scrutiny applied to requests. The choice affects security, day-to-day operations and the scope for later reuse.



Product names require the same care. Contentsquare’s documentation, updated on October 26, 2026, describes masking before data leaves the browser and, separately, an option to collect selected content under encryption. In that option, the customer’s private key enables decryption. The key arrangement differs from the CNIL’s M2 example. Comparing the two requires establishing who actually holds the key. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t09">T09</a></sup>

Information also needs to be followed across the page. A protected field may be copied into a summary, a heading, a page address or a custom property. This is a risk scenario whose realization depends on the site and the tool. Contentsquare explicitly distinguishes protections across channels and identifies values for which the integrator remains responsible. An interface change may therefore require a fresh check of the rules. Maintaining protection is part of maintaining the product. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t09">T09</a></sup>

## Documented risks, dated evidence

In November 2017, Princeton researchers reported detecting recording by the services they studied on 482 of the top 50,000 Alexa-ranked websites. They described the finding as a lower bound associated with their method. It is a historical observation for that sample and those services. Measuring current prevalence in France would require a contemporary investigation. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t10">T10</a></sup>

*Leaky Forms*, published at USENIX Security in August 2022, drew principally on data collection in May and June 2021. The researchers identified 52 websites where session replay scripts inadvertently captured passwords. They disclosed the problems and reported remedial changes. A separate finding on email addresses transmitted before submission covered a wider range of tracking technologies. Combining the two would attribute observations to session replay that extend beyond its scope. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t11">T11</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t12">T12</a></sup>

More recent work examines mobile applications. A public version of *TapSpy*, accepted for CCS 2026, studies Android integrations through static analysis and language-model-assisted reasoning, with partial dynamic validation. The conference is scheduled for November 15–19, after this investigation’s cutoff. Risks classified by that method must be distinguished from directly observed leaks. We do not use them to estimate a leakage rate for the French web. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t13">T13</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t14">T14</a></sup>

Together, these studies document risk mechanisms and observations within defined boundaries. They support checking actual collection, exclusions and the durability of those exclusions as software changes. The material assembled here provides no basis for a monetary estimate of average harm per recorded visit.

## A rapid click leaves room for interpretation

Analytics interfaces use the term *rage clicks* for rapid, repeated clicks within an area. The suggested interpretation is obvious: something has irritated the visitor. Yet Fullstory documents false positives, including calendar controls designed to receive repeated clicks. The same pattern can be part of ordinary use. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t15">T15</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t16">T16</a></sup>

A team has several links to establish. Does the action reveal a difficulty? Does that difficulty explain an exit? Was the visitor intending to buy? Would a change help them finish? Each question needs its own evidence. A persuasive recording may demonstrate an interface defect while leaving the scale of its commercial effect unresolved.

Selection adds another layer. Which people can be observed depends on consent, the pages instrumented, technical compatibility and any sampling applied. The analyst then chooses cases that attract attention. Our methodological inference is straightforward: a striking scene demonstrates a possibility; estimating its frequency across traffic requires a documented denominator.

Measurement can take the form of an **A/B test**: randomly assigning eligible visitors to the existing version or a revised one, using a metric defined before examining the results. Research on online experiments explains the importance of population selection, sample sizes and measurement rules. The associated collection protocol must also meet the applicable requirements. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-r01">R01</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-e14">E14</a></sup>



A sound test can estimate the effect of the change. Evaluating the instrument raises a further question: would another method have found the same defect, at the same time, and at what cost? Replay may speed up bug reproduction or help teams agree on a diagnosis. Attributing the whole benefit of a redesign to the tool would require examining that alternative. This distinction runs through our assessment of its economic value.

## The boundaries around a conversion claim

Contentsquare’s Diptyque case describes such a sequence. Journey and click analysis led the team to investigate the checkout sign-in interface. Replays helped refine the diagnosis. Two variants were then tested with AB Tasty. The supplier reports a 9% increase in mobile conversion on the page, defining conversion as reaching the shipping-address entry step on the French and US sites. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-e01">E01</a></sup>

That definition places the result at a particular stage. The page supplies neither starting rates nor sample sizes, an uncertainty interval or a precise testing calendar. It also leaves the effects on completed orders and contribution margin unreported. We therefore retain the figure as a supplier-reported commercial result, alongside its definition. More visitors reaching the address step could herald a useful gain; establishing the effect on purchases requires another measurement.

At MOO, a company blog post dated October 11, 2024 confirms the use of Fullstory alongside interviews and customer feedback. Fullstory’s commercial case study gives a drop-off rate falling from 41% to 29% in the optimized journey. Those levels imply a reduction of **12 percentage points**, or approximately **29.3% in relative terms**. The same document nevertheless highlights a “12%” decline. The calculation and clarification of units are ours. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-e04">E04</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-e05">E05</a></sup>



The customer’s own account confirms use of the tool. The numerical outcome remains a supplier-published claim, with no observation window, sample sizes or control group allowing the evaluation to be reproduced. These cases give the reader something concrete: a problem, work undertaken and a reported result. Their reach remains local, with uneven access to methods and underlying data.

Misreading the numbers creates an economic error. Applying an improvement in an intermediate step to total revenue turns a journey metric into assumed income. Subtracting percentages without examining their starting levels can alter the apparent size of a result. The denominator deserves as much scrutiny as the dashboard presenting it.

## Clarity and the second use of visitor data

Microsoft presents Clarity as a free service and states in its FAQ that it does not sell data. It also describes itself as a data controller. Its technical documentation provides a more specific account of one use: an advertising consent signal governs sharing with Microsoft Ads. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-t07">T07</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-e09">E09</a></sup>

In Microsoft’s table, updated on May 21, 2026, `ad_storage=granted` enables that sharing to improve retargeting and campaigns. The `denied` state blocks sharing with Microsoft Ads. This documents a conditional mechanism. It establishes neither activation on a particular website nor revenue earned from an individual visit. Our analysis rests on this directly accessible evidence; it makes no assumption that recordings are sold. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-e09">E09</a></sup>

This architecture connects with [the economics of personal-data collection](/en/analysis/personal-data-economics-collection/): the same information can have different values for different users. For a retailer, it may help resolve a problem or reduce time spent on support. A supplier with purposes of its own may use data in other products or services under the applicable conditions. A software invoice of zero therefore leaves questions about purposes, recipients and integration costs. The public documents reviewed offer no average monetary value for this second use.

<SessionReplayFigure kind="circuits" lang="en" />

Another detail affects measurement itself. Microsoft explains that, in the mode described without cookie consent, each page view becomes a session and each recording shows one page. Continuity across pages is lost. Its more recent consent-management page describes cookieless collection with limited functionality when `analytics_storage` is denied. These documents set out the product’s stated behavior. We have not instrumented a website to examine a particular deployment. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-e09">E09</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-e10">E10</a></sup>

An analytical consequence follows: changing a setting can alter the session count and apparent progression rates. Before diagnosing a deterioration in business performance, a team needs to check that the measurement system remains consistent. The same journey may have been divided differently.

The phrase “cookieless” also requires looking at what actually happens on the device. Article 82 of France’s Data Protection Act covers accessing and storing information, and the CNIL requires prior consent for the replay purposes it examines. A technical mode’s name cannot settle its legal classification. Assessing a deployment would require its actual behavior, purposes and disclosures to visitors. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l02">L02</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l06">L06</a></sup>

## How much improvement would pay for the work?

For a retailer, the decisive calculation compares additional orders with the costs incurred to obtain them. **Contribution margin per order** means here the amount remaining from an extra order after the relevant variable costs: the product, payment processing, fulfillment, subsidized delivery or returns, depending on the business. That definition must stay consistent between the groups compared.

Our teaching model simplifies the calculation as **net incremental contribution = N × Δp × m − C**. N is the relevant traffic exposed to the correction over the period; Δp is the absolute change in the completed-order rate; m is the average contribution per additional order; and C is the cost included for the work over that same period. This is a scenario identity whose usefulness depends on the inputs. N and the denominator of the order rate must use the same unit of observation. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#methode-m01">M01</a></sup>

Costs include any subscription, integration, time spent reviewing sessions, the fix itself and the maintenance of safeguards. A one-time cost needs an explicit allocation to the chosen period. The calculation must also identify the decision being made: a new installation, a diagnostic project or an extension of use. A cost already incurred and unchanged between alternatives should not be counted a second time in comparing them.

Consider **an entirely hypothetical example** to make the units clear. With 100,000 visits exposed to a correction, €30 in contribution per additional order and €3,000 in costs over the same period, breaking even requires 100 extra orders. The purchase rate must rise by 0.10 percentage points. From a starting rate of 2%, that means reaching 2.10%, a relative increase of 5%. These assumptions describe neither Diptyque nor MOO, and they are no estimate of an average tool’s return. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#methode-m01">M01</a></sup>

<SessionReplayFigure kind="margin" lang="en" />

N refers to traffic receiving the change. It may differ from the number of sessions recorded to discover the problem. Applying an estimated effect to a different population, however, requires justification. Changes in basket size, returns or service costs can also make the model’s average margin inappropriate. In that situation, directly comparing contribution per randomly assigned visitor provides a more relevant measure.

Uncertainty around Δp remains crucial. A positive result based on few orders may be compatible with a very small, or even adverse, effect. The financial threshold helps frame the question; the experiment must then estimate the effect and its uncertainty. Research on online testing documents pitfalls in the interpretation of metrics and populations. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-e14">E14</a></sup>

## Consent becomes part of the product

The French recommendation distinguishes two stages. Reading and writing information on a device fall under Article 82. For the replay purposes examined, the CNIL concludes that prior consent is necessary. Processing the personal data obtained must then have a lawful basis under the GDPR. According to the authority, consent will generally be the most appropriate basis, subject to context. Establishing a basis for that second stage leaves the first requirement in place. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l02">L02</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l06">L06</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l07">L07</a></sup>

The reasoning starts with the service the person requested. Buying a product requires certain information to fulfill the order. A detailed recording of the journey gives the retailer an additional diagnostic benefit. In its response to consultation submissions, the CNIL distinguishes that benefit from what is strictly necessary to provide the visitor’s service. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l04">L04</a></sup>

This makes the disclosure important. A general reference to audience measurement may suggest an aggregate counter to a reader. The CNIL recommends specific information about replay at the first layer of the consent interface, separate from the purposes presented. Users should be able to understand that an individual sequence of navigation will be recorded and viewed. Withdrawal must also bring the relevant reading and writing operations to an effective stop. Collected data must be deleted where no other lawful basis justifies retaining it; the processor must receive the corresponding instruction. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l01">L01</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l02">L02</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l05">L05</a></sup>

A supplier can occupy different roles. When it processes data solely for purposes defined by the website publisher, it acts as a processor. When it uses data for its own purposes, responsibilities change for those operations. The CNIL describes, among other situations, possible joint controllership for device access that serves both parties. This assessment follows the actual uses of data and affects the contract, evidence of consent and arrangements for exercising rights. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l02">L02</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l07">L07</a></sup>

A session associated with a pseudonym may still be personal data. Under the GDPR, identification is assessed in light of the means reasonably likely to be used. A detailed history, information that can be combined with other sources or an identifier linked to an account therefore needs examination in context. Demonstrating anonymization requires more than replacing a displayed name. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l07">L07</a></sup>

## Retention follows the purpose

[Retention and deletion of data copies](/en/analysis/personal-data-after-the-contract-ends/) depend on the systems holding them and the purpose still being served.

The periods a product offers and the periods a business needs answer different questions. Microsoft documents 30 days for Clarity playback data and nine months for labeled or favorited sessions. Those product settings describe availability. An organization’s appropriate retention period depends on its purpose and the associated risk. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-e11">E11</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l07">L07</a></sup>

The CNIL’s annex gives purpose-specific benchmarks: a few months to understand errors or work on usability in the current website version; a few hours after the session for support, with documented justification taking account of when requests arrive. The recommendation allows room for alternative measures that meet the obligations. These benchmarks should be read alongside their purpose, rather than converted into a single retention period for every use. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l02">L02</a></sup>

Ordinary product changes also test the arrangement. A new form, a conversation with an assistant or an added summary may change the data accessible to a collector. Access permissions evolve as teams change. Deletion must work at the relevant level, and people’s requests must be handled using the available information without routinely creating a permanent identifier solely for that purpose. The GDPR specifically addresses processing that does not require identification. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l02">L02</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-l07">L07</a></sup>

## Funding a better decision

The public evidence documents a useful role for replay: retaining enough context to understand an interaction, share a diagnosis and decide on a correction. Commercial cases show local outcomes within a broader process. They leave open the instrument’s average return and the share of the benefit that a less intrusive method might also have delivered.

Investment may make sense when a team has a specific problem, the capacity to act and a way to evaluate the outcome. Accumulating sessions without a defined question can increase the burden of reviewing, protecting and retaining them while leaving their expected value uncertain. That is an inference from our analysis of the mechanisms, rather than a statistical estimate of the market.

The CNIL’s recommendation brings the issue back to a practical economic requirement: define the information needed before collecting it. Value develops through a better decision. Measuring it requires knowing which data helped, which change was made and which final outcome actually changed.

## Terms used in this article

**DOM:** the structure of page elements managed by the browser. **Session replay:** reconstruction of an individual journey from recorded elements. **Masking:** a protection whose mechanism must be specified; content may be replaced, retained under restricted access or excluded. **A/B test:** comparison of variants randomly assigned to a defined population. **Percentage point:** the difference between two rates expressed as percentages. **Contribution margin per order:** revenue remaining after the variable costs included for an additional sale. **Pseudonymization:** replacement or separation of identifiers while a possibility of linking data to a person remains. **CMP:** the interface and system used to manage consent choices.

## Research method and calculations

Documentary research closed on **November 8, 2026**, Europe/Paris. We reviewed CNIL texts, technical documentation, original research and supplier-published cases. MOO’s use of Fullstory was checked against MOO’s own website. Commercial performance claims remain attributed to their publishers. Clarity pricing and terms pages available only through search indexes were excluded from the article’s contractual conclusions. No third-party site was instrumented, no participants were interviewed and no conversion experiment was conducted for this investigation.
