**CMA CGM’s ships are earning more while its logistics business retains less profit from growing sales. In logistics, the gap between rising sales and falling EBITDA runs through all three reported quarters of 2026. Understanding the divergence means following vessel capacity, the transport services purchased by forwarders and the timing of acquisitions.**

*Analysis as of 16 November 2026. Third-quarter results were published on 13 November. Monetary amounts are in nominal US dollars.*

The same cargo travels from a vessel to a terminal and then a warehouse. Profit follows a less predictable route. In the third quarter of 2026, CMA CGM’s shipping [EBITDA](/en/glossary/ebitda/) margin reached **32.4%**, against **24.9%** a year earlier. In logistics, the margin fell from **9.3% to 6.7%**. EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. Its margin expresses those earnings as a share of revenue. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

The separation also appears in dollar earnings. Shipping revenue rose from $8.97 billion to $12.32 billion and EBITDA from $2.24 billion to $4 billion. Logistics increased revenue from $4.58 billion to $5.46 billion, while EBITDA fell from $428 million to $366 million. Both businesses generated higher revenue, with earnings moving in opposite directions. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

This raises a question about the group’s integration strategy: following cargo throughout its journey to retain more value. A carrier sells vessel space. A transport organiser buys it to serve customers. Warehousing and distribution add their own costs and investments. The same freight-rate increase affects each position in the chain differently.

## Available vessel capacity supports shipping revenue

A vessel that slows down or takes a longer route completes fewer rotations. Its technical capacity stays the same, but the space it can offer over a given period shrinks. If demand remains strong, that constraint can push rates higher. Detours, waiting and insurance also increase operating expenses. Earnings depend on how much additional revenue remains after those costs, a mechanism explored in our analysis of [the price of buying time at shipping chokepoints](/en/analysis/shipping-chokepoints-cost-of-buying-time/).

In its second-quarter release, CMA CGM said volumes and freight rates had offset additional costs from disruption in the Middle East. In November, Maersk described congestion absorbing capacity while raising network and handling expenses. These operator accounts connect disruption with both available capacity and costs. The precise contribution of each crisis to CMA CGM’s earnings remains to be isolated. [S04](#source-S04) [S09](#source-S09)

Hapag-Lloyd provides another example of revenue separating from volumes. Its average freight rate rose from **$1,413 to $1,808 per TEU** between the third quarters of 2025 and 2026, a calculated increase of approximately 28%. Volumes slipped from 3.401 million to 3.383 million TEUs, around −0.5%. A TEU, or twenty-foot equivalent unit, puts container capacity on a common basis: a forty-foot container represents two TEUs. [S11](#source-S11)

At CMA CGM, shipping volumes rose **5.5%** and average revenue per TEU increased **30%**. Using the published rounded inputs, 1.055 × 1.30 gives 1.3715, a reconstructed revenue increase of **37.15%**, close to the reported **37.3%**. Revenue per unit explains most of the sales increase within this accounting identity. [S01](#source-S01)

Average revenue combines routes, customers, contracts and surcharges. It can change with rates and with the mix of business. CMA CGM and Hapag-Lloyd thus report a similar broad direction, read within each carrier’s definitions and network.

## A forwarder’s invoice includes transport bought from others

A [freight forwarder](/en/glossary/commissionnaire-de-transport/) arranges a shipment and purchases services from carriers. At unchanged volumes, passing the entire transport-cost increase on to the customer raises revenue while leaving the amount available for the forwarder’s own expenses and profit unchanged. Passing on only part of the increase uses up some of that amount.

Kuehne+Nagel’s income statement makes that mechanism visible. Services purchased from third parties are deducted from net turnover to arrive at gross profit. Personnel and other expenses follow, before EBITDA. The presentation traces how revenue pays for purchased transport and then for the organiser’s own operations. It provides an economic framework for understanding forwarding, while CEVA’s accounts retain their own scope and methods. [S12](#source-S12)

Contracts determine how price changes are shared. A price promised to a customer may stay fixed while vessel space becomes more expensive to buy. Conversely, capacity secured in advance can gain value as selling rates rise. The length of commitments, indexation clauses and the ability to renegotiate determine the retained margin.

Logistics also includes warehouses, fulfilment, road transport and finished-vehicle distribution. An underused facility or fleet spreads fixed expenses across fewer operations. CMA CGM attributes pressure on its logistics business to forwarding and the automotive sector. Successive releases identify those activities without quantifying their respective contributions to the decline. [S01](#source-S01) [S03](#source-S03) [S04](#source-S04)

## Logistics earnings declined in all three reported quarters

First-quarter 2026 logistics revenue rose **6.6%** year on year while EBITDA fell **17.2%**. The second-quarter changes were **+8.5% and −15.4%**. In the third quarter, they were **+19.2% and −14.4%**. Each pair compares the quarter with the same period of 2025 on reported scopes. Rising revenue and falling earnings were therefore established well before the autumn. [S03](#source-S03) [S04](#source-S04) [S01](#source-S01)

Higher transport costs passed through to customers can dilute a percentage margin by enlarging its denominator. The decline in dollar earnings requires another explanation: third-quarter logistics generated approximately **$62 million less EBITDA** than a year earlier, based on the rounded $366 million and $428 million figures. Analysis must then examine the margin retained on services, volumes in the most profitable activities, operating expenses and scope changes. The 14.4% decline is the company’s reported rate. [S01](#source-S01) [S02](#source-S02)

<CMACGMMargins2026 figure={3} lang="en" />

The market allows other earnings paths. Maersk’s Logistics & Services grouping increased revenue from **$3.983 billion to $4.488 billion**, around 13%, and EBIT from **$218 million to $259 million**, around 19%. Its EBIT margin rose from **5.5% to 5.8%**. EBIT is measured after depreciation and amortisation, unlike the EBITDA used above for CMA CGM. The businesses grouped together also differ. This provides evidence of different outcomes within the same market, rather than a ranking of margin levels. [S09](#source-S09) [S10](#source-S10)

## FedEx Supply Chain joined after the reporting quarter ended

An earnings release combines results for a completed period with developments available at publication. FedEx Supply Chain makes the distinction tangible. The agreement was announced on **1 July 2026**, but both FedEx and CEVA date completion to **1 October**, the day after the reporting quarter ended. The announcement’s **$1.4 billion** figure is an enterprise value. [S05](#source-S05) [S06](#source-S06)

Note 14 of FedEx’s accounts at 30 September provides the seller’s perspective. The assets were still classified as held for sale at the balance sheet date. The 1 October disposal appears under subsequent events. FedEx reports **$1.4 billion in cash proceeds**, net of approximately **$35 million in direct transaction expenses** incurred during the third quarter. This documents the seller’s reported cash receipt, a separate measure from the announced enterprise value. [S17](#source-S17)

IFRS 3 dates an acquisition to the transfer of control. That normally coincides with legal completion, with a different date possible where the facts establish it. The documents reviewed place completion on 1 October. Attributing third-quarter sales to FedEx Supply Chain would therefore require evidence of an earlier transfer of control. [S14](#source-S14) [S05](#source-S05) [S06](#source-S06)

Other purchases are compatible with a scope effect on the year-on-year comparison. Borusan Lojistik joined CEVA on **5 November 2025**, after the comparison quarter. The deal excludes Borusan Port. Fagioli, a project-logistics and heavy-lifting specialist, joined CEVA on **31 March 2026**. Their inclusion in 2026 can alter the comparison with the previous summer. Closing announcements establish the dates, while their quarterly contributions still need to be detailed. [S07](#source-S07) [S08](#source-S08)

A revenue bridge separating like-for-like activity, acquisitions and currencies would measure that part of the growth. Kuehne+Nagel’s annual report provides an example of this breakdown. Applied to CMA CGM’s data, the presentation would help distinguish commercial growth from the expansion of the corporate perimeter. [S15](#source-S15)

## AI can fill ships and change the organisation of work

CMA CGM’s November release describes two relationships with artificial intelligence. Customers’ investment in infrastructure and data centres creates freight demand. Separately, the group is deploying MAIA, its platform developed with Mistral AI, across its own businesses. The first channel concerns cargo. The second concerns how work is organised. [S01](#source-S01)

Growth in technology-related shipments could benefit the carrier before its own AI tools produce savings. Measuring these effects requires identifiable cargo flows, their evolution and operational gains observed with MAIA. The release describes both channels without assigning them a quantified share of earnings.

## An integrated chain has to earn a return on its capital

Diversification can bring recurring customers, improve service and reduce dependence on particular shipping routes. Acquisition and restructuring costs may also precede expected benefits. For 2025, CMA CGM described growing revenue and profitability in contract logistics, alongside more difficult conditions in forwarding and automotive activities. That annual distinction helps explain the diversity of the business, to be examined within each subsequent reporting period. [S13](#source-S13)

EBITDA illuminates part of operating performance. Depreciation, maintenance investment and financing enter the next stages of the analysis. A vessel, a warehouse and a transport organiser commit different amounts of capital. The purchase price of acquired businesses also matters for the return on the money invested. Kuehne+Nagel’s income statement shows the transition from EBITDA to EBIT after depreciation and amortisation. [S12](#source-S12)

An economic assessment of CEVA would therefore bring together earnings after depreciation, required investment, [working-capital needs](/en/glossary/bfr/) and the capital committed to acquisitions. Customers won through the integrated offering, followed by the margin retained on their contracts, would help establish the strategy’s commercial benefit.

Consolidation adds one final step. Shipping services sold to a logistics subsidiary create an internal group transaction. Sales between subsidiaries must be eliminated in consolidated accounts to avoid counting part of the journey twice. Reconciling revenue and internal transactions, as illustrated by Maersk’s reporting, helps readers understand those scopes. [S10](#source-S10)

CMA CGM’s figures show stronger shipping earnings and weaker conversion of logistics revenue into EBITDA. **Integration should be judged by returns on capital and the margin earned from external customers.**

## Limits

This documentary analysis compares corporate disclosures, reporting periods and, for transactions, statements from the parties. It preserves each operator’s scope and definitions. Published margins are separate from returns on capital and net earnings. The rounded $366 million and $428 million amounts produce a calculated decline of approximately 14.5%, while CMA CGM reports 14.4%. The shipping-growth reconstruction also uses rounded inputs. Detailed integration bridges, private contracts and the buyer’s consolidation entries remain to be examined. Management explanations are attributed. A quantified allocation of the effects of freight rates, disruptions and AI would require further disclosure.
