**Washington wants taxpayers to share in the gains of the companies they finance. The agreements also reveal industrial conditions being relaxed, revenues being protected and risks borne by the federal budget. Assessing these investments requires following the contracts as closely as the shares.**

*An l0g documentary investigation · Information checked as of 8 November 2026 · All monetary amounts in nominal US dollars.*

“Complete” is a consequential word in a government programme that pays for factories. It suggests equipped buildings, working production lines and usable output. Under Intel’s renegotiated award, completion has a different test. The six projects covered by the commercial CHIPS funding can be treated as finished once the company has incurred eligible expenses equal to the award. The Commerce Department considered that condition already satisfied when it amended the agreement in August 2025. Outstanding project milestones were removed. The Government Accountability Office describes the change in its report published on 6 August 2026. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a01" aria-label="Source A01">A01</a></sup>

In return, Washington received equity. Part of the machinery that tied public funding to the execution of industrial projects was exchanged for a claim on the company’s value. That changes the question the government needs to answer: how much production capacity will the programme deliver, what will its financial assets be worth, and how closely will those outcomes coincide?

MP Materials offers another view of the same industrial policy. In its July 2025 agreements, the Pentagon acquired securities, protected the price of certain rare-earth products and arranged an outlet for a future magnet factory. The public partner participates in both the financing of the business and the revenues supporting it. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a06" aria-label="Source A06">A06</a>, <a href="#source-a08" aria-label="Source A08">A08</a>, <a href="#source-a09" aria-label="Source A09">A09</a></sup>

These two cases bring “Trump’s portfolio” into focus. The phrase refers to federal government assets acquired or negotiated under the administration. The useful question is what the government has agreed to pay, protect or relinquish alongside the shares it receives.

## Intel’s new definition of delivery

The announcement on **22 August 2025** paired roughly **$8.9 billion** with **433.3 million shares**, described as **9.9% of Intel’s equity on the basis used at the time**. The funding combined two existing allocations: about $5.7 billion of commercial CHIPS support that remained unpaid and $3.2 billion under Secure Enclave, a programme serving national security requirements. Their purposes and conditions differ. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a02" aria-label="Source A02">A02</a>, <a href="#source-a03" aria-label="Source A03">A03</a></sup>

At closing on 27 August 2025, Intel said it received the remaining $5.7 billion of commercial funding. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a35" aria-label="Source A35">A35</a></sup> The agreement also provided for Intel’s corresponding obligations to be discharged to the extent permitted by applicable law. Secure Enclave continued under its own terms. Some of the shares were placed in escrow, held separately for release according to the agreement. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a03" aria-label="Source A03">A03</a></sup>

The GAO’s account makes the industrial significance clearer. Intel’s original commercial award totalled **$7.865 billion**, part of which had already been disbursed. In August 2025, the remaining required milestones for its six projects were removed. Eligible costs already incurred became the completion test. Intel no longer had to report progress against the deleted milestones. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a01" aria-label="Source A01">A01</a></sup>

Money spent and capacity delivered answer different questions. Eligible expenditure establishes that resources have been committed to a project. A qualified process, an operating line and products delivered to customers show how the project functions. Spending can be entirely real while the intended capacity still needs equipment, engineering work, customers or a viable cost structure.

The rights attached to the securities matter as much as the stake. Intel described a passive government shareholder without a board seat. The agreement generally requires the government to vote with the board’s recommendations, subject to exceptions. Washington also received a [warrant](/en/glossary/warrant/), a contractual right to acquire shares on specified terms. It becomes exercisable if Intel ceases to own at least 51% of its foundry business. That creates a financial consequence for separating the business; it operates differently from a general right to block a sale. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a02" aria-label="Source A02">A02</a>, <a href="#source-a03" aria-label="Source A03">A03</a></sup>

The headline percentage also needs its original date. Subsequent share issuance changes the denominator, while escrowed shares follow their contractual timetable. At **26 September 2026**, Intel reported approximately **132 million shares** still in escrow. A current ownership figure would require reconciling the government’s holdings, the escrow provisions and the company’s capital at the same date. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a04" aria-label="Source A04">A04</a></sup>

## Industrial progress still needs measuring

Industrial progress requires its own evidence after the agreement has been relaxed. Intel’s third-quarter 2026 filing says that its first products manufactured on the Intel 18A process in high-volume production were released at the start of the year. It also says that the pace of future expansion will depend on committed demand for Intel 14A. These are the company’s operational statements in a regulatory filing. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a04" aria-label="Source A04">A04</a></sup>

That evidence matters. Intel can continue to invest and advance its manufacturing technology after obtaining greater contractual flexibility. Establishing the success of the public programme would still require connecting that progress to the capacity originally sought, its location, its timetable and its availability to the relevant customers.

The share price responds to a different set of expectations: future margins, product demand, technological prospects and the value of managerial flexibility. Better commercial prospects can support the stock. Relief from obligations can be valuable to shareholders too. Either development can coexist with several different industrial outcomes.

The taxpayer consequently has an interest in Intel’s financial health and a continuing need for evidence about the programme’s strategic objective. Treating the stock price as certification that the industrial mission has been fulfilled would assign financial markets a task they were never given, using information they may not possess.

A recent agreement shows that equity can be structured differently. On **14 September 2026**, Anderon, an IBM subsidiary working on quantum wafer manufacturing, finalised an award of up to **$1 billion**. Commerce received a minority equity interest. IBM reported that $650 million was made available at closing and that the remaining $350 million would depend on contractual milestones. Clawback provisions and a termination right remained, while IBM retained control. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a13" aria-label="Source A13">A13</a>, <a href="#source-a14" aria-label="Source A14">A14</a></sup>

Equity ownership can therefore coexist with industrial conditions. Intel and Anderon embody different choices in contract design. The size of a shareholding says relatively little about the strength of the commitments attached to the public money.

## MP Materials: financing the production chain

MP Materials’ agreements address several stages of production. The July 2025 package included **$400 million of convertible preferred stock**, accompanied by warrants. Preferred shares carry particular economic rights; conversion allows them to become common shares under agreed terms. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a06" aria-label="Source A06">A06</a>, <a href="#source-a07" aria-label="Source A07">A07</a></sup>

The **7% dividend** on the preferred stock requires care. It is normally [paid in kind](/en/glossary/pik/), increasing the holder’s entitlement rather than delivering an annual cash payment of the same size. It compounds quarterly. The public investor accumulates a claim on the company whose eventual realisation depends on the relevant conversion, sale or redemption provisions. Describing the rate as an assured annual cash return to the Treasury would erase that timing and risk. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a07" aria-label="Source A07">A07</a></sup>

The announced arrangements also included a **$150 million public loan** for heavy rare-earth capabilities. Separately, banks committed **$1 billion**, subject to conditions, to finance the planned magnet facility. That second commitment is private financing. Public support may help make a project bankable, but the sources of capital retain different owners and exposures. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a12" aria-label="Source A12">A12</a></sup>

The production chain explains the arrangement. Neodymium and praseodymium, commonly grouped as **NdPr**, are used in the magnets covered by the agreements. Extraction and separation supply later stages of metal and magnet production. Protecting the revenue of one stage can support financing for the chain; securing an outlet for the finished product addresses another uncertainty. MP’s contracts intervene at both points. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a08" aria-label="Source A08">A08</a>, <a href="#source-a09" aria-label="Source A09">A09</a></sup>

The state’s equity is consequently one part of a larger position. A shareholder may benefit when expected profits rise. A price guarantor pays more when the contractual benchmark falls. A purchaser receives goods in exchange for its payments. The public partner occupies each of these positions, with different timing and risks.

## How the $110 floor reallocates risk

The price-protection agreement sets a [price floor](/en/glossary/prix-plancher/) of **$110 per kilogram of NdPr equivalent**. According to the quarterly filing, the arrangement began on **1 October 2025** and runs through **31 December 2035**. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a11" aria-label="Source A11">A11</a></sup> When the quarterly benchmark defined by the contract falls below that threshold, the Pentagon pays a supplement on eligible volumes. The calculation depends on the products included, their sale or contractual classification and the applicable pricing rules. Certain stockpiled material and internal transfers are included. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a08" aria-label="Source A08">A08</a></sup>

The calculation combines a contractual weighted average and precisely defined eligible volumes. Some published terms are redacted. Reconstructing actual payments from outside the company therefore requires more information than a spot quote and headline production figures.

Consider an **illustrative scenario** with one million eligible kilograms in a quarter, equivalent to 1,000 metric tonnes. At a benchmark of $60 per kilogram, the gap is $50 and the public payment is **$50 million**. At $90, the payment is **$20 million**. At $130, the supplement falls to zero. These are selected assumptions, designed to explain the mechanism.

The agreement also gives the government **30% of the amount above the threshold**, applied to the contractual volume base. That return applies to quarters beginning after 10X’s contractual capacity milestone. The company must notify the government that the plant is operating commercially and can achieve the annual production target. In the $130 scenario, the return would be **$6 million** once the condition had been met. Before the milestone, this part of the sharing arrangement is inactive. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a08" aria-label="Source A08">A08</a>, <a href="#source-a09" aria-label="Source A09">A09</a></sup>

The asymmetry has an economic purpose. The government makes part of the producer’s revenue more predictable and recovers a share of potential upside. It absorbs some price risk. The business and its investors retain other exposures, including execution, costs and financing conditions.

The arrangement is already visible in the accounts. For the quarter ended **30 September 2026**, MP reported **$8.1 million of price-protection agreement income**, separately from **$147.7 million of revenue**. PPA income for the first nine months was **about $68 million**. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a10" aria-label="Source A10">A10</a>, <a href="#source-a11" aria-label="Source A11">A11</a></sup>

Cash flow reveals the payment lag. During those nine months, MP said it received **$110.9 million** under the arrangement for income recognised in the final quarter of 2025 and the first half of 2026. The collections therefore include a receivable originating in the previous year. Adding the cash received to 2026 PPA income would count some of the same flows twice. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a11" aria-label="Source A11">A11</a></sup>

MP also reported **1,018 metric tonnes of NdPr production** during the quarter and completion of the magnet qualification process with General Motors. Its 5 November release placed the start of saleable deliveries and the associated revenue in the fourth quarter. Qualification is an industrial milestone. Delivered volumes and their margins will show its commercial significance. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a10" aria-label="Source A10">A10</a></sup>

## The public customer behind the magnet plant

The second major agreement concerns 10X. A [long-term purchase commitment](/en/glossary/offtake/), or *offtake agreement*, gives the producer visibility over demand. MP’s agreement runs through ten years after the contractually defined commercial operation date, with provisions allowing sales to third-party customers. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a06" aria-label="Source A06">A06</a>, <a href="#source-a09" aria-label="Source A09">A09</a></sup>

Its financial structure includes a **$140 million annual EBITDA threshold** at full capacity, increased by 2% a year after 2025, and provisions for the production ramp. The Pentagon buys magnets at the production cost specified by the agreement and makes quarterly payments equal to one quarter of the applicable threshold. An annual true-up adjusts the payments, and the agreement returns certain excess earnings to the government. EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation. The threshold concerns the 10X facility. Group net income, available cash and shareholder dividends are separate measures. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a06" aria-label="Source A06">A06</a>, <a href="#source-a09" aria-label="Source A09">A09</a></sup>

Future funding is part of the agreement itself. At signing, section 6.8.1 stated that, apart from an exception specified in the transaction agreement, appropriated funds were not yet available for the purchase obligations. The Pentagon undertook to seek the necessary budget resources. The contract also provides information and audit rights, quality controls and remedies for default. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a09" aria-label="Source A09">A09</a></sup>

This creates a relationship extending across industrial execution and future budget decisions. Adding the initial equity investment to ten years of the EBITDA threshold and an assumed price-protection bill would produce a striking number with little economic meaning. Payments may depend on one another, on production volumes, on costs and on commercial sales. Purchases deliver magnets to the buyer. Loans create claims that may be repaid.

A useful assessment follows each relationship through time. When was the money committed, and when was it paid? What asset, right or good did the government receive? Which repayments and revenues come back? How much remains to be funded? The same discipline applies to stock-market gains. A higher share price increases estimated wealth; a sale generates cash at the price actually realised.

## Public investment and the risk of supply disruption

The strongest case for intervention begins with a problem of incentives. A customer may prefer the cheapest supply chain while the wider economy bears the cost of a disruption. An investment that reduces that vulnerability can create a collective benefit which the investor cannot readily charge for.

Public funding may then buy resilience. Revenue protection can also help a new producer through the period when its plant is expensive, output is limited and customers are still being qualified. These are plausible economic mechanisms. Their relevance to an individual project depends on the available alternatives, the price of support and the capacity eventually delivered.

If the government has already decided to finance that capacity, taking equity may improve its position. It receives a share of success that assistance without a return mechanism might have left to private shareholders. CHIPS awards already included provisions to share gains above specified thresholds: the GAO identified them in 27 of the 40 projects reviewed as of July 2025. A serious critique therefore needs a relevant comparison: a conditional grant, a loan, a long-term purchase agreement, an equity investment with different rights, or some combination capable of pursuing the same objective. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a01" aria-label="Source A01">A01</a></sup>

That comparison could support the administration’s decision to acquire shares. It could also show that the equity came with overly generous guarantees, weak delivery conditions or inadequately compensated budget exposure. The public documents reveal the design choices. They do not establish a single net social value for the entire portfolio.

American history offers a useful reference. The **Capital Purchase Program** of 2008–09 invested approximately **$205 billion in 707 financial institutions**, receiving preferred securities and warrants among its instruments. At 30 September 2023, Treasury’s cash-basis table showed approximately **$16.3 billion of cumulative net income** for that programme, after its recorded interest expense. The figure belongs to this particular programme and to the context of a banking crisis. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a21" aria-label="Source A21">A21</a>, <a href="#source-a22" aria-label="Source A22">A22</a></sup>

Public equity can make money. The precedent also shows how that result is established: investments, repayments, income and costs are reconciled within a defined perimeter. Calling a collection of deals a portfolio leaves that work ahead.

## Korea Zinc: ownership through several layers

The Korea Zinc arrangement adds another complication: legal entities between the government and the operating asset. The company’s shareholder letter dated **1 January 2026** describes **Crucible JV**, a joint venture financing a subscription for **10.59% of Korea Zinc**. The percentage belongs to the joint venture, which has several financing partners. Direct federal ownership must be established separately from each contributor’s rights. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a15" aria-label="Source A15">A15</a></sup>

The financing shown for the venture includes **$150 million of Pentagon equity** and **$1.25 billion of Pentagon debt**, alongside private investors and a Korea Zinc contribution. The project sponsor’s application published on the federal permitting portal also describes **$2.349 billion of direct Pentagon loans** to the industrial project and the same amount of bank financing. The sponsor states that the **$1.4 billion** for the joint venture has already funded an equity contribution to Korea Zinc, which then contributed the money to Crucible Metals. The $2.349 billion direct loans and matching bank financing are described as prospective commitments in that document; their disbursements require separate tracking. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a15" aria-label="Source A15">A15</a>, <a href="#source-a16" aria-label="Source A16">A16</a></sup>

Flattening these layers into a single entry creates several traps. Washington may be assigned the whole percentage held by a vehicle with several investors. The same dollar may be counted more than once as it moves between entities. Debt may be treated as a grant that will never return.

Tracking each instrument answers practical questions. Who bears the first loss if the project disappoints? Who receives repayments? Which assets are subject to which claims? The answers can substantially change an assessment based on the headline amount alone.

## Power lies in the rights

The government can own common shares while accepting a limited governance role. It can also gain considerable influence through a specialised security. At U.S. Steel, the **[golden share](/en/glossary/golden-share/)** comes with consent rights over specified plant closures, agreed investment commitments, headquarters and transfers of production abroad. It also gives its holder the right to appoint one director. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a17" aria-label="Source A17">A17</a>, <a href="#source-a18" aria-label="Source A18">A18</a></sup>

Its function would be obscured by a comparison with Intel shares based only on financial value. The important issue is which decisions it constrains. Conversely, a valuable common-equity portfolio can leave the government with little day-to-day power over industrial choices.

Combining public functions nevertheless creates a tension worth monitoring. An administration sets rules, distributes support and may own shares in selected companies within the same sector. A decision serving the public interest could reduce the value of those shares. A measure helping one holding could disadvantage its competitors. This institutional risk supports the case for clear criteria and reviewable procedures. Any finding of favouritism or price manipulation would require evidence specific to the case.

The agreements examined contain contractual controls, and the GAO conducts audits. Those forms of scrutiny would be more useful to the public if connected to a consolidated account of objectives, commitments and results. Taxpayers should be able to see which part of government is responsible for financial value, which verifies industrial delivery and who resolves conflicts between them.

## Public money and industrial results

Even the overall count depends on the source. The publicly accessible *Wall Street Journal* graphic, consulted on 8 November, records **43 announced deals**. The Council on Foreign Relations page, displayed as updated on **9 October 2026**, reports **48 deals** and **$30.3 billion in announced investments**. A reconciliation of the two lists deal by deal remains to be established. These announcement counts therefore do not establish verified holdings as of 8 November. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a19" aria-label="Source A19">A19</a>, <a href="#source-a20" aria-label="Source A20">A20</a></sup>

A dated register would distinguish announcement, definitive agreement, securities issuance, disbursement, warrant exercise and sale. The agreements examined here show why those distinctions matter. The register should preserve the dates and unresolved points specific to each transaction.

The financial account would reconcile disbursements with repayments, dividends and sale proceeds. It would identify open commitments, remaining assets and financing costs. Loans would require credit-risk estimates; purchases would require an account of the goods received. Any present-value calculation would specify its valuation date and discount rate.

The industrial account would track promised capacity, commissioning, qualified output, deliveries and remaining dependencies. A factory can be technically available while lacking orders. It can also provide useful insurance despite a modest financial return. Evaluation should make those situations distinguishable and explain the price accepted for each.

As of 8 November 2026, the documents establish a shift in method: federal equity positions are embedded in agreements that redistribute industrial and financial risk. Intel obtained relief from certain commercial-award conditions in exchange for securities. At MP Materials, the government holds financial claims while supporting part of the company’s revenues and prospective demand. Anderon shows that taking equity can preserve milestone requirements. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-a01" aria-label="Source A01">A01</a>, <a href="#source-a03" aria-label="Source A03">A03</a>, <a href="#source-a06" aria-label="Source A06">A06</a>, <a href="#source-a08" aria-label="Source A08">A08</a>, <a href="#source-a09" aria-label="Source A09">A09</a>, <a href="#source-a13" aria-label="Source A13">A13</a></sup>

The next evidence to examine is concrete: public funds actually mobilised, financial rights that produce income, and industrial capacity operating at the end of the process. Shares give taxpayers an ownership interest. The surrounding agreements determine what that interest costs.
