**XpresTest has secured $38.8 million in federal commitments for health programmes. Its parent XWELL was financed by a vehicle connected to Dominari, a company in which Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump own shares. Official records and securities filings identify the financial interests, compensation and proposed sale of the operating businesses.**

*Documentary investigation checked through 10 November 2026. Scope: the United States and a health programme in the Democratic Republic of Congo. All monetary amounts are nominal US dollars.*

For years, XWELL’s airport business meant massages and wellness treatments. Its subsidiary XpresTest also does something very different: collecting samples to monitor infectious diseases among travellers. That health business now sits at the intersection of a private financing deal, federal awards and a proposed change of ownership.

Official records show **$38,815,410.68 in federal obligations to XpresTest**, across a contract from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, or CDC, and a State Department cooperative agreement. The contract covers a year; the cooperative agreement runs for two. Their sum measures cumulative commitments across different missions. The outlay fields in the retrieved records are unreported. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s26" aria-label="Source S26">S26</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s29" aria-label="Source S29">S29</a></sup>

XpresTest is the recipient of the awards. The Trump brothers’ connections run through the financing of its parent, XWELL. Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump own shares in Dominari, whose entities arrange the placement or manage investment vehicles. Assessing the conflict-of-interest risk requires following those relationships through to the decisions and gains that the evidence actually establishes. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s12" aria-label="Source S12">S12</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15" aria-label="Source S15">S15</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s28" aria-label="Source S28">S28</a></sup>

## Two federal awards with different health missions

CDC contract **75D30126C21193** concerns Ebola entry-screening services. The official notice gives an award date of **6 July 2026**, followed by publication on 28 July. USAspending’s transaction history shows **$18,137,975 obligated on 6 July**, with a further **$677,435.68 on 25 September**. The resulting total is **$18,815,410.68**, for performance running through 5 July 2027. Today’s $18.8 million figure therefore includes an addition made after the initial award. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">S01</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s26" aria-label="Source S26">S26</a></sup>

The State Department award is cooperative agreement **SAQMMA26CA0383**. Official transactions record **$20 million obligated on 25 September 2026**, with performance from 28 September 2026 to 30 September 2028 and a place of performance in the Democratic Republic of Congo. This is federal assistance. Treating it as another procurement contract would erase differences in the instruments’ purpose and oversight. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s29" aria-label="Source S29">S29</a></sup>

An obligation commits government resources to an award. Payment depends on performance; recognised revenue follows accounting rules; profit reflects costs. The records establish the commitments. Actual disbursements and the profitability of each assignment require additional evidence. An unreported outlay field remains missing data. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s26" aria-label="Source S26">S26</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s29" aria-label="Source S29">S29</a></sup>

## Choosing a supplier under emergency conditions

The CDC record identifies a non-competed procurement, one offer and an urgency justification. The official notice describes Ebola response and a sole-source acquisition. The important questions concern the reasons for selecting the supplier: readiness, alternatives considered, negotiated pricing and supervision of delivery. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s01" aria-label="Source S01">S01</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s26" aria-label="Source S26">S26</a></sup>

Federal acquisition rules provide an exception for unusual and compelling urgency, alongside justification requirements and an obligation to request offers from as many sources as circumstances permit. The notice refers to both the traditional numbering and the acquisition-rule overhaul. Evaluating the procurement requires the applicable rules and, above all, the signed justification for this particular award. HHS had aligned its acquisition supplement with the overhaul through a deviation signed on 21 November 2025, effective immediately for the covered actions. This provides context for the dual numbering; the signed award-specific justification remains unavailable. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s04" aria-label="Source S04">S04</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s05" aria-label="Source S05">S05</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s30" aria-label="Source S30">S30</a></sup>

XpresTest had a health business before American Ventures financed XWELL. In January 2026, the CDC said its traveller genomic-surveillance programme, launched in 2021, involved a partnership with XWELL and Ginkgo. The agency reported more than one million participants across the whole programme. That operating history provides a concrete basis for examining the supplier’s capabilities and the resources required by a new assignment. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s06" aria-label="Source S06">S06</a></sup>

On 8 January, XWELL also announced that Cindy Friedman, formerly a CDC official and leader of its traveller-surveillance programme, would become a biosecurity adviser. Her background brings directly relevant expertise. The release leaves her possible role in the July procurement unspecified; the ethics records governing any contacts with her former agency remain to be established. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s07" aria-label="Source S07">S07</a></sup>

The parent company was financially vulnerable. For 2025, XWELL reported $29.2 million in revenue and approximately $17 million in net loss attributable to the company. Year-end cash and equivalents were $2.6 million, excluding restricted funds. Those figures explain the importance of fresh financing and a reorganisation of the businesses. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s08" aria-label="Source S08">S08</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s27" aria-label="Source S27">S27</a></sup>

## The Trump brothers’ interests run through Dominari

Dominari’s proxy filed on **6 November 2026** provides an ownership snapshot dated **20 October**. Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump each held **1,398,414 shares**, or **5.55%** of common stock on the published denominator of 25,201,979 shares. September filings gave 5.77% using another denominator. The reported share count for each brother is unchanged between those records. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s13" aria-label="Source S13">S13</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s14" aria-label="Source S14">S14</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s28" aria-label="Source S28">S28</a></sup>

Several functions sit below Dominari. The group reports owning **90% of American Ventures Management and American Ventures IM**, which are management entities. American Ventures Series XXIV holds the investment in XWELL and appears as a direct holder of its securities. Dominari Securities separately acts as the compensated placement agent. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source S09">S09</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s12" aria-label="Source S12">S12</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15" aria-label="Source S15">S15</a></sup>

The structure creates different potential sources of return: placement services, management rights and the value of securities held by investors. The series’ private agreements would be needed to explain how those returns are allocated. We did not obtain those agreements, a complete investor register or records documenting any realised personal gains. Multiplying 5.55% by 90% and applying the result to federal awards would combine different interests and rights.

The Trump brothers deny intervening in awards, ProPublica reports. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s25" aria-label="Source S25">S25</a></sup> Our primary-source collection establishes the financial relationships described here; communications capable of demonstrating intervention in the award process remain to be obtained.

## Fees, refinancing and the cash left for XWELL

The February placement raised **$31.333 million gross** in two closings: $30.832 million on 27 February and $501,000 on 3 March. It combined convertible preferred shares with [warrants](/en/glossary/warrant/), instruments giving their holders the right to obtain shares on specified terms. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source S09">S09</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source S10">S10</a></sup>

Dominari Securities’ compensation terms included a cash fee of **8%** of funds raised, a **1%** expense allowance and placement-agent warrants. Applied to the actual gross proceeds, the first two components amount to approximately **$2.507 million** and **$313,330**. They concern the intermediary. Establishing any amount personally received by a shareholder would require separate evidence. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source S09">S09</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s10" aria-label="Source S10">S10</a></sup>

The first-half cash-flow statement reports **$28.269 million net proceeds**. The **$3.064 million** difference from gross proceeds measures cash financing costs deducted from the funds raised. The 10-Q reports a higher accounting cost of issuance, **$10.216 million**, including **$7.152 million in the fair value of placement-agent warrants**. That non-cash component explains the difference. The cash reconciliation includes only financing costs actually deducted from proceeds. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s11" aria-label="Source S11">S11</a></sup>

Of the net cash, **$9 million** redeemed earlier instruments. That leaves a subtotal of **$19.269 million before other cash flows**. The company’s operations and other transactions must then be included to assess its position. At 30 June, cash including restricted funds stood at **$12.346 million**; operating activities had used **$9.117 million** during the first half. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s11" aria-label="Source S11">S11</a></sup>

A headline financing amount can therefore support the business while paying intermediaries and allowing earlier capital providers to exit. That allocation matters when assessing each participant’s incentives and the resources available for the operating business.

## The $13 million offer arrived in March

On **6 July 2026**, the date of the CDC’s initial award, XWELL signed an agreement to sell XpresTest and XpresSpa to Express Wellness Group, an entity affiliated with Face Haus. The **$13 million base price** is subject to adjustments. It covers two businesses, with XWELL as the seller. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s17" aria-label="Source S17">S17</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source S18">S18</a></sup>

The coincidence deserves scrutiny. The negotiation history given to shareholders adds an important earlier date: **Face Haus submitted a non-binding $13 million offer on 11 March**, followed by an exclusivity agreement on 17 March. Discussions had started in August 2025. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s16" aria-label="Source S16">S16</a></sup>

That sequence changes the investigation. Establishing the health business’s influence on price would require comparing forecasts, risks and information exchanged in the spring with those available in July. An unchanged base price can coexist with different commercial assumptions or adjustments. The public record supplies the chronology and some assumptions; the complete communications between the parties remain unavailable.

The buyer would acquire the subsidiaries’ equity. A share acquisition can preserve the legal entity performing an existing contract. Federal rules provide for certain stock acquisitions in which a novation agreement is unnecessary. The particular contract’s obligations and any required agency arrangements still need to be checked against the applicable records. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s22" aria-label="Source S22">S22</a></sup>

## Existing cash explains Roth’s $30.1 million valuation

Roth’s model uses a financial snapshot dated **31 March 2026**. It adds a **$13 million base price** to **$17.1 million of existing cash**: $15.6 million would be distributed before closing, while $1.5 million would remain in the subsidiaries and enter the price adjustment. The result is **$30.1 million of value in that model**. Recovering money already held within the group preserves existing wealth; it does not create another premium paid by the buyer. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s16" aria-label="Source S16">S16</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source S18">S18</a></sup>

The agreement also provides for **$2.65 million in escrow at closing**: $750,000 for adjustments, $1.65 million for indemnification and $250,000 for litigation. The final proceeds depend on cash, debt, unpaid transaction expenses and working capital, the resources tied up in day-to-day operations. The announced price can therefore differ from the cash immediately available to the seller. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source S18">S18</a></sup>

A financial fairness opinion addresses a question for the seller and its shareholders. A public supplier’s selection requires a separate examination, with its own documents and decision-makers. Each assessment has to remain within its remit.

## Executives received cash ahead of the proposed sale

A personal cash flow was reported in October. XpresTest repurchased **279.5 securities** awarded to executives, former employees or service providers as [share-based compensation](/en/glossary/remuneration-en-actions/) for approximately **$2.7 million**. The 8-K states that unvested rights were accelerated and that a payment originally due at the sale was brought forward with the buyer’s agreement. Bruce Bernstein received approximately $1.031 million, Ezra Ernst $530,000 and Ian Brown $96,000. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s19" aria-label="Source S19">S19</a></sup>

Those payments raise concrete questions about award dates, valuation and recipients’ involvement in decisions. A payment can follow contractual rights while influencing the options a recipient has an incentive to favour. The proxy dates the repurchase to 5 October; the 8-K gives 8 October. We preserve that discrepancy. The bank-level origin of the funds and any connection to CDC disbursements remain unknown. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s18" aria-label="Source S18">S18</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s19" aria-label="Source S19">S19</a></sup>

The economic treatment must also respect the timing. Money already paid belongs to earlier cash flows; deducting it again as an unpaid disposal expense would distort the calculation of sale proceeds.

## Financing instruments become part of the vote

The convertibles include a **4.99%** beneficial-ownership blocker, which can be raised to **9.99%** after notice. This limits an ordinary-share position at a given moment. Economic exposure also depends on conversions, remaining warrants and any sales actually made. Calculating a return would require those transactions and their realised prices. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s09" aria-label="Source S09">S09</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15" aria-label="Source S15">S15</a></sup>

Series XXIV describes notice on 7 July, effectiveness on 7 September and a conversion on 8 September yielding **993,617 common shares**. The proxy supplement links the increase and conversion to a **support agreement with the buyer dated 6 July**. The series agreed, among other things, to support the sale and oppose certain alternatives. The financing thus connects directly to transaction governance through a documented agreement. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15" aria-label="Source S15">S15</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s20" aria-label="Source S20">S20</a></sup>

The company subsequently corrected the voting-share denominator after omitting 650,000 shares awarded to directors. The count rose from 9,970,883 to **10,620,883**. Holding the series’ 993,617-share numerator constant changes the calculated proportion from 9.97% to **9.36%**. That movement reflects the denominator rather than a newly observed disposal of shares. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s15" aria-label="Source S15">S15</a></sup> <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s20" aria-label="Source S20">S20</a></sup>

On **28 October**, the proposal received **5,423,862 votes in favour**, or **51.07%** of eligible shares. Shareholder approval is one step towards completion. The SEC submissions register reviewed on 10 November lists XWELL’s latest filing as the 29 October 8-K reporting the vote. Our collection obtained no subsequent public confirmation of closing. Completion therefore remains to be confirmed. <sup className="source-ref"><a href="#source-s21" aria-label="Source S21">S21</a></sup>

## Transparency must follow the financial interests

The records go beyond a coincidence of famous names and large numbers. Dominari receives placement compensation; a vehicle holds securities and commits to support a sale; executives receive an early payment; XpresTest obtains two federal awards. The family connections to the president make scrutiny of those decisions and their beneficiaries particularly important.

Oversight of the CDC procurement should make the urgency justification, alternatives and price analysis examinable. Oversight of the cooperative agreement must address performance obligations and supervision of the funds. For the sale, the forecasts used in the spring and July, followed by the closing statement, would help connect commercial expectations with the amounts ultimately realised.

This investigation documents financial interests and public decisions. Establishing preferential treatment would require examining selection against alternatives and applicable rules, then documenting any improper advantage and its beneficiaries. Correspondence or interventions could help establish how the decisions were made. That evidentiary standard strengthens the case for transparency now, while decisions are committing public money.

## Method and limitations

The financial facts rest on SEC filings and company documents, with their statements explicitly attributed. Official USAspending award records and transaction histories were reviewed on 10 November for both federal awards. Their obligations are added cumulatively across different instruments and performance periods. Outlays remain unreported. Calculations are explained in the text and the figure methods.

We did not obtain bank records, the series’ complete investor list, the signed procurement justification or a final closing statement. No requests for comment were sent. The opening illustration was generated with the built-in image tool; the analytical graphics use the cited records and calculations.
