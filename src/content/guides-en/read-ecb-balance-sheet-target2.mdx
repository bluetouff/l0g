A central bank balance sheet records both the assets acquired or lent and the money or other liabilities matched against them. Following its total size alone misses how those liabilities change. The balance sheet can shrink during a week in which banks' deposits rise. The Eurosystem's [statement for 6 November 2026, published on 10 November](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/annual-reports-financial-statements/wfs/2026/html/ecb.fst261110.en.html), provides a practical example.

This guide shows how to trace those movements, distinguish repayments from revaluations and place TARGET balances in the appropriate accounts. Figures are a dated snapshot, checked on **11 November 2026**. The [Federal Reserve H.4.1 guide](/en/guides/read-h41-fed-balance-sheet/) explains the corresponding US release and its different table conventions.

## Choose between the ECB and Eurosystem accounts

The ECB's own accounts cover only part of monetary policy implementation in the euro area. The **Eurosystem** comprises the ECB and the national central banks of countries using the euro. There have been **21 national central banks** since [Bulgaria joined on 1 January 2026](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr260101~c830245e42.en.html), in addition to the ECB itself.

These institutions conduct operations and hold assets on their individual books. Their consolidated balance sheet retains positions against third parties and eliminates claims and liabilities between Eurosystem central banks. TARGET balances are internal positions, so they disappear on consolidation. To examine them, open the disaggregated data or the individual central banks' accounts. The [ECB's accounting guide](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/other/wfs-userguide.en.html) explains the distinction.

This matters immediately when comparing portfolios, losses or capital. A figure described as belonging to “the ECB” might refer to the Frankfurt institution alone or to the consolidated Eurosystem. Read the document's heading before comparing amounts.

## Check the observation date, unit and columns

The [weekly financial statement](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/annual-reports-financial-statements/wfs/html/index.en.html) normally appears on Tuesday and reports the previous Friday's closing positions. Exceptions include publications after quarter-end and TARGET closing days. The publication date and the balance-sheet date serve different purposes.

The table reports **millions of euros**, while the commentary generally uses billions. Figures in this guide are divided by **1,000**: €5,913,173 million becomes €5,913.173 billion. Decimal places allow readers to reproduce the conversion; they do not imply that accounting values are certain sale prices.

The [6 November statement](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/annual-reports-financial-statements/wfs/2026/html/ecb.fst261110.en.html) separates the closing balance from the weekly change **due to transactions**. Quarter-end statements show revaluation effects separately. A change in the reported stock, a transaction and a valuation gain answer different questions.

## Identify the assets and their purpose

The asset side records the Eurosystem's holdings and claims. Start with monetary policy securities, lending to banks, gold and foreign-currency assets. They have different risks, valuation rules and relationships with liquidity.

| Assets on 6 November 2026 | € billion | Source item |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Total balance sheet | 5,913.173 | Total assets |
| Monetary policy securities | 3,276.834 | Item 7.1 |
| Gold and gold receivables | 1,286.235 | Item 1 |
| Monetary policy lending to euro area banks | 38.433 | Item 5 |

Source: [ECB, consolidated statement for 6 November 2026](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/annual-reports-financial-statements/wfs/2026/html/ecb.fst261110.en.html). The selected items do not add up to total assets.

**Monetary policy securities** are assets the central banks purchased and hold. **Lending to banks** records credit operations against collateral. Distinguish the claim on a borrowing bank from the securities it pledges: accepting collateral does not automatically turn those securities into a portfolio purchase. The [official guide to balance-sheet items](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/other/wfs-userguide.en.html) describes main refinancing operations, longer-term lending and the marginal lending facility.

A rise in lending may indicate greater use of central-bank facilities. Interpretation still requires checking maturities, operation terms and market rates. The aggregate balance sheet cannot, by itself, identify a bank in distress.

## Locate money and other obligations on the liability side

Banknotes and banks' deposits are central-bank liabilities to their holders. Banks use central-bank money to settle payments. Their customers' deposits are liabilities of commercial banks and must be distinguished from these settlement balances.

The useful lines in the [6 November statement](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/annual-reports-financial-statements/wfs/2026/html/ecb.fst261110.en.html) are:

| Liabilities on 6 November 2026 | € billion | Source item |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Banknotes in circulation | 1,633.819 | Item 1 |
| Banks' current accounts | 370.074 | Item 2.1 |
| Deposit facility | 1,897.040 | Item 2.2 |
| General government deposits | 86.440 | Item 5.1 |
| Revaluation accounts | 1,261.631 | Item 11 |
| Capital and reserves | 27.952 | Item 12 |

Current accounts and the deposit facility together amount to **€2,267.114 billion**, adding items 2.1 and 2.2. Current-account holdings include balances used to meet minimum reserve requirements. They are not identical to the required amount: compliance is assessed on average over a reserve maintenance period, under the [ECB's accounting conventions](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/other/wfs-userguide.en.html).

Excess liquidity has another definition: current-account balances above required reserves, plus the deposit facility, less marginal lending facility use. Calculating it therefore requires the reserve requirement and consistent observation dates or averaging periods. The [ECB's liquidity analysis](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/liq/html/index.en.html) provides the relevant inputs. Counting every bank deposit as excess liquidity would overstate it.

## Reconcile falling assets with rising bank deposits

Begin with the accounting identity: **assets = banks' deposits + every other liability-side item**, including capital and revaluation accounts. With assets unchanged, a transfer from a government's deposit to a bank's settlement account changes the composition of liabilities. It need not create another central-bank asset.

In the week ending **6 November 2026**, total assets declined by **€32.237 billion** through transactions, while monetary policy liabilities to banks, item 2, rose by **€24.262 billion**. Government deposits fell by **€43.775 billion** in the same statement. Other items also changed, so these three numbers do not reconstruct the entire week. They demonstrate why total balance-sheet size is an inadequate substitute for banks' balances. [Source: ECB, weekly transaction changes](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/annual-reports-financial-statements/wfs/2026/html/ecb.fst261110.en.html).

The underlying mechanism is straightforward. A government payment from its central-bank account to a supplier with a commercial-bank account reduces the government's deposit and increases the recipient bank's settlement balance, all else equal. Tax receipts or some debt settlements can move balances the other way. The aggregate statement records the resulting positions; attributing each movement to a particular operation requires additional evidence. Our [US Treasury liquidity guide](/en/guides/read-net-liquidity-tga-rrp/) explores a related accounting mechanism within a different institutional framework.

## Separate QT, repayments and valuation changes

**Quantitative tightening**, or QT, reduces portfolios accumulated through asset purchases. In its [29 October 2026 decision](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp261029~9a493adf17.en.html), the ECB confirms that APP and PEPP holdings are declining as principal repayments are no longer reinvested. That does not imply an identical reduction every week: maturity schedules vary, and other assets can move in the opposite direction.

The [10 November commentary](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/annual-reports-financial-statements/wfs/2026/html/ecb.fs261110.en.html) breaks securities down by programme and distinguishes purchases from redemptions. Its note also allows for occasional sales, for example for risk management. An absolute claim that no securities are ever sold would go beyond the source.

Valuation needs separate treatment. Under the [weekly statement's accounting guide](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/other/wfs-userguide.en.html), debt securities in item 7.1 are recorded at **amortised cost** and tested for impairment. Their balance does not track daily market prices. Gold and foreign-currency positions are revalued quarterly. A rise in the euro value of gold can increase both assets and revaluation accounts without a gold purchase or a corresponding payment to banks.

## Read TARGET in the individual central banks' books

TARGET brings together infrastructure services for settling payments and other transactions in central-bank money. **T2** replaced TARGET2 for large-value payment settlement in March 2023. The historical name remains common in commentary and in this guide's address. The [ECB's T2 overview](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/paym/target/t2/html/index.en.html) distinguishes today's service from its predecessors.

Consider a payment from an Italian bank to a German bank. All else equal, the Italian bank's settlement balance falls and the German bank's increases. Banca d'Italia's TARGET position deteriorates; the Bundesbank's improves. At the end of the day, each central bank's position is reduced to a single net claim on, or liability to, the ECB. The ECB also has its own TARGET position and belongs in the full picture. [Source: ECB explanation of TARGET balances](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb-and-you/explainers/tell-me/html/target-balances.en.html).

These balances accumulate payment flows arising from trade, investments, interbank lending, deposit movements and monetary policy operations. The ECB explains that asset purchases increased TARGET balances when cross-border settlement moved deposits into other countries. A large balance alone cannot establish capital flight or a fiscal transfer.

TARGET claims and liabilities are actual accounting positions. Consolidation offsets them across the Eurosystem while leaving them visible in the individual institutions' books. Their movements reveal settlement patterns; diagnosing financial stress also requires funding-flow and market evidence. The [ECB additionally explains](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb-and-you/explainers/tell-me/html/target-balances.en.html) that non-reinvestment of a domestic security does not directly reduce TARGET balances, although indirect effects are possible. Portfolio runoff and changes in TARGET balances have no automatic proportional relationship.

## Distinguish losses, capital and policy capacity

A central bank may hold older bonds with fixed coupons while paying interest on liabilities that reprice quickly. If its expenses rise faster than its income, its interest margin deteriorates. Exchange-rate changes and other costs also affect the result. The balance sheet reports stocks; the income statement accounts for a year's revenues and expenses.

For **the ECB alone**, the [2025 accounts published on 26 February 2026](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr260226~15dbe4febc.en.html) show a loss of **€1.254 billion**. The ECB carries it forward to offset against future profits and makes no profit distribution for 2025. These are neither the Banque de France's accounts nor a consolidated result for the entire Eurosystem.

The ability to issue its currency distinguishes a central bank from a commercial bank. Losses still matter for government distributions, financial resources and institutional independence. In its [explanation of central-bank profits and losses](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb-and-you/explainers/tell-me-more/html/ecb_profits.en.html), the ECB states that it can operate with losses and also stresses the principle that national central banks should be sufficiently capitalised. A return to profit depends on interest rates, earnings and asset composition; the passage of time alone does not guarantee it.

## Connect balance-sheet quantities with market rates

The aggregate volume of deposits does not show every bank's access to funding. Examine their distribution, available collateral, refinancing operations and money-market rates. The [operational framework announced in March 2024](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2024/html/ecb.pr240313~807e240020.en.html) aims to keep short-term rates near the deposit facility rate, with collateralised lending among its tools.

On **29 October 2026**, the official rates were **2.50%** for the deposit facility, **2.65%** for main refinancing operations and **2.90%** for marginal lending. Those are the levels in the [dated policy decision](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp261029~9a493adf17.en.html), separate from the balance-sheet observation. The deposit rate is an anchor; calling it an absolute floor for every transaction would be too strong. Counterparties' access to facilities and market frictions can generate deviations.

## Build a reproducible weekly reading

1. Open the statement and commentary. Record the observation date, publication date, unit and institutional perimeter.
2. Identify the main assets, banks' deposits, banknotes and government deposits.
3. Compare changes across all material items, separating transactions from revaluations.
4. Check programme-level redemptions before linking a fall in securities to QT.
5. Read TARGET in disaggregated accounts, keeping dates consistent and including the ECB.
6. Compare quantities with rates and refinancing conditions before diagnosing a shortage.

The [weekly statements](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/annual-reports-financial-statements/wfs/html/index.en.html), their [user guide](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/other/wfs-userguide.en.html) and the [disaggregated data](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/annual-reports-financial-statements/wfs/dis/html/index.en.html) provide the starting documents. Continue with the guides to [repo and SOFR](/en/guides/read-repo-market-sofr/) and [European sovereign debt](/en/guides/read-european-sovereign-debt/) for funding markets and the wider fiscal setting.

Earlier year-end 2026 projections and approximate national TARGET positions have been removed.

Links throughout the guide lead to the relevant official documents. The ECB is the source for its accounts and decisions; assessments of its own policy capacity are explicitly attributed. Balance-sheet amounts are converted from millions to billions of euros without annualisation or price adjustment. Rounding in the source table can prevent items from adding up to the final million.
