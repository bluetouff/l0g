A company can deliver to customers, report a profit and still struggle to pay suppliers. The explanation often lies in timing: a sale precedes collection, inventory is purchased before delivery, and equipment remains in use for years after it is paid for. A cash flow statement connects those different calendars.

This guide examines a non-financial company through a fictional annual example in millions of currency units (CU million). Every movement is specified. Banks and insurers require a separate treatment of their assets, liabilities and prudential constraints.

## Connect earnings with operating cash

The income statement recognises revenue and expenses under accounting rules. The cash flow statement records cash movements within **operating**, **investing** and **financing** activities. An indirect reconciliation starting with net income adjusts for non-cash items and differences in the timing of operating receipts and payments. The [SEC’s financial-statement guide](https://www.sec.gov/about/reports-publications/beginners-guide-financial-statements) explains the relationships among these statements.

Depreciation allocates an asset’s cost over its useful life. Adding it back in the reconciliation reverses an expense that lacks an equivalent cash payment in that reporting period; the asset purchase appears separately when cash is paid. Operating cash and investment therefore belong in the same analysis, particularly when equipment must be replaced regularly.

Accounting standards and their effective dates also matter. [IAS 7](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-7-statement-of-cash-flows/) governs the IFRS statement and records amendments to its presentation. Before comparing companies, align cash-flow classifications and consolidation boundaries. Similar line-item names can conceal different reporting choices.

## Trace the working-capital requirement

For this example, **operating working capital** means inventory + trade receivables − trade payables. More inventory or receivables ties up additional funding; more supplier credit defers a payment. A real company may have other material operating balances that must enter the reconciliation.

Assume **60 CU million of net income** and **20 CU million of depreciation**, the only non-cash adjustment in the example. Receivables rise by 25, inventory by 15 and payables by 10. The working-capital requirement increases by **30**, calculated as 25 + 15 − 10. Operating cash flow is **50**: 60 + 20 − 30. All balance movements are assumed to arise from operations, with no acquisition, disposal, currency translation or reclassification.

After **30 CU million of cash capital expenditure**, the free cash flow measure used here is **20**: 50 − 30. The waterfall preserves the direction of each movement. Adding back depreciation creates no additional wealth; customers and inventory absorb funding, while suppliers temporarily provide part of it.

Raw changes between two balance sheets can include changes in the reporting perimeter. Buying a subsidiary brings its inventory onto the consolidated balance sheet, for example, without making that entire increase an operating inventory purchase during the period. Use the cash flow statement and explanatory notes to identify the appropriate bridge. The [SEC’s statement on cash-flow information](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/munter-statement-cash-flows-120423) emphasises classification and reporting controls.

## Investigate the quality of cash conversion

Now increase receivables by 45 instead of 25, keeping every other assumption unchanged. Working capital absorbs 50, operating cash falls to 30 and free cash flow reaches zero. Net income remains 60. The next question concerns the source of the receivables: expanding credit sales, seasonal timing, longer contractual terms or collection difficulties.

Compare receivables with sales, inventory with cost of sales and payables with purchases where that information is available. Match period length and seasonality. Receivable ageing, allowances, commercial terms and management’s discussion can help distinguish among explanations that a single annual ratio leaves open.

Stretching supplier payments can support one year’s cash flow while placing pressure on commercial relationships. Building inventory may support expected growth or protect supply continuity. A useful assessment connects working-capital movements to operations and future commitments, then checks whether the proposed explanation fits the evidence. The [10-K guide](/en/guides/how-to-read-10-k-sec/) helps locate the relevant disclosures.

## Define free cash flow before making comparisons

**Free cash flow**, or FCF, commonly means operating cash flow less capital expenditure. The [SEC’s interpretation, question 102.07](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/corporation-finance-interpretations/non-gaap-financial-measures), explains that the measure has no uniform definition and requires a clear calculation and reconciliation to the relevant accounting measure.

Our convention is FCF = operating cash flow − cash capital expenditure. Business acquisitions, debt repayments, dividends and share repurchases remain separate. Required payments can still have claims on the calculated 20 CU million, so assessing a distribution requires a further review of obligations.

Separating maintenance from growth investment also requires evidence. Management may provide an estimate, whose method should identify the capacity being replaced, the capacity added and the spending required to sustain operations. Where the disclosure is insufficient, retain the reported investment total and distinguish explicit scenarios from documented amounts. An unexplained adjustment can make companies appear comparable while concealing different reinvestment needs.

## Reconcile the final movement in cash

Add a debt repayment of 15 and a dividend of 8 to the original case, with no other cash movement or currency effect. The 20 CU million left after capital expenditure becomes a **3 CU million fall in cash**: 20 − 15 − 8. This last bridge connects operating capacity with financing and distribution decisions. The [SEC’s cash flow statement explainer](https://www.sec.gov/files/cash-flow-statement-bblocks.pdf) distinguishes the three activity categories used in the reconciliation.

Across several reporting periods, rebuild the same measure and explain changes in earnings conversion, working capital, investment and financing. Lower cash generation can accompany a coherent industrial expansion, whose sustainability then depends on available funding and maturities. ## Sources and limitations

SEC and IFRS documents establish reporting concepts and presentation boundaries; the numerical case is entirely illustrative. Deferred taxes, equity compensation, leases, acquisitions and exchange-rate effects are excluded to isolate the mechanism. Restore material items and their disclosures before analysing a real issuer. Cash quality depends on where cash originates, how repeatable it is and the obligations it must still finance.
