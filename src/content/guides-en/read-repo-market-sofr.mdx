*Repo turns securities into short-term funding. This guide explains the transaction, SOFR’s construction and the Fed’s influence on overnight rates. The [September 2019 episode](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/what-happened-in-money-markets-in-september-2019-20200227.html) shows why a rise in funding costs needs to be read alongside cash flows and intermediaries’ ability to lend.*

## Repo, the basic block of market funding

A repurchase agreement, or [repo](/en/glossary/repo/), is deceptively simple: one party sells a security, in the US Treasury repo market, a Treasury, while agreeing to buy it back at an agreed later date at a slightly higher price. Economically, it is a cash loan secured by collateral, and the difference between the two prices determines the interest paid. The cash lender who receives the security is doing a “reverse repo”; the security lender who receives cash is doing a “repo.” The terminology depends on the side of the transaction.

Two settings govern the risk. The first is the [haircut](/en/glossary/haircut-repo/): the lender does not advance 100% of the security’s value but slightly less, a margin that protects against collateral price moves. A low haircut allows high leverage; a rising haircut forces the borrower to find cash or sell. The second is the type of collateral. When the cash lender does not care which exact security it receives, as long as it is high quality, the trade is “general collateral” and the rate reflects the price of cash. When a specific security is in high demand, it becomes “special” and finances at a lower rate because owning that security has value.

## How SOFR is built

[SOFR](/glossaire/sofr/), the Secured Overnight Financing Rate, is a volume-weighted median of eligible overnight Treasury repo transactions. The [New York Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/reference-rates/sofr) publishes it around 8 a.m. Eastern Time on business days, using the previous business day’s activity. Its coverage combines tri-party repo, GCF and cleared bilateral DVP transactions, with filtering intended to reduce the influence of securities trading “special.” It does not cover every Treasury repo transaction.

The [published methodology](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/reference-rates/additional-information-about-reference-rates) explains the data inputs and exclusions. A daily rate should be read with the accompanying volume and percentiles: a median can remain stable while the more expensive part of the distribution moves. A transaction-based benchmark still depends on its coverage and calculation rules. Daily volumes are observations to date explicitly, not a permanent size attached to the market.

## The Fed’s short-rate framework

The Fed targets a range for the federal funds rate. [EFFR](/glossaire/effr/) measures that unsecured market; SOFR measures secured Treasury funding. Their differences matter when interpreting a spread.

The [New York Fed’s operating framework](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/domestic-market-operations/monetary-policy-implementation/repo-reverse-repo-agreements) uses administered rates and repo operations to influence market rates:

| Instrument | Role in the framework |
| --- | --- |
| [IORB](/glossaire/iorb/) | Interest on eligible institutions’ reserve balances; a reference return for banks. |
| [ON RRP](/glossaire/rrp/) | An overnight investment alternative for approved counterparties that supports a floor under short rates. |
| [Standing repo](/glossaire/srf/) | Cash against eligible collateral for approved counterparties, limiting upward funding pressure. |

These are economic incentives with access conditions. They do not guarantee that every market participant can transact with the Fed at the quoted rate. The [standing-repo FAQ](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/repo-agreement-ops-faq) specifies counterparties, collateral and operating windows. It describes full allotment subject to proposition limits. “Unlimited access for everyone” would be an incorrect reading.

## Interpreting SOFR minus IORB

A persistent rise in SOFR relative to IORB deserves investigation. It does not, by itself, identify a system-wide shortage of [bank reserves](/glossaire/reserves-bancaires/). Check whether the change affects the whole rate distribution, coincides with Treasury settlement or tax dates, and persists beyond a reporting date.

Access and balance-sheet costs also matter. A [New York Fed staff survey analysis](https://tellerwindow.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/14/surveys-suggest-improved-willingness-by-counterparties-to-use-standing-repo-operations/) finds that some counterparties require a spread above the standing-repo rate before using the facility. Respondents cite, among other factors, the inability to net those trades against customer activity. That helps explain why market rates can exceed a facility rate without every borrower immediately switching to the Fed.

## September 2019: a funding squeeze

The [Fed’s retrospective](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/what-happened-in-money-markets-in-september-2019-20200227.html) describes Treasury settlement of about **$54 billion** and corporate tax payments that jointly reduced reserves by roughly **$120 billion** over two business days. SOFR rose from **2.43% on September 16 to 5.25% on September 17**, as recorded in the [New York Fed series distributed by FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SOFR). The difference is **2.82 percentage points**, or **282 basis points**.

On September 17, the Fed offered up to **$75 billion** and supplied approximately **$53 billion**, according to the [same retrospective](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/what-happened-in-money-markets-in-september-2019-20200227.html). Offered capacity and actual lending are different quantities. Upward pressure had been expected; its magnitude surprised market participants. Intermediation constraints complicated the response to the reserve drain.

## Calendar effects and balance-sheet changes

Treat quarter-end as a hypothesis to test against dated observations. Compare the days before and after the event, rather than assigning every rate increase to regulatory reporting. The [New York Fed staff discussion of repo operations](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/09/the-role-of-repos-in-monetary-policy-implementation/) explains how central-bank repos add reserves while outstanding and reverse that addition at maturity. Adding daily allotments across a week would therefore overstate the amount simultaneously outstanding when loans roll over.

The [FOMC announced on October 29, 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20251029a.htm) that the reduction of its aggregate securities holdings would end on **December 1, 2025**. That decision did not freeze reserve balances. Changes in the Treasury’s cash account, currency and other liabilities can still alter the composition of the Fed’s balance sheet. Our guides to [Treasury liquidity](/en/guides/read-net-liquidity-tga-rrp/) and the [Fed balance sheet](/en/guides/read-h41-fed-balance-sheet/) explain those accounting links.

## Reading the market in practice

Use a dated observation sheet: SOFR and its percentiles, transaction volume, IORB, EFFR, facility rates and actual repo take-up. Keep rate observation dates separate from publication dates. Compare the same business days and distinguish overnight operations from outstanding balances.

Then formulate the diagnosis explicitly. A short-lived quarter-end spread, a broad and persistent funding squeeze, and difficulty obtaining a particular Treasury security call for different explanations. A spread is a starting point for this investigation; it cannot establish the cause alone.

Repo funding is central to the [basis trade](/en/glossary/basis-trade/) and complements our guide to the [Treasury market](/en/guides/read-us-treasuries-market/). The [English glossary](/en/glossary/) defines the terminology.
