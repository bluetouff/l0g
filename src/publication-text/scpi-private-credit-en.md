An exit request left waiting raises a simple question: where will the cash come from? After six chapters on French SCPI property funds, that question sounds familiar in discussions of private credit. The comparison is worth exploring, starting with the contracts that sit behind the symptoms.

## Different claims on the economy

A SCPI offers an interest in a portfolio of rental property. In the Financial Stability Board’s definition, private credit means direct lending negotiated between medium-sized companies and nonbank lenders. The property fund carries buildings and their operating economics; the credit fund holds claims on borrowers. [AMF, SCPI guide](https://www.amf-france.org/fr/espace-epargnants/comprendre-les-produits-financiers/placements-collectifs/scpi-comment-bien-sinformer); [FSB, 6 May 2026, pp. 1 and 3](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P060526.pdf).

A tenant leaving and a company struggling to repay a loan therefore affect different assets. In credit, collateral and seniority also matter: a claim that ranks ahead of another creditor has a different exposure to losses. Researchers Cai and Haque describe how seniority and collateral vary across lending strategies. A discount on SCPI shares cannot simply become an assumed loss on a credit portfolio. [Cai and Haque, February 2024, definition of private credit](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/private-credit-characteristics-and-risks-20240223.html).

## Who pays the investor who leaves?

The strongest parallel concerns investments offering some form of exit while holding assets that take time to sell. A variable-capital SCPI may need a new subscription to match a withdrawal. In a secondary transaction, a buyer takes over the shares: that trade does not itself draw its purchase price from the property portfolio’s cash. Chapter one explains these routes. [AMF Ombudsman, 3 February 2025](https://www.amf-france.org/fr/le-mediateur/journal-de-bord-du-mediateur/dossiers-du-mois/scpi-contrairement-aux-ordres-de-vente-des-parts-les-demandes-de-retrait-sont-sans-duree-de-validite).

Private credit includes traditional closed-end funds with long-term committed capital and vehicles offering periodic repurchases. The latter, often called semiliquid, provide the more useful comparison for exit pressure. Their repurchase limits constrain cash leaving the fund; they cannot make loans instantly tradable. [FSB, pp. 7–8 and 31](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P060526.pdf).

In April 2026, the IMF emphasised the role of such limits in containing systemic risk. That was a dated assessment, not a guarantee of any individual redemption. The Bank of England’s July report still noted elevated requests and restrictions at several retail-oriented funds. A mechanism can protect collective management while leaving an investor waiting. [IMF, 14 April briefing](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/04/15/tr-04142026-press-briefing-transcript-global-financial-stability-report-spring-meetings-2026); [Bank of England, July 2026, overview](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability-report/2026/july-2026).

The practical comparison is therefore: who supplies the departing investor’s cash, and under which rule? It may be a new investor, the fund or, under a life insurance contract, the insurer according to its obligations. Those are separate arrangements. A similar-looking account statement does not give them the same legal meaning.

## When a valuation meets a market

Property appraisals and trades in fund shares have distinct dates and methods, as chapter two showed. Private markets face a related problem. The UK’s FCA explains that infrequent trading requires judgement-based estimates. Its March 2025 review found weaknesses in procedures for updating valuations after significant events. It examined processes rather than independently validating individual asset values. [FCA, sections 1.2–1.3 and 2.5](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/multi-firm-reviews/private-market-valuation-practices).

The shared lesson is a reading question: how old is the estimate, what does it assume, and what price does an actual transaction reveal? A steady valuation might reflect a stable asset or slowly updated information. Stability alone cannot decide between them. Authors of a BIS bulletin also explain how traded vehicles could bring price signals into private credit, including persistent discounts to net asset value. That is their analysis, not a rule governing SCPI funds. [Aldasoro, Doerr and Todorov, July 2025](https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-106-retail-investors-private-credit).

## Banks remain in the picture

Chapter five followed the repayment of property loans. In private credit, a bank may finance the lender itself. An August 2026 Federal Reserve staff study examines the US chain from bank to Business Development Company (BDC, an investment vehicle that lends to businesses) to company. It shows how the cost of bank funding enters the economics of private credit. Its scope is the market studied, and the conclusions are the authors’ own. [Haque and Wang, sections 7–8](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/the-price-of-bank-funding-behind-private-credit-evidence-from-business-development-companies-20260807.html).

In both settings, it is useful to look for debt at each level. That does not justify adding all exposures as independent losses. An available credit line, a drawn loan and a decline in fund-share value are different things. The next questions concern maturities, collateral and each borrower’s resources.

## What the comparison gives us

This comparison produces neither a ranking of the investments nor a forecast of a shared crisis. It offers a reading sequence: examine the exit contract, locate the cash, date the valuation and trace the financing commitments. The chapters on income and buildings remain essential to understanding SCPI funds. Borrower analysis and loan documentation do a different job in private credit.

These parallels are l0g’s interpretation of the cited documents. We have established no numerical correlation between losses, no equivalence between repurchase rules and no observed contagion between the SCPI cases and private-credit funds. Understanding why money may take time to come back is already a useful comparison. The contract governing that wait still needs to be read.
