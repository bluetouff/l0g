Trump says he considered a US stake in Nvidia

Part 26

Published 2026-11-25T08:18:27+01:00 · Original article

On November 12, we argued that Nvidia’s crisis gave Donald Trump an opening to shape the succession and might prompt him to consider a government stake. We got that right. Trump pressed for Jensen Huang’s return. Nvidia’s board says it took advice from senior US officials into account. Now Trump says he thought about making the federal government a shareholder. “It’s probably not too late,” he told the New York Post. S01 S04 S05

In part III, we looked at Intel, which gave Washington stock in exchange for funding. Part V examined MP Materials, which agreed to seek the Pentagon’s approval for specific industrial decisions. Intel shows how the government can obtain shares. MP Materials shows the say it can negotiate alongside them. S06 S07 S08

In November, Nvidia reported up to $105 billion in guarantees for SB Energy’s Ohio data center project. That cap covers specified obligations for land, power and buildings, with the guarantees taking effect in phases. Nvidia’s management can help finance facilities that will use its own chips. Influence over the choice of CEO means influence over who will be making those decisions. S14

Five days changed the board’s position

On November 15, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called for Nvidia to hire an outsider. On the 17th, Nvidia chair Harvey Jones told investors that the board had no plans to reinstate Huang. On the 22nd, directors voted unanimously to restore him as chief executive, president and a member of the board. They said that they had spoken with Huang several times and were satisfied with his candor and ability to lead. S03 S04 S11

Trump publicly urged the board to bring Huang back. His post went out at 9:54 p.m. Washington time on November 22, before Nvidia’s announcement. Nvidia’s SEC filing gives November 22 as the date of the vote and leaves its time unspecified. Trump subsequently told CNBC he had spoken to directors and been represented at a board meeting. Nvidia confirms that its directors received input from senior US officials and used it in their security assessment. Part XXIII traces the reversal and the vote. S02 S04 S05

In a separate interview on November 22, Bessent said his team was in touch with the interim management, directors and their lawyers. He left open the question of Huang’s return. Trump subsequently told CNBC he wanted to keep an executive he considered strategically important in the United States and that the market sell-off probably weighed on his thinking. The president’s intervention came in a succession the directors had to resolve themselves. Their independent inquiry continues. S02 S04 S11

From CNBC to the Oval Office interview

On the 23rd, CNBC’s Carl Quintanilla asked if Trump had considered a government stake in Nvidia and whether it remained a possibility. Trump began by saying he would have liked it, then joked about the government suing itself for insider trading. The French edition of part XXIV gave most of the attention to the joke. The opening answer deserved weight. Trump expressed interest in federal ownership in the same interview in which he took credit for Huang’s return. S02

In the New York Post interview, published on November 24 at 11:56 p.m. Paris time, Trump said he had thought about taking a stake. He added that he had not raised it at that point and that it was probably still possible. The article places the interview in the Oval Office and discusses the Intel deal alongside his answer. S01

Here Trump was speaking about the US government. His financial disclosures separately show Nvidia holdings in accounts he reported as part of his own finances. That exposure matters when the president intervenes in a board decision. The White House told PBS that Trump and his family could not direct the portfolio’s investment decisions. The available documents provide no figure for a personal gain realized on Huang’s return. S01 S10

Intel issued shares in return for federal funding

Intel’s contract shows how a government stake can work. In August 2025, the Commerce Department agreed to accelerate an existing funding commitment and retain a second stream of payments tied to a secure manufacturing program. The agreement provided for up to $8.8698 billion in total disbursements. At the August 27 closing, Intel received $5.695 billion and issued 274,583,000 common shares to Commerce. Other shares went into escrow, with releases tied to program payments and special terms for any shares left at the end of the eligibility period. S06 S07

The stock gives the government a financial interest in Intel. The contract also limits how Commerce uses its votes. It generally follows the board’s recommendations, subject to exceptions that protect the deal and its relationship with the government. Intel described the holding as passive, with no board seat. Commerce also received a warrant, a right to buy shares at a fixed price, exercisable if Intel ceases to own at least 51% of its foundry business. That term gives a possible cost to separating the manufacturing business the funding is meant to support. S06 S07

At MP Materials, Washington negotiated a different form of power. The July 2025 agreement pairs preferred shares, which give their holder priority over ordinary shares on specified terms, with a warrant, financing and a buyer for magnet production. During the period defined in the contract, certain asset sales and changes of control require the Pentagon’s consent. The right can last while a purchase agreement remains in force or a specified loan remains unpaid. Washington can secure leverage over an industrial decision by negotiating which actions require its consent. The size of its shareholding tells only part of the story. S08

A deal with Nvidia would turn on the terms

If Nvidia issued new shares, it would receive the proceeds. Shareholders who did not subscribe would own a smaller fraction of the company through equity dilution. If the government bought from existing shareholders, the money would go to the sellers. A narrower deal could give Washington rights over reserved computing capacity or the sale of assets in a particular project. Those choices have very different consequences for Nvidia’s financing and for its board.

Intel had an identified source of public money to bring forward. After the Intel deal was announced in August 2025, Bessent said Nvidia did not appear to need federal financial support and set aside the idea at the time. Trump has reopened the question for Nvidia. A deal would tell us how much the government was putting in, which shares it would get and which decisions it would have a say in. As of the morning of November 25, the public record goes as far as Trump’s remarks. S13 S01 S06 S12

Lawmakers are asking for the records of the succession. On November 24, four senior House Democrats wrote to Trump seeking all communications between administration officials and Nvidia’s board from November 12 to 23, along with answers about warnings the president received and steps he took. They requested a response by December 8. Their letter concerns the contacts surrounding Huang’s return. The records could clarify which assurances reached the board and whether directors made commitments in return. S09

We raised the question of power in the conclusion to The Nvidia System. Huang’s return shows how far presidential influence can reach into a boardroom. An agreement could give that influence a longer life. If Washington buys a stake, which Nvidia decisions will it want a say in, and who will hold it to account? S01 S04 S09
