Megaspeed’s US listing puts data center contracts under scrutiny

Part 25

Published 2026-11-24T15:27:49+01:00 · Original article

A Malaysian data center operator with a customer on the new US Entity List has decisions to make before the next equipment delivery or renewal. Who will receive the servers and control them once they are installed? Is a pending order still eligible to go through? What does the lease say if the operator cannot provide the equipment or services its tenant bought? Those questions arise from a US export rule covering seven named entities and a separate announcement by Malaysia about the advice it intends to give operators. Nvidia’s board had voted on November 22 to bring Jensen Huang back. Suppliers now have to respond to a new rule as Congress asks what the White House told his directors. Our previous installment follows the timing of his return and the questions it raised about Trump’s holdings. S01 S02 S26 S27

The order a supplier can still fill

The Bureau of Industry and Security, or BIS, put its final rule on public inspection on November 23. The text makes the listing effective on that date and is scheduled for publication on November 25. It places three entries under Malaysia, three under Singapore and one under Thailand. They include the Singapore company Megaspeed International, its Malaysian subsidiary Speedmatrix, and Apex Enterprise Solutions (Singapore), part of Alibaba’s group. The US interagency committee cited a significant risk that the entities were or could become involved in diverting advanced computing products to unauthorized users or uses. That is the threshold the rule invokes to restrict transactions. Criminal liability would be determined through a different process. S01 S02 S05 S06

A license is required for exports, reexports and transfers within a country of items subject to the US Export Administration Regulations when a listed entity is a covered party to the transaction. The rule removes access to license exceptions for the entries and directs BIS to review applications under a presumption of denial. The requirement applies across items covered by the regulations. Identifying the purchaser, consignee and end user therefore matters as much as identifying the chip. A supplier can have a licensing obligation even when its immediate customer is a different company and the equipment never moves directly from an American port. The rule provides a narrow saving clause for qualifying shipments already en route aboard a carrier under actual orders on the inspection date, with 30 days from that date to complete the transaction. An order on the books by itself gives a supplier no such protection. S01 S06

For a landlord or a colocation operator, the first question is what it has agreed to deliver. Leasing floor space, supplying a managed cluster and handing equipment over to another end user are different transactions. A lease renewal may require an operator to install replacement hardware or to transfer control of existing units. A cloud contract can give a customer computing access while the equipment remains in place. The export analysis depends on the items, the parties and the actual changes in use or control, alongside the separate US rules on remote access. The operator needs to check how the hands-on work at its site fits the rule, then look at what Malaysia asks it to do. S01 S02 S06

Kuala Lumpur and Singapore are checking different records

On November 24 Malaysia’s trade ministry, MITI, said the Malaysian Investment Development Authority would send a notice advising the country’s data center operators against hosting, leasing space to, or providing services to any of the seven entities. That is the ministry’s announcement of intended advice. The circulated notice, an order directed to a particular site or evidence of a terminated lease would establish what happened next. The ministry also says its agency’s records identify Speedmatrix and MSFusion as Malaysian entities. Those records show no Malaysian entity, data center operator or tenant operating under the name PT Megaspeed International. The US rule puts that same name under Malaysia and attaches Malaysian addresses. Company registration, occupancy records and the addresses BIS associates with an entity answer different questions. As of November 24 the entry remained in the US text. S01 S02 S06

Singapore faces another evidentiary problem. Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said at a November 24 press conference that the Nvidia chips he was discussing had been routed to other countries. The two governments’ transcripts differ in their description of transit through Singapore. He said the authorities need help tracing end users and checking documents, and that some information sought from the Americans had yet to arrive. Singapore’s own investigation concerns possible violations of Singapore law. Wong said authorities had investigated and released Alice Huang because they had found no basis for further prosecution of her. The Home Affairs Ministry had already distinguished its inquiry into possible offenses involving server purchases from an espionage investigation. In their November 16 statement, the US Justice and Commerce departments said Commerce opened its own Megaspeed investigation in 2024. The agencies’ public descriptions leave the equipment’s end use and the extent of the evidence exchanged between governments unresolved. S03 S04 S08

Apex’s collateral comes into view

Alibaba says the Apex listing is erroneous, that its subsidiary has never diverted advanced computing chips subject to US export restrictions, and that it will pursue legal avenues to protect its rights. The rule gives the committee’s assessment of diversion risk. The company’s response addresses its own conduct. Any request to amend the listing would go to the US interagency committee for review. S01 S05 S06

The records cover two kinds of collateral. Bloomberg, citing Singapore corporate records, describes a January 8, 2024 services agreement between Apex and Megaspeed and a June 18 arrangement for up to nearly $474 million in prepayments, with Megaspeed’s shares in its Malaysian subsidiary Speedmatrix offered as security. Bloomberg describes a prepayment up to that ceiling. The amount actually paid under the arrangement remains unclear. The Malaysian charge reviewed by The Straits Times and SOFX, also dated June 18, covers specified Speedmatrix equipment, intellectual property and related rights. The registry excerpt describes a charge securing certain advances. It excludes obligations arising from service fees prepaid on or before the execution of a supplemental agreement. One arrangement concerns shares in the subsidiary. The other concerns operating assets and rights held by it. The Straits Times reported the charge as active on November 11, 2026. Recoveries depend on the operative contracts, amounts advanced and steps any creditor has taken to enforce its rights. S07

Reuters separately reported, on the account of one unnamed person familiar with the matter, that Apex had paid about $4.2 billion in advance to rent computing capacity from Megaspeed and other companies in Southeast Asia. The source described payments across several counterparties. How much each received, and which contracts the payments related to, remains unclear. If capacity has to move to a different site, the contracts will help determine who bears the cost. S07

From the White House to the steps of Air Force One

Trump and Huang first met at the White House on January 31, 2025, according to accounts of the meeting. The presidential diary records a discussion of developments in Chinese AI. Trump’s inaugural committee declared it had received $1 million from Nvidia on January 13. His accounts report a Nvidia purchase in the $1 million to $5 million band on January 29. In April the US told Nvidia that H20 exports to China and other covered destinations and parties would require a license, then said the rule would apply indefinitely. In Riyadh in May, Trump greeted Huang at an investment forum, and Nvidia and HUMAIN announced a first phase built around an 18,000-unit GB300 supercomputer. In China, Nvidia was waiting on Washington. In Riyadh, it was announcing a customer for a new system. S10 S12 S13 S14

Huang met Trump again in Washington in July. On July 14 US time, already July 15 in Beijing, Nvidia said it was again applying for H20 licenses and expected approval. In August the government granted licenses for certain customers. The financial disclosure records a $5 million to $25 million Nvidia purchase on August 18. Trump publicly described a negotiation over a 15 percent share for the US. Nvidia told investors that officials expected a share of licensed H20 revenue and that the government had yet to publish a rule codifying it. In September Huang attended the state banquet at Windsor during Trump’s UK visit. Nvidia later confirmed a donation to the nonprofit Trust for the National Mall for the White House ballroom project. The Washington Post reported a $25 million pledge from an internal donor list. The amount actually paid to the trust remains undisclosed. S10 S15 S16 S17

That December Trump publicly announced a change of course on the H200. His disclosure reports a Nvidia purchase on January 6, 2026, in the $500,001 to $1 million band. BIS detailed its licensing policy on January 13 and the rule took effect on January 15. Senator Elizabeth Warren later asked Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent about the timing. The public December announcement preceded that trade. Establishing a link to any unpublished information would require the order record and evidence of who knew what when the transaction was placed. In May an image from Beijing put the relationship on display. The White House’s account shows Trump introducing Huang to Chinese officials at the airport on May 13. The presidential press pool says Huang boarded during the Anchorage stop on May 12 local time. According to The New York Times, citing people with knowledge of the matter, Secretary of State Marco Rubio briefed Trump orally on intelligence concerning Huang’s relationship shortly before he boarded. A memo prepared earlier and circulated to a small group of officials was reportedly separate from that conversation. Trump denied having known about the matter at the time of the trip, and the White House disputed the reporting. The public record fixes the itinerary. The account of Rubio’s briefing comes from sources quoted by the Times. S20

Trump’s disclosures put three trades close to that trip. They list a $15,001 to $50,000 purchase on May 15, a $500,001 to $1 million sale on May 18 and a $100,001 to $250,000 purchase on May 21. The White House says independent financial institutions made the investment decisions through model portfolios, without direction from Trump or his family. The disclosed amounts give a range for the trades. Account instructions, the information the managers had and any profit on the sale remain unknown. S11 S21 S22

CNBC put the Nvidia holdings disclosed for the end of 2025 at $12.5 million to $58 million. The network counted 38 purchases and sales between January and August 2026, with $9.4 million to $28.3 million in combined trade values. The Washington Post estimated the position at the end of August at $13 million to $62 million. One number counts money changing hands. The holdings estimates describe the value of an investment on specific dates. The size of the position on the day of Huang’s return is still unknown. S11 S21 S22

The board’s vote and Congress’s next questions

On September 29 Huang signed a voluntary AI safety accord at the White House alongside Trump and five other business leaders. Trump awarded him the National Medal of Science on October 8. Nvidia’s filing dates Huang’s departure to November 11. The company announced it on November 12. On November 16 Trump told reporters he would be open to a return, and on November 20 he urged the company to take Huang back after an apology. Nvidia’s filing dates the unanimous reinstatement vote to November 22. In its announcement the next day, the board said that it had received input from senior officials and was confident the return posed no national security risk. The board said its independent inquiry would continue. Nvidia released the decision at 8:55 a.m. Eastern on November 23. The export rule went on inspection later that day, at 4:15 p.m. Eastern. S01 S23 S24 S25 S26

The Washington Post reports that incoming Senate Foreign Relations chair Chris Coons plans to pursue inquiries into Nvidia and Huang in January, citing a person familiar with the plans. December appearances are already on the calendar. Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter have agreed to transcribed House interviews on December 1 and 2 respectively. Huang’s December 3 Senate Banking hearing is a separate proceeding. These sessions give lawmakers a chance to ask which officials advised the directors, what they relied on and how Nvidia will oversee compliance with Huang back in charge. The listing gives the questions a concrete counterpart overseas. Suppliers and operators now have to explain who receives the equipment and services, and under what authority. The next documents that could settle those questions are specific. Lawmakers want the advice given to Nvidia’s board. Customers and hosts will be looking for the Malaysian notice and decisions on individual contracts. S01 S02 S09 S26 S27
