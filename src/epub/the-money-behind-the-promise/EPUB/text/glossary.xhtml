Twenty-one terms for following the promise

Pension risk transfer

An arrangement that transfers some or all of the risks associated with defined-benefit pension promises to an insurer, usually through an annuity purchase. The transaction structure and applicable law determine which liabilities move, who is legally responsible for paying benefits and which protection arrangements cover beneficiaries.

Buy-in / buy-out

In the UK, a buy-in is an insurance policy held as a pension scheme asset: the insurer pays the scheme, whose trustees remain legally responsible for benefits. A buy-out makes the insurer directly responsible to covered members. The distinction rests on who owes the benefits, including when a buy-in covers every member.

Asset-intensive reinsurance

Reinsurance that transfers investment risks and risks associated with life insurance or annuity liabilities to a counterparty. The reinsurer agrees to fund covered benefits; the cedant remains responsible to its policyholders. Assets may transfer or stay with the cedant, depending on the contractual structure.

Funds withheld

A reinsurance structure in which the cedant retains assets while the treaty allocates agreed income and risks to the reinsurer. Legal ownership, cash-flow rights and accounting recognition depend on the structure and applicable rules.

ModCo

A proportional reinsurance structure in which the cedant retains both assets and reserves on its balance sheet. The treaty allocates agreed risks and returns to the reinsurer and sets settlements between the parties. Protection depends on rights over the assets, their valuation and recapture conditions.

Recapture

The insurer takes back risks previously ceded to a reinsurer under treaty-defined triggers, sometimes before formal insolvency. Recapture requires assessment of recoverable assets, access rights, liquidity needs and required capital. The amount of liabilities recaptured is not a measure of permanent loss.

ALM : Asset-liability management

Managing investments and obligations together. For an insurer, ALM aligns the amounts, timing, currencies and sensitivities of assets with promised policy benefits while planning for liquidity needs. Policyholder surrenders and other contractual options can change the expected cash flows.

AUM : Assets under management

Assets managed or advised under the manager’s disclosed scope and valuation method. The fee-generating base can differ from total AUM. Managing a portfolio and legally owning its assets confer different rights.

Apollo’s adjusted pre-tax earnings measure for its retirement-services business, based on investment income less the cost of liabilities and the expenses included in its definition. The relevant publication specifies the scope, adjustments and earnings attributable to the group.

FRE : Fee-related earnings

An adjusted earnings measure based on fee revenues after the expenses and adjustments specified by the company. Included revenues and methods vary by issuer and period. Testing a forecast requires comparable earnings levels; gross fee revenue is a separate measure.

Related party

A person or entity connected to a company through control, significant influence or other relationships defined by the applicable accounting framework. These connections require disclosure and governance suited to conflicts of interest. For an investment, identify the manager, the vehicle and the debtor.

ABS : Asset-backed security

A security whose payments depend primarily on cash flows from a pool of claims. Securitisation finances the pool through securities whose tranches have different payment priorities and loss exposure. Risk transfer depends on the structure and on which tranches are sold or retained.

CLO : Collateralized loan obligation

A securitisation backed by a portfolio of corporate loans. It finances those loans by issuing securities with different levels of seniority. A contractual waterfall allocates cash flows, paying senior tranches before subordinated tranches, while losses first reach the residual tranche. The protection afforded to a security depends on its seniority, the portfolio and the contractual terms as well as its rating.

RBC : Risk-based capital

A US prudential framework that assesses an insurer’s capital needs according to its risks. The complete calculation combines several risk categories and adjustments. A factor applied to an asset is one step in that calculation. Published ratios must be read with their definitions and denominators, particularly for measures specified in a reinsurance treaty.

Matching adjustment

A UK prudential mechanism requiring permission, which increases the discount rate for eligible liabilities backed by a portfolio meeting cash-flow matching criteria. The adjustment deducts compensation for retained risks from the asset spread. It lowers the present value of liabilities without generating immediate cash.

Collateral

An asset provided to secure an obligation, through a pledge or transfer of title according to the arrangement. Following a breach, the beneficiary can exercise the rights established by the contract and applicable law. In reinsurance, the assets, their valuation and access conditions determine the collateral’s usefulness to the cedant.

Unit-linked option

A life insurance obligation expressed in units whose euro value tracks a financial or property investment. The insurer commits to a number of units, which policy charges can reduce; the policyholder bears changes in unit value, subject to any additional contractual guarantees. The policy surrender timetable and the underlying fund’s liquidity are separate questions.

Consolidation perimeter

The accounting boundary within which a parent combines, line by line, the assets, liabilities, income and expenses of entities it controls. Internal transactions are eliminated to present the group’s dealings with outside parties. Each company’s accounts identify the rights and obligations that remain between them.

Revolving credit facility

A facility allowing a borrower to draw funds, repay them and borrow again during an agreed period, subject to contractual limits and conditions. It can fund a gap between outgoing and incoming payments. The committed limit, amount drawn and cash actually available are separate measures.

Counterparty credit risk

The risk that a counterparty defaults before a transaction’s cash flows are finally settled. A loss can arise when the contracts have positive economic value to the other party at default. Exposure changes with market conditions; collateral and netting affect the remaining loss.

Reinsurance

A contract through which an insurer transfers all or part of specified risks to a reinsurer in exchange for a premium. The cover and limits determine which losses are shared. The original insurer remains responsible to its policyholders, including if the reinsurer fails to pay.
