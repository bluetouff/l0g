Chapter 7

When risks return to the insurer

Published: 2026-10-23T22:41:15+02:00 · Updated: 2026-10-23T22:41:15+02:00

When investment firms buy insurers · Article 7/8 · Research cutoff: 23 October 2026

In a stress exercise published in November 2025, the British supervisor models insurers taking back £12.3 billion of liabilities after a simulated counterparty failure. The firms absorb the shock. Understanding that result means following the collateral, the rights over the assets and the funding of payments, including the test’s assumptions about liquidity.

This seventh investigation moves from the prudential treatment of insurance promises to the return of risks, rights over assets and the funding of payments.

An annuity payment still falls due while the companies financing it work through their difficulties. An insurer may have passed some of the investment and longevity risks to a reinsurer, but its obligation to the policyholder remains. The International Association of Insurance Supervisors, or IAIS, expressly distinguishes reinsurance from a novation that replaces the debtor under the policy. The cedant, the insurer transferring the risks, remains legally responsible for paying the benefit. 1

Recapture, the taking back of ceded risks, puts that responsibility to the test. Collateral may come back under the insurer’s control, financial hedges need reviewing, and the capital requirement changes as the firm takes on the risks again. The challenge is to make the assets serve the annuity payment schedule when their availability, currency and value may have changed. British supervisory expectations require insurers to prepare for precisely this sequence, including the long-term management of assets that are difficult to sell. 2

The Prudential Regulation Authority, the UK’s prudential supervisor for insurers, examined this question publicly in its Life Insurance Stress Test 2025, known as LIST. Results published on 17 November 2025 use balance sheets at 31 December 2024. The exploratory recapture scenario brings back £12.3 billion of liabilities, approximately half the funded reinsurance exposure within the defined scope for the firms concerned. That amount measures the obligations being taken back. The report then assesses firms’ ability to support them, allowing for recovered collateral and the actions permitted by the exercise. 3

One crisis, two branches of the calculation

The core scenario begins with a market shock: lower risk-free interest rates, wider credit spreads and falling equity prices. Conditions deteriorate further through defaults, rating downgrades and property losses. At the third stage, markets begin to stabilise. Firms can recognise certain management actions and must restore part of their investment portfolios to their original credit quality. The exercise is designed around severe but plausible conditions and a common balance sheet date. 3, 4

Recapture follows a particular branch of this story. Section C of the instructions starts from the second stage and modifies the third to bring a reinsurer’s risks back to the cedant. For each firm, the chosen counterparty is the one whose recapture would have the most adverse immediate effect on its solvency ratio, agreed with the PRA. The choice is specific to the insurer. The publication therefore does not describe a single identified reinsurer failing across all participants. 4

The modelled trigger is a breach of a solvency condition applying to the reinsurer. This matters because a contract can give the insurer a recapture right before formal insolvency. Supervisory expectations ask firms to examine trigger thresholds, opportunities to remedy a deterioration, and the merits of exercising recapture. Depending on the circumstances, continuing the treaty and taking the assets back expose the insurer to different risks. 4, 2

The collateral assumptions are tightly specified. The test uses the contractual minimum held at the end of 2024 and applies the market and credit shocks from the second stage. Any collateral above that minimum is stripped out, as are additional recoveries the insurer might hope to receive later. Benefits from existing protection within the group are recognised as a separate movement where the conditions allow. These conventions limit the resources firms can count in the calculation. 4

The population matters as much as the scenario. The core test covers eleven UK legal entities; the recapture exercise applies only to those with material funded reinsurance exposure. For the latter, qualifying agreements were entered into from 1 January 2016 and exclude reinsurance within the group. The individual results published on 24 November 2025 concern the core scenario. Recapture results are published only in aggregate, leaving the effects on individual insurers undisclosed. 3, 5

The capital requirement rises with the risks

For the subset of firms taking liabilities back, the capital coverage ratio falls from 154% in the core comparator to 144% with recapture. The PRA attributes most of the change to a 6% increase in the solvency capital requirement, or SCR. Eligible own funds fall by 1%, mainly because the risk margin increases. These are the supervisor’s published, rounded changes. 3

The ratio relates two amounts: the financial resources a firm can count as available to absorb losses, and the capital required for the risks on its balance sheet. When credit or longevity risks return, the second amount can grow much faster than the first declines. Coverage then falls without an equivalent loss draining a bank account. In the exercise, the PRA points to the return of risks already present at the cedants, whose impact depends on their interaction with the rest of the balance sheet. 3

A simple normalisation makes the mechanism visible. Set the comparator’s SCR to 100. Coverage of 154% then implies eligible own funds of 154. Applying the rounded published changes gives approximately 152.5 of own funds against a capital requirement of 106, producing coverage close to 144%. These index values explain the ratio movement; they do not establish the corresponding sterling amounts. 3

The risk margin enters elsewhere in the balance sheet. It supplements the best estimate of obligations within technical provisions. An increase can therefore reduce available own funds, while the SCR remains a separate capital requirement. This structure helps explain why recapture can consume prudential resources even when the recovered collateral continues to make its payments. The test captures the return of risks and the cost of supporting them, reaching beyond a single collateral valuation. 6, 3

The outcome supports the PRA’s assessment of resilience for the exposures held at the end of 2024. Its scope remains the firms, assets and conditions examined. The exploratory recapture scenario carries no target probability. The broadly one-in-a-hundred severity assigned to the core scenario does not become an estimate of how often reinsurers will fail. 3

Rights over the assets follow their own timetable

Access to collateral begins with the rights the treaty allows the insurer to exercise. LIST’s instructions require firms to assess obstacles involving counterparties, other stakeholders and the reinsurer’s regulator. To recognise collateral in the scenario, the insurer must be able to obtain beneficial ownership from the first day of recapture and legal ownership within six months. Those timings are conditions for the calculation required of participants. They do not bind a court to complete proceedings within that interval. 4

Derivatives add another operation to prepare. In this simulation, derivatives within the collateral are assumed to be settled immediately before recapture, after the shocks have been applied. They are not transferred intact to the insurer through novation. The insurer must then consider the interest-rate, inflation or currency hedges needed for the returning portfolio. The instructions permit certain swaps during the calculation, while the results report highlights the significance of the cost of new currency hedges when accommodating recovered assets. 4, 3

Recapture can also concern assets already held by the cedant. Just Group’s 2025 annual report provides a concrete example. For one funded reinsurance partner, collateral can consist of corporate bonds in a segregated account or lifetime mortgages under a funds-withheld arrangement. These loans are secured on the home and are generally repaid with interest when the homeowner dies or enters long-term care. Just explains that the loans remain legally held and managed by the group, while their investment returns are paid to the reinsurer. Holding the loans and receiving their returns are therefore separate parts of the arrangement. 7

This is the company’s disclosure for 2025, separate from the end-2024 portfolio used in LIST. The note does not supply the complete treaty, the thresholds for this particular relationship or a detailed recapture timetable. It does, however, show why tracking only where assets are held would miss part of the transaction: entitlement to income, conditions for returning assets and responsibility for risks must be read together. 7, 2

Method and scope

The two timetables distinguish asset rights from cash receipts. Day 1 and six months are LIST collateral-recognition conditions, with no guaranteed real-world deadline. Holding and selling assets follow separate paths; a permitted sale requires legal ownership and a liquid secondary market. Prearranged facilities are scoped to Section C movements a–d. Qualitative mechanism, with no numerical cash flows.

Once assets have been recovered, another assessment concerns their regulatory use. Subject to conditions, the matching adjustment allows insurers to recognise part of the yield on assets whose payments match future benefits. It affects the present value assigned to liabilities. Under LIST, recovered collateral can enter a portfolio receiving this adjustment only if it meets the insurer’s existing permissions. Participants assessed most of the recaptured collateral as eligible. The PRA identifies that as an important factor limiting the shock. 8, 4, 3

Eligibility makes assets immediately useful in the regulatory balance sheet. Their ability to provide cash on a particular date still depends on coupons, repayments, currency and market conditions. Supervisory expectations consequently require preparation both for managing the recovered assets and for meeting the liquidity needs associated with recapture. 2, 9

Cash needs run through the scenario

Annuity payments and collateral calls on financial hedges have their own due dates. A portfolio with ample value can need temporary funding if its receipts arrive after its outgoings. The British liquidity framework asks insurers to examine those timing gaps, the assets they can mobilise and the availability of funding under stress. Solvency and liquidity therefore need to be assessed together, using their respective measures. 9

LIST includes a substantial modelling convention here. During the first two stages of the core scenario, firms may assume an unlimited liquidity facility. The regulator explains why: it prevents liquidity constraints from dominating the capital impact while sales and other management actions are heavily restricted. Among other things, this assumption allows firms to meet margin and collateral calls without the exercise turning on that funding need alone. Identified shortfalls must be explained in the qualitative report submitted to the PRA. 4

The recapture branch tightens the framework. Permitted drawings rely on facilities already arranged; external trades require a liquid secondary market and control of legal ownership. Firms can use the permitted hedges. Additional reinsurance is excluded, and recapitalisation can be assumed to be in place only where existing contractual arrangements allow it. The test thus brings several funding decisions into view, within a deliberately standardised set of options intended to make results comparable. 4

Just’s disclosures illustrate tools that exist outside this modelling convention. Its 2025 solvency report describes a £400 million revolving credit facility, with direct access extended during the year to its two insurance subsidiaries, Just Retirement Limited and Partnership Life Assurance Company Limited. The report expressly separates liquidity management from the SCR calculation. It also describes a funding plan that considers the time, cost and difficulty of the available options. 10

The facility is a documented funding resource whose drawing conditions and competing uses then need to be examined. The share of the £400 million available for any particular recapture remains to be established. Reconstructing a transaction’s funding would require matching expected benefit payments and margin calls against cash inflows from assets and the facilities the relevant legal entity could actually draw on. That reconciliation is also missing from LIST’s aggregate results: I found no published amount for a recapture-specific cash shortfall. 10, 3, 4

The cost of restoring credit quality

Asset sales appear in the exercise within a scope that can be traced. In the core scenario, the PRA requires firms to restore 25% of downgraded assets in each matching-adjustment portfolio to their initial credit quality, excluding equity-release mortgage securitisations, which receive separate treatment. Firms must sell some securities damaged by the shock and buy higher-quality investments at a cost specified by the test’s conventions. In the recapture branch, this prescribed rebalancing expressly excludes the collateral recovered from the reinsurer. 4

The PRA estimates that prescribed rebalancing leads to £3.4 billion of sales of sub-investment-grade bonds. Firms also exchange a further £0.9 billion of sub-investment-grade bonds for higher-rated investments through management actions. These volumes describe adjustments to the eleven entities’ portfolios in the core scenario. No amount is published for sales specifically involving recaptured assets. 3

Prescribed rebalancing as a whole reduces eligible own funds by £800 million after tax. That overall cost covers a wider scope than the £3.4 billion of sub-investment-grade bond sales alone, so dividing the two amounts cannot establish a sale haircut. The published effect on the coverage ratio is limited to one percentage point because improving investment quality also reduces the capital requirement. 3

Method and scope

Core scenario, eleven entities: PRA estimate of £3.4bn in prescribed sales and £0.9bn in additional trades of sub-investment-grade bonds. The £0.8bn after-tax cost covers prescribed rebalancing as a whole; it cannot establish a haircut on these sales. The 25% rule excludes ERM securitisations and, in the recapture branch, recovered collateral. Sales specific to recapture remain unquantified.

The balance sheet explains the movement. Buying a higher-rated asset reduces credit risk but can also lower the yield recognised through the matching adjustment. A fall in that adjustment raises the value assigned to liabilities and weighs on own funds. The insurer restores portfolio quality at a financial cost that the final ratio expresses only in part. The PRA reports that several participants were struck by the limited benefit of rebalancing in stressed and illiquid conditions. 3

For recovered collateral, retaining the assets can therefore be a rational choice when their payments fit the insurer’s obligations and the firm can manage the risks. SS5/24 asks insurers to prepare the capabilities needed when illiquid assets must be held for a long period because selling them at the expected price proves difficult. Preparing for recapture is as much about administering a portfolio as it is about selling it. 2, 11

Resilience depends on the portfolio that comes back

The strongest argument in favour of the arrangements lies in the results themselves. Using end-2024 exposures, the participating firms absorb the simulated return of liabilities from the counterparty selected for the greatest immediate adverse effect on each firm’s coverage ratio. Much of the collateral is assessed as suitable for their matching-adjustment portfolios. Assets intended to finance annuities over many years can thus continue to serve that purpose after the economic risks return to the insurer. Restrictions on excess collateral, later recoveries and new capital also give weight to the result. 3, 4

Other scenario choices define its limits. Collateral composition is fixed at the mix observed at the end of 2024, with no forward-looking allocation changes. No currency shock is applied, although a high proportion of collateral is denominated in US dollars. The PRA says sterling appreciation would have worsened the outcome. It also notes that greater volumes, more complex assets or reduced matching-adjustment eligibility could produce non-linear effects. 3

The supervisor identified gaps in the modelling of some recapture costs and announced individual follow-up. The publication does not quantify those gaps. Their financial importance therefore remains an open question, alongside the cash required, the prices at which recovered assets might be sold and the full cost of rebuilding hedges. Coverage of 144% answers a solvency question within the defined scenario; continuity of payments also requires the operational evidence. 3, 9

That connection appears in an IAIS paper published in July 2026 on preparing for insurer resolution. Its discussion of asset-intensive reinsurance highlights legal and operational obstacles to collateral availability and the potential effects of recapture on the different parties. The paper provides implementation guidance without creating new standards; the mechanisms described depend on authorities’ powers and contractual arrangements. It encourages the authorities concerned to prepare coordination and examine potential contagion between the reinsurer and its cedants. 12

To take the investigation further into a specific transaction, the decisive document would be a shared timetable: enforceable rights over each asset, cash that can be collected, annuities and margins falling due, confirmed funding, and the choices between holding and selling. Public documents allow the stages and several constraints to be reconstructed. They show why protecting an annuity has to be judged over time: the insurer must be able to turn returning resources into payments at every due date under the policy. 2, 9, 4

Method. Investigation based on public documents, distinguishing simulation results, supervisory requirements, company disclosures and explanatory calculations. Just’s 2025 information illustrates a mechanism; it does not replace LIST’s end-2024 balance sheet. No interviews or access to confidential treaties are claimed.
