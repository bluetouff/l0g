Dated records and bounded conclusions

This edition dated 28 October 2026 brings together six investigations with their own research cut-offs: 27 October for chapters 1–5 and 28 October for chapter 6. The introduction and conclusion are original editorial syntheses based on those chapters. Dates of events, decisions, publication and future deadlines retain their distinct functions.

The book draws on judicial records, tax publications, parliamentary work, supervisory reports, research and the reporting identified in the chapter bibliographies. Statements by administrations, banks, their representatives and the authors of models are attributed. Proposals, enacted legislation and administrative guidance are distinguished. Each chapter records limitations of access and the underlying material that was not obtained.

French agreements are described through their own legal effects. Judicial approval of a CJIP is distinct from a criminal conviction; the position of third parties requires their own records. Foreign judgments, appeals, remittals and reported schedules retain their procedural status and dates. The book offers a documented selection of proceedings, including favourable findings and defence positions available in the cited material.

Historical estimates, notified assessments, payments already made and agreed fines measure different things. Their periods and scopes are retained. Scenarios, decompositions and elapsed-time calculations are teaching devices with stated inputs and rounding. Illustrative rates and amounts explain mechanisms; transaction-level evidence and a national fiscal account require further data.

Each chapter retains its complete bibliography. Source numbers S1 or S01 and onward restart in every chapter, so a reference always needs the chapter number. The 93 notices in each volume can cite a document more than once; the count measures notices rather than 93 independent sources. The synthesis references locate the evidence used in the introduction and conclusion.

The book retains 26 figure compositions, their captions and explanatory methods. The comparison reader and numerical lab receive static adaptations for offline reading, with their illustrative assumptions and results made explicit. The self-contained glossary reuses seven concepts from the site. Cross-chapter links are adapted for the book; external documents and interactive tools on l0g.fr require an internet connection.

Provenance files identify the articles and compositions used in this edition. Collecting the investigations into a volume does not turn the earlier chapters into an exhaustive update as of 28 October. Developments discussed in the final chapter, including treaty changes, the EBA’s supervisory review and the June 2026 EU proposal, retain the scope and limitations given in their source notes.
