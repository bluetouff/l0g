Conclusion

Who bears the cost of a delay?

Across these six chapters, the consequences of slowing down depend on how a decision is implemented. An evaluation requirement can reduce risk while imposing a cost of entry. A manufacturing delay can leave a supplier able to serve some uses while limiting its ability to expand. Understanding a pause means following the activity affected, the alternatives available and the commitments already made.

The first requirement is therefore to examine the terms of oversight. Who defines the tests? Who can challenge them? What happens to a disagreement? Commercial links between evaluators and the companies they assess give reason to ask those questions. Documented incidents, in turn, give reason to examine permissions, isolation measures and human responses. Both inquiries benefit from being conducted together, with accessible criteria and precise attribution.

Competition needs to be observed where a user can actually switch providers. An advertised price captures only part of that decision. Quality for the task, licence terms, migration work and human corrections may change the calculation. At an industrial scale, the inquiry continues through delivered volumes, memory availability and manufacturing yields. A commercial announcement leaves those questions open until the corresponding evidence becomes available.

Finance makes these schedules more demanding. The interval between an expense and a receipt enters the calculation of value. A project that waits also retains the obligations imposed by its contracts. The documents discussed in the book show why an ordinary maturity, an event-triggered repayment and a conditional guarantee must be distinguished. Adding their amounts as though they all became payable together would hide the mechanism being examined.

Following what happens next requires documents that connect a promise with its execution: evaluation results, incident accounts, terms of use, manufacturing deliveries, published financial statements and financing agreements. Each answers a limited question. Bringing them together means preserving dates, units, counterparties and applicable conditions. Where parts of the picture remain opaque, the gap needs to stay visible rather than being filled by an attractive narrative.

The investigation therefore does not prescribe one ideal pace for all artificial intelligence. It offers ways to examine a particular decision: the risk addressed, the authority granted, the alternative available and the burden transferred. A rule can be necessary while its implementation remains open to criticism. An investment can secure funding and still be exposed to delayed revenue. Each situation calls for reading the relevant documents on their own terms.

The final question is for readers, policymakers and investors: when someone asks for more time, which obligations keep running for everyone else? Identifying who benefits from a decision, who bears its cost and the terms of their commitments makes the slowdown debate something that can be examined in detail. The timetable of AI concerns the organisation of power as much as the speed of models.
