Bond-market glossary

Definitions reproduced from the l0g glossary. Chapter links to these entries work offline; the online version may change.

Government bond auction

A procedure that allocates securities based on submitted bids. French OAT auctions award each successful bid at its submitted price. US Treasury auctions award successful bids at a uniform price corresponding to the highest accepted yield, rate or discount margin. Bids, awards and settlement describe different stages.

Bond coupon

An interest payment specified by a bond’s terms. For a fixed-rate bond, the payment depends on face value and the contractual coupon rate, with the stipulated frequency. The buyer’s yield also depends on the purchase price and other cash flows; a secondary-market sale does not alter the issuer’s coupon.

Repo

The sale of a security with an agreement to repurchase it on agreed terms. Economically, it provides financing secured by that security. A repo can be overnight, longer term or open-ended; the repo rate and the collateral haircut are distinct parameters.

Basis trade

A strategy seeking to profit from the relative prices of a bond and a futures contract. Its long Treasury version combines buying the bond, selling the future and funding in repo. Returns depend on prices, coupons, funding costs and delivery terms. Hedging interest-rate risk leaves basis risk and margin-related liquidity needs.

Collateral

An asset provided to secure a debt, through a pledge or transfer of title according to the arrangement. If the borrower fails to meet its obligations, the lender may realise the asset under the contract and applicable law. For Eurosystem operations, collateral must be eligible and its recognised value reflects haircuts and other risk controls. Providing collateral creates borrowing capacity without creating bank equity.

Rehypothecation

Using received collateral in another transaction. Strictly, rehypothecation concerns pledged assets; reuse under a title-transfer repo involves an obligation to return equivalent securities. Rights depend on the contract and governing law. Reuse supports funding while connecting counterparties’ risks.

Repo haircut

The gap between cash lent and the value of repo collateral. Its calculation must identify the denominator, securities value or initial cash according to the convention. A zero or negative haircut does not describe all portfolio margins or offsets. The effects of an increase depend on contractual terms and refinancing.

Margin call

A request for additional cash or collateral when the value or risk of a position changes. It may be variation margin settling or collateralising current exposure under the contract, or an increase in initial margin covering potential losses after default.

Initial margin

Cash or liquid securities posted at inception and during a position to cover a potential loss while a defaulting participant is closed out. Its amount depends on risk, volatility and recognised portfolio offsets.

Variation margin

A transfer linked to a position’s change in value that settles or collateralises current exposure according to the contract. Cleared derivatives generally require cash. Receipt can lag a payment due on another position.

LDI

A strategy aligning the interest-rate and inflation sensitivity of pension assets with future pension liabilities. Bonds, derivatives and sometimes borrowing provide the exposure. Leverage creates collateral demands: a better-funded pension scheme can own an LDI vehicle facing cash shortages or depleted capital.

TPI

ECB instrument announced in July 2022 for secondary-market purchases in response to unwarranted tensions threatening monetary-policy transmission. The Governing Council assesses fiscal policies and debt sustainability, among other criteria. An excessive deficit procedure alone does not exclude a country taking the required effective corrective action. Activation remains discretionary.

CNRF

A Bank of England facility open for applications since January 2025 to certain admitted insurers, pension schemes and LDI funds. It lends cash against gilts for a short term and is activated at the Bank’s discretion during severe dysfunction threatening UK financial stability. Pricing is calibrated at activation; admission does not establish actual borrowing.

APF

The Bank of England vehicle holding monetary-policy assets, financed by a Bank loan and covered by a Treasury indemnity. Cash transfers can flow to the Treasury or in the reverse direction. They do not by themselves measure QE’s comprehensive fiscal impact or the return from the temporary LDI financial-stability purchases.

QE

Central-bank asset purchases intended to ease monetary conditions, affecting yields and financing. Their fiscal effects include portfolio cash flows and broader influences on public borrowing costs and the economy. Purchases undertaken for financial stability have a distinct purpose.
