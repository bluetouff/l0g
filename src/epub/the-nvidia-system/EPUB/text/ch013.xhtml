Nvidia after Huang: contracts, credit and the cost of the crisis

Published: 2026-11-15T09:39:42+01:00

Original online edition

One billion dollars has been paid. Another billion is still expected. Nvidia’s investment in Naver was completed on November 6, before Jensen Huang’s departure. Its additional investment in Nscale is due to close around November 16, according to the cloud company’s prospectus. Between those transactions, a governance crisis has changed Nvidia’s leadership. Each commitment now deserves closer scrutiny: who benefits, which entity is bound and which conditions must be met? S05 S04

Huang made Nvidia a chip supplier, an industrial partner and a source of capital. Those roles remain closely connected as the company chooses his successor. Customers need money to buy servers. Lenders want predictable revenues and assets they can recover. The manufacturer can invest in a buyer or guarantee certain obligations to help a deal proceed. When confidence in corporate oversight weakens, the parties turn to the documents governing their rights.

This twelfth installment brings together the documented consequences of Nvidia’s crisis and develops the mechanisms examined in our investigation into AI credit and our report on the board’s inquiry. Some transactions were completed before the resignation. Others still need financing. Shareholder litigation opens another front, where the interests of Nvidia and its executives can diverge.

Musk raises supply concerns as Seoul stresses continuity

On November 14, Elon Musk said several supply arrangements had rested on a handshake with Huang rather than a written contract. He predicted that some critical deals would be lost. Reuters reported the statement, which identified neither a supplier nor a terminated transaction. Assessing the warning requires knowing the arrangements involved: prices, volumes, reserved capacity or a commercial preference. S03

A personal relationship can speed negotiations, secure a scarce allocation or persuade a partner to accept more risk. Once an agreement is signed, its clauses and the entities bound by it govern the obligations. The industrial question concerns future allocations as much as deliveries already contracted.

South Korea offers an initial comparison. Reuters reported on November 13 that minister Bae Kyung-hoon had obtained confirmation that Nvidia would honor its commitments under the 260,000-GPU program. In the government’s account, also reported by Korean media, the cooperation belongs to the company and will continue despite Huang’s departure. That public assurance provides a benchmark. Allocations, firm orders and accepted deliveries will show how the announced continuity works in practice. S07

Interim management must handle these relationships while the succession remains open. Nvidia’s filing records the board’s November 11 decision, announced on November 12: Colette Kress serves as interim CEO while remaining CFO, and Harvey Jones chairs the board. On November 13, the board told CNBC it was still selecting an executive search firm and had not formally engaged any candidate. The Financial Times report that Rene Haas had been approached describes a reported contact at this stage. S01 S02

Nvidia’s investment in Naver is complete

Korean disclosures take the evidence beyond an investment announcement. Naver’s amended filing on November 3 names NVIDIA Corporation as the subscriber for 6,558,435 shares, under a contractual investment of $1 billion. Its November 6 issuance report confirms that the shares were issued, records KRW 1,341,199,957,500 and confirms payment on that date. Read together, the filings establish completion of the investment. The amendment provides for a one-year mandatory holding period. S05

Payment preceded Huang’s resignation. Nvidia already holds the shares issued in that transaction, while the industrial development still has future milestones to meet. Naver’s October 27 announcement describes another part of the arrangement, negotiated with Brookfield. S06

Brookfield is to provide up to $9 billion to a project company provisionally called Asset Co. That company acquires GPUs and data centers, then supplies computing capacity to Naver Cloud in exchange for service payments. Naver undertakes to guarantee its subsidiary’s payment obligations for up to eight years. Equipment financing, Nvidia’s equity investment and Naver’s guarantee are distinct commitments within the wider arrangement. S06

The project company organizes the assets and payment flows. Its corporate form alone does not determine where the risk appears in the accounts. Naver says Asset Co. is expected to be consolidated in its financial statements under Korean accounting standards, K-IFRS, because of the control it exercises, even though Brookfield supplies all the equity funding. Assessing the exposure therefore requires reading the service contract, guarantee and control arrangements together. S06

Future compute revenues must provide a return on capital tied up in machines and buildings. If utilization takes longer to develop, the effect on Naver will depend in part on minimum payments and the conditions under which capacity becomes available. The announcement explains the structure. The full contracts are needed to assess those triggers.

Nscale’s funding needs extend beyond the next billion

Nscale’s amended prospectus of November 12 still schedules Nvidia’s additional $1 billion investment for around November 16. The financing structure includes Nvidia’s participation in an initial convertible funding tranche, followed by this additional transaction. Our review of filings and company announcements on the morning of November 15 found no confirmation that the second transaction had closed or been funded. S04

The expected investment sits within a broader funding requirement. Nscale says some GPU and networking equipment purchase commitments are firm and that it will need additional financing for projected 2027 obligations. The passage does not quantify that need. Our tenth installment also examines delivery conditions and the financing still needed for the Anthropic contracts. Receipt of another billion dollars would add resources, while each project would retain its own funding requirements and deadlines. S04

Nvidia also has an older commitment through a different instrument. The prospectus describes a guarantee relating to the Ward County lease in Texas, agreed on October 14, 2025. It is capped at $860.34 million for covered obligations during the lease’s first five years. The 2025 annual financial notes describe $470 million held in escrow as recourse for Nvidia. The balance available on November 15 is unknown in the documents reviewed. S04

The guarantee comes with rights over shares, including warrants granted independently of a default and others linked to a contractually defined default. It illustrates the rights a supplier can negotiate when helping a customer obtain financing. Nvidia’s exposure depends on the obligations covered, the trigger, available recourse and rights actually exercised. We identified no newly reported call on this guarantee. Subtracting the escrow from the guarantee’s ceiling would produce a misleading net loss figure: the dates and conditions governing both sums must first be reconciled. S04

Credit elsewhere in the ecosystem provides a test of spillovers

Nscale deserves close attention, but financing across the ecosystem takes several forms. In its November 10 results, CoreWeave recalled the closing of $2.6 billion in financing through DDTL 5.5, a delayed-draw term loan facility. The transaction had been announced on August 10. It can fund equipment supporting customer contracts shorter than those historically targeted by CoreWeave’s large facilities. Repayment schedules and customer renewals become important parameters. S08

CoreWeave also forecast on November 10 that it would receive approximately $9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits over the following three months. Expected advance payments can finance infrastructure before all the services have been delivered. Subsequent accounts will show the cash actually received and the services associated with it. S08

Bloomberg on November 11, followed by Semafor on November 13, described another loan of at least $4 billion, backed by a Meta contract. Semafor reported a favorable initial reception among lenders, citing a person familiar with the transaction. This was a financing being marketed to lenders. We found no confirmation of its closing. S16

These examples provide a practical way to assess consequences for Nvidia. A customer that obtains funding can take delivery. A lender seeking more protection may shift some exposure to a supplier, shareholder or insurer. The effects will emerge in signed terms, drawdowns and delivery schedules. Following those stages across several customers gives a more useful picture of AI credit than drawing a sector-wide verdict from a single transaction.

Shareholder lawsuits separate the company’s interests from its leaders’

Two civil actions filed on November 9 and 11 have already given the crisis a procedural form. On November 9, Mark Kistenmacher sued Nvidia and Huang over statements he alleges were misleading about Megaspeed, the relationship with Alice Huang and the consequences for the business. He seeks certification of an investor class and compensation. The complaint sets out allegations for the court to examine. S09

On November 11, Flor Angela Vanegas filed an action on Nvidia’s behalf against executives and directors, including Huang, Kress, Jones and Teter. This derivative action seeks recovery for the company and changes to its governance. Among the requested reforms is a right for shareholders to nominate at least four board candidates. Nvidia is named as a nominal defendant. The proposed changes remain requests for judicial relief. S10

The distinction has practical consequences. In the first case, investors are suing the company as well as Huang. In the second, a shareholder claims to defend the company’s interests against people alleged to have harmed it. Legal representation, defense costs and the beneficiaries of any eventual recovery can differ accordingly.

Directors’ and officers’ liability insurance, usually called D&O, affects that allocation. On November 14, The Insurer reported estimated Side A coverage of $800 million to $950 million for Nvidia’s executives and directors, citing anonymous market sources. Side A covers certain liabilities that the company cannot indemnify. Beazley declined to comment, while Inigo and Aon had not responded. The lawyer interviewed by the publication had not examined the policies. S11

The Financial Times separately reported possible exposure for Nvidia itself to litigation, given the scope of coverage described by its sources. The policy wording remains unavailable in our evidence. A limit reported in the press provides no basis for calculating an insurance recovery or a net cost. Nvidia’s proxy statement also describes indemnification agreements with its executives and directors, subject to conditions. Those agreements and the insurers’ responses will help establish how the burden falls among the parties. S11 S15

The Pentagon already buys compute through Nvidia’s ecosystem

Public demand provides another part of the picture. On September 9, Parallel Works and CoreWeave announced the deployment of a computing environment for DARPA’s NODES program. It uses a dedicated reservation on Nvidia HGX H100 systems, with storage and Quantum InfiniBand networking. Parallel Works manages access, allocations and support through its ACTIVATE platform. S12

NODES studies the dynamics of biological systems, proteins and the characterization of biological threats. In this arrangement, Nvidia hardware provides research infrastructure through service providers. The industrial roles are identifiable: a public program accesses computing capacity, while a cloud operator provides the underlying infrastructure. S13

The example gives substance to the Pentagon avenue discussed in our fifth installment. Reserved capacity is part of the announced arrangement. The full contracts and payment records are still needed to establish the public financial commitment, duration and exit conditions. The announcements describe no loan, guarantee or public equity investment in Nvidia. S12

An expansion of this model could secure demand for particular equipment or projects. Advances, guarantees or negotiated rights would each require their own instruments and legal authority. The MP Materials precedent illustrates the possibility of contractual rights in return for public support. The DARPA arrangement shows a channel of public demand already described within Nvidia’s ecosystem. Each supplies a different piece of evidence for examining a scenario of greater government influence.

Export controls could reshape access to the platform

The crisis also concerns the terms on which customers can use Nvidia’s chips. Investigations involving Nvidia, Megaspeed and Supermicro have separate scopes, explained in earlier installments. A decision affecting an intermediary, a license or remote access to compute could alter commercial routes regardless of the leadership transition.

A study published by Will DePue of Unemployment Labs on November 12 offers another way to investigate access. It examines the published parameters of Chinese models for traces of how their initial values were generated. The author reports configurations compatible with Blackwell for six models and publishes verification code. S14

The observation concerns the hardware used to create the initial parameters. Extending it to the full training run requires an inference, and the machines’ location remains unknown. A laboratory can use equipment abroad or move a model between systems. The author also discusses a particular AMD configuration. We read the report and code repository without independently reproducing the work. This lead calls for technical verification followed by comparison with licenses and responses from the companies involved. It adds another route for examining access to computing capacity. S14

In Washington, Howard Lutnick’s position and Trump’s decisions remain important to commercial predictability. Axios reported on November 14 that the commerce secretary faced mounting pressure while Trump continued to support him. The article includes a general Commerce Department rebuttal whose precise date has not been established. Our review on the morning of November 15 found no official decision that he would leave. Material developments will need to be traced through amended licenses, entity listings, published instructions or signed agreements. S17

The board needs a clear view of its commitments

Musk’s warning raises a real question about dependence on the founder. Naver’s filings establish an investment already completed. South Korean statements defend industrial continuity. Nscale discloses financing still to be arranged. Shareholder complaints challenge the board’s oversight and the company’s communications. The evidence points in several directions, helping define the decisions that matter next.

Nvidia’s next leadership will have to choose which projects to finance, which protections to offer and which risks to retain. The board will need enough information to challenge those choices. Findings from the internal inquiry, financing agreements and the first accepted equipment deliveries will offer more decisive evidence than the successor’s name alone.

Nvidia’s crisis reaches into the economics of AI: who advances the money, promises the revenues, guarantees payment and absorbs delays. The available documents describe obligations that run across companies and borders. Their execution will show how well that network holds together.

Limits

This analysis is current to the morning of November 15, 2026. Older sources retain their event dates. Finding a document during this review does not make it a new announcement. The review of filings and company statements is bounded and cannot cover nonpublic proceedings.

The complaints set out the parties’ allegations and requested remedies. D&O policies and several full contracts remain unavailable. The documents reviewed provide no verified litigation-cost figure, confirmation of a new guarantee call or evidence that Nscale’s additional Nvidia investment has been paid. Insurance coverage estimates remain attributed to the specialist press.

The technical study of Chinese models was not independently reproduced for this article. Its observations about initialization, inferences about training and the legal questions of export controls are assessed separately. No market price or valuation ratio is used in this analysis.
