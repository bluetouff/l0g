Nvidia: Trump backs Tim Cook as Lutnick faces pressure

Published: 2026-11-12T21:27:00+01:00 · Editorial revision: 2026-11-15T11:39:19+01:00

Original online edition

As Nvidia’s directors begin searching for a chief executive, Donald Trump is publicly promoting Tim Cook for the job. Howard Lutnick faces questions over export enforcement, while Justice Department inquiries reach across the chipmaker’s ecosystem. Washington has several ways to shape the company’s future, and each gives the administration a different kind of power.

On 12 November, Trump proposed that Cook replace Jensen Huang, praising both executives in a Truth Social post. Cook is Apple’s executive chair, having handed the CEO role to John Ternus in September. Reuters reported that Apple and Cook did not immediately respond to requests for comment. His willingness to move, any approach from Nvidia and the board’s interest remain unestablished. S05 S16

Meanwhile, Reuters reports that senior White House officials are discussing whether Commerce Secretary Lutnick should be pushed out, citing two people familiar with the situation. The same report carries a White House official’s assurance of Trump’s confidence in him. At publication, Lutnick’s departure remained unannounced amid those internal discussions and public expressions of support. S01

These events place Nvidia’s succession and Lutnick’s future inside the same political crisis. Nvidia’s directors must choose a chief executive, while the administration must explain its handling of a strategic technology and the warnings surrounding its customers. Our earlier investigations followed the warnings reaching the top, the export-control decisions, the board and the reported NDA and Singapore’s requests for evidence. The next decisions will show how these institutions use the authority they hold.

A presidential preference enters a board decision

Nvidia’s formal position remains clear. Its board accepted Huang’s resignation as President, CEO and director on 11 November, effective immediately, and announced the transition the following day. Colette Kress serves as interim CEO while retaining her CFO responsibilities. The board is searching among internal and external candidates. S04

Trump’s intervention gives Cook a distinct status in the succession debate: a name advanced by the president in public. It may attract attention from customers, directors or investors. Cook and the board would each have to agree to an appointment. Whether the message reflects private negotiations remains an open question.

The company’s bylaws put executive appointments and the filling of vacancies in the board’s hands. They also allow several offices to be held by one person, subject to law. Kress’s combined role therefore creates a practical oversight question: how will directors challenge financial commitments while the interim chief also supervises the accounts? Clear delegation can preserve continuity and make those decisions easier to scrutinise. S08

A prospective CEO must work with US export restrictions, government customers and industrial priorities. That gives the administration influence over the business and makes its preferences consequential for the search. Trump has now made one preference unmistakably public. Evidence of contacts with the board would help establish whether the intervention has gone further.

The Justice Department’s different fronts

Semafor reports three inquiries, citing two administration officials: an antitrust investigation focused on Nvidia, and inquiries concerning Megaspeed and Supermicro that examine Alice Huang’s involvement. It reports no indication of a current intention to charge Nvidia or Jensen Huang criminally. S02

Bloomberg separately reports scrutiny of whether entities linked to Alice Huang helped smuggle Supermicro products to China, citing one informed person. The connection with existing prosecutions remains unclear in the public excerpt. S03

Supermicro’s response now sits alongside Singapore’s account of the investigation. The company tells Semafor that Megaspeed has never been its customer and describes cooperation with US, Taiwanese and Singaporean authorities. Singapore’s Home Affairs Ministry describes limited information or assistance concerning Supermicro after requests to US authorities. The correspondence could clarify the difference through the records sought, the answers supplied and any explanation for the limits on assistance. Tracing the supply chain would also require purchase and delivery records. S02 S18

Some proceedings have a much longer public history. On 19 March, federal prosecutors announced charges against Yih-Shyan “Wally” Liaw, Ruei-Tsang “Steven” Chang and Ting-Wei “Willy” Sun over an alleged server-diversion scheme. The charges included conspiracies to violate the Export Control Reform Act, smuggle goods and defraud the United States. Supermicro itself was not a defendant in that indictment. Its subsequent filing disclosed a grand-jury subpoena, showing that compulsory requests for company records already preceded today’s headlines. S09 S10

There has also been a concrete change in one individual’s case. Reuters reported on 9 October that Sun had pleaded guilty the previous day to four counts covering export-control violations, smuggling, fraud against the United States and obstruction of justice. The plea concerns Sun and those offences. Establishing responsibility elsewhere in the supply chain requires evidence specific to the person or company concerned. S11

Nvidia’s competition exposure also predates Huang’s departure. Reuters reported on 28 October that officials were examining the proposed Hugging Face acquisition within a years-long antitrust investigation. Its account describes questions about acquisitions, investments, sales practices and pressure on customers, alongside scrutiny of transactions involving Groq and Run:ai. That chronology matters: a continuing competition investigation has its own origin and evidentiary record. S12

These cases may eventually connect through customers, transactions or shared intermediaries. Establishing those connections requires the corresponding records. The history already available shows why the latest headlines need to be read alongside earlier proceedings: some describe new scrutiny, while others follow allegations that prosecutors have been pursuing for months.

Investigators obtain evidence before courts decide remedies

In a civil antitrust investigation, the Justice Department can issue a civil investigative demand to obtain documents, written answers or testimony. The demand must identify the conduct being examined and the applicable law. It allows officials to collect evidence before deciding whether to bring a case. S13

A grand-jury subpoena obtains evidence within a criminal investigation. Rule 17 and the Justice Department’s manual describe its use and limits. A company may hold records about employees, customers or intermediaries whose conduct is being examined. Assessing the company’s own exposure requires evidence about its role in the conduct under investigation. S13

If a civil antitrust case follows, the government can ask a federal court to restrain unlawful conduct. A negotiated consent judgment also requires publication and judicial review. A remedy can change the way a business operates, but its terms must emerge from that legal process. The complaint, proposed agreement and court decisions would identify the obligations imposed. S14

Producing records, defending an acquisition and negotiating a remedy can put considerable pressure on management. That helps explain the interest in a possible bargain between Washington and Nvidia. To establish that officials were using an inquiry to obtain ownership, however, the investigation would need evidence of the exchange: a demand, negotiating instructions, a draft agreement or testimony describing its purpose.

At MP Materials, public money buys specific rights

The Pentagon’s July 2025 agreement with MP Materials offers a concrete example of public industrial intervention. The initial package set out $400 million in convertible preferred stock, a warrant, a $150 million loan commitment for heavy rare-earth separation at Mountain Pass, price protection and a ten-year magnet-purchase agreement for the planned 10X facility. The instruments address different needs and carry different rights. S06

The preferred stock gives the Pentagon an economic claim that can convert into common shares, while the warrant allows it to acquire further shares under agreed terms. The loan must be repaid. Alongside those financial claims, the purchase and price-protection agreements help support the business expected to supply the material. S06

The holding condition applies the contractual threshold to securities defined at closing. The clauses cover specified assets, control transactions and other defined restrictions. The loan commitment concerns Mountain Pass; offtake concerns 10X.

The financing also follows an industrial perimeter. Mountain Pass separation and the new magnet facility are different parts of the production chain. The contractual purpose of each commitment helps explain the capacity the government wants delivered and the risks taxpayers accept. Our examination of Intel and MP Materials follows the agreements in greater detail.

Applied to Nvidia, the precedent points towards several possible bargains. Washington could help finance a defined project in exchange for delivery commitments, or seek securities carrying rights across the company. A guarantee would put public money at risk if the borrower failed, while a capacity purchase would make the government a customer. Each arrangement would connect the public interest to a different part of the business.

Those differences determine the extent of influence. A supply agreement might let the government monitor delivery and recover payments if conditions fail. Shares could carry voting or other rights. A power to choose the CEO would require its own basis. Reading the agreements, including their exit provisions, would reveal far more about control than an ownership percentage alone.

A Pentagon project would need a defined purpose

Nvidia’s latest available quarterly report describes two operating segments: Compute & Networking and Graphics. Its broad commercial presentation identifies no standalone military division ready for a separate transaction. Any defence-related proposal would first need to define the assets, technologies, projects or supply commitments being financed. S07

This is an analytical possibility. A public financing proposal could concern secure deployment, resilient supply or a defined computing-capacity commitment. Each would require a description of the public need, the beneficiary and the performance expected in return. Protecting access to strategic computing is a plausible industrial objective. Demonstrating that an actual Nvidia transaction pursues it would require the corresponding documents.

Federal programmes also operate within statutory boundaries. The Industrial Base Fund under 10 USC §4817 addresses industrial capacity and supply-chain vulnerabilities, with expenditure subject to available appropriations. The Office of Strategic Capital under §149 supports eligible investments in critical technologies and assets with potential defence benefits. Programme authority, eligibility and funding all matter to a proposed commitment. S19

The terms would need to turn that national-security objective into something the recipient must deliver. Funding could be linked to construction milestones, available capacity or continued access to technology. The budget and legal authority would define how much the government could promise, while the agreement would determine how it could enforce delivery or recover its money.

The scenario becomes politically consequential because Nvidia’s leadership is unsettled while its access to important markets depends on government decisions. That creates scope for bargaining. Its content remains to be demonstrated: protecting continuity, obtaining industrial commitments and acquiring corporate rights are distinct objectives that could produce very different agreements.

Two successions under different rules

Nvidia’s directors can allow the Kress interim to continue while completing their search. An internal appointment could preserve technical relationships and organisational knowledge. Analysts interviewed by Reuters suggest Ian Buck on the strength of his product and computing background. The discussion remains at the level of observers’ recommendations, with a board decision still to be established. S04 S17

An external search would bring questions about availability, incentives and the distribution of authority. Semafor reports Apple hardware chief Johny Srouji being considered, citing one person familiar with the matter. Reuters records observers suggesting Arm CEO Rene Haas. Cook now has Trump’s public endorsement. The sourcing gives these names different standing, and any eventual appointment would require the board’s own process and the candidate’s agreement. S17 S05

Commerce has a different succession mechanism. Lutnick could remain in office while facing scrutiny and demands for a change in enforcement. If a vacancy occurred, the department’s succession order places the Deputy Secretary first, subject to the governing law and the president’s lawful alternatives. Paul M. Dabbar is the deputy identified by recent sources. A permanent Commerce secretary is appointed by the president with Senate confirmation. S01 S15

An acting official would keep the department functioning during a vacancy while the president pursued a permanent nominee through confirmation. At publication, the debate concerned Lutnick’s position and the policy he oversees. With his departure unannounced and a shortlist still undocumented, continuity in office and an interim transition remained scenarios to consider.

At both institutions, the immediate test concerns decisions that remain to be made. Nvidia must distribute authority over technology, customers and financial commitments. Commerce must account for export enforcement and cooperation with other jurisdictions. A personnel change can alter the people responsible while leaving the need for records and explanations intact.

Nvidia’s financial reach brings Congress into the argument

According to Reuters’ public preview, Senator Jack Reed wants Congress to examine whether Nvidia is too important to fail and the implications for the banking system. His proposal is a request for assessment. A decision to grant the company systemic status or public support would be a separate step. S20

An operator preparing a new AI site has to spend before it can sell computing services. It needs a building and power, then equipment that must arrive, be installed and work together. A lender assessing the project has to connect that construction schedule with customer demand and the money expected to come in. Turning an order for chips into a functioning service requires financing that can support the whole process.

Consider a project supported by a large customer’s purchase commitment or a guarantee. The lender would examine when equipment can be delivered, how firm the demand is and precisely when the guarantor must pay. A cancellation right or an unmet delivery condition could change the cash available to service the loan. These are different forms of support, with different failure points. Our previous instalment examines Nvidia’s CoreWeave capacity agreement and SB Energy guarantees, including the conditions attached to those obligations. S21

Existing agreements remain governed by their terms as leadership changes. The next management team inherits responsibility for supervising them, while decisions about new commitments offer more room to change course. If Nvidia became more cautious about supporting future projects, some operators might need more equity from their owners or longer to raise financing. That is a possible transmission mechanism through the industry, whose scale would depend on the projects affected and the alternatives available.

A public customer could make part of a project’s expected revenue easier for lenders to assess. Its contract would still have to be read for delivery requirements, termination rights and any dependence on future budget appropriations. Those provisions help determine how much financing the commitment can support. The same reasoning applies whether the promise comes from a major technology company or the government: the wording decides when money must change hands. Our AI financing atlas follows these links between industrial plans and the balance sheets backing them.

Washington now faces a test of its own decisions as well as Nvidia’s governance. A public investment proposal would make the intended bargain visible through the capacity sought, the money committed and the rights received. Congress could then examine the benefit to taxpayers and the influence acquired. Any evidence that officials had traded an investigation or an export decision for those rights would warrant scrutiny of that conduct too.

Trump has made his preferred name public. Nvidia’s directors retain the formal task of choosing a permanent chief executive. The inquiries, export decisions and any future financing agreement will show whether political preference develops into a more durable form of influence. Its reach will be measured through the rights obtained and the obligations accepted.
