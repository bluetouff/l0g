Chapter 3

Nvidia’s board faces the Huang problem

Published: 2026-11-12T10:45:00+01:00 · Editorial revision: 2026-11-15T11:39:19+01:00

Original online edition

The crisis has reached inside Nvidia. Directors are investigating the founder, a shareholder is challenging the company’s statements in court, and leadership uncertainty surrounds a man central to the business of AI. Washington also faces a corporate counterpart whose bargaining position may be changing.

In the New York Times account published on 11 November, a bystander films Huang with an Nvidia executive at a hotel. Security workers then ask the person to sign a confidentiality agreement. The paper says it has not seen the video. Its account therefore describes a request whose document, signature and terms remain unknown. S01

That reported request gives investigators a concrete question. Who sought to protect which information, acting under whose authority and using whose resources? A public company can become involved through the means deployed around its chief executive, even when an episode begins in his personal life. Documents, the people informed and the procedures followed can establish the answers.

Our earlier investigations examined warnings at the top and interventions around chip export enforcement. This instalment examines Nvidia’s ability to exercise oversight over a founder whose success has given him exceptional authority.

Since 8 November, directors have been investigating a possible foreign intelligence effort targeting Huang. The commercial strand concerns Megaspeed, a Singapore-based customer suspected of facilitating Chinese access to chips subject to US restrictions. The alleged Powell relationship adds a workplace governance question to that security case. S07 S19

The phone, the security workers and the NDA

The executive named in the new report is Kimberly Powell, Nvidia’s healthcare vice president, who reports to Huang. The Times cites five people who worked at the company. Reuters carried the allegation and said Nvidia had not immediately answered its request for comment. Powell’s official biography separately confirms her role. S02 S21

A non-disclosure agreement, or NDA, sets confidentiality obligations for specified information between parties. Establishing the terms of the hotel episode would require identifying those parties, the information covered and the commitments proposed. The document would also show the rights retained by the bystander and the consequences attached to disclosure. S22

There is already a public reason to examine the resources involved. Nvidia’s 2026 proxy describes a security programme for Huang and his family, approved by the board, with its nature and cost reviewed annually by the compensation committee. The reporting period ended in January, before the alleged March incident. Connecting that programme to the hotel would require identifying who employed the security workers and authorised their reported intervention. Both links are missing from the records reviewed. S06

An NDA can also raise questions about reporting channels. US Rule 21F-17 protects direct communications to the Securities and Exchange Commission about a possible securities law violation. A confidentiality provision that impeded such communications could fall within the rule, including outside an employment relationship. Applying it to the hotel account would require the agreement and the relevant circumstances. S11

The KBR case illustrates the boundary. In 2015, the SEC took action over clauses used in internal investigations: witnesses were required to obtain legal department approval before speaking outside the company, under threat of discipline. The restriction on reporting possible securities violations was the issue. Nvidia’s investigators therefore have reason to examine both the confidentiality sought and the reporting channels preserved. S23

The founder is covered by the code

Nvidia’s code applies to every employee and board member. It lists personal relationships among potential conflicts and calls for withdrawal from the relevant decisions when a conflict arises. A waiver requires written Compliance Committee approval. Disclosure for directors and executive officers follows applicable rules. S03

The available evidence establishes no career advantage for Powell. A reporting relationship can nevertheless affect compensation, promotions, budgets and performance reviews. The code therefore points investigators towards decisions involving Huang and Powell, any disclosures made and the safeguards adopted.

Confidential interviews can protect witnesses. Their credibility depends on investigators being able to obtain information about the chief executive, including material he might prefer to keep from directors. The code provides internal and anonymous reporting channels and assigns the handling of concerns to the compliance committee and its delegates. S03

On 10 November, independent directors appointed Joon H. Kim and Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct their investigation. Directors have yet to clarify publicly how Powell and the reported NDA episode fit within that remit. The inquiry’s independence will also be judged through its work, from access to documents, staff and service providers to the action taken on its findings. S08

Directors have the power to remove the CEO

Huang founded Nvidia and has personified it for decades. The bylaws nevertheless give directors an explicit power: Section 32 allows a majority of serving directors to remove an officer, with or without cause. Removal from an executive position and removal from the board follow separate provisions. S04

The harder task is exercising that power. Removing a CEO means arranging continuity, distributing responsibility and maintaining the confidence of employees and business partners. A board’s ability to do that work is central to its purpose in a company dominated by its founder.

Nvidia’s governance policies provide for independent directors to meet without management and retain their own external advisers. The proxy describes the Lead Director’s role in facilitating independent oversight. These arrangements give directors ways to inform themselves beyond the CEO’s account. S05 S06

On 8 November, the board expressed confidence in Huang while announcing an investigation. Three days later, Reuters reported a meeting about his future. Bloomberg described a search for candidates with an outside firm and the possibility of an interim appointment. Both accounts rely on unnamed sources. At the time of verification, the official announcements reviewed concerned the investigation and its legal counsel. The outcome of any leadership vote remained unknown. S07 S19 S20

The lawsuit puts public assurances before a judge

Mark Kistenmacher filed a complaint against Nvidia and Huang on 9 November, seeking a class action for purchasers of securities between 9 October 2025 and 8 November 2026. It challenges, among other statements, descriptions of dealings with Megaspeed and Alice Huang as ordinary. The plaintiff invokes Sections 10(b) and 20(a) and Rule 10b-5 of US securities law. S09 S10

The timing matters. The filing predates the Powell revelation and concerns information supplied to investors about Megaspeed. The existing complaint has its own challenged statements and proposed class period. Governance questions raised by the new allegation would follow their own course.

The complaint brings the company’s public assurances back to an investment decision. The plaintiff alleges that important information about a sensitive commercial relationship was withheld and that purchases relied on a misleading presentation. The court must examine those allegations through the specific statements, the defendants’ knowledge and the connection to the alleged loss. S09

Shareholders also differ over Huang’s future. Armina Rosenberg, a portfolio manager at Minotaur Capital, which Reuters identifies as an Nvidia shareholder, stresses the cost of losing him. Her opinion is that of a manager at a fund holding Nvidia shares. A coordinated campaign by the largest investors would require further evidence beyond litigation and individual views. S19

AI finance depends on the next decisions

The governance uncertainty surrounds a company whose commercial relationships extend beyond selling processors. At CoreWeave, Nvidia is a supplier, shareholder and buyer of computing capacity. Each role creates a different relationship with the same operator. S14 S24

The capacity agreement announced in September 2025 had an initial value of $6.3 billion. Subject to delivery, availability and termination conditions, Nvidia agreed to buy residual unsold cloud capacity through 13 April 2032. Such a customer can improve revenue visibility for the operator and its lenders. Assessing that support today requires knowing how much of the initial commitment remains available. S14

The arrangement illustrates concentration risk. A supplier helps a customer expand its equipment, holds equity and may buy its service. Decisions at the supplier can then affect several parts of the financing. The resulting support for investment depends on contractual obligations, capacity delivery and ultimate demand.

CoreWeave’s results published on 10 November provide a sense of scale: $132.5 billion of revenue backlog at 30 September. Its definition adds other estimated contractual revenue, subject to delivery and availability conditions, to remaining performance obligations, or RPO. The scope is broader than accounting RPO. It measures anticipated future business, with conditions separating that expectation from realised revenue. S13

For lenders, the questions are practical. Will equipment be available on time, will customers take the capacity, and will payments service the debt? A leadership crisis at Nvidia can add uncertainty to future decisions. Delayed financing, tighter terms, withdrawals or defaults would allow that uncertainty’s transmission to credit to be traced. Research published by the BIS provides a framework by describing the links between those commitments and their providers of finance. S16

Corporate obligations extend beyond the CEO

The Nvidia–CoreWeave master agreement binds the companies and their successors. Its provisions govern termination and give order forms a particular role. Some specific conditions remain unavailable in the redacted public versions. An executive’s departure and the ending of a commercial obligation therefore involve separate decisions. S25

The SB Energy arrangements show another dimension. In August, Nvidia signed guarantees with an initial cumulative cap of $105 billion for a campus intended for an OpenAI affiliate. They respond to defined default circumstances and account for opportunities to recover value through reletting or sale. The cap describes a conditional exposure. S12

The public guarantee form binds the parties and their successors. It provides for choices following a default and written amendments. Several termination provisions are redacted. Directors must therefore oversee long commitments while retaining control over new ones. Subsequent decisions will allow a management scenario to be tested: a succession could alter appetite for additional risks before changing performance of existing contracts. S26

This oversight requires an organisation capable of challenging commitments proposed by the CEO. As Nvidia participates in financing demand for its own products, directors need to assess the commercial return, counterparty concentration and guarantee triggers together. Our investigation into insurance for AI loans follows related promises across multiple balance sheets.

Washington may seek greater influence

The crisis raises a further political question. Could a weakened leadership team give the federal government an opportunity to negotiate additional commitments, potentially including an equity stake? Completed transactions and publicly discussed options provide a way to examine that scenario.

Intel offers a concrete mechanism. In August 2025, the company announced an $8.9 billion federal investment for a planned 9.9% stake, using previously scheduled public funding. The announced holding was passive, without a board seat or additional information rights. Voting would follow board recommendations, subject to exceptions. The transaction establishes a financial holding with explicit limits on the exercise of shareholder power. S27

On 5 June, Trump said his team would examine public stakes in AI companies. On 17 June, Semafor reported two structures still under discussion: shares for the savings vehicles known as Trump Accounts, favoured by Bessent, and a sovereign wealth fund, favoured by Lutnick. The beneficiary and investment vehicle matter alongside the decision to obtain shares. S28

The February 2025 order preparing a sovereign wealth fund called for analysis of funding and legal arrangements, including potential legislative requirements, subject to available appropriations. An Nvidia transaction would require identifying the authority, funds, securities and attached rights. An export decision and an investment agreement follow different routes. S32

Our hypothesis concerns a shift in bargaining power. Investigations and leadership uncertainty could make Nvidia more receptive to demands concerning its markets, governance or investments. A negotiated federal stake would be another possible route, requiring its own financing and terms. At this stage, a federal investment project tied to the crisis or a deliberate strategy to drive down Nvidia would be speculation beyond the sources reviewed.

US decision-making also contains internal disputes. CNN reports, citing two sources, disagreement between Bessent and Lutnick over placing Megaspeed on the Entity List, restricting the customer’s access to US technology. The Commerce Department disputes the confrontation. These accounts concern export enforcement. They call for tracing each decision to its responsible officials and stated reasons. S29 S30

Trump’s public support for Huang also belongs in the analysis. On 9 November, he said he believed Huang’s account that he had not known about the alleged spying. In June, Huang had told AP that he had held no dialogue about a public stake. His account describes the situation at that date. Prolonged instability at the supplier would also weigh on the US ambition to expand AI. The administration has substantial negotiating tools alongside an interest in industrial continuity. S31 S33 S17

Restoring control over decisions

The next financial appointment is 17 November. Nvidia will publish quarterly results and take questions from analysts and institutional investors on its conference call. The quality of answers about governance, leadership continuity and financial commitments will matter alongside any new organisational announcement. S18

The board already has rules, powers and external advisers. Its task is to establish the facts, apply the procedures and maintain decision-making continuity while exercising oversight that can function independently of one man.

Washington faces a corresponding test: make security decisions traceable and explain the terms of any economic intervention. If the crisis opens a new negotiation, records should allow scrutiny of the public interest, industrial choices and benefits granted to the parties. Nvidia’s power makes that oversight more consequential as its commitments reach other companies and balance sheets.
