Chapter 10

Nvidia: AI guarantees put the whole credit chain at stake

Published: 2026-11-14T08:35:00+01:00 · Editorial revision: 2026-11-15T11:39:19+01:00

Original online edition

At Loughton in the UK, a withdrawn electricity connection schedule brought down a $3.22 billion compute contract. Nscale explains in its November 12 prospectus that the local power provider withdrew the planned connection date and power allocation. A Microsoft statement of work was subsequently terminated. Nscale says it is exploring alternative power sources and that its other contracts remain intact. The $3.22 billion represented future services. S02

The episode makes the financing risk described in the Federal Reserve’s November 13 Financial Stability Report tangible. Data centers consume capital before they earn their first compute revenue. Developers borrow, negotiate advances and seek help from suppliers to bridge that interval. The financing then depends on equipment delivery, electricity and customers able to pay. S01

This tenth installment follows those obligations through the credit chain. It builds on our reporting on Nvidia’s guarantees and Chinese credit and US export controls. Jensen Huang’s successor will inherit a difficult question: how much risk should the GPU supplier take to finance demand for its own products?

Debt issuance accelerates with the data center buildout

The Fed identifies more than $680 billion of advanced computing-related debt issuance in 2026 through October, compared with $130 billion in the whole of 2023. These flows cover a broader group of borrowers and financing structures, dominated by investment-grade issuers. The estimate includes Rule 144A private placements and excludes Section 4(a)(2) transactions. Private credit makes a comprehensive measure harder to obtain. S01

Borrowing reflects the industrial model behind the expansion. A cloud operator purchases servers to supply an AI lab. Its lender examines the customer contract, the customer’s creditworthiness and the value of equipment it could recover. A supplier’s promise to protect a minimum equipment value can make the loan easier to arrange. Chip sales and the customer’s ability to finance those purchases become closely connected. S01

Other obligations stretch across future years. As of the second quarter of 2026, for seven companies including Nvidia, the Fed identifies more than $1 trillion of leases yet to commence and more than $2 trillion of guarantees and other contractual commitments, measured on an undiscounted basis. These categories have different terms and time horizons from balance-sheet debt. Adding them together would misrepresent immediate cash requirements. S01

A guarantee can bring risk back to the supplier

A residual value guarantee protects a defined equipment value at an agreed date or under specified circumstances. If the value recovered falls below the covered level, the guarantor may have to pay part of the shortfall. The beneficiary, cap and payment conditions determine the protection. Our residual value guarantee analysis explains the mechanism.

For a lender, this can provide credit enhancement: another party absorbs a defined share of risk. For the supplier, it supports orders while creating exposure to the future value of its equipment and the finances of its customers. The Fed describes these relationships between GPU designers, major compute buyers and special purpose entities, the structures that hold particular assets and their financing. S01

There is a stabilizing side to the arrangement. A large company with substantial resources can absorb a loss that might otherwise damage an operator or its lender. The same structure can concentrate trouble if customer revenue disappoints while server values fall, bringing several protections into play together. The Fed highlights both effects and the possibility that large firms’ economic exposure exceeds their recorded debt. S01

Nscale commits to equipment while still arranging funding

Nscale’s prospectus shows the tension at contract level. In September, the company estimated that cash, available borrowings and customer prepayments would cover at least twelve months after its financial statements were issued. The corresponding section of its November 12 filing changes the account: new GPU, networking and equipment orders have increased near-term commitments, some of which it cannot contractually defer, reduce or cancel. S03 S02

Nscale now says it must obtain additional financing to meet its projected 2027 obligations. It leaves the funding requirement unquantified. The company explains that orders help secure pricing and allocations while limiting the risk of penalties under its Anthropic agreements. It is committing to equipment to preserve the ability to serve a customer while still raising the money required. S02

At the prospectus date, Nscale says it has no binding commitments for the financing needed to fulfill its Anthropic agreements. The contracts organize delivery in tranches, with services starting after acceptance. They give the customer termination rights if certain deadlines are missed. Exceptions for industry-wide shortages depend in part on binding equipment orders placed in time. S02

That creates a difficult operating decision. Waiting for financing can cost an equipment allocation or a customer deadline. Ordering promptly creates obligations that must be funded even if commissioning slips. Loughton illustrates another dependency: a grid connection problem can remove expected contract revenue before a site begins delivering compute. S02

Nvidia’s billion-dollar commitment still has a closing date

Nvidia’s proposed investment is part of this capital-raising effort. Nscale’s September 25 announcement describes $3.36 billion in convertible financing: an initial $2.36 billion tranche at closing and a further $1 billion Nvidia commitment, expected in mid-November. The securities convert at the IPO, into non-voting shares in Nvidia’s case. S04

A closing expected around November 16 already appeared in the September prospectus and remains in the November 12 version. The documents reviewed on November 14 therefore describe an anticipated transaction. Confirmation of that closing and financing for the 2027 obligations are separate milestones to follow. S03 S02

Investing in a GPU buyer can support a customer’s expansion. Nvidia’s board must also weigh concentration: sales, investments and guarantees can all become sensitive to the success of the same projects. That governance question connects Nscale’s financing to the succession process.

The next CEO will decide how far future support should go

Colette Kress is interim CEO and remains CFO under Nvidia’s November 12 announcement. On November 13, the board told CNBC that it was still selecting a search firm and had not formally engaged any candidates. The Financial Times separately reported an approach to Rene Haas, discussed in our ninth installment. S05 S06

An internal successor could continue the expansion while tightening the terms of new financial support. An outside executive could seek a broader review of project selection, caps and expected returns. The terms already signed will frame the successor’s room for manoeuvre.

The practical decisions concern the next deals. A supplier can prioritize buyers able to raise their own financing, offer capped protection or commit more of its balance sheet to accelerate sales. Each choice allocates risk differently among the manufacturer, operator, investors and lenders.

Washington has an industrial stake in the financing dependencies

Those dependencies give substance to the scenario of an administration seeking greater influence over Nvidia. Trump could seek leverage over industrial decisions or the terms of possible public support if strategic projects came under pressure. A decision, financing arrangement and negotiated rights would turn that scenario into a concrete intervention, as explained in our analysis of state leverage.

The Fed report maps financial vulnerabilities that could make a shock more costly. Its information generally stops at October 28, before Huang’s departure. The obligations therefore have a history that predates November’s governance crisis. S01

The next stage of the investigation will follow commitments as they become effective: financing signed, a tranche delivered, a contract amended or a guarantee called. Promised billions acquire a different meaning once the payer, payment date and funding source are visible.
