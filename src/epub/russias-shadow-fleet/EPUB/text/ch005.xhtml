Russia’s shadow fleet: who pays when a tanker runs aground?

Published 2026-11-20T00:26:00+01:00

Original online investigation

On 17 November 2026, The Maritime Executive reported that unloading had begun on the Caroline Bezengi, aground off Oman since June. As crews worked to recover the cargo, a document published on 6 November had already narrowed the compensation options. The Director of the International Oil Pollution Compensation Funds considers that the damage falls within the exclusion for war or hostilities. Several delegations supported that view, subject to new facts emerging. S04 · S05 · S07

After tracing ownership of the tanker Arrow and Lidoil’s bond financing, this instalment examines a different obligation. Who pays after a casualty? A separate case, involving insurer Maritime Mutual, shows how older policies are handled after an insurer stops writing cover. S03 · S17 · S19

Salvage off Oman

The Caroline Bezengi, IMO number 9224439, appears in Britain’s notice of 24 February 2026. A September paper from the Funds places the first report of damage on 8 June, after an explosion thought to have been caused by limpet mines. The tanker later ran aground, and a leak was observed in late June. Accounts differ on the exact day of the grounding. S04 · S16

Oman announced a response on 7 August. On 13 August, Ambrey confirmed it had been engaged to salvage the tanker. On 23 August, Oman’s transport ministry described the ship stranded on rocks near Al Qibliyah and listing by about 17 degrees. The condition of the deck, the lack of power and the sea conditions made the job harder. The November report of unloading marks another stage in the operation. S01 · S02 · S03 · S07

On 13 August, Lloyd’s List reported that AlfaStrakhovanie was understood to be funding the response and that sanctions waivers were needed before salvors could engage. The public summary attributes a financing role to the Russian insurer. The policy, the terms of the waivers and the actual payments remain undocumented in the public material we examined. S06

From certificate to claim

Hull insurance, cargo insurance and cover for liability to third parties address different risks. The last is commonly called P&I, or protection and indemnity. For pollution damage within its scope, the 1992 Civil Liability Convention, or CLC, imposes strict liability on the registered owner, subject to the convention’s exceptions. A ship carrying more than 2,000 tonnes of oil in bulk as cargo must have insurance or other financial security. That cargo threshold is distinct from the gross tonnage used to calculate the liability limit. S08 · S09 · S10

The insurer typically issues a blue card confirming cover. The competent state reviews the security and issues or certifies the CLC certificate, which the ship must carry. The International Maritime Organization recommends examining the guarantor’s financial strength and reinsurance. Denmark specifies the evidence it requires from an insurer outside the International Group of P&I Clubs, including authorisation and reinsurance records and an external auditor’s statement. The Funds’ guidance calls for a prompt review of the insurance and the insurer’s finances after an incident. S08 · S12 · S13 · S14

The convention lets victims bring a claim directly against the insurer or guarantor. It limits the defences available. The owner’s bankruptcy alone, for example, is unavailable as an insurer’s defence. Payment still depends on recognition of the claim and the guarantor’s ability to meet it. S08

How the owner and the Funds share costs

The CLC scale starts at 4.51 million special drawing rights (SDR) up to 5,000 gross tonnage units. It rises by 631 SDR per additional unit to a maximum of 89.77 million SDR. The 1992 Fund can cover eligible damage when CLC protection is inadequate, including when the owner and guarantor cannot meet the claims financially. Its 203 million SDR event limit includes amounts paid under the CLC. S08 · S09 · S25

Oman is a member of the 1992 Fund. France also belongs to the Supplementary Fund, with a total ceiling of 750 million SDR, inclusive of the preceding tiers. These limits apply to eligible damage within the relevant territory. The Funds draw most contributions from receivers whose qualifying oil receipts exceed the convention’s threshold of 150,000 tonnes in a calendar year. Pooling costs this way can shift part of a spill’s burden to companies receiving oil elsewhere. S11 · S23 · S25

The cause of the damage shapes access to the Fund

Article 4 of the Fund Convention excludes damage when the Fund proves that it resulted from an act of war, hostilities, civil war or insurrection. The CLC provides a corresponding defence for the owner and insurer. In the Oman case, the Director links the grounding and spill to the damage attributed to the mines. Ghana asked for careful consideration of the nature of the attack and its link to hostilities. The view recorded by the Funds concerns the 1992 Fund’s own obligations on the evidence available at the time. S04 · S05 · S08

The International Group told the Funds that war risks are normally covered separately from ordinary P&I. Identifying anyone liable to pay under war cover would require the applicable policy for the Caroline Bezengi, if any, including its dates and exclusions. That question remains open. S05

Maritime Mutual’s older contracts

Britain designated its New Zealand and Gibraltar entities on 24 February 2026. On 3 March, Belize’s maritime administration removed the New Zealand insurer from its approved list and required replacement cover for affected vessels. On 5 June, New Zealand’s central bank said the insurer had reported a wind-down and termination of its cover. It revoked an exemption concerning use of the word insurance in the company’s name. The central bank says the company operated as an unlicensed insurer in New Zealand and served customers outside the country. S15 · S16 · S17 · S18

On 7 October 2026, the UK’s Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation extended and expanded a licence allowing certain payments connected to written contracts predating the designations. The conditions depend on the counterparty and purpose of the payment. The licence is scheduled to expire on 6 October 2027, subject to amendment or revocation. Designated entities relying on it must report their activities within 14 days after each month ends. Those reports could connect the permissions in the licence to payments actually made under older contracts. S19 · S20
