The number on the hull

In a 2010 bond prospectus, Sovcomflot lists a tanker called NS Asia. In a classification record consulted in November 2026, the same ship appears as Arrow, with Citrine Marine named in the owner field. It went by Alliance in between. A seven-digit number identifies the hull across all three names. Finding Citrine’s main shareholder takes a different document. The EU Council names a company incorporated in Hong Kong in its reasons for imposing sanctions. Ultimate control remains an open question.

The ships are the most visible part of Russia’s shadow fleet. This book follows the commitments that keep them in business and the companies that make those commitments. One company can own a tanker while another runs it. A lender advances money and expects the borrower to repay it. If a spill occurs, the shipowner, an insurer and an international fund can each be liable for certain losses, depending on the cause of the damage and the rules that apply.

After Arrow, we leave the shipping records and turn to filings from the Moscow Exchange. Lid Capital’s accounts and its investment memorandum trace the proceeds of a bond issue to a loan paid to Lidoil in Dubai. They take us as far as that loan. Following the cash into a particular tanker would take records beyond the published accounts.

The A7 case starts later in the life of a sale. A company that sells oil abroad still needs money where its own bills fall due. Britain says the payment network handles proceeds from Russian oil. Separately, the US Treasury’s FinCEN counts movements of A7A5 tokens. The same tokens can change hands many times, and tying a transfer to an invoice takes commercial records that identify the payment.

Off Oman, the grounding of the Caroline Bezengi makes the cost of these obligations tangible. Salvors went to work while the Director of the International Oil Pollution Compensation Funds took the view that the war or hostilities exclusion applied to the 1992 Fund, subject to new facts. Between work on the stranded tanker and settlement of a claim lie two questions: which guarantee applies, and who has the means to pay? The answers matter to the people and businesses dealing with the damage.
