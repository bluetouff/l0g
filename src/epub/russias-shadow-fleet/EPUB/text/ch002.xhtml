Russia’s shadow fleet: tracing ownership of the tanker Arrow

Published 2026-11-19T21:15:26+01:00

Original online investigation

Sovcomflot’s bond prospectus called it NS Asia. Sixteen years later, the Indian Register of Shipping (IRCLASS) lists it as Arrow, with Citrine Marine SPC in the owner field. It is the same tanker, identifiable by IMO 9413561. On 16 November 2026, the Council of the European Union named Citrine’s main shareholder: Prospect International Shipping Limited, a company incorporated in Hong Kong. The size of its stake remains undisclosed. S01 · S03 · S04 · S08

The regulation added 12 people and 101 entities in all to the EU’s Russia sanctions list. The first instalment of our investigation into Russia’s shadow fleet traces the tanker to its listed owner, then to the shareholder named by the Council. The documents at each step speak to different rights. S08

Three names for one tanker

An IMO number stays with a ship for life. Its name, flag and owner can all change while that identifier stays the same. In the IRCLASS record accessed on 19 November, it ties Arrow to its former names, Alliance and NS Asia. The record lists the Omani flag and a contact for Citrine in Duqm. It is a classification record. Dating a change of ownership would require a bill of sale or a historical record from the flag registry. S01 · S02

A bond prospectus issued by SCF Capital on 22 October 2010, with Sovcomflot as guarantor, lists NS Asia among the vessels the group wholly owned on 30 June 2010, under the Liberian flag. The fleet table gives its name and year of construction. The match rests on the IRCLASS record and the UK designation of 11 September 2024, which directly links the name NS Asia to IMO 9413561. S03 · S01 · S04

The UK listed Gabon as the presumed flag and put Stream Ship Management FCZO in a combined owner/operator field, leaving its precise role open. The UK cited carriage of Russian oil or petroleum products from Russia to a third country as its reason for designating the ship. By 15 December 2025 the EU was calling the tanker Arrow. The Council included it among vessels managed by Nova Shipmanagement LLC-FZ and previously managed by Sovcomflot. The same decision listed Citrine, describing it as an owner of vessels managed by Nova. The timing of any change of ownership remains open. S04 · S05

Owning the hull and controlling the company

When one company sells a tanker to another, title to the vessel changes hands. When an investor buys a controlling stake in the shipowning company, that company can keep the hull. The transaction then leaves a trace in company records. The diagram shows how each kind of transfer works. The underlying agreements for Arrow are still missing. S12 · S15 · S16

Operations introduce another relationship. The ship manager handles the vessel’s day-to-day operations. A separate ISM company, named after the International Safety Management Code, can take responsibility for safety management. Equasis distinguishes those functions from registered ownership. BIMCO’s standard SHIPMAN contract can cover crewing, technical and commercial management, and insurance arrangements. A flag places the vessel under a state’s registration regime, which can admit ships owned or controlled abroad. S11 · S12 · S13

The ownership trail reaches Hong Kong

Hong Kong’s Companies Registry records the incorporation of Prospect International Shipping Limited on 6 March 2024, under number 76276778. The details match the EU’s listing of 16 November 2026. Hong Kong’s record confirms when Prospect was formed. The Council describes Prospect’s stake in Citrine. Citrine’s shareholder register and the date when Prospect took its stake remain outstanding. S08 · S09

IRCLASS also lists Citrine as the owner of Amulet (IMO 9413547, formerly Altair and NS Arctic) and Bobcat (IMO 9422457, formerly Blossom and SCF Baikal). Both records list the Omani flag and the same contact in Duqm. The Council grouped the two ships with Arrow in its December 2025 entry for Nova. S05 · S06 · S07

The November regulation describes two other corporate ties. It names Seychelles-based Cariba Carriers as the sole shareholder of White Agate Marine, and Nitara Carriers, also based in Seychelles, as the sole shareholder of Serpentine Marine. In the case of Prospect and Citrine, the Council uses “main shareholder”. A sole shareholder owns the entire share capital. The leading shareholder’s percentage remains undisclosed. S08

The management links

All three IRCLASS records for Citrine’s tankers list a contact address on the techshipman.com domain. On 30 July 2026, risk intelligence provider Kharon linked that domain to operators’ contact details and recruitment adverts attributed to Sovcomflot. It also reported its use by Citrine, White Agate and Serpentine. Our checks cover Citrine’s three vessel records. S01 · S06 · S07 · S10

The Council also names Igor Konfino and Daniil Mosin as registered managers of the three companies and describes management links with Sovcomflot. Teams, contractors and administrative services can remain in place after a sale. To determine how much influence those links confer, investigators would need the management agreements, including who gives instructions and who gets paid for the work. S08 · S10 · S11

The contracts that could trace the money

A ship’s Continuous Synopsis Record records changes to its name, flag, owner and company responsible for safety. Britain’s maritime authority says it must remain aboard when a ship is sold or changes flag. Arrow’s record could help date the changes we see. In Hong Kong, annual returns and company reports can identify declared shareholders and directors. The separate Significant Controllers Register is kept by the company itself. S14 · S15 · S16

Sale documents would identify the parties and agreed price. Payment records would show who provided the money. Charter agreements and invoices would show who pays for transport and who collects the freight. Tracing the proceeds from the oil sale would also require following the trade and its cargo financing. For Arrow, the public trail reaches Citrine and the shareholder identified by the Council. The next question is who gives the orders and where the payments go.

The second instalment follows a separate case, tracing credit raised in Moscow for a Dubai company.

Scope of the investigation

This investigation uses public documents and covers three tankers, with Arrow as the main case. The IRCLASS records reflect the information displayed on the access date. We attribute the grounds for sanctions to the issuing authorities. The documents we gathered contain no ship title, sale agreement, evidence of payment or register establishing ultimate control. The tankers’ current managers remain unverified. We conducted no interviews or exchanges with the companies for this article. The cover image is an AI-generated illustration.
