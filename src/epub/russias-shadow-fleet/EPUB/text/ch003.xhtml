Russia’s shadow fleet: Moscow bonds and a Dubai borrower

Published 2026-11-19T22:03:24+01:00

Original online investigation

The Moscow Exchange published the result on 30 December 2025. Lid Capital had sold, between 3 and 29 December, 66,515 bonds with a face value of 100 dollars each and received 515.4 million roubles in payment. The issuer’s audited accounts show a loan with the same face amount, 6,651,500 dollars, disbursed in roubles to Lidoil, a Dubai company. Lid Capital’s investment memorandum links the two transactions. S02 · S03 · S04

On 16 November 2026, the European Union sanctioned both companies. The Council links Lidoil to the Russian oil trade and describes Lid Capital as its route to the Russian capital market. The first instalment traced ownership of the tanker Arrow. This separate trail starts with investors on the Moscow Exchange and reaches a loan recorded in the lender’s books. Where the money went after the loan remains open. S01 · S02 · S03

A loan from Moscow to Dubai

Lid Capital was incorporated in Moscow on 3 October 2024. Its memorandum describes a company that sells bonds to fund Lidoil’s projects. Lidoil’s principal and interest payments are meant to service the issuer’s own debt. The Council calls the companies affiliated. The financial documents name different legal shareholders. The Basis-17 foundation owns Lid Capital, according to the memorandum, and Horizon Maritime Associates Holding Limited is Lidoil’s sole shareholder, according to its accounts. Any common ultimate controller remains unverified. S01 · S04 · S14

Note 4.1 to the Russian accounts says the loan agreed with Lidoil on 2 December 2025 was fully disbursed. It pays 13.8% a year, 0.30 percentage points above the first bond’s coupon. That spread is contractual. Over its first reporting period, ending 31 December 2025, Lid Capital made a net loss of 5.681 million roubles. At year-end the loan receivable from Lidoil and the long-term bond debt each stood at 520.325 million roubles on the balance sheet. The balance sheet and the 515.4 million roubles of placement payments capture different dates. S02 · S03 · S04

A separate loan ran the other way. Lid Capital’s accounts show an advance of 11.6 million roubles from Tongoil, another Dubai company, still outstanding at the end of 2025 under an agreement dated 30 October 2024. The Council refers to a loan agreement between the two companies in 2025. The accounts establish who lent to whom under the contract they describe. The difference in dates remains unresolved. S01 · S03

Dollar debt paid in roubles

The second issue, from 3 to 28 August 2026, sold 117,025 bonds with a nominal value of 11.7025 million dollars. The exchange reported payments of 994.9 million roubles. Together the two issues raised 1.510 billion roubles in gross payments against 18.354 million dollars in face value. For the second deal, the records show a new loan to Lidoil was authorised and the placement completed. Evidence of the onward disbursement remains outstanding. S02 · S05 · S06

The second issue’s terms separate the value of the debt from the payment currency. Each bond has a face value of 100 dollars. Principal and coupons are paid in roubles at the official Bank of Russia rate specified in the terms. At 13.5% a year, a 30-day coupon comes to 1.11 dollars per bond after rounding. On 2 November 2026, Lid Capital reported paying 89.76 roubles per bond for the third coupon, or 10,504,164 roubles in all. The reported payment predates the EU sanctions. S07 · S08 · S11

The bank the documents identify is Gazprombank, appointed to place the second issue. That role concerns the sale of the bonds. The ultimate bondholders and the banks that executed the first transfer to Lidoil remain unverified. In March 2026, Asari Legal, a law firm that worked on the earlier deal, described how it was structured and how Russian government approvals were obtained. The law firm was a party to the deal’s preparation. S10 · S15

Lidoil’s profit and its cash

Lidoil’s standalone IFRS accounts report 3.727 billion UAE dirhams (AED) in 2025 revenue. The shipping line accounts for about 2.4484 billion and trading for 1.2785 billion. Lidoil booked a net profit of AED 120.4 million while its operations used AED 108.3 million of cash. An increase in trade receivables absorbed AED 325.7 million in the cash flow statement. Sales can enter the accounts while the money is still due. S14

The adjustments for depreciation and accrued interest bring the subtotal to AED 161.3 million before movements in operating assets and liabilities. After the increase in receivables, the decrease in other current assets and the increase in trade payables contributed AED 56.1 million between them. The prior-year balance for other current assets included a loan to the shareholder. Working capital helps explain the cash that sits in bills awaiting payment. The full reconciliation also includes items outside trade receivables. S14

Financing brought in AED 55.8 million. Cash fell from AED 81.1 million at the start of the year to AED 28.6 million at the end. The auditor issued an unmodified opinion. The operating cash outflow measures one year. The ability to repay also depends on future collections, debt maturities and money from funders. S14

The risk runs back to bondholders

Lid Capital expects interest and repayments from Lidoil to service its bonds. A persistent delay at Lidoil would reach the issuer. For the second issue, Lidoil, renamed Lidoil FZCO on 1 June 2026, published an irrevocable purchase offer with conditions, including certain payment delays of more than ten business days. In August 2026 ACRA classed the issue as senior unsecured debt and rated it A−(RU) on its Russian national scale. The offer creates an obligation under its terms. A claim secured on a tanker would require separate collateral. S04 · S08 · S09 · S13 · S14

The documents linking Lidoil to tankers

The Council says Lidoil uses shell companies to manage vessels that carry Russian oil and engage in high-risk shipping practices. It names Spikoil as a manager acting for Lidoil. Two British records from 2025 link Lidoil to Seabass and Spikoil to Marquise in a combined owner/operator field. Those are the links identified by the sanctioning authorities. To tie a particular loan to a ship would require a bill of sale, the buyer’s identity and proof of payment. S01 · S16

In November 2025, ACRA reported a plan for six additional tankers and about 160 million dollars of prospective bond funding. Lidoil’s standalone 2025 accounts record zero additions to property, plant and equipment. That line covers this company and this year. It puts a documented boundary around the comparison with the announced plan. Transactions elsewhere in the structure or in 2026 remain open. S12 · S14

Scope of the investigation

This investigation uses exchange disclosures, bond terms, the companies’ audited accounts and sanctions records available on 19 November 2026. The figures describe reported receipts or identified contracts. The documents stop short of a bank record for the transfer, allocation of the loan to a shipping transaction or evidence of the sanctions’ effects on subsequent payments.
