The bill after the salvage

Near Al Qibliyah, Oman’s transport ministry described a tanker listing on the rocks with limited power on board. The maritime press later reported that unloading had begun. Salvors work on a timetable set by the sea and the condition of the ship. Compensation moves at the pace of evidence and decisions. In the Caroline Bezengi case, the IOPC Funds Director’s view concerns the obligations of the 1992 Fund. The applicable war risk policy, if any, and the payments for salvage still need to be documented.

The IMO number in the opening chapter let us recognise one hull under three names. These cases have no equivalent identifier for financial obligations. The lender records a loan in its accounts. A seller’s invoice and its books identify an amount owed. An insurance policy sets out a guarantor’s obligations. To find out who benefits from a deal or carries the loss, a reader has to match different parties’ records to the same transaction.

That gap between a name and a payment runs through the book. Naming a shipowner’s main shareholder takes the story to Hong Kong and leaves the size of the stake open. A Moscow bond sale leaves a trail as far as a loan to a Dubai company, where the published accounts reach their limit. It is possible to measure a large volume of token transfers while the underlying invoices remain unidentified. Each file calls for a different next document, whether it is a shareholder register, a bank record, an invoice matched to a payment or a policy backed up by evidence that a claim was settled.

The grounding gives those distinctions a material consequence. People have to go out and do the work while liability is still being examined. When compensation under the Civil Liability Convention falls short, the 1992 Fund can cover eligible oil pollution damage. Businesses that receive oil above an annual threshold help finance the system. In a case that qualifies, the cost can therefore travel well beyond the tanker and its owner.

The next sanctions notice or salvage report brings us back to the question raised by Arrow: whose right or obligation does this document establish? For the case off Oman, the decisive record could be a payment statement or the terms of a guarantee. Those records could identify who paid, which work they funded and how much of the bill they covered.
