  1. Chapter 1, figure 1

    A vessel sale changes the registered owning company in the example shown. Selling shares in that company can retain the same registered owner while changing control.

    Two theoretical scenarios, without reference to a real vessel. On the left, a vessel sale from company A to company B leads to a change in the registered owner in the example shown. On the right, a sale of a controlling shareholding in company S changes its shareholder while S can remain the registered owner. The vessel’s IMO number stays the same in both cases. Identifying ultimate control requires tracing the ownership chain and control rights; this diagram does not attribute control to any person.

  5. Chapter 3, figure 1

    The model described by the sources matches import and export payment needs around pools of rouble and foreign-currency liquidity.

    Illustrative offsetting mechanism; no amounts or quantities are represented. An export sale links a Russian exporter with a foreign buyer. The buyer supplies foreign currency to an overseas liquidity pool; the exporter receives roubles from a pool in Russia. An import links a Russian importer with a foreign supplier: the importer provides roubles and the supplier receives foreign currency. The A7 ledger reconciles payment obligations between the two pools. Dashed arrows represent bookkeeping, not a cross-border transfer. Orders are pooled rather than matched one for one. Each pool requires liquidity and rebalancing. Oil is one possible source of foreign currency among other exports. Sources S01: A7 FAQ consulted 19 November 2026; S03: NCA August 2026 pp. 4–5; S04: FinCEN 5 October 2026 p. 63213. No particular vessel, bank account or customer is represented.

  7. Chapter 4, figure 1

    The Blue Card records the insurer’s or guarantor’s undertaking, on which the competent State bases its CLC certificate. After damage occurs, the convention provides for direct action against the insurer or guarantor, with defined defences.

    Before an incident, the insurer supplies a Blue Card, the competent State issues a CLC certificate and the ship presents it for inspection. After an incident, victims may bring a direct claim against the insurer or guarantor. Cover depends on the cause, territory and admissible damage. Certification and compensation are separate processes.
