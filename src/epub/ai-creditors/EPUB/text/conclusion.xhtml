Following claims through to payment

By the end of this investigation, AI financing has acquired names, instruments and dates. Public records remain fragmented, yet some connections can be followed into a creditor’s accounts. Six funds report holdings in the same CoreWeave loan at September 30. Their schedules, reconciled in the final chapter, document part of the balance outstanding. That partial coverage separates an identified claim from a relationship still awaiting evidence. It also supplies a method for extending the search to further portfolios and counterparties. [B15]

One of those funds, Blue Owl Technology Finance, leads to bank lenders through Athena III. The documents establish the subsidiary’s financing and the banks’ commitments. Extending that path to a particular CoreWeave claim requires the composition of the pledged portfolio. The map leaves that final connection open. Each level nevertheless has its own debtor and contractual rights, with capital and assets that may absorb some deterioration before the lender suffers a loss. The terms determine where those resources sit and who can claim them. [B03] [B04]

Guarantees widen the inquiry to parties promising payment after a specified event. They can help a project proceed and distribute losses among institutions equipped to bear them. Their value depends on coverage, timing and the guarantor’s resources. In Big Sky, the reimbursement arrangement benefiting AHL adds another claim to examine. The third party’s identity and the remaining covered purchase obligation are still needed to assess its economic amount. The available disclosure identifies a route by which money could be called upon. [B05]

The ECB’s November 18 publication puts sector-based lending at about €75 billion, or 1.1% of euro area banks’ corporate commitments. A named selection of 165 firms and their subsidiaries accounts for €24.3 billion, including €11 billion undrawn, using data compiled up to June 2026. The populations overlap only partly, with neither contained within the other. Reconciling them requires identification of the entities and their loans. [B06]

An aggregate banking measure addresses only part of the question. Creditors can hold debt, finance its holder or supply protection. Each position has a particular sensitivity to the same project. Ranking and collateral can limit a lender’s eventual loss. A liquidity constraint may arise earlier, when a borrowing base shrinks or a new draw becomes conditional. The order of payments therefore deserves attention alongside the size of commitments. Protection can preserve creditor resources while changing the borrower’s room to manage the next stage of construction. [B03] [B12]

The contracts provide practical checkpoints. At CoreWeave, some resources are reserved for power-related obligations, while DDTL 5.5 links funding to physical and commercial conditions. At Applied Digital, the quarterly report says the escrow release condition was satisfied in June. These stages need separate tracking: the promised protection, the assets allocated to support it and an executed payment each answer a precise question. Reading the sequence helps explain how a financing can progress and where a further document is needed. [B01] [B02] [B08] [B09]

The picture can remain compatible with losses that investors and institutions are able to absorb. On November 16, Michael Barr expressed relatively less concern about direct losses given the debt accumulated so far, while calling for attention to leverage and counterparties. His assessment places the mechanisms within a system that also has capital and protections. A stress scenario requires evidence about its starting conditions and the resources available to meet obligations. Its outcome depends on those inputs. [B07]

The next developments will appear in investment schedules, loan registers, acceptance dates and renegotiated terms. Financing may continue, claims may change hands and protections may remain unused. A delay or a fall in receipts could also reveal creditor ranking and recourse in practice. This book offers a consistent thread through those developments: identify the obligation, find its holder and follow the resources that will enable it to be paid.
