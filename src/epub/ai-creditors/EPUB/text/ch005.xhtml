Following AI debt through insurers’ investments and guarantees

Published: 2026-11-17T21:40:40+01:00

Original online edition

AI’s creditors · 4/6. Previous instalment: the bank loans behind AI’s private lenders. Explore the series in our AI financing dossier. Documentary investigation with an information cut-off of 17 November 2026.

Project Miner surfaces in a report on credit insurance. In May 2026, broker WTW reported that several insurers were nearing their sector limits for data centres or had already reached them. Miner and Jacquard were among the projects insurers frequently described as fully allocated. The report leaves those insurers and their project exposures unnamed. S01

Miner is also known as Jupiter, the New Mexico campus that opened this investigation. A grading-permit application explicitly links the names. The matching names connect WTW’s observation to the same campus, extending the map beyond the banks already identified. S02 S01

Athene’s accounts open a different trail. The insurance group reports investments in computing infrastructure, a guarantee from its holding company and an agreement to reimburse part of any payments under that guarantee. Mapping financial exposure to AI means following invested money alongside promises that could require cash in the future. S08 S09

Insuring a loan held by a bank

On 11 November, The Banker summarised Financial Times reporting that Barclays had bought cover, HSBC had explored the product and JPMorgan had considered it before deciding against it. The FT’s information came from unnamed sources. The publicly accessible summary identifies neither the insurers nor the policies and insured amounts. S03

With loan non-payment insurance, a bank gains another source of recovery after a covered default: the insurer owes compensation under the policy’s terms. The bank can keep the loan and its interest income while sharing part of the credit risk. Protection may also reduce the regulatory capital required when it meets the applicable prudential conditions. S04 S14

Contract wording determines how much that promise is worth. The bank needs to examine the losses covered, its disclosure obligations and the requirements for payment. Britain’s Prudential Regulation Authority explicitly expects lenders to assess these conditions and the residual risks of disputes or settlement problems. S04

Timing can matter just as much. In an answer published in 2019 and reviewed in 2021, the European Banking Authority accepted that a guarantee can continue paying on the loan’s original schedule. A contractual clause may make the entire loan immediately repayable by the borrower, while the insurer remains bound to scheduled payments if the guarantee excludes that acceleration. The bank then has credit protection whose cash payments arrive over time. S05

An insurer can pass some risk to a reinsurer. It still owes the bank, but now relies on another institution to recover part of its own payments. International supervisory principles call for these relationships to be examined together, including the resulting concentrations and liquidity needs. S07

Identifying the customer behind the project company

Willis Re’s October report, prepared for the January 2027 reinsurance renewals, describes an identification problem. The name submitted to a reinsurer may be an SPV, a dedicated company borrowing to build a data centre. According to the broker, that legal identity can obscure the project’s business and the tenant supporting its revenues. Several contracts can appear under different names while relying on the same major customer. S06

In a lease-based structure, the project company owes the loan and its customer’s rent helps fund repayments. A lender needs to understand both parties to assess the credit. The contracts then determine the rights against each: the lease sets out the tenant’s obligations, while any guarantee specifies the debts it has agreed to cover. S06

One site may also carry construction, property, cyber and credit insurance. Each policy has its own triggers. A fire claim could help the operator restore revenue, whereas a lasting drop in demand for computing would affect its income through another channel. Analysis needs to connect those economic scenarios with the relevant policies’ limits and exclusions. The IAIS risk-management guidance addresses interactions between assets, obligations and economic conditions. S07

Willis Re’s warning concerns accumulation: a portfolio of differently named borrowers may offer less economic diversification than it appears to. The broker describes a potential information problem rather than measuring its frequency or reporting losses at a named insurer. S06

Athene also owns the securities

Athene, Apollo’s retirement-services business, quantifies a separate route into the sector. In its 30 October presentation, the group reported $12.9 billion of funded digital-infrastructure investments at 30 September 2026, roughly 3% of the gross invested assets used in the presentation. This management measure covers more than AI and includes noncontrolling interests in its ACRA co-investment vehicles. Its denominator differs from total accounting assets. S08

The portfolio combines chip financing, property loans, energy and bonds issued by large cloud companies. A footnote illustrates why credit classifications need careful reading: some commercial mortgage loans are assessed by reference to the tenant’s credit quality. Athene expressly distinguishes that assessment from a guarantee in its favour. S08

Different instruments can consequently depend on the same technology company. It may rent one facility, buy computing elsewhere and issue bonds held by investors. Connecting those relationships helps identify shared sources of repayment while preserving the specific rights attached to each claim.

The holding company’s purchase guarantee

Financing equipment for Anthropic makes this map more concrete. On 9 June 2026, Apollo announced an initial $35 billion transaction with Broadcom, Blackstone and banks. BNP Paribas and UBS appear among the A1 tranche’s arrangers, alongside Citi and Wells Fargo. Those roles establish their involvement in organising the financing. The banks’ final retained exposures remain undisclosed. S10

The September accounts describe WarehouseCo Intermediate Holdings LP, or WHCO, as carrying an obligation to purchase computing infrastructure. An affiliate, Big Sky Funding LLC, provides secured financing. Athene reports $691 million of debt securities issued by Big Sky and a further $4.9 billion of investment commitments. The note separately reports other interests through consolidated vehicles. The $691 million therefore describes one investment category, with $4.9 billion still to be invested within that scope. S09

A separate note sets out an undertaking from Athene Holding Ltd., or AHL, the group’s holding company. AHL guarantees 15% of WHCO’s outstanding purchase obligation if WHCO defaults on that obligation. The group expects committed financing to meet the purchases as funds are drawn, by 2028 at the latest. A third party, unnamed in the disclosure, would reimburse AHL for 35% of any amounts it actually paid under the guarantee. S09

The percentages apply in sequence to different bases: outstanding purchases for the guarantee, then any AHL payments for reimbursement. Calculating a dollar amount requires the covered purchase balance at the relevant date. The initial $35 billion transaction amount does not supply that balance.

The reimbursement agreement gives AHL a further counterparty exposure. Assessing that recovery would require the third party’s identity, resources and payment terms. The disclosure describes a reimbursement agreement whose classification as reinsurance remains unestablished. The guarantee covers equipment purchases, whereas the bank insurance discussed earlier covers non-payment of a loan.

This guarantee had already appeared in the June accounts, filed in August. At 30 September, Athene considered payment not probable because committed financing was expected to meet WHCO’s purchases. It therefore recognised no liability for the guarantee. The note nevertheless identifies a contingent obligation and its trigger, allowing the holding company’s commitments to be traced beyond recorded debt. S11 S09

Banks sometimes finance the protection

A broader market mechanism also connects banks to insurers taking credit risk. In an interview published on 30 October, ECB Supervisory Board member Pedro Machado described non-bank platforms repackaging synthetic securitisations. Insurers assume the notes’ credit risk while banks finance their purchase. The ECB observed the trend emerging in late 2024 and growing in 2025. S12

Through these significant risk transfers, or SRTs, a bank shares potential portfolio losses. If another bank lends to the investor providing protection, that financing creates a banking connection to the transferred risk. Machado described a development worth monitoring and said he had seen no particular instance of the risks he discussed materialising at that point. S12

Protection can, however, draw on money already available. The Basel Committee’s February 2026 report describes transactions as generally fully funded or backed by initial collateral equal to the protected amount, with exceptions including some insurers. Those resources reduce dependence on a future payment. The report also details safeguards introduced since the financial crisis, whose effectiveness depends on each structure. S13

Mapping obligations and rights of recovery

Insurance can distribute risk among institutions with suitable capital and expertise. Willis Re’s October report describes abundant overall reinsurance capacity. That broad supply can coexist with limits already reached on individual projects or at particular insurers. S06 S07

The documents reveal several relationships with AI financing: loan protection, securities holdings, investment commitments and purchase guarantees. In Athene’s case, they connect a computing transaction with bank arrangers, group investments and a holding-company undertaking. Each link has its own instrument, date and measurement scope.

The borrower’s debt and its creditors’ rights of recovery provide complementary views. Adding them would count some financing more than once. A guarantee can change who bears a loss and the payments due between institutions while leaving the operator’s debt unchanged.

The documents needed to allocate potential losses are now identifiable: bank policies and their reinsurance cessions, the outstanding purchase balance guaranteed by AHL and the identity of its reimbursement counterparty. Connecting those records would also help measure the dependence of different creditors on the same customers.

Limitations

Sources were checked on 17 November 2026. This instalment develops our earlier investigation into AI loan insurance with market reports and Athene’s commitments. WTW and Willis Re are brokers with a commercial interest in the market. Apollo and Athene describe their own transactions. SEC filings contain unaudited interim accounts. This investigation uses public documents, without interviews or access to confidential policies.

The publicly available Banker summary repeats the FT’s bank reporting, providing a secondary account rather than independent confirmation. No identified document connects Athene to those policies or establishes their use of the SRT platforms Machado described. The US and Bermuda Athene case documents international links without measuring the European insurance sector.

The $12.9 billion gross investment portfolio, Big Sky securities, future commitments and WHCO guarantee involve different instruments or scopes and remain separate in the analysis. The May report dates the capacity survey to January but supplies no precise date for the Miner comments. The figures distinguish the generic mechanism from Athene’s disclosed arrangement.
