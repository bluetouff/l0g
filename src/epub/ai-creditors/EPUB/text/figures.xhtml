  13. Chapter 5 · FIG. 02

    A contractual trigger can retain residual cash at waterfall step six and block new advances. If it persists across three consecutive monthly payment dates, DDTL 5.5 requires prepayment within five business days.

    Under DDTL 5.5, a continuing material customer-contract breach that has not been waived in writing and permits termination, or a projected contract-value ratio below 2.40, creates a Cash Trap Event. Residual cash is retained at the sixth waterfall step, after eligible expenses, interest, principal and the liquidity reserve. New advances also require the absence of such an event. If it persists for three consecutive monthly payment dates, the contract provides for prepayment within five business days using cash in collateral accounts, to bring the ratio above 2.40, limited to cash held there. The detailed numerator formula is redacted. This is a contractual mechanism; the investigation establishes no activation. Conditions may be waived by authorised lenders.

Amounts are initial allocations, not current balances. No reserve exhaustion or full delivery is inferred.
