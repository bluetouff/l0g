Chapter 4

Trump’s CoreCivic trade and the Appleton insider trading question

Published 2026-11-26T13:26:05+01:00 · Updated 2026-11-26T15:48:07+01:00

Original online investigation

The US government had until 5 p.m. on April 13, 2026, to walk away from buying a prison in Appleton, Minnesota. Its review of the site was covered by a confidentiality agreement. That day, Donald Trump’s disclosed portfolio bought shares in CoreCivic, the company that owned the prison. In August, the government bought it for $495.6 million. S04 S38 S41

The timing raises an insider trading question. Local news had reported since 2025 that the prison might reopen as an immigration detention center. The agreement detailing the government’s purchase came out in August. Did the person who made the April trade already know something about the deal that would have mattered to investors? US insider trading rules apply to the president. S28 S41 S57 S58 S60

A shuttered prison and two deals

Appleton had been idle since 2010. On August 3, CoreCivic won the contract to operate it. The next day, it sold the building to the government. Washington became the owner and agreed to pay CoreCivic to run the site. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, or ICE, was expanding its detention capacity. For an operator like CoreCivic, that created business on both sides of a sale. S04 S41

Congress had funded the expansion in July 2025. Over a few months, the administration paid $3.6 billion for eight facilities owned by CoreCivic and GEO Group. Trump had reported owning shares in both companies. ICE bought three sites from GEO in one deal at Adelanto, California, and GEO kept running them. In Tacoma, it also kept the operating contract and announced the day after the sale that it planned to put some of the proceeds toward buying back its own stock. S01 S02 S04 S07 S12 S13 S20

CoreCivic had already started an accelerated share repurchase after its four property sales. The company used some of the cash from the government to take shares out of circulation. Investors who keep their shares can end up owning a larger piece of the business. To see whether Trump’s portfolio benefited, the account records would have to show how many shares it held at the time and the price of any later sale. S04 S05 S06 S20 S22

The price Washington paid

The Appleton sale agreement fixes the dates. The appraisal would explain the price. Minnesota’s register lists exactly the same figure for the buyer-paid appraisal and the sale, without the report itself. CoreCivic says appraisers hired by the government factored in replacement cost and depreciation. Minnesota lawmakers have asked the department for the full file, including records of a much lower earlier offer. Other lawmakers have asked the inspector general to review the purchases in Tacoma and Adelanto. S15 S37 S40 S43 S51

There is a separate question about fees. The Appleton and Tacoma agreements name CBRE as the broker paid by the sellers. NBC has reported allegations that former adviser Corey Lewandowski sought a fee tied to GEO’s contracts and that the company refused. His representative denies that he made the request. The public records tie CBRE to the sales and the Lewandowski allegations to GEO contracts. An illicit payment connected to these purchases remains unsubstantiated. S36 S41 S48 S49 S50

The decision to buy for Trump’s account

The Appleton paper trail goes back to February 26, the date CoreCivic and contractor SK2 put on their access and confidentiality agreement. Trump’s portfolio sold CoreCivic that day, while two other company sites were under review. After the April purchase, the disclosures show another sale in May. The same portfolio traded GEO as its federal detention business expanded. Trump’s portfolio sold GEO shares on the day the company announced a new ICE contract for Rivers, a facility it continued to own. The forms give the dates of the trades. They leave the time of day and the decision-maker open. S21 S22 S38 S39 S41 S53 S54 S61

The White House says outside managers make investment decisions through models designed to track market indexes. It cited the Schwab 1000. The fund that tracks that index held neither CoreCivic nor GEO as of May 31, though other Schwab funds held both. The account mandate would show which model was actually in use. In August, Elizabeth Warren, Robert Garcia and other members of Congress asked for account mandates and records of trading decisions made for the president’s portfolio. S25 S56 S62 S63

The April 13 order is the record to get, down to the time it was entered and who or what triggered it. That would give investigators something to match against the acquisition team’s communications. Those records can show who knew about the Appleton deal when Trump’s portfolio put money into CoreCivic. S38 S41 S56 S57
