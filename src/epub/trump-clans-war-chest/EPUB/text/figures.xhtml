List of infographics

  1. Chapter 1, figure 1

    Simplified path for federal payments processed by the Bureau of the Fiscal Service (BFS). OMB apportions funding upstream. The DOGE team access examined by GAO involved Treasury systems further down the chain.

    Congress appropriates funds. OMB apportions them. The agency incurs the obligation and certifies the payment request. The Bureau of the Fiscal Service processes the payment and sends it to the Federal Reserve for settlement. The DOGE access examined here involved Treasury systems.

  2. Chapter 1, figure 2

    The Government Accountability Office (GAO) selected 14 security controls to assess DOGE team access from January 20 to April 11, 2025. Each square represents one control. Findings published April 28, 2026.

    Of the 14 security controls selected for its review of DOGE team access, GAO found 5 fully implemented, 8 with gaps in implementation and 1 not applicable.

  3. Chapter 1, figure 3

    The site that went offline hosted OMB’s decisions on the apportionment of federal funds. This timeline ends with publication restarting in August 2025. Intervals are schematic.

    The Office of Management and Budget’s website was taken offline on March 24, 2025. A U.S. district court ordered publication to resume on July 21. OMB resumed publishing apportionment documents on August 15.

  4. Chapter 2, figure 1

    Fictional example. Termination releases $15 million before payment, with no termination costs. The funds stay in the same account. Reuse depends on the appropriation’s purpose and period of availability. Moving funds to another account requires statutory authority.

    Fictional example in millions of U.S. dollars before and after termination without costs. Cumulative payments stay at 40. Unpaid obligations fall from 20 to 5. Unobligated funds rise from 40 to 55 after a deobligation of 15. The law and the period of availability govern reuse.

    The President proposes rescissions on September 25, 2026. GAO puts the expiration date at September 30 and the earliest end of congressional review at November 9. The final disposition of the funds is unverified.

  6. Chapter 2, figure 3

    GAO documents the transfer from the Navy’s working capital fund through the Pentagon’s counterdrug account. The split between the two uses comes from the military officials it interviewed. The lines trace the transfer and its reported purposes.

    The Pentagon transferred $447 million from the Navy working capital fund through its counterdrug account. Military officials said $442 million was for ICE detention support and $5 million for reimbursements related to military detention spending at Guantánamo.

  7. Chapter 3, figure 1

    Buyers pay into the custody account when their licenses require it. Venezuela retains ownership under Executive Order 14373. Venezuela’s central bank (BCV) submits requests. The State Department directs disbursements, and Treasury executes them. The operating steps follow the preserved copy of the KPMG report.

    Buyers subject to OFAC license requirements pay into the custody account. Venezuela retains ownership. Its central bank submits disbursement requests, the State Department issues instructions, and Treasury carries them out. Funds go toward foreign exchange operations with commercial banks or to approved vendors.
