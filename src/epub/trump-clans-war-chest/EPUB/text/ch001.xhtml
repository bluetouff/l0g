Before the money moves

On May 19, 2025, the Pentagon took $447 million from a Navy operating fund and moved it through a counterdrug account. Military officials told congressional auditors that most of the money was intended to support immigration detention. That transfer, traced in chapter two, gives the investigation a place to start. The name of an account tells us why it was set up. Finding out which policy the money served takes the records that authorized its movement.

A few months earlier, a DOGE employee assigned to the Treasury sent a file of USAID payments to colleagues. Auditors reconstructed the transfer, identified the employee’s system permissions and checked how they were used. That case opens the first chapter. Between a vote in Congress and the settlement of a bill, people decide when an agency can sign a contract, check payment orders or see the underlying data. Their authority depends on where they sit in the process.

Venezuelan oil proceeds reveal another kind of control. Venezuela still owns the money, while the United States controls some distributions. In the detention cases, Washington buys facilities from operators that can keep earning money from running them. In Appleton, the planned deadline for a confidential property review fell on the same day as a stock purchase in Donald Trump’s disclosure. That puts a concrete question about inside information to the people who can produce the order record and the acquisition team’s communications.

Factory financing, a potential export licence and the draw of an invitation to a gala can also affect the value of a private business. Each involves different actors and different rules. In each case, the investigation looks for the right or opportunity that was granted, the business that could benefit and the records needed to measure the benefit.

The final chapter returns to Congress. In February 2026, money approved the previous summer let ICE continue funding detention while regular funding for its department lapsed. That episode gives the book’s title a practical meaning. Spending power rests on decisions made at different times. A new majority can take office after the vote that opened the funds, before the contract is signed or well before the last bill falls due.
