Public records and the limits of this edition

This edition, dated 8 November 2026, brings together the seven investigations in their documentary state of 7 November. The individual article dates are retained. A bibliographic clarification of the release date of the American wiretap report was verified on 8 November; its figures and reporting period are unchanged. Its original introduction and conclusion synthesise those chapters while separating observations, interested parties’ positions and the analysis’s recommendations.

Legislation, parliamentary work, company publications, court documents, studies and surveys answer different questions. Statements remain attributed to their originating actors. A provider’s description of a capability and a legal requirement retain their documentary scope, separate from a complete observation of implementation.

Signal, EncroChat, ANOM and San Bernardino retain the dates and limits of their cited records. Telecommunications precedents remain separate from insured losses. Chinese documents describe rules within particular fields and jurisdictions; they establish no general capability to read communications.

Statistics retain their units, periods and populations. The European survey measures views, while the AES study presents an estimate under stated assumptions. This edition establishes neither a global backdoor-loss figure nor a general solved-case rate or causal return on an unspecified architecture.

Chapter bibliographies contain 160 reference notices per volume. Several notices point to shared material, so this is a count of chapter references rather than independent documents. All twenty-one infographics retain their captions and methodological qualifications. Fourteen glossary entries and references for the synthesis pages support return visits to the concepts and evidence.

Access limitations and historical versions remain explicit. The initial LADC methodology comes from an FBI document preserved by an unofficial archive; the form used in November 2026 remains outside the verified material. Online articles may receive later corrections. Canonical links lead back to their current versions.
