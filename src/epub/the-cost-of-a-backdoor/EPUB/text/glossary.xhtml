The language of access and oversight

End-to-end encryption

Communication protection in which content is encrypted on the sending device and decrypted on the recipient’s device. In this architecture, a relay carrying the message handles encrypted content. Confidentiality also depends on participant authentication, device security and the handling of backups and copies. Metadata may remain available, depending on the service.

Backdoor

A mechanism deliberately introduced to allow access by bypassing protection normally provided by a system. It may involve an additional key, a hidden function or another privileged access route. That deliberate introduction distinguishes it from an accidental vulnerability. Its reach and the risk of misuse depend on the architecture and controls.

Recovery key

A secret used by a service or application to regain access to protected data. The copies it can unlock, required permissions and additional protections depend on the architecture. Its storage needs to be planned alongside backups and restoration rights. Copies already obtained require separate assessment when keys are changed.

Forward secrecy

A cryptographic property limiting the effect of a later compromise of secrets on earlier exchanges. Its guarantee depends on the protocol, the mode used and deletion of the secrets needed to reconstruct earlier keys. Histories retained or backed up by the application have their own protection. Protecting future exchanges after a compromise is the separate property of post-compromise security.

Audit log

A record of events useful for examining a system: access, activation of a function, permission changes or delivery of data. Its value depends on the events collected, their timestamps and protection against unauthorized changes or deletion. Separate storage and distinct administration rights help preserve the evidence when a system is compromised.

Least privilege

A security principle limiting authorized access to what a person or process needs for its assigned tasks. It applies to data, functions and service accounts, including during system development and operation. Its effectiveness depends on the permissions actually enforced and reviewed. Separation of duties complements this principle by assigning certain functions to different roles.

Client-side encryption

Encryption performed on the customer’s device or within its environment before data is sent to the cloud provider. Its effectiveness in limiting provider access depends on control of the keys and the software used. Metadata and some search or collaboration functions need separate assessment.

Reversibility

An organised means of taking a service back in-house or transferring it to another supplier, with the data and other resources needed to keep it working. It requires people, technical capabilities, funding and contractual arrangements, supported by tests suited to the service.

Remediation

Actions intended to regain control of a compromised system and restore sufficiently secure operation. They include containing the intrusion, removing the attacker, eradicating persistence mechanisms and rebuilding. Backups and restored assets need verification. Restoring the service and assessing information already copied outside the organisation are separate tasks.

Loss accumulation risk

The possibility that one event causes losses for several insured parties, including when they depend on the same service, software or infrastructure. An insurer may then face simultaneous claims. Assessment requires identifying dependencies and the cover concerned. A breakdown of claim amounts by company size does not establish that the losses share a common cause.

EPOC

The certificate used to transmit a European Production Order for electronic evidence to the addressee under Regulation (EU) 2023/1543. It contains information identifying the request and competent authorities. Receipt starts the Article 10 deadlines, with different branches for notification and emergencies. Production concerns data held by or for a provider. Recital 20 specifies that the regulation introduces no duty to decrypt those data.

Chain of custody

Procedures and records used to follow evidence through collection, transfers, storage and analysis. For digital data, they document provenance, handling and preservation of integrity. Tool validation and the ability to repeat operations complement that traceability. The legal weight of the evidence also depends on the rules applicable to the case.

Validation scope

The elements and conditions actually examined in a validation. In NIST’s CMVP, the certificate identifies the cryptographic module, its version and, where applicable, its operating environment. The security policy describes functions and conditions of use. Embedding the module in another product does not automatically validate that whole product or establish that it uses the module correctly. The reach of a security assurance therefore needs checking against its validation documents.

Counterfactual

An estimated scenario describing the path that would have occurred without an intervention. It provides a reference for assessing an additional effect and depends on the assumptions chosen. In the AES study it describes the path towards another standard without NIST’s programme.
