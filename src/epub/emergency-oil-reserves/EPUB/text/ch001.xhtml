Introduction

Follow the fuel all the way

An oil reserve starts with a quantity. Its usefulness depends on a journey. Between a political decision and fuel available to a consumer, rights must be exercised, contracts agreed, facilities mobilised and prices formed. This investigation follows those stages while preserving the dates and limits of the public documents used to describe them.

Chapter one opens the tanks. Crude oil and refined products wait at different points in the industrial chain. A crude cargo still needs processing; finished fuel can enter the network further downstream. The product stored, its quality and the available equipment determine which operations remain. Refinery yields and product inventories retain their respective units throughout.

Chapter two follows ownership and money. In France’s system, owning the fuel, operating the tank, financing the purchase and authorising a release are different functions. Legislation, SAGESS documents and its bond terms allow part of that structure to be reconstructed. Company statements remain attributed to the company; the book includes no independent inspection of the depots.

Logistics then brings the emergency down to a local scale. An administrative transfer can precede collection and delivery. Loading rates, transport availability and stations’ continuing sales matter along the route. Chapter three uses dated historical observations from 2022 among other sources to explain why a national stock total is insufficient to describe local availability.

Chapter four enters the refinery. Installed capacity, utilisation and output of the required fuel answer different questions. US series, Donges notices and French refined-product trade each illuminate part of the system. Their scopes remain separate: additional diesel is not mechanically inferred from nominal capacity, an announced maintenance operation or an annual trade balance.

Chapter five reads the release terms. Selling oil, exchanging it for future repayment and making products available create different obligations. A solicitation ceiling, an awarded quantity and an actual delivery represent distinct stages. A contractual premium in barrels remains a quantity commitment, without being converted into an annualised yield or an estimated profit.

Chapter six reaches the pump. Crude and refined-diesel markets, exchange rates, distribution contracts and taxes transmit changes on different timetables. A price decline observed before an announcement cannot be attributed to it. A gross margin includes costs. Measuring the effect of a release also requires asking what the price would have been without it, a question descriptive series alone cannot settle.

This edition brings together the six available instalments, their complete texts, references and twenty-four infographics. Its documentary cut-off is 3 October 2026. The online series is announced as seven parts; this book collects the first six, with an original introduction and conclusion. It offers a route through the public evidence, without a physical stock audit, operator interviews or a new estimate of the impact on prices.

The mechanisms and references behind this overview are developed in the six chapters.
