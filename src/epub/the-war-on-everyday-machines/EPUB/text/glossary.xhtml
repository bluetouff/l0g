Sixteen terms for following the machines

Operational technology (OT)

Digital systems that monitor or control a physical process, such as refrigeration. Security has to account for physical operation, availability and personal safety.

Target temperature

The target value requested of a control system. It differs from the temperature measured by a sensor and the temperature reached within the stored product.

Cold chain

Maintaining the temperature conditions needed to preserve food during production, storage, transport and sale. Requirements depend on the product and applicable rules.

Supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA)

Systems that collect process data, display operating conditions and enable specified control actions. Functions can be distributed across components; supervision and local control have dependencies that require examination.

Remote maintenance

Maintenance performed remotely that can modify equipment settings or programs. It differs from observation-only remote diagnosis and from routine remote operation.

Reversibility

The ability to change a solution or supplier while retaining the functions and information needed to operate. For machinery, this depends in part on access to configurations, documentation and relevant expertise.

Timestamping

Assigning a date and time to an event. Comparing logs requires knowledge of timezone, clock offsets, precision and the coverage of the records.

Cyber attribution

Analysis linking cyber activity to operators or an organisation through technical and contextual evidence. Identifying activity, a person and a sponsor involves distinct conclusions.

Business interruption loss

The consequences of interrupted operations, including lost contribution or margin and some additional expenses. Economic calculations and the definition in an insurance policy must each be read within their own scope.

Insurer subrogation

Following payment, the insurer succeeds to the insured’s rights against the responsible third party, up to the amount paid and subject to legal conditions. The recovery action has its own outcome and timetable.

Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)

EU legislation on the cybersecurity of digital products within its scope. It organises product requirements, vulnerability handling and reporting, with distinct application dates.

NIS2 Directive

EU directive addressing cybersecurity risk management and reporting at organisations within scope. Activities, size and other criteria determine coverage; national transposition has its own timetable.

Support period

Under the CRA, the period during which the manufacturer must handle vulnerabilities in the covered product. Its end date differs from the availability period of previously issued updates and from the final customer’s purchase date.

Minimum viable operations (MVO)

The level of activity that lets an organisation operate safely, meet obligations and retain stakeholders’ trust during disruption. It is defined through services delivered and the resources they require.

Redundancy

Provision of another resource able to take over a function. Its value depends in part on possible common failures: two devices may share administration access, power or another dependency.

Service continuity

Arrangements to preserve priority functions and restore services during disruption, taking account of people, suppliers, dependencies and operating conditions.
