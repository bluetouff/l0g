Twenty-one terms for following recovery

Free cash flow

Cash flow remaining after the investment cash outflows included in a company’s definition. The measure may contain issuer-specific adjustments. Its reconciliation, period and scope must be checked; it differs from accounting profit and the cash balance available on a given date.

Working capital requirement

Funding absorbed by the timing of inventories, customer receivables and operating payables. Rising inventories or receivables can use cash, while increasing payables can defer an outflow. The contents and sign of a reported measure require checking. Resuming production may require rebuilding inventory before the associated receipts arrive.

Supply chain finance

Arrangements in which a financier advances payment within a buyer–supplier relationship. Finance may be based on an approved invoice or, in the JLR programme examined here, a purchase order before invoicing. Eligibility, fees, the final debtor and reconciliation against the invoice depend on the agreement. An announced limit differs from an actual payment.

Dependent business interruption

Interruption of an insured business caused by an incident at an organisation on which it depends. Coverage follows the relationship defined in the policy: suppliers, IT providers and customers may fall under different provisions. Covered events, exclusions, time conditions and limits also depend on the contract. Provider-related wording establishes no coverage for a customer’s outage.

Time excess or waiting period

A time condition in an insurance policy governing when specified business-interruption losses become eligible, under that policy’s precise terms. It is distinct from a monetary deductible and a coverage limit. Its expiry establishes no date for settlement or receipt of cash.

Value added

The value of output less the intermediate inputs used to produce it. It measures an activity’s contribution to economic production while avoiding repeated counting of purchases between businesses. GDP is obtained by adding taxes on products and subtracting the corresponding subsidies from aggregate value added. Revenue and value added are different measures.

Base effect

The influence of the starting level on a measured growth rate. A rise from an unusually weak month can appear large even when activity remains subdued. Reading a recovery requires attention to levels, the comparison period and cumulative output as well as the rebound percentage.

Gross domestic product

A measure of economic production within a territory over a period. The production approach adds gross value added and taxes less subsidies on products. A sectoral volume index, a manufacturer’s sales and a modelled economic cost have different scopes from GDP.

  • A guide to the UK National Accounts: March 2020 · Chapter 4, reference 9

Minimum viable operations

A reduced set of activities an organisation aims to maintain or restore during disruption. Functions, service levels, dependencies and acceptable controls need to be specified. These operations may be available before the full service returns and require validation in their intended setting.

Incident-response retainer

An advance agreement for access to specialist incident-response capacity. An offering may combine an annual commitment, mobilisation priority, prepaid days and charges for time worked. Response times, included work, surcharges and limits during concurrent demand depend on its terms. Reserved capacity differs from completed recovery.

Service availability

The ability to use a function within a stated scope and under specified conditions. After an incident, a restarted component may still need validation, another service or reconciliation of temporary transactions. Availability needs to be described by function and user group; an active component leaves the complete service to be assessed.

Dealer management system

Software supporting dealership operations, which may include sales, aftersales and accounting depending on the installation. Several functions can depend on one provider. In the CDK case, dealership disclosures distinguish the return of core applications from ancillary functions. A DMS outage establishes a dependency without proving an intrusion at every customer.

Common dependency

A resource, service or provider on which several organisations depend, directly or through intermediaries. Disruption can affect multiple users simultaneously and increase demand for shared alternatives. Assessment needs to cover the functions involved, further dependencies and replacement capacity. Concentration can also bring benefits that require consideration in context.

Exit capability

The organised ability to bring an activity back in-house or transfer it to another arrangement. It requires usable data, rights, skills, known dependencies and a destination capable of running the service. A planned migration can illuminate that capability, while its timescale and conditions differ from an emergency switch during an incident.

Loan guarantee

A commitment under which a guarantor covers a defined part of a lender’s risk on contractual terms. The bank provides the loan and the borrower remains responsible for repayment. Issuing the guarantee, making a facility available, drawing it and any eventual payment by the guarantor are distinct events. Coverage may apply to only part of the loan.

Maximum guarantee liability

The maximum amount of claims payable under a guarantee commitment, including interest in the UKEF definition used here. It differs from loan principal, exposure, estimated expected loss and sums actually paid after default. A register entry needs to be matched to a contract before assigning its amount to a specific transaction.

Bridge loan

Financing intended to cover a transition before refinancing, a cash inflow or another longer-term arrangement. Its existence, availability and drawdowns require separate observation. JLR’s bridge facility documented in its accounts is distinct from the loan supported by the October 2025 UKEF guarantee.

Refinancing risk

The risk that debt must be replaced on less favourable terms or that the funds needed at maturity cannot be raised. It depends on maturities, access to credit and market conditions. An available facility can affect financial room for manoeuvre while remaining undrawn; its precise effect on decisions requires separate evidence.

Recovery time objective

The target time for restoring an IT resource to limit the consequences of its unavailability. An RTO is a planning objective that needs a defined service and scenario. It must allow for the processing and validation required within the business’s maximum tolerable downtime. A stated target differs from a duration observed in a test or incident.

Maximum tolerable downtime

The length of disruption a business process can tolerate before consequences become unacceptable to the organisation. MTD concerns the business process and must accommodate resource recovery and the work needed to make operations usable. It helps set priorities and compare technical recovery objectives with business requirements.

  • NIST SP 800-34 Revision 1 : Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems · Chapter 8, reference 4

Recovery point objective

The point in the past to which data must be recoverable after an incident. Expressed as an interval relative to the disruption, RPO describes the data-loss window accepted in planning for reconstruction or treatment. It differs from the time needed to restore a system. Transactions after the backup, including manual records, require reconciliation.
